Before he could turn Tookyzyl’s head,
Before he could steady himself and thrust his spear,
Brave Seytek, upheld by the spirits,
Had already wheeled his horse around.
Before a word could be said,
Before Tookyzyl could close his mouth,
He wheeled about with a shout,
Raising his battle cry,
And before he could thrust at Seytek,
The descendant of the leopard-lion,
The dark-maned, dauntless lion,
The hero of surpassing courage,
Who had ridden into battle there,
Against that mountain-sized villain,
Against that warrior versed in every ruse,
Against that snow-demonic infidel,
Against that mighty, full-blooded champion,
Who deemed himself a hero and a lion,
Who vaunted himself before the whole world,
The matchlessly nimble warrior,
Turned Kulansur’s neck,
And the spear inherited from his forefathers
The heroic prince leveled.
Such was his valor,
His forefather was a mighty, peerless lion,
And there they met in combat.
At the monster-born warrior,
At the infidel who smeared the earth,
“The face of his belt,” he thought,
“The edge of his right shoulder;
If the tempered spear goes lower,
The rim of his ribs;
Here lies the crown of his heart,
His lungs, heart, and windpipe;
If the tempered spear strikes true,
The very core of his heart—
That is the measured mark;
If God wills that it pass through,
His hour of death has come.”
Thus he stood, measuring the spot:
“Support me, my sovereign father;
Straighten whatever swerves,
Guide it true as you desire.”
Such was his consummate skill
As he leveled the wrathful spear,
Straining every sinew,
Hardening himself in fury,
His two strength-giving arms
Gathering all their wondrous power,
His lip bitten through,
His waist bent and body braced,
His rib bones creaking,
His lion eyes flashing,
Swooping like a hawk,
And in that noble lion himself
Unseen wisdom suddenly arose.
The breath from his mouth
Hissed like a speeding bullet,
And the white teeth beneath his brow
Scattered like grains of millet.
The deft lion sprang aside,
And once more within the hero
Unseen wisdom suddenly arose;
He readied himself, spear in hand,
Praying, “O God!”
With matchless agility,
He drove it forward with a sharp crack,
And struck the mountain-huge boar
With a resounding blow.
When he aimed at the measured mark,
When he struck the appointed place,
The tempered spear Bölököñ had made—
Such was that spear:
Torn from a young birch,
Bent true by strongmen,
Perfected by master craftsmen,
Wrapped in mare’s hide,
And hafted by Bölökbay.
