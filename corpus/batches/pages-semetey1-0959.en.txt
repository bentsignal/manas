He lays flat whoever attacks him.
Konurbay, khan of Beijing,
Would flee without facing him;
If he faced Manas's heroic son,
He would fall dead.
If Toltoy and Kojo
Charged in with spears and cries,
He would throw them into turmoil.
Broad-chested and wide-breasted,
He was a majestic lion;
Among the giants upon the earth,
He was a warrior fit to stand alone.
Never turning back before death,
This gray wolf, let alone Toltoy,
Would not retreat even from hell.
No one could come against him;
Whoever faced him would die.
Tall and broad-shouldered,
Both champion and lion,
She had found the man destined for her
And stood there amazed.
He was an exceptionally born lion,
Tiger-hearted and thick-necked;
Fortunate Semetey
Was a red fortress of iron
Established for these Kyrgyz.
Standing proud and taking none as his equal,
Refusing to count any man his peer—
Such conduct suited the gray wolf.
That she had sought so long without finding him
Was a God-stricken affair.
Could there be a man who would not join
In true amusement and delight,
Who would not lie playfully pinching
And teasing the gray wolf?”
Aychürök rejoiced greatly;
Sitting and looking sidelong,
Filling her gaze with his bearing,
She sat looking at him imploringly,
Filling her heart with him.
The mount beneath him,
The peerless courser Narbuudan—
If you look to his origins,
He was kin to Kyz Saykal's Algara.
He was born of the same stock as that horse.
Manas had taken a liking to him as a foal,
When he was brought as a gift.
“Someone may cast the evil eye,” he said,
And had an owl plume fastened to his head;
He had him raised in a dark place,
And many judges of horses admired him.
He curved his neck like a stag;
When game appeared within bowshot,
He sprang without hesitation.
Gold ornament was laid on him by the foot,
And khans who saw him asked for him.
He was not given to those who asked;
Far from being given away, not even
A ritually unwashed person had ridden Buurul.
He was a horse raised with honor;
Anyone who slighted him became an outsider.
Narbuudan stood apart;
The White Falcon was on his arm,
And the White Hound followed him.
Semetey, son of Manas,
Loomed like Opol-Too;
Wherever Chürök saw him,
Her heart broke away from her:
“His eyes are like the depths of a lake;
If Konurbay came openly,
He could swallow him with all his countless host.
If Chynkojo, son of Shygay,
Came to contest him, he could slaughter him;
Toltoy, son of Bagysh,
He could leave utterly destroyed.
I flew over Uch-Tum, Beijing and Bakburchun;
I saw the great men of those peoples.
Ever since then I have wandered,
