His companion destined for the next world.
“Khan Bakai!” he called.
“You who find words in sudden danger,
Who find sight in the darkness,
My lion uncle, come here!
Auspicious when placed before me,
Like a countless host when behind me,
My lion uncle, come here!
My countless army on a journey,
My sure aim when entering a spear fight!
When Khan Bakai rides on patrol,
He is my rugged black mountain.
He feeds the hungry belly,
And restores me when I am worn thin.
Uncle Bakai, my elder,
Did you see what I saw?
Did you learn what I learned?
Seething and writhing like worms,
His horses fill the land.
The men who guard those horses
Seem to number nine hundred.
He has livestock of every kind,
And among his horses
There are tulpars too.
The vast herd has no discernible pattern;
Among those horses,
What is there, and what is not?
Merciless turmoil—
Is this not a place to unleash it?!
Raising a battle cry and leveling the spear—
Is this not a place to thrust it forth?!
Driving the horses with cries ringing out—
Is this not a place to pursue them?!
Clearing away the herdsmen—
Is this not a place to slaughter them utterly!”
When Semetey said this,
Lion Bakai answered:
“My dear only one,
The infidels are as numerous as ants,
And hero Kongurbai is a great warrior.
Against infidels like black worms,
Even the strongest cannot prevail
In fighting hero Khan Kongurbai!
Come with fierce wrath,
And come heeding your uncle's words!
The Chinese people beyond the ridge—
If they surge forth, they are a flowing flood.
One cannot match them in battle,
Nor overcome them in wrestling.
The reason they will not come is this:
From the squared pass and cliff,
We alone have emerged from the nest.
Parted from their horses,
Külchoro and Kanchoro,
Your boys, remained back there.
You have no support on whom to rely,
No place where you lie entrenched!
We revealed our sluggishness;
We let Muradyl, son of Kyrym,
Raid the horses from among us,
The hero who was our support at the waist,
We left him with the baggage train.
With the accursed Chinese people,
If we grapple back and forth,
There is no army to give us support.
In my old age, I, Bakai,
Have none of the people I longed to bring;
To stand and fight without fleeing,
I no longer have my former power.
I have no strength to do battle;
My foal, for you
I have no means to serve as support.
I was keen, but I have grown weary;
My twin was lion Manas;
Parted from my match,
My noble one gone, I have aged.
I was sharp steel, but I am chipped;
This wretched life has passed,
