When she saw brave Töshtük,
The old woman neighed,
“Reap, my reaper, reap!”
And made ready to reap him down.
Giving him no choice,
The Töshtük who had come—
She made ready to suck him dry.
The old woman grew wrathful
And made ready to imprison him.
She made ready to beat Töshtük
With resounding blows.
Unable to withstand her strength,
Dear Töshtük grew alarmed.
He whipped Chalkuyruk beneath him
This way and that,
Spurring him on both sides.
Brave Töshtük fled at a gallop
And reached Zhelpinish.
In fear, he fled down
Beneath the earth.
Among the people beneath the earth,
He came to know many.
Brave Töshtük suffered torment
And spent seven years there.
It had been about seven days
Since that Töshtük came
To Kökötöy's memorial feast.
Since he emerged from beneath the earth,
Seven days had now passed.
The giants Koshoy and Töshtük were there;
Brave Koshoy, a master of wisdom,
Had then come to Karkyra.
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Had tethered the horses in the Sart manner.
For honored guests such as these,
He boiled the meat of barren yearlings
In the cooking pits.
He spread out in full
Every delight of the broad world.
These were Bokmurun's words:
“From all the mighty giants
This is the place where I seek counsel.
The giants Koshoy and Töshtük are here;
I have no grievance against God,
For at my dear father's feast
There are men enough to oversee it.
Bokmurun has the might
To surround the four corners
Of the whole world four times.
My father has four kinds of livestock,
Spreading like ants,
For us to slaughter.
I seek the counsel of your people,
Uncle Koshoy, Baymyrza,
And all of you gathered here.
These are the words of counsel I give:
I will strike the Koroson fire-steel;
I will invite every one,
Infidel and Muslim alike.
Of the red-collared Chinese,
Let not even the least remain;
Of the black-collared Kalmyks,
Let not one among their people remain.
From the Shibee and Soloon,
From the Tyrgoot and Manchu,
Let not a single one remain behind.
Of all people upon the earth,
I say let every one come.
If, after hearing it, through some mishap,
They should happen not to come,
Let them see Kökötöy's red banner
With its golden finial above their yurts.
From hollows where no maiden has been seen,
Let not one soul remain;
From the bends and uplands,
Let no people remain without seeing it.
Say they must come to this feast of mine;
If they do not come to this feast of mine—
