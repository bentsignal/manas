Dust will fly in the wind.
Dust clouds will stream and billow;
The people who have gathered
Will shout, unable to find water—
Would that not be utter hardship?
If we go to Suusamyr,
The land on both sides is narrow;
The points of spears will clash,
And people's heads will knock together.
The people will not fit;
They will stand and quarrel with darkened brows.
Its slopes are steep, with no open ground;
The confined land of Suusamyr
Has nowhere to race horses.
The people will crowd without fitting;
It will become a wretched affair,
The uproar deafening the ears.
The people will seethe in crowds;
Your multitude, unable to fit,
Will throng in disorder.
May a black day not befall them
As they steal fire-steels and knives.
I have seen many lands,
Wearing down my horse's flanks,
Roaming and traveling
Around the whole wide world.
There is a place named Karkyra;
That land surpasses all others.
There is no pit to stumble into,
No bank eaten away by water,
No stone to crack a shin.
From every ravine, roaring,
A thousand springs pour down.
In the hollow of Ükürchü,
Along Üch-Kapkak,
There is ground for digging hearth pits;
The broad valley of the Kegen River
Has space along it to race horses
And room to hold great festivities.
The valley of Üch-Karkyra—
That is the place to hold the feast.
Bokmurun, if you heed my words, we shall see
The lances bend
When heroes joust there;
Infidels and Muslims alike
Will gaze in amazement.
There the festivities will be held.”
Dear Koshoy at their head,
Those were the words he spoke.
Koshoy went, and I went;
That was when I stood watching.
Koshoy went, and I went—
Alas, my friend Chynkozho,
When did you ever go with us?
Having heard the elder's words,
Khan's son Bokmurun,
Hearing that Karkyra was good,
Hearing a discerning man's account,
The nobleman's son Bokmurun
Heard that bountiful Karkyra
Was an exceedingly fine summer pasture.
He heard its grazing livestock grew fat,
And that Karkyra, the place for his feast,
Had every blessing in full.
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Issued a decree to the Kyrgyz in Tashkent,
Gathering his strength,
And whoever refused to heed him—
