I found my own husband.
Setting aside my weariness, unfortunate as I was,
I entered your village.
I hung my necklace round my neck;
'Let him lie with you for six days,' I said,
And begged his wife.
Though I begged, the woman would not agree;
She would not listen to my words.
Still weary, unfortunate as I was,
To the shores of Ala-Kol
I went as a white swan.
'If my dear one is a man,
He will come in search of me,' I thought;
Along the shores of Ala-Kol,
I remained a white swan
And your noble one's Akshumkar
I snatched away.”
Saying this, Aichurok,
Her white throat glowing,
Her moonlike face shining,
Your king's Buurul
To a plane tree in the light breeze
She tethered now with his head drawn up.

As he laughed and played,
His thundering lord
From the companion, beset by trouble,
Now slipped entirely out of mind.
That rogue who had never seen a girl,
Seeing girls, grew interested;
Unconcerned and heedless,
'My mighty lord has been left behind'—
The lad thought nothing of it.
That wayward Kulchoro,
With thirty young wives and thirty girls,
And Aichurok in their midst,
Played without a care.
The girls' conversation was delightful;
Absorbed in their games,
His lord slipped from his thoughts.
With the girls who had caught his interest,
Let us leave the boy thus.
Having lent Buurul and been left on foot,
Lord Semetey, the courser—
