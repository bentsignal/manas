Kokotoy's Bokmurun
Issued another proclamation.
The Chinese Tukubay,
The hairy-soled champion,
Stepped out to wrestle.
"No one who grapples
With a wrathful infidel prospers;
No one who challenges him survives," they said.
Holy Khan Manas
Was troubled.
Before much time had passed—
God spare us such a sight—
The saintly Khan Bakay,
Agybay of Argyn Khan,
Chubak of Akbaltay,
Almambet of Azoozil,
Jamgyrchy of the Esheks,
Hero Toshtuk, son of Eleman,
Urbush of the Kipchaks,
With an armful of weapons at his belt,
Muzburchak, son of Buudayyk,
Kokcho of the Kazakhs,
Synjybek of Andijan,
Akun Khan from Afghanistan,
And the two giants Janaaly and Karach,
Who dwelt in Chambyl-Bel
And were called peerlessly mighty heroes—
The Muslim people were there,
And heroes who boasted of courage
Had all arrived together.
All the khans assembled;
Destroyer Khan Manas was there,
And all the saints were there.
The infidel champion who came out
Could seemingly tear apart
Any man he grappled with.
His bulk was half a mountain;
He looked able to devour his opponent.
"What can be done?" they asked,
And the Muslims were distressed.
They gathered together
And could find no solution.
Poor prophetic Kanykey
Walked with measured steps
Into the midst of all those people.
Your poor prophetic kinswoman
Sat down then
And began an ominous speech:
"A person has many turns of fortune.
When I was fourteen, still a girl,
Destroyer Bakay was there too;
My gray-maned lord went to Bukhara
And left a scar upon my breast.
Gray-maned heroes, you stand here;
I still have much to tell.
Yesterday, to the city of my father Temir Khan,
Loading gold coins on eighty camels,
Saying, 'I will bind her head in marriage,'
My padishah lord went.
When he became an in-law, slaughtered livestock,
And made his obeisance,
My father Temir Khan
Had the gold poured out
And stored in the treasury.
When he gathered khojas and mullahs,
Made the marriage rite at high noon,
And eighty brides and eighty girls
Spread bedding from the waist-high dais,
They brought me there with ceremony,
Intending to lay me down.
When I reached the drawn curtain
And was about to lie down,
The white tinte bought
For sixty foals and forty sheep
Leapt from its sheath,
And grazed your wrist:
"Before I have even taken this shrew,
She has wounded my wrist," you said.
