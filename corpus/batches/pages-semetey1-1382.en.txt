Bucked and screamed like foals;
Every one of their halters
Snapped apart,
Bursting wholly into pieces.
The wide world became narrow,
And then the face of the earth
Lay under snow up to the saddlecloths.
(The gray wolves longed
For a single glimpse of the earth’s face.)
Crusted snowdrifts gathered in the hollows;
Living creatures and beasts
Were struck by hail and killed.
Semetey’s Narbuurul
And Khan Bakai’s Kökcholok—
Those two alone remained tethered.
As for the other horses,
Kulchoro and Kanchoro
Turned them loose and drove them on.
The whirlwind of the weather raged fiercely;
Muradyl had brought this calamity.
All fourteen horses together,
Muradyl, the son of Kyrym—
That scoundrel drove them thundering
And raided every one away.
He reached them all together;
Muradyl drove all fourteen horses,
Beating them onward in a single herd,
And raided every one away.
He concealed them at Chaar-Zhon,
Drove them over Üch-Kashka,
And made them cross Sary-Zhaz.
All fourteen horses,
Muradyl raided and took away,
Reaching his home together with them.
The mist scattered from the steppe;
A cold wind blew with a shriek,
And God’s day cleared.
Black steam rose billowing from the earth;
From the wooded hollows,
Larks sang and trilled.
The clouds dispersed one by one,
Breaking apart and going in every direction,
And the world appeared bright again.
Your elder Bakai, that noble lion,
Came out of the tent and looked;
Seeking to know whether the horses were safe,
He saw Kökcholok and Taibuurul—
Those two remained at the tethering place.
They champed upon their bits
And slapped their armfuls of tail
Against their hind legs.
Flattening the right ear,
They flung the crust of the black earth
Up toward the sky.
Two thoroughbreds remained safe;
Apart from them, the fourteen horses
Could nowhere be caught by the eye,
And no one knew where they had gone!
Those fourteen horses were gone;
After the fourteen horses,
The two orphans too were gone!
Then old Bakai
Tore at his beard and flung it aside:
“Alas, if only Manas had not died,
So Bakai need not live to see this shame!
If only God had taken this old man,
And this wretched soul had not lived into old age!
While my mighty Manas was alive,
If only this miserable Bakai had died,
And, never parted from those falcons,
Had looked upon the face of Judgment Day!
If one rode for six months upon the road—
Alas, on such a grievous day,
If only the horse would never tire!
Until he departed for the other world,
If only a hero would never grow old!
Why did I not long ago behold
The narrow road of Judgment Day?”
