From the beginning, God had upheld him.
When extinguished, his line flared anew;
With every death, it multiplied,
And whenever diminished, it broadened.
He was the descendant of a blessed lion,
The tempered point of keen steel,
A precious lion who never faltered,
A gray-maned hero who knew no grief.
He sliced through valleys and turned them to fat,
And when grievously provoked,
He laid even his own kinsmen low.
The peerless sultan Semetey—
This was Semetey’s descendant,
Who grew to manhood among the Zhediger.
He was nurtured by the land and became mighty;
When his wrath was roused,
Black blood flowed down the slopes.
Of the many enemies who fixed him with hatred,
How many were laid to rest!
Brave Seytek, lion-born,
Prepared himself like a panther.
From the fairies came the heavenly steed,
So that precious Seytek,
When he armed himself, might ride against the foe
And strike them without hindrance.
Kulunsur, who came from the fairies,
Whom no wind could overtake at a gallop
And no livestock could match at a walk,
Was broad-chested and wide-breasted,
With a distinct back and a sharp-ridged spine;
When he entered the fray, he stirred up turmoil.
He would not stand still when tethered,
And when prey appeared a bush-length away,
He sprang without swerving aside.
His tail was distinctive and his spirit fierce,
His frame colt-like and his back straight,
His straddling legs were like ravines,
And the white blaze upon his brow was like a tooth,
Like steel beaten by a smith.
His poll was like a ewer,
His triple-folded withers
Could bear a six-span cauldron
If the precious vessel were set upon it.
When fat, he was slender as a lance,
When leaping, nimble as a flea,
His calf was thick as a child’s waist,
And his stride was like the wind of the highlands.
A wild ass that swerved aside,
He could turn and trample as though devouring it.
No winged thing could flee and escape him,
And unless a dark calamity befell,
No enemy in battle could capture him.
Kulunsur, the thoroughbred steed,
Chewed barley when it was given,
A creature of splendid animal form,
Whose eyes shone like an argali’s.
He cracked wheat beneath his teeth;
Perfectly fashioned Kulunsur,
Whose eyes shone like a wild ram’s.
That day, precious lion Seytek
Took off his saddle and left him bare-backed.
With a great hobble fitted with golden handles,
He hobbled and tethered him,
Securing him with an extra tie.
He drew in the ill-fated steed’s flesh
By tying him down tightly.
His tether was golden at the hitching place,
Glittering and gleaming,
While in Akbermet’s palace
Seytek reveled in boundless delight.
For ten days Seytek did not stir,
The hapless, radiant hero,
But lay with the fairy maiden.
Your noble-born Bermet—
The purest wearer of a flowing gown,
Whose speech no words could equal,
Was herself the foremost among women.
Her brow was radiant and her words were gold,
Her tongue was sugar, the finest nightingale,
And when you looked upon her, she was the brilliant Morning Star.
