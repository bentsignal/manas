The bravest was at the front,
And the black-horse rider at the edge.
All those bearing spears
Rode ahead and separated;
Champions who struck with battle-axes
Appeared along either flank.
Those skilled at drawing bows,
The finest marksmen with guns,
Spread out and inflicted hardship.
Those who would not bend beneath a whip
He separated as champions;
Those who would not flinch beneath a sword
He separated as formidable men;
Those who would not flee
When a black musket cracked,
Those who would not start
When a fierce gun snapped,
He gathered and drove together.
Chynkozho, son of Shygay,
Kozho, resolute in his deeds,
Had begun a great upheaval.
If we do not give Chürök in tribute,
An army comes that will overrun us;
When he rages and attacks,
Mighty Toltoy comes,
One who will begin the great tumult.
All the Zhediger,
With the accomplished elder Tölök,
Brandishing their spears,
Had made themselves ready.
The Zhediger had gathered
Around Buloo Khan.
If we do not accept his demand,
If we do not give Aychürök,
They will overrun us utterly and attack,
Turn water into their orchards,
And make gardens of our land;
They will channel water all around,
And every one of them will spur in,
And emerge everywhere alike.
If we do not give Aychürök,
If we do not accept his words,
They will demolish every house,
Cut down and topple the willows,
And raze it all smooth.
In Akun's old age,
Saying, "He will not give his old maid daughter,"
They will humiliate him!
Azhybay and Tümönbay—
They will enslave us both
And send us to tend their horses!"
So saying and tormenting himself, Azhybay,
After he had seen this,
Lost the strength of his arms,
And the courage left his heart.
The Zhediger had begun their affliction;
When he saw this display,
Azhybay proved a coward
And nearly fell
From the top of the tower.
Taking the shortcut down the tower,
Tümönbay led Azhybay down,
With the greatest struggle,
Until he had brought him below.
Azhybay, terrified and distraught,
Pleaded with Tümönbay:
"My dear Tümönbay!
Because of Aychürök,
Mortal danger has come upon us;
With mighty Kozho at their head,
An army comes that will overrun us.
If we do not go out to meet them,
Driving eighty fat barren mares,
All of them matching gray beasts,
And carry them as tribute,
Halt their great army,
And gain a seven-day reprieve,
