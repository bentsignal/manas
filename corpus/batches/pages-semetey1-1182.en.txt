Noble prince and lion,
I have a matter for your counsel.
I am mint sprung from the water;
From your Sultan Kökötöy,
I am the only one left.
When my valiant father passed,
I had no life apart from him;
Left behind by my father to bear the burden,
I have no younger brother beneath me for support.
I have the swift horse Maaniker;
There is no charger besides him.
Nor have I elder kin behind me;
I have only my solitary life.
Across the four corners of the world,
I have livestock surrounding me.
After my dear father passed,
After he departed without turning back
To the world from which none return,
I shall institute contests for the people.
How can I remain empty-handed
Without arranging contests?
For Khan Kökötöy's memorial rites,
Look, assembled people:
I have strength enough for the people;
If I held feasts for another five years,
I have livestock enough to last.
Prepared for the horse-race prizes,
I have nine camel loads.
I have ninety thousand barren mares;
I have a feast to give the people.
I have sheep beyond counting;
When told, I have such wonders.
I have cattle beyond counting.
I had fire struck for Koroson
And invited every one,
Infidel and Muslim.
Horse trainers who tether horses,
Competitors who race horses—
For my dear father's memorial feast,
You have gathered in great numbers.
I have seventy divisions of people;
To all the accomplished people,
I have words to speak today.
For my father's memorial feast,
I shall slaughter countless black-blazed mares;
I shall slaughter tens of thousands of camels.
I shall load goods upon camels and offer them;
I shall offer slaves together with their children.
The treasury filling forty rooms—
I shall divide and offer twenty rooms of it.
The people and khans are here;
To rich Bokmurun's words,
All of you listen closely.
There are prizes for one hundred nine horses;
Where is there a prize like this?
I shall make a thunderous commotion;
For my dear father's memorial rites,
I shall turn my wealth to ash.
I have spoken boasting of all I possess;
For my dear father's rites,
I shall spend my wealth.
Let no people gossip afterward;
Out of sparing it from my father,
May not even one yearling remain to me.
People who have come to the feast,
For my dear father's memorial feast,
Leave my two and stake one;
Leave my other and stake my family.
Stake my people migrated from Tashkent.
If my wealth begins to fall short,
My dear people,
Stake me as the chief prize.”
Bokmurun of Kökötöy
Before he had closed his mouth,
Before he had finished his words,
The bell was struck firmly;
The red camel of service
Came crowding in,
