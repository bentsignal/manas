There on the banks of Talas,
At the feast of hero Seytek,
The Russians ate their fill and departed,
Saying, “If this Seytek lives,
The eighteen thousand worlds
Will marvel at him.”
The Chinese ate their fill and departed,
Saying, “If this Seytek lives,
Upon every crooked mortal
He will bring destruction.”
For nine days they had
The treasury doors thrown open.
To orphans, the poor, and widows,
To paupers who owned no livestock,
They scattered wealth lavishly.
His generosity was revealed;
To the hungry and destitute,
To the weak and impoverished,
Without valuing wealth by so much as a hair,
They measured it out with a whip,
And ordered it given to them.
They set the people’s hearts at ease
And made rich all who had lacked.
They had Kushkun and Durkun sing,
And thus delighted the people.
Their hearts surged with joy,
And many among the people reveled.
Each solitary soul became as a thousand,
And each presence shone like the Night of Power.
“Father Seytek, hero Seytek,
May he be the bulwark of the people,”
Many among the people blessed him.
“We escaped certain slaughter,
We were spared an open grave;
From the wretch who ruled in our fathers’ day,
We escaped his wrath,” they said.
The people’s hearts had been oppressed;
“Let the people proclaim his glory,” they said,
And the people’s hearts were stirred.
They appealed to Khan Seytek,
As though now destined to see wonders,
And pronounced their blessing,
As though he would allot each one an inheritance.
Spoken words cannot describe their dignity;
Among the people giving blessings
There were saints and every kind of soul.
Among those pronouncing blessings,
Some wore white turbans large as cauldrons,
And when they cried out, their voices rang like the call to prayer.
Seated, they were broad as hearths;
Their mouths seemed thick as forearms,
And their haunches broad as an embrace.
They were masters of royal encampments,
Khans of many different kinds.
Crying “Allah!” they raised their hands
And turned their faces toward the qibla.
Their inspired spirits surged;
Was he perhaps a saintly hero,
An interpreter of moon and stars,
A companion of the magnanimous lion,
Ready to render faithful service
And raise the fallen standard again?
He had been born into this world
As though he alone had come
To help lion Manas prevail,
And provide him wise counsel—
A herald upheld by the spirits,
A saint in whom all gifts converged.
The time came when he could no longer rise.
The old man had indeed been a hero.
Was any man ever so long-lived?
His ears had grown deaf and unhearing,
His eyes blind and unseeing.
Dear uncle Bakai
Had become level with the black earth,
His beard still full upon his chin.
That year, for Bakai,
The hour came to depart on his journey,
To leave this world without returning;
His time to go had come.
