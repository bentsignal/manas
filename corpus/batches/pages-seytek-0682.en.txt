Almambet’s sword Joikuma—
Encountered by divine chance,
Found upon the open steppe.
Laid upon grass, it set it ablaze;
Whatever it struck was swiftly destroyed.
When brandished with a cry against the foe,
Its edge glowed red with fire;
When swung against a formidable enemy,
It lengthened to forty fathoms.
Whenever it was drawn forth in valor,
It never returned without shedding blood;
Until it had drunk its fill of blood,
It would not go back into its sheath,
Its green-hued edge gleaming.
No enemy who faced it
Ever remained alive.
Such was the nature of the sword.
When swung against a formidable foe,
It satisfied the surging hunger for battle;
The enemy who faced it
Was subdued in spirit.
Behold the sword Joikuma,
Joikuma drawn naked from its sheath;
He appealed to the spirits
Of the heroes who had vanished,
Your warrior Külchoro.
To bring Sarybai down,
He recalled what such an enemy was.
From Almambet, who passed away long ago,
Syrbarang remained as a bequest;
Almambet of former days
Had received Syrbarang as a sacred prize.
Its might was heavenly steel,
A possession of Chylaba,
The khan of Beijing.
In a garden planted in rows,
Beside the Khakhan’s palace,
He had turned from the black faith to the white,
Fleeing the infidels and becoming an exile.
He had it fitted with a black-hair cord,
And approved it for leveling at the foe.
He had it plated in armor,
And, lest it warp or soften,
Had it wrapped in leather.
To strike the enemy without breaking,
Great David himself had tested it.
When he clenched and struck with it,
No opposing enemy remained standing.
When he cried out and thrust the spear,
It split whatever place it pierced.
The foe who timidly received it,
Though clad in armor, died.
Külchoro’s ornate spear
Had singular torment and mighty venom;
It bore the vapor of diamonds and rubies.
Wherever it struck, the wound was no light matter;
It held a dragon’s poison—
Such was the nature of the spear.
Külchoro’s battle-axe—
Its socket was steel and its edge was sharp,
Its haft was overlaid with gold,
And poison was spread upon its blade.
Its edge was joined with steel;
Any enemy it struck directly
Collapsed like a mountain.
When he brought it down with full force,
Not only men, wretches—
Cliffs, mountains, and rocks came crashing down.
The youngest of sixty nobles,
The favorite of Alooke—
The infidel son of Kaldai,
Your warrior Khan Kongurbai,
Had fallen beneath this axe.
Eskara, khan of the Manchus,
Had fallen beneath this axe.
It had dyed its socket in blood,
And was truly suited for war.
