The gray wolf traveled on in high spirits,
Taking the road toward the Kakanchin.
From the iron-gated fortress,
Just as the hero was leaving
Kanykei's palace,
He wore upon him
A double coat of ring mail.
At the mouth of the gate,
Your gray wolf happened to meet
Ayçürök, Akun's daughter.
The unfortunate daughter of Akun
Had many a sorrow!
With the beauty of an owl's plumage,
With a voice like a nightingale's,
Breathing musk and fragrant copper,
She spoke smoothly and asked,
Showing a white throat like an argali's,
Noble lady Ayçürök,
Her white breast rising.
The unfortunate daughter of the peri
Had a grace unlike mortal women;
The forlorn woman's words
Were sweeter than honey.
She walked with a soft, graceful stride,
Her eyes shining like the morning star.
Her white throat gleamed,
Her moonlike face shone.
Her gold earrings, large as bowls,
Sparkled upon her breast;
When she turned, she gave off perfume.
The renowned lady Ayçürök,
Turning like a peri-born beauty,
Walked in sorrowful grace,
Wrapped in a fine elechek.
Arching like a lynx,
Reluctant to utter her words,
She held herself like a white doe
And began to speak of every concern:
“My prince, you have set out on a six-month journey!
Let us come out to meet you on your return;
You have set forth to the people of the dark day—
Turn and speak to us before you go,
So that we too, though women, may hear!
You will fight the Kakanchin;
You have begun the journey to Beijing.
You have left your peerless wife,
The infidel woman of surpassing wisdom,
And sixty sisters-in-law widowed,
Leaving your camp desolate!
You set out without telling your secret;
My valiant one, we are hurt by you!
They say Beijing is a distant land;
What living soul inhabits Beijing?
Radiant Beijing—
No enemy who faced it ever prevailed.
From what I hear of the Kakanchin,
They are a people from whom none return!
Even your khan father did not go against
Those seething infidels.
No one who challenged
Kakanchin's Beijing remained alive.
They say the road to calamitous Beijing
Is one from which none return;
Even sultans entered but could not conquer
The grim-visaged Chinese!
Turn Buurul's head around;
Do not struggle, my prince—stay!
They say assembled Beijing
Has never been overcome by mortal man.
The heroes could not wreak slaughter,
