Double-ringed mail with lark-eye links,
And white coats of mail.
If the clamorous Chinese foe appeared,
Ravaging the people and ruining the nation,
If some dread calamity arose,
We entered battle without tiring.
When our stomachs grew hungry,
We carved and ate the horse’s haunch.
If a foe came to seize our gathered wealth,
We entered battle without flinching.
While our gray-maned champion lived,
We asked, ‘Where could calamity be?’
Now I have no mountain ridge to gird me,
No sheltered place where I may hide,
No crag where geese wheel overhead,
No dark mountain where ravens cry.
Who now will sharpen
The black-steel Zulfiqar without letting it sway?
Who will lead the honored Kyrgyz,
Without bringing grief upon the dear nation?
Keng-Kol thick with tamarisks, great Talas—
Will your lands be left desolate?
Golden homeland of the white-capped Kyrgyz,
Oh, my child, will your people be left desolate?
You have mountain rams before you;
Where is a stronghold like you, broad Talas?
Though all six worlds came at once,
Where is a hero like you, noble lord?
You were the defender of the nation;
When the fierce hero was our shield,
To whom were we inferior?
While the warrior’s eyes were open,
We said no calamity could come.
When bored, we hunted deer;
When not, we lay at ease.
We poor souls, raised in freedom,
Lay down whenever we pleased.
When our dear spirits surged,
We stirred turmoil throughout the world.
As for the enemy who faced us,
While our valiant gray-maned hero lived,
We dashed his brains against the stones.
Whenever we wish, where shall we find
Another night like the holy Night of Power,
Or a lion like our bloodthirsty hero,
Who sat as our protecting presence?
Without greeting us or saying farewell,
Where have you begun your journey?
Where have you abandoned
Your white-capped Kyrgyz people to desolation?
You were a towering pass
No lion could stride across;
You were a surging river, a lake
No thoroughbred could cross.
From the Russians on your right,
The Chinese on your flank,
And the massed guns before you,
You were our guarding fortress!”
The people wailed in grief,
Bereft of their renowned lion.
Without tail or mane,
We have been left like hornless beasts.
Bereft of our horned champion,
We have become polled cattle.
We have withered like autumn grass
And been left utterly undone.
The royal summer pasture where the people grazed,
Where we migrated in splendor and tethered mares—
We can no longer encamp there.
We have become like a perfectly level plain
Over which the waters spread.
Bereft of our shepherd,
We have become like sheep.
All these dear people gathered here,
While our noble lion lived,
Dwelt in the Ala-Too and drove their herds,
A people peacefully encamped.
By day we laughed, by night we played—
Such a people we once were.
