The sable hat upon his head?
While we had wealth and fortune,
Who did not stride about proudly?
When they saw our warrior lion,
The watchful enemy quailed.
Üyrü-Maral and Kümüsh-Tak,
Where are the uplands where the people autumned?
He joined what had been severed
And gathered what had been scattered.
He rekindled extinguished fires
And restored the dead to life;
He showed the Kara-Kyrgyz people
That he was their mighty camel.
He was a stronghold like a mottled mountain range,
A lion surpassing all the world.
When the noble lord was our shield,
The Kyrgyz of the Ala-Too
Said, ‘Where could suffering be?’
Will he begin a journey far away?
Will our dear lion-like hero
Mount and depart upon a journey,
Leaving his village desolate?
Before one can blink or raise a brow,
Before one can utter a word,
Will God not strike this nation?
Bereft of our precious lion,
Will He not leave these sobbing people
Like a fire extinguished?
The khagan’s nation is a teeming multitude,
As numerous as a mass of black worms.
If they destroy us, will God strike them down,
Or will they unleash a mighty war?
Bereft of our powerful lion,
Will they surround and slaughter us?
Is this how God will strike us?
We have no forest to flee into,
No Nogai to be our shelter,
No mountain refuge where we may hide,
And no renowned gray-maned lion
To deliver us
From toil, anguish, and affliction.
So wretched is this filthy world!
Bereft of our noble lion,
Dressed in black and swallowing blood,
We have been left in a night
So dark it blinds the eyes.
We have been left in a desert
Where no person can walk with sense
And no human being can endure.
We have been left in a grave
With neither door nor place of honor.
Oh, ill-fated people,
We have been left in a lake
Whose bottom is too deep to fathom.
Though the people gather in clans and encamp,
Bereft of the noble lion,
How shall the villages pitch camp?
What remedy can there be?
Many merely squint their eyes at us;
Many enemies jeer and mock us.
Many among us would unleash chaos,
Saying, ‘Father, a son of the Kyrgyz!’
And many nations bear us grudges.”
The people wailed and the nation clamored,
The multitudes grieving;
All the people sobbed,
Lamenting in anguish,
The nation wailing in sorrow.
It became clear that the dear, doomed gray wolf
Was truly departing,
And the people despaired of life.
They inhabited the Ala-Too;
No man could defeat them in combat,
And no enemy dared approach.
Their countless herds of four kinds
Spread in abundance across the land.
At the sight of their livestock and people,
The enemy’s courage failed.
Whenever a man came against them,
No attacking enemy could prevail.
