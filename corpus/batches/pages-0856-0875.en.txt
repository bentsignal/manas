Your warrior who cultivated wisdom,
At the foot of the tower
Had made a field for spear-fighting.
‘If someone falls, let it be soft,’ she said;
Paying out gold by weight,
She had it bedded with black sand.
She had bagfuls of sand poured down,
And champions trample it firm.
She wrapped the posts in gold.
For spear-fighting and wrestling,
The ill-fated one beset by hardship
Had planned it suitably.
Of Karakan's treasury,
When that arena was built,
A great deal had been spent.
Onto the spear-fighting field,
Taking and brandishing her blue spear,
The sight drew one's attention.
Venom poured from her mouth;
Excited and fierce, she roared:
‘Come here for single combat!’
Calling hero Chubak,
They stood upon opposite sides,
Swearing an oath to the death.
They stood face to face,
Swearing an oath not to flee.
Speaking softly, delivering firmly,
Ill-fated Kanykey began:
‘Wearing a blue rep-cloth coat,
Riding a horse like Kögala,
Leaving no part of the earth,
You look as though you have searched it all.
Smashing whoever you encountered,
You look as though you have conquered them.
Coveting great Bukhara,
