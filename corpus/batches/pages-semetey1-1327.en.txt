Those who praise their wives like maidens—
Such are the boastful Kazakh people,
Such are the men to bind at your waist.
At the word “enemy” they bolt forward,
Brandishing tufted spears;
When they descend into a river valley, they cry aloud,
Singing their ölöng and raising a din.
A formidable people of grave custom,
They do not stand idle at the word “enemy.”
Ümötöi, son of Kökchö,
Is of the same year as you,
Equal to you in age;
Ümötöi and you
Are cousins through your mothers.
Ümötöi, son of Kökchö—
Unless he proves a true dog,
If you ride to Beijing, his road is with yours.
He is kin to you;
His poor mother Akerkech
Shared a nest with Kanykei,
Of the same root and lineage,
And so he is your kinsman.
Unless he proves the worst of dogs,
He will pursue the blood-price of his father Kökchö!
I have more words, more still;
Let me tell of the people as a whole:
Beyond Bukhara and Sharip,
On this side of ruined Chambyl,
With a warrior’s gear filling an embrace,
Is Muzburchak, son of Buudaiyk Khan,
From the fascinating land beyond the pass,
From the people called Kyiva.
Buyanbek, son of Muzburchak,
Was left with little sense,
Left young when his father died.
Burulkan, whom Muzburchak married,
Shared a nest with me.
Buyanbek, son of Muzburchak,
Would properly be
Your cousin through your mothers.
Those peoples of Kyiva
No one has conquered,
No enemy has brought under submission.
Such is the nature of their land;
Their caps are an armful across.
At every hero’s side
Hangs a great knife broad as a breast.
Heed the words of your mother Kanykei
And take Buyanbek himself.
He is the renowned one, that is the man,
The hero to bind at your waist.
Against the infidels of the Kakanchin,
Against hero Khan Kongurbai,
He is the man who will go on campaign.
My son, Beijing is no easy land;
You have begun a campaign to Beijing,
Leaving your mother crying aloud,
Leaving the poor woman here behind!
Beijing with Kaspang is a black city,
And has many places of battle.
They say Beijing is a strong land,
No mortal who faced it has prevailed.
Great Beijing, where the sun rises,
Has heat that gives no respite.
There is no good in this undertaking;
You have no coat to wear against the heat!
As I have learned from tradition,
Mortals cannot endure the heat;
They say a person’s eyes glaze over,
And a cauldron set upon the sand
Boils rumbling without fire.
In the blink of an eye,
Before one can say this or that,
Of the water in the cauldron,
Not a spoonful remains, they say.
Swift foot soldiers without clothing—
Kongurbai’s numerous army
Throngs that desert, they say;
