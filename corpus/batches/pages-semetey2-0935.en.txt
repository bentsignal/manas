He showed that he was a lion among heroes,
Not once, but a thousand times,
The idol made of bronze
To win the Chinese people’s favor,
He pressed it to his breast a thousand times.
No clear thought remained,
The valiant falcon Külchoro
Took the Chinese idol in his hands
And cleverly deceived the Chinese.
Ezkara had taken the idol,
But from Ezkara’s hands
Külchoro had snatched it away.
Among the spear-wielding warriors,
His resourcefulness proved genuine;
Seeking out the Chinese people on purpose,
The warrior who had come after wandering astray
Proved truly mighty.
His fortune was complete.
For the falcon who came from the Kyrgyz,
Brave Kongurbay found a champion;
Fleeing the Kyrgyz and becoming a stranger,
He found the countless Chinese nation.
Who could endure the desert?
The warrior who never faltered in the wastes
Proved a true thoroughbred;
The fierce falcon who came saying,
“The Chinese are my people,”
Proved truly their kinsman.
Kongurbay and Ezkara,
And all the Chinese,
Grew excited and raised an uproar;
They all plunged into the clamor.
Heeding no man and hearing no counsel,
They all plunged into frenzy.
Into ceaseless, wild commotion,
Into the tumult they all plunged—
Ah, valiant falcon Külchoro!
The son born of Almambet
Proved a true lion;
Parted from the Kyrgyz people,
He turned toward the Chinese nation.
This warrior who deemed himself formidable
Had truly found his place.
No hero like him exists in all the world;
He proved a true lion.
The idol he called the true God
He had truly found;
He was a mighty champion and a lion.
They clad him in armor,
Proclaimed him throughout royal Beijing,
And found him worthy to be raised as khan.
“We shed blood and became enemies—
Those intentions were all in vain;
Kongurbay and Ezkara,
He stands above both of you.
Ah, that God-stricken falcon!
That he should have gone back to the Kyrgyz
Is a God-accursed thing.
Here in Beijing where we stand,
That damned, firmly planted
Iron-barred fortress is impregnable.
If he were set upon the throne,
He is one who could become khan;
Here in Beijing where we stand,
He could be a lord and a prince,
A hero fit to become khan.
Above the giants Orgo and Döödür,
This warrior holds supremacy.
For the people he left behind,
He was both hero and lion;
Against all the giants of Beijing,
Though storms beat upon it, no wind could pierce it—
He is a fortress of dense cornered woods.
The forty tribes of great Chinese Beijing
Have found the hero who can guard them.
He truly regarded the Chinese
As a great nation,
And without looking back, the Burut came to China—
