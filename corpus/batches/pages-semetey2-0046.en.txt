When a piece two fingers long broke off,
It remained in the bone, they say.
The spearhead was barbed;
It gnawed through his backbone, they say,
And reached the spinal cord.
From the spear that struck his spine,
He beheld the face of doom, they say.
When pain entered his spine,
He suffered anguish and grieved.
The two heroes who sat beside him,
Almambet and Chubak,
Were gone from him;
Suffering anguish and grief,
That blood-drinker died, they say.
The true abode from which none return,
The tiger Manas saw, they say.
The giants gathered and buried him;
I hear the tale from caravans.
Kanykei departed in mourning;
While her child was still in the cradle,
Her son Semetey wandered homeless.
Of the great wealth Manas left,
She saw not a sliver, but scattered it, they say.
To Temirkhan's city
The fierce woman fled in exile, they say.
The mountain-like Manas,
On whom the Burut relied, is dead, they say.
The wealth he left behind,
With Abyke and Köbösh at their head,
Six men divided, they say.
This place where his father died—
He must have come to see it;
That fool has broken custom
And come to die by my hand.
Hero Kongurbai said this,
Relating the calamity of former days.
Wrapped in a gray wolfskin,
Kongurbai himself, behold,
Raged like a gray tiger.
With a fist-sized button of jewel,
Such was the sight to behold.
The tower on the square—
Do not even ask of that tower.
So lofty was that tower,
Its joints had iron bolts,
Its height reached a distant shore.
Atop the tower
Hung an elephant hide—
This was the Chinese custom.
It was three fathoms thick,
A hide entirely of elephant skin.
When it was time to beat it, a wrestler struck;
Its blow deafened the ears,
And its booming carried far.
The glory of great Beijing,
It raised its cast-iron head;
At command a wrestler struck it,
And lesser ears
Came away unable to hear.
The war drum was beaten hard;
Army upon myriad army massed,
Coming all together, none left behind.
There were peoples of many kinds;
The implacable Kalcha
Had warriors of many kinds prepared.
When he pondered, his words meant great turmoil;
His speech had no end.
Because of Tügöt's young hero,
He had reason to be troubled.
“Word has come from the khan;
What has mighty khan Kongurbai,
The great Kalcha, beheld?” they said.
Their iron-mesh armor,
Their gauntlets wholly of cast iron,
Their spears with peerless points,
Their swords with fearsome edges,
Their foot soldiers too heavy for horses,
Their giants of many kinds—
