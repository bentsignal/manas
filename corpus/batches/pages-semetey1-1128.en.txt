Into a dungeon forty fathoms deep
I will take you away and cast you;
Damn you, dog of a Burut,
Thus will I take my revenge!
I will not stay for this feast of yours;
I will not rest until I take Maaniker.
I will take your steed Maaniker;
I have seen your old man Koshoy.
If you do not give me Maaniker,
I will not leave you alive.
If I unleash my great fury,
Here at this feast of yours
I will raise an uproar,
And water your ravine with blood.
I came wishing to fight,
To make strife where there was none.
From Beijing in Chin-Machin,
Truly venting my rage,
I came wishing to do battle.
Upon Maaniker's back,
Load nothing but gold;
Put an end to the quarrel, Burut,
And present him to me now.
For riding to feasts and celebrations,
For making him prance,
Maaniker is a splendid horse."
As Ezkara spoke,
As he used such force,
Manas, who used to make havoc, was gone—
So God had struck them!
Willow-covered Keng-Kol and broad Talas,
His homeland came to mind;
The joyous leopard Manas,
That hero came to mind.
Ezkara began his speech;
Kokotoy's noble son,
From atop the blue-gray pacer,
Nearly flew off;
The strength left his arms,
And Kokotoy's Bokmurun
Lost heart.
God's sun in the sky grew overcast;
Sixty cares descended upon his head at once.
His wits in disarray,
Without saying a word in reply,
Kokotoy's Bokmurun
Fled away from the people.
To Koshoy, father of the people,
Bokmurun went.
He wept out his grief,
And from his uncle Koshoy
He sought counsel:
"Kokotoy's abundant riches—
Heeding no one's words,
I seem to have come to scatter them.
Humiliation no dog has seen,
I seem to have come to endure.
At the hands of the Black Chinese and Manchus,
In sorrow before my destined death,
I seem to have come to die.
I have come to be beaten
By the panting Kalmyks,
And, saying I would hold a memorial feast,
I have come to let all the people
Be plundered for all time.
A mighty force has come from the Kalmyks, uncle;
When I consider it, all the Manchus have come, uncle.
If I stumble, be my support, uncle;
Be a prop for this mortal soul, uncle!
A khan like Ezkara has appeared, uncle;
His wrath drives the life from me, uncle.
Will the Kyrgyz die at the Kalmyks' hands, uncle?
Will the Chinese divide my people, uncle?
Will they heap tribulation on our heads, uncle?
Will the grim-faced Black Chinese
Raid us and wipe us out, uncle?
