But if you stiffen in wrath,
You will only bring ruin on yourself.
That would be sheer folly;
Know this and take heed.
Thus Zhelzhetpes spoke,
Offering words as a warning,
Driving them home and speaking firmly:
My champion, listen to me.
When they heard this counsel,
The chiefs of the Kaldai said,
Are his words not reasonable?
If he rejects the mediator,
The khan's good fortune will depart;
The khan's strength will be broken.
If he torments the innocent,
The people's spirit will be corrupted.
What fault is there in this advice?
So Suradai asked him:
— Heed what the envoy says;
Without starting quarrel or strife,
Spare this one spoonful of blood.
This conduct of yours would be cruel,
O warrior lord and sovereign;
Restrain your seething anger.
Zhelzhetpes, who knows the nations—
Spare his spoonful of blood!
The robed men repeated it day after day,
The men of the stony lands spoke plainly,
The Kaldai and the common people spoke,
The entire multitude spoke,
And from Chyngtengir's hands,
From his encircling net of iron,
From his blood-shedding bullet,
From his burning red ember,
Zhelzhetpes, father of the people,
Was barely saved from death!
May God never show such a sight:
When the people are moving uphill,
In the season when horses grow black-tongued,
Would Lord Tengir remain unprovoked?
Without casting the world into strife,
Would Chyngtengir, your elder khan,
Will God strike him as he lies peacefully?
His whole mind turned to scheming;
The eighteen thousand worlds
Could not fill his belly by one hair.
I shall be sovereign of the world, he thought;
Leaving no one alive who makes the slightest move,
I shall slaughter and butcher them.
Those upon whom I loose my fury,
I shall neither kill outright nor let escape;
I shall flay the skin from their crowns.
To the forty khans' peoples of China,
To Manchu, Shibee, and Torgut,
To Solon and Kalmyk,
And to the Black Chinese tribes,
He sent word to muster troops.
They prepared stout horses to ride,
And chose garments to last ten years.
Misery boiled among the stricken peoples
As he issued a harsh command;
Ignoring every deficiency,
He began this vast calamity.
He had his wrestlers and warriors
Examined by his judges of men,
Made known to those with knowledge,
Measured with the closest care,
And appraised by keen-eyed judges.
When the fearsome musket cracked,
He chose the men who would not flee,
The champions who would not flinch,
Who would neither move nor stir,
Who, though doomsday's raging fire engulfed them,
Would not budge under hardship,
And would not sag beneath a load of stones;
Warriors who could drink and never sate on blood,
Who, once they charged, spared no life—
These he drove apart and selected.
The shallow-witted and fainthearted,
All those who bared their backs,
Those who rushed about in panic,
Who unsettled the people and ruined the realm,
And when grave trouble came
Trembled and fled—
He culled them with exacting care
And sent each to his proper place.
He wrought destruction beyond measure,
Choosing with hairbreadth care
One hundred souls from every thousand.
Chyngtengir gathered his army;
Over every thousand men
He appointed one worthy commander,
And over every hundred men
He placed a single captain.
There rose a clamor that split the earth,
An uproar that rent the sky;
He drew a myriad troops from his people,
Mustered solid as blue steel,
