To Allah Most High, Japar,

His white beard grown sparse,
Of your uncle Bakai,
Tears poured from his eyes;
He sobbed as he offered his blessing,
Your uncle trembling, unable to stand.
Spreading his hands and saying, “Amen!”
Bakai the holy sage strode forth,
To Manas, who had gone to the other world,
He dedicated his prayerful blessing.
The splendid hero, the noble man’s son,
Felt Allah’s sun cloud over in the sky,
And sixty cares came upon him at once:
“If the moon is dark and troubled,
What will the night become?” he thought.
“If death overtakes me and I fall to a bullet,
“For my uncle left behind me,
What will his days become?” he thought.
The splendid hero, son of Manas,
Mounted thoughtfully, that valiant man,
His dear eyes, like a saker falcon’s,
Filling with tears without blinking.
Tormented that he had not gathered the people,
Formed an army, and brought it with him,
The young champion, the lion’s son,
Stood grieving and lamenting aloud.
Yet grieving was of no avail;
He had no choice but to go to the horses!
His eyes blazed like a lion’s;
The young champion, the noble lord’s son,
Was seized fast by bloodlust.
“If fate arrives, I will die;
If I do not die, I will see the face
Of my leopard, Sultan Khan and uncle!
If the fiercest of the bastard Chinese
Falls into my hands,
I will make a spectacle of him!”
The gray wolf pondered and said this,
Put on Akolpok,
And gathered himself like a white tiger—
The splendid hero, the lion’s son.
From valiant Manas, the gray wolf,
The gray kise remained as a gift;
To pursue the blood-price from Beijing,
He girded it tight around his sturdy waist.
Gathering himself like a lion,
He donned the bulletproof coat,
Prayed to his Allah,
And the valiant man’s son prepared
To make a lone raid upon Beijing.
He put on the coat Akolpok
And slung Akkelte across his back.
His face turned wrathful,
Zulpukor, left by Manas,
Your young champion girded on.
The helmet of blue iron
Had been fashioned by poor Kanykei;
Your valiant mother Kanykei
Had given it as a gift, weeping bitterly.
Valiant Lady Kanykei
So no axe would pierce his head,
She had dedicated it as a gift.
The splendid hero, the valiant man’s son,
Lifted the helmet of blue iron
And placed it upon his head.
The poor father’s son
Crossed its fastening cords
And tied them beneath his throat.
The armor for his ribs,
Chosen and given by Kanykei—
He fastened the bulletproof iron
Over his entire body,
Every part of his broad frame
He plated over with blue iron.
He armed himself in fury,
Readying all his weapons of war,
