My noble mother, my valiant one.
My need while this life is held in trust,
My radiant-browed angel.
My precious mother,
Who sat as my protector.
Bare your breast, and let me kiss it;
If you offer your breast, let me suckle.
If pure white milk flows from it,
The illness rooted in my bones will depart,
All sorrow will vanish,
And your son will recover once more.
If milk flows from your breast,
Then God has truly granted our desire.
If black blood flows from your breast,
Then God has truly struck us down.
If pure white milk flows from your breast,
Then God has brightened His countenance;
Your lion will return again,
All anguish will scatter,
And his eyes will open wide.
Your grief and sorrow will vanish,
And your fortunes will mend again.
If dark-black blood flows,
Bow your head before grief
And cut off all your hope.
Let your Venus-like eyes shine.
Kanykey and old Bakai,
Tear wounds in your own flesh.
Behold the true world;
You elders who truly grieve,
Die without further torment.
My white wife Aichürök,
Loosen your hair until it darkens the earth,
Claw your bloodied face,
Put on black and sob,
Crying, ‘My dear prince has died,’
Put on black and wail.
Companion of this present age,
O Külchoro, brother of my nest,
Speak words that could shatter mountains.”
He had lain there peacefully.
Before anyone could say another word,
His poor mother sobbed,
Every tiny bone in her body shuddered,
Her body went limp and tossed about.
Pleading, “My Morning Star,”
She bared her breast and settled down,
Crooning, “My flower-like colt.”
When his mother came near,
His troubled heart found peace.
The poor lion guarded by the spirit said,
“My mother’s scent has come;
The Creator has granted me goodness.
If only I see her, my heart will be content;
I have no sanctuary but my mother.
One day I shall meet death and die;
Before the five stars of the wild sheep set,
What can I do
For my valiant mother, my sanctuary?
When I was an infant,
I clung to you, wailing and crying.
When six months had passed,
I fought for your breast.
In the seventh month you swelled with me;
When eight months had passed,
I kicked your dress until it tore.
You made my valor surpass all others;
I chafed the flesh of your calves,
Made your heels ache,
And blood flow from your legs.
You invoked God, moaning in anguish,
Saying, ‘My plain where no coot flies,
My child who will become my protector.’
Kara Khan came riding in;
When you went to Kara Khan,
You had an amulet hung around my neck
And had me raised apart from enemies.
Though once you prospered, poor mother,
You suffered barren grief
And were parted from wretched me.
