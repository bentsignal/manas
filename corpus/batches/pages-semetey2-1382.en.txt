Whoever became khan,
It was the custom of those ill-fated champions
To go over to him.
They made Kanchoro khan,
And forty champions remained with him;
Like wolves, those forty champions
Tore apart whomever he named.
Those forty champions humbled
Anyone who met their gaze.
At their khan’s command, those forty champions
Unleashed tumult.
Like eagles, those forty champions
Seized whomever he ordered them to seize.
Those forty champions stirred the nation,
Slaughtered the people, and remained.
When he pointed out a victim, those forty champions
Brought him down like a boar.
Striking terror into every spirit,
With every oath they swore,
Those forty champions remained.
Khan Kanchoro’s fame
Spread throughout the Kyrgyz people;
The menace of the forty champions
Spread throughout the world.
At the word “Speak!” the forty champions
Stood ready to pounce;
At the cry “Strike!” the forty champions
Charged with a roar.
Those forty champions stood
At Khan Kanchoro’s side.
Damn those forty champions!
They caused many a mighty outrage.
Ah, Kanykey in her old age,
The blessed, fierce-hearted woman,
The dark beauty,
The noblest of women,
The graceful one with long hair,
The finest of all womankind—
They heaped humiliation upon your lord
And inflicted unbearable suffering
Upon your unfortunate mother Kanykey.
Not merely his mother, but his sanctuary,
She had nursed the renowned, bloodthirsty prince
Until the Five Argali constellation had set,
Giving him the breast five times along the road.
She had a young mare slaughtered,
Made him bite off
The mare’s honeyed fat with his teeth
And suck it with his tongue,
Nurturing his little life into strength,
Restoring his frail soul,
Giving him drops when he was fading,
Reviving him when he was dying,
Raising him in her arms until he grew strong.
Eighty-year-old Kanykey,
As though she were Kanchoro’s own mother,
Had tended him in this way—
Semetey, her gray-maned prince.
Now that Kanykey had grown old,
They subjected the old woman of the palace
To monstrous humiliation.
The forty champions brought her before the khan:
“Here is the slave who shall serve you,” they said,
And delivered her to him.
Bridling with fury,
When he saw Kanykey,
His eyes flared like fire;
His bloodlust seized him,
His front teeth were as broad as a gate,
Teeth unlike those of ordinary men;
He ground them separately.
From Kanchoro’s mouth
A blue flame billowed.
Bereft of her young camel,
The aged woman Kanykey
Keened bitterly through the smoke.
Then the unfortunate Kanykey
Was mauled by the champions
And beaten by their warriors.
