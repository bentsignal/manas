His hooves thundered on the earth,
And the black-steel bit
Clattered in his throat.
When he ran he shrieked;
Stones beneath his hooves
Scattered like millet.
When visible, he appeared;
When Surkoen was unseen,
He became a bank of blue smoke.
The earth split in a rushing wake;
Then the Jediger man
Fled back toward his people.
Almambet's Kulchoro
Grieved in distress:
"By not drawing close at the end,
I have acted foolishly.
By pursuing Toltoy from afar,
I have acted like a child.
Will he escape beyond my reach
And reach his people now?
Failing to catch him like this,
Will God take the Roan from me?
My uncle deliberately sent
Me, young Kulchoro.
Hero Toltoy was mountain-like,
Yet he fled bobbing away.
God has struck down
The calamity who came to take a wife,
The infidel of perfect courage."
Hero Toltoy looked behind
As he fled.
Kulchoro seized the black-jawed whip,
Swung it around,
And lashed the doomed Roan
Across the belly,
Pursuing him.
Straining as though unable to catch him,
Kulchoro came then,
Quarreling with Taybuurul.
"You were a racehorse, damn you!
Roan, what has happened to you?
Will Toltoy truly get away,
Fleeing beyond my reach?"
The mountain-like Taybuurul
Had failed to catch him at a run.
Aychurok, daughter of Akun,
Had pampered and fed him,
And he had grown too fat.
Unable to lower his head,
Unable to stride like an argali,
The doomed beast
Was covered in foaming sweat.
Clean sweat broke out and restored him;
He bent his neck like a stag.
When a ravine a spear's length wide appeared,
He leapt without hesitation.
His mind was truly a tulpar's;
Was he not a mountain-like tulpar?
The wind could not match his gallop;
The wind-spirit beast
Lowered his head to the earth
And took an argali's stride.
The wind could not match his gallop;
He ran with perfect speed.
No livestock could match his pace;
He ran in an extraordinary way.
Shimmering like a spirit,
The doomed Taybuurul
Drew close enough
To catch Toltoy of the Jediger.
From the feather-grass ridge,
From the high-shouldered rise,
He rode alongside.
Clever Kulchoro
Was deceiving Toltoy of the Jediger
As they went.
"Bridegroom Toltoy, wait!
Turn your horse's head!"
