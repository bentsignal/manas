Thinking, “I will take this infidel captive.
Here inside this palace
I will split her like a lean sheep.”
Yesterday, while bloodthirsty Manas lived,
It descended from heaven and came to him,
So he might gird himself and ride against the foe:
Zulpukor, found for valiant Khan Manas.
Zulpukor, which hews down whatever is thrust before it,
Which, once drawn, could not be stayed,
Which, when a man swung it as he wished,
Never returned without drinking blood.
When eagerly drawn from its sheath,
Its reach lengthened with every stroke;
Its blue-green edge glowed red,
And set the grass afire when laid upon it.
Whatever it struck was cut down at once;
Struck against stone, it raised dust,
Struck against man, it drew blood.
From renowned Manas of old,
Zulpukor remained as an heirloom.
Her necklace rattling on her breast,
The wretched woman, heedless of all,
Her camel-calf eyes flashing,
Made early words become late deeds;
To strike down brave Kyyaz,
The renowned infidel Chürök
Watched with all her eyes.
Kyyaz of the Jediger,
Your sister-in-law stood watching.
Without drawing near that sorceress,
Brave Kyyaz stood apart.
He could not approach the raging woman,
Could not endure her cold,
Could not lie down in her heat;
At the disposition of that fairy-born woman
He stood astonished.
To the sly, alluring beauty
He could not cast his cold noose;
He could not face and seize
The beautiful woman Chürök.
He could not draw near the brow
Of the bitch he had never desired.
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger,
When he saw Aichürök,
Took in the grace of her form
And marveled at her beauty.
Moonlike Aichürök
Was no ordinary mortal.
Because of what had happened before,
Because of Aichürök,
The gathered people had quarreled;
It seemed fitting that they should perish.
She was beauty in perfect form,
The very one to inspire longing,
A soul surpassing humankind;
Chynkojo and Toltoy both had died
While contending for her.
As a wife she had divided nations;
As a maiden, this Chürök
Had shaken custom and divided peoples.
She was a being radiant as the sun,
Who set the world at strife
And made slaughter seem ordained.
She was the very firebrand,
Aichürök, for whom men died,
With deep black eyes.
To keep calling Chürök merely “Chürök”
Was a God-stricken saying.
She was Chürök who could enter
Great revels and games!
She was Chürök who could lie
And converse in a narrow bed!
She was Chürök who could preside
Over pure play and mighty passion!
She was Chürök who could satisfy
The desire of the man who possessed her!
Meeting face to face as allies,
Eating meat from a single cauldron,
