Was destined to come into being.
When he subdued the teeming enemy
And waged war against them.
From the desert of Medina,
From the shore of Jön Turbas,
From this side of Künümdük,
From the fortress of Jan-Baspas,
It appeared to him from the unseen.
There was no cawing crow,
No croaking raven in flight,
No serpent slithering away,
No wild ass frolicking there;
By many decrees it came to him.
“It is unique upon the earth,” they said;
By Allah’s favor it appeared,
And was found upon the empty steppe.
There a mortal marked for death was found;
For the blue-maned lion hero
It was found by Allah’s decree.
Laid upon grass, it set it aflame;
Those it struck died in an instant.
When drawn in the dark of night,
Its edge burned red with flame.
When swung against a formidable foe,
It lengthened to forty fathoms.
When drawn in wrath,
It never went without drinking blood.
Until it was fully sated with blood,
It would not return to its sheath.
My father-in-law’s Zulfiqar—
I desire to take it.
Against the infidel son of the ogre,
If I reach him, let me wield the sword.
Let me test myself in true combat,
And truly spill his blood.
The Zulfiqar inherited from our forefather—
If you give it, I shall gladly take it.
Against the infidel son of Sarybai,
I shall confront him and wage battle.
Seizing each other by the collar and dragging,
Struggling and hauling one another,
Striking with keen-edged swords,
Letting precious scarlet blood
Flow as we slash and crush—
Such a battle shall I wage.
If fate arrives and my days are done,
I shall surrender my dear life.
Does not every living mortal behold
The decree laid down by my Lord?
Does one not die when fate arrives?
Does a hero not die for honor?
Let me attempt the struggle;
If I do not die then, one day I shall.
For dignity and honor,
Let me join the fray.
To wage battle without restraint,
To ride out onto the field,
To brighten the hero’s brow,
To ease the ache of longing,
To issue the command,
To thrust and duel with spears,
To fight until all are spent,
To take a warrior’s life in hand,
To thrust the spear without tiring—
I learned from the transmitters,
And asked those who knew.
Its shaft is stout, its point is sharp,
With black steel fixed upon its tip;
Master of every craft,
A friend of the prophet David,
The forest smith fashioned it.
The base of the grain-dark spindlewood shaft
Was torn out with its roots.
At the fullness of summer,
Bull-necked and camel-thighed,
It destroyed men when they struggled.
The ill-omened, doom-bringing weapon
Was braced by champions.
They sheathed it in elephant hide
And thus began this great commotion.
