You killed your mountain-like uncle
And fled beyond the pass;
You have not a knucklebone’s worth of sense.
You cast the great Kyrgyz nation,
A people with a royal encampment, into hell,
And were swept away in the flood!
Think what turmoil this will bring—
With six swift horses,
And a letter small as a palm,
Over rugged hills and passes,
Across deserts no man treads,
By night, though it be dark,
If word reaches your people in Dyngsha
That a choro has become a murderer,
And the report travels this far—
To the bloodline of Kaldai,
To the city of Kakan,
And to every living people
Whose ears hear and eyes see—
Will not the khan ride out upon the road,
And the land fill with his army?
Will not bows thunder and tumult rise,
And royal blood be shed?
When the great host comes roaring,
My Kanchoro, will they not slaughter you?
Will they not cast your band into hell,
And massacre them without restraint?
If myriad enemies come to war,
Their poison burning in the quiver,
Brave Kyyaz, khan of the Jediger,
Will meet his doom together with you.
You dog-born, bootlicking slave,
You truly have no sense!
China lies on your left,
Russia lies on your right;
They have guns to confront you,
And many enemies contend with you.
They will raid your villages from Talas;
The Kakan people you challenged
Will destroy every trace of you,
Seize your mares as booty,
Smash your brains across the ground,
And vent their every spite upon you.
They will ignore your pleas;
May you burn, glowing like an ember,
You fawning madman—
May God give you your due!
While my magnanimous lord lived,
You turned my gold to charcoal;
Once fattened, you ruined the village.
You turned my silver to charcoal;
Once fattened, you set all ablaze.
While my prince, vast as the sea, lived,
Did you seize and devour your equal?
You depraved madman,
When the hour of combat came,
Did you think yourself his equal kinsman?”
Burning with fury, Aichürök
Raged and gathered force,
Denouncing Kanchoro;
She began a grave, instructive speech
And lashed him with words like these.
Upon her proud head,
Beside her wretched mother Kanykey,
Her bloodied face was torn,
And her precious hair, long as meadow grass,
Spread across her breast.
Before anyone could utter a word,
In the blink of an eye,
Looking neither up nor down,
Not sparing her life from death,
The hapless woman began the slaughter.
Parted from her mighty lion,
She bound her dog-born, wretched life
To a single strand of hair.
Semetey’s Aichürök
Plunged into the fray,
And entered a mighty conflict
