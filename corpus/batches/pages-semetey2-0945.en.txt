I believed you would spread slaughter across the world—
My dear one, you alone were my hope.
For God’s sake and mine,
And for the people left by your one father,
Spare nothing—deal out slaughter.
On Alööke’s son Kongurbay,
Take vengeance as one would upon a dog.”
Thus spoke Khan Bakay:
“The khan himself will be your opponent;
Only a lord of immense courage is fit
For that fierce and bloody battle.
My Külchoro, it calls for a lion like you.
My spring flowing down from the hills,
My colt whom I fondly raised,
Knowing he would one day serve in vengeance.”
After Bakay had spoken thus,
The gray-maned warrior of old said:
“Against grim-faced Kongurbay,
That doomed Kalmyk warrior,
Let us unleash a storm of gunfire.
Let us empty the bullets from our powder flasks
And stand amid the thunder of the guns.
Let us stand face-to-face,
Shouting as we thrust our spears,
Firing muskets and drawing bows,
Clashing fiercely in combat;
Let us wage war in earnest.
Until the soft reeds ripen,
Until the ashes of the steppe grass fly,
Until the birches shed their covering,
And the poplars lose every leaf,
Let us wage war in this fashion.
Let us begin the clash with fury;
Let us leave sixty young wives widowed
And their villages desolate.
In broad Beijing, once conquered by our fathers,
Let us open fire and begin the slaughter.
With our bearded elder Bakay,
Let us all enter the fray—
Can one who fears death prosper?
Can a soul that recoils from its destined end
Prosper as a man?
My companion, one of seven days brings death;
When doom arrives, who does not die?
If Almighty Allah wills it,
Will one not surrender one’s tiny life?
Will not the five-fingered angel reach out?
When the naked devourer of souls
Draws near to one’s life,
Will it not lower one into the dug grave,
And sink one beneath the black earth?
Can one who fears death prosper?
Can there ever be two brave men
Who turn back from their destined end?
Damn you, Kulchoro,
Lionhearted companion sustained by the spirits—
A warrior whose swiftness knows no bounds,
A dashing, vigorous companion!
Kulchoro, when I first saw you,
We swiftly invoked Baabedin,
And for what I had newly received,
We quickly recited the ayat.
Exactly ninety-five red camels
Were slaughtered for the feast;
Minstrels were made to sing,
And judges of men to appraise you,
While an amulet was hung around your neck.
“He will become a peerless sultan,” they said.
My khan father’s confidant,
Dear Bakai, his old companion,
With sixty khojas and forty ishans,
All examined you and gave their blessing.
They had a golden cradle brought,
And, wishing you to become an exceptional man,
They placed you in that cradle.
Kulchoro, raised with loving care,
You are the companion I made my cherished one.
