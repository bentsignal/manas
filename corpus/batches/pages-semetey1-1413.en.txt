Bakay came riding in haste,
Pleading with his son Semetey:
"Kuuördök is a cunning swine;
Do not cast your falcon too soon;
Do not slaughter your old uncle Bakai
And leave him full of regret!
Your uncle Bakai tells you this:
Judge the bird's distance;
Do not ruin things, my son—heed my words.
Set the falcon's mind upon it,
And only then cast it loose!
Do not make me lament, son—heed my words!"
Bakai spoke these words;
The orphaned hero, son of the Bloodthirsty One,
Now drew near the duck.
When he drew near the duck,
A matchless thoroughbred,
Its gait rolled soft and smooth,
Its steel shoes and sharp nails
Shining on all fours.
The matchless Narbuurul,
A fleet thoroughbred with mighty legs,
Mattock hooves and a broad rump,
A thoroughbred of the spacious land.
Tossing his forelock to the sky,
Driving his hooves into the earth,
He moved with the glide of a sighthound,
Wherever he passed,
Gliding without a sound.
The splendid hero born of Manas,
To cast at Kuuördök
And have his fill of joy,
Coiled the long leash;
The noble hero loosed Akshumkar,
Letting it go on a shortened tether,
Stepping obliquely and drawing it close,
Making the short tether taut.
Circling in from the side,
Behind him, Khan Bakai
Girded his firm waist;
Though old, hero Bakai
A crescent-marked golden doolbas drum,
Striking once with a ringing blow.
When the doolbas sounded,
Kuuördök at the watch-post
Circled in the air,
Flying backward and banking.
The remarkable Kuuördök—
His beak is long, his neck is copper,
Its girth two fathoms,
With an iron tail, swiftest of all;
When frenzied Kuuördök
Rose, no living creature could catch it.
As it rose,
Semetey, born after
His father Manas the gray wolf,
Shouted in anger,
Raised the cry of "Bloodthirsty One!"
He set the falcon in position,
Brought it keenly onto the quarry,
And watched it soar,
Looking to either side.
Semetey's Akshumkar
Was a champion beyond compare,
Utterly peerless in the hunt.
No quarry escaped before it;
Its talons were iron, its beak brass,
Its girth two fathoms.
Black eyes, blue legs,
It never turned back from what it saw,
And left no morsel in a pail uneaten.
It would not let a goose rise from the lake,
But folded its wings and stooped upon it;
It would not let a swan rise from the lake,
But gathered its tail and stooped upon it.
The champion who trained it as a hunting bird
Was a young man touched by Khizir,
To whom God himself had granted it!
