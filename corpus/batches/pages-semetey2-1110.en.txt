I strutted proudly,
And we played together, singing laments,
In the midst of our rejoicing,
When we split a whole otter skin
Lengthwise between you,
And you swaggered like kings
With it sewn onto your cuffs—
Have you forgotten the honor you planted in Keng-Kol,
You accursed wretch?
Have you forgotten the days when you strode so proudly?!
Drinking kumis and courting girls,
Have you forgotten that happiness?
Can the sole heir of a khan
Rightly commit such treachery?
Can there ever be another fool
With thoughts as black as yours?
Can the only son of a noble
Harbor such evil intent?
Can a true kinsman prosper
Who plots against his own khan?
Is there any man like you,
Who would bring distress upon his lord?
If you shoot at one who shared your mother’s breast,
Can you prosper in the end?
Can any man be like you?”
So Külchoro
Spoke these words as if alarmed,
And renowned Choro spoke of the outrage.
When he had finished speaking thus,
The gray wolf, son of Manas,
The lion upheld by holy powers,
The steadfast warrior,
The dear lion with the ringing voice,
Flew into a towering rage;
Wisdom came to him from the unseen,
And he took offense at the two traitors:
“I gathered thoroughbreds and tended horses—
A curse upon all that labor
Spent on steeds like Arbuurul!
I raised brave men and gathered lions,
Yet never gained the least benefit—
A curse upon those men,
Upon my kinsmen Abyke and Köbösh!
Yet may I ever be grateful
To the one who turned enemies into brothers.
When I took you two cherished lions in,
When I made you mine,
Slaughtered ninety beasts for your feast,
Gave you rhyming names,
Carved up haunches for you to eat,
And had you both suck
At Kanykei’s breasts,
We uttered every blessing
That you might become true brothers.
We swiftly invoked Baabedin,
And called upon Allah by name;
We said you would counsel a renowned hero,
And if the two traitors circled in flight,
They would be supporting wings behind my back—
Those two vaunted traitors!
In the marshlands of Jylgynduu and Talas,
I said I had found twin brothers,
And while we reveled in delight,
My dear mother Kanykei,
Her senses overcome, said:
‘When Külchoro suckled,
My whole body thrilled,
My tiny soul cried aloud,
All my ten joints and eighty bones
Softened completely and trembled;
From the right breast Külchoro suckled,
Pure white milk surged forth—
He is your brother,’ she said.
My mother also said:
‘From the left breast Kanchoro suckled,
And the bones of my joints cracked;
