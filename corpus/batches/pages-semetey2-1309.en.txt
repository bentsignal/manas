No one remained to turn back his lasso.
He was a lion at the nation’s frontier,
Created by my Lord on high;
Now among the sons of the Kyrgyz
He will never let himself be captured.
Wherever he passed the night,
A deep-rooted plane tree grew;
Beneath it, a sheer cliff rose.
Wherever he stopped to camp,
Poplars as broad as a sheepfold stood.
Any enemy who openly opposed him
Never prospered or multiplied.
“This Choro has struck at the twins;
Perhaps he will never thrive,
But will rescue heroes, ruin nations,
And plunge us into suffering;
Perhaps he will poison
All the Jediger;
Perhaps he will cast them
Into incurable affliction and turmoil;
Perhaps he will throw
All these Jediger into the water;
Perhaps he will plunge them
Into a clamor unknown to men and unseen by nations.
Kanchoro has won such renown,
His quarrel will touch the whole world;
Will he lie still without raising strife?”
The massed black Jediger—
All the Jediger,
Young and old—
Considered what would follow and raised an uproar;
Thinking it over, they made a commotion.
With evil thoughts about the choro,
The Jediger stood there.
Before the Jediger could close their mouths,
Kanchoro came out shouting,
Summoning every last man.
Swelling with anger,
Against the ill-fated choro,
He found counsel as if from the unseen
And began speaking wrathfully.
When Kanchoro grew fierce—
That cringing madman,
That treacherous choro—
He buckled on his unsheathed sword,
Set all his weapons in place,
And made ready to speak.
His black eyes filled with blood;
He roared menacingly,
Calling all the massed black Jediger
To come without exception:
“On the plain of Chogulma
Hold a gathering together;
Devise a plan and find a way.
Bakay Khan is their elder—
Strike down Bakay Khan!
His beard is white as a swan;
While his final journey is near,
His eyes have reddened like a raven’s,
His swan-white beard has grown long,
His remaining eye festers,
And his chest wheezes.
That accursed Bakay Khan—
Before his head enters the grave,
Before his soul reaches the earth—
Still devises perfect counsels
And will not abandon his mischief.
For Bakay’s many offenses,
I will make him taste misery;
I myself will give his punishment.
I will strip him and bend his back,
Load him upon a yellow gelded camel,
And heap boiling anguish upon him.
I will make eighty powerful wrestlers
Use Bakay as their kökbörü carcass
Three times each week;
I will have fools maul him,
And, granting Bakay no counsel,
