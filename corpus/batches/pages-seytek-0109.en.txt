They raided my camp from Talas,
In hardship, misery, and dark disaster,
I am unable to take my revenge.
If the wound on my shoulder should heal,
If your towering Choro should recover,
Would I not sever the fated thread,
Would I not begin the upheaval?
Saying, “Choro has seized Kyyaz,”
I would begin the struggle,
I would make my fame known.
My belly boils, my heart burns—
Such is the state you see me in.
My strength is spent, my vigor gone—
I am as one already dead.
I, once an eagle, have become earth;
I, once a lion—alas, my head—
Have become a dug black grave:
Such is the state you find me in.
If you call this living, I count among no living men;
I am as one already dead.
Bring that dead soul back to life,
Show me your power as my sister-in-law.
While my mighty elder lived,
I was a keen warrior who scattered foes.
A bullet struck and pierced my chest,
And I cannot turn about.
My arm is broken, my leg disabled,
And I cannot rise to my feet.
Abandoned to the enemy’s scorn,
I cannot stand tall.
Carrying firewood on my back,
Fallen among a wrathful people,
I cannot show my face.
Karagul has become our master;
At his shout, we lose our peace.
My moon-bright sister Aichürök,
You have cast great turmoil among men.
Do not let your thoughts be divided;
Do not let them call him a stranger’s son,
The child born from your own body.
The seething Jediger—
Let me bring their proud banner down.
Kyyaz, son of Bagysh—
If my strength and power suffice,
Let me thrust his head into a pit.
If battle begins, let me do this:
Let me search every saddlebag
Of that sprawling swine;
Let me reap his grain while still green.
If fortune favors me, Kyyaz
I shall drive before me and torment.
Striking his mares out on the plain,
Let me smash his brains,
Take his camels as plunder,
And neatly slaughter his giant.
While splitting his belly like a donkey’s,
May my mind stay fixed upon the fool,
Until somehow I sever his head.
The man I fought was brave Kyyaz;
The man I sent flying was Semetey.
The one I must battle is Kanchoro;
The one I lost was Semetey.
My thoughts stray, sister-in-law,
To the torment suffered in humiliation.
My grief is vast, my anguish fierce:
May I die fighting.
I value my blood less than a spoonful;
For my khan and uncle, the gray-maned lion,
I shall spill every spoonful of my blood,
And spare my life not at all.
Kyyaz, son of Bagysh—
If only I might somehow beat the fury from him;
The Jediger who fill the Seven Valleys—
If only I might drive them all away;
If only I might slaughter their champions!
I have words to speak in tears,
A vengeance that will never leave my heart.
If I clash with him and raise a battle,
May that sprawling dog and I
Fall together where no one sees us.
