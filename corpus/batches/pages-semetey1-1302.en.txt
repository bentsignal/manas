A mighty servant who knew the enemy’s stratagems.
Akkelte, entrusted and left
By his father, noble Manas,
With a seven-jointed brass ramrod—
Your hero drew it out.
Saying, “Its bore has become dirty,”
He scraped it with a file,
Pounded it with a striker,
Pulled it with a draw-tool;
Without tiring, Külchoro
Cleaned and wiped its inside.
Once the bore was clean,
Külchoro took
A large cloth from the gray kise
And pulled it through again and again.
The half-bullet sewn into the collar,
And the half-charge of powder,
Into Akkelte’s muzzle
He now poured by the palmful.
The half-bullet in his hand—
Taking the bronze ramrod,
He drove it down with a ringing blow.
Gripping the gun at the waist,
Displaying his valor,
He swore a firm oath then,
Touching his mouth to its muzzle:
“My bloodthirsty father was a lion-warrior;
Akkelte remained from him.
If I fail to shoot
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
May my father’s spirit
Strike me down in the end;
If you fail to hit where I point you,
May my sultan father’s spirit
Find and strike you in the end!”
Having sworn his oath with Akkelte,
Külchoro hurried away.
Saying, “The stallion left by my father,”
He pleaded with Taibuurul:
“I spread my hands to the holy sage;
Be my blessing, Taibuurul.
I have met an overwhelming foe;
Be my father, Taibuurul.
My mother remains far away;
My beloved Taibuurul,
Protect me as a mother.”
His noble lord’s Akolpok,
With golden collar and copper sleeves,
Which no bullet pierced when worn in battle,
Külchoro took
And now draped crosswise
Over Taibuurul.
The great reins tied in a loop
He flung trailing ahead;
Külchoro, a master of judgment,
Now entered upon his stratagem.
Beside the moonlike stallion’s
Fat flank he took shelter;
Covering himself with one skirt of the armor,
He lay watching Khojo.
The lion-born Choro’s
Eyes reddened like fire,
His neck stretched like a drake’s;
The gray kise that fell from heaven
Was slung around his neck.
He pleaded with the spirits
Of the lions gone to the other world.
Early words became late;
For one exchange of shots
With Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
His two eyes became four with eagerness.
His gaze clouded in a strange way;
From behind the white saddle’s pommel,
He edged Akkelte’s muzzle out
So slightly one could scarcely tell.
He promised a sheep to Koroson,
Promised a horse to the holy sage,
And to the spirits of the lions,
