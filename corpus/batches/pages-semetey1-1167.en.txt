Was it not Koshoy who came?
Was it not Koshoy who laid out
All the boastful Chinese
At full length?
Was it not Koshoy who won
Victory in many places?
Was it not Koshoy who fought many men
Until he had his fill?
Before the Kalmyk could say, "Strike,"
He tore the flesh from his arm.
Accursed old age!
The skin of his arm
Went into the hand
Of Kalmyk champion Joloy.
"I have lived to see old age,
The mark of my decline;
I have suffered shame from an infidel," he said.
Taking courage, Khan Koshoy,
Old tiger Khan Koshoy,
Seized millstone-sized Joloy
By the quivering ribs.
From his side he tore
A bowl-sized piece of blue flesh,
And ripped it free.
Your old uncle Koshoy's
Arm ran with blood;
Kalmyk Khan Joloy's
Side became like bread dough.
God spare us such a sight!
They butted like blue rams,
Tearing whatever they reached,
Jerking like hungry stallions.
God spare us such a sight!
They butted like blue bulls,
Tearing whatever they saw.
Dust swirled in confusion;
Whatever they gripped became pulp,
And both were covered in red blood.
They tugged at their trouser cuffs.
If you are a champion, be like this:
They crashed chest against chest,
Tore the clothes they grasped,
And butted harder than wild rams.
They grappled by the trouser cuffs,
Testing the manner of their strength.
Joloy went about grieving:
The great trousers on his legs,
Made from sixty goatskins,
Were ripped into shreds.
"Those were sturdy trousers," he said,
And Joloy went about grieving.
When they pulled the kangdagay trousers,
They stretched out like a spring;
When they let the kangdagay go,
They gathered like a fold.
When he grasped them in fury,
Joloy's hands were cut.
The fainthearted fled and scattered;
Among infidel and Muslim,
All those with courage
Watched as entertainment.
The two champions met,
And those who watched ached inside.
Kokotoy's abundant wealth
Was then lavishly scattered
For the spectacle.
The Karkyra plain,
Where the champions' feet landed,
Was gouged out like a trench.
All the sorrow of those watching
Was relieved.
They wrestled for six days,
Neither able to defeat the other.
When their wrestling
Had lasted six full days,
He was a man grown old—
Your uncle, giant Koshoy,
Of the Kalmyks' Joloy,
