He burdened the people’s hearts
And gathered every last one of them.
The people murmured and the army gleamed,
And when they stood encircling him,
His vast strength became apparent.
He donned dark-blue iron armor,
Wrapped himself in a blue cloak,
And took up a blue-gray shield.
Such was the splendor of his appearance,
With jewel buttons large as fists.
Rousing himself like a blue lion,
Chyngtengir strode forth,
Roaring with menace,
And summoned the wise men
Of the teeming Chinese host:
—Among the vast Chinese nation of forty khans,
Does anyone have news?
With six swift horses
And a letter small as a palm,
I sent riders through swirling dust,
Dispatching them four times around
The four corners of the world.
I sent scouts among the nations
And gathered news from the peoples.
Old Jangju among the messengers—
No other man is like him—
Traveled alone and saw the lands,
Rode alone and saw the nations.
He says Akjoltoy and Seytek,
Who dwelt in Ala-Too, are dead;
They have died and seen
The true abode from which none return.
Seytek has left an heir,
And the treasuries overflow beyond counting.
Those wretched Kyrgyz people
Have returned to their old encampment.
They have made Ala-Too their homeland
And made Kenen their hero.
His forefather was hero Manas Khan,
His grandfather was Semetey,
And his own father was hero Seytek.
He is not a hair’s breadth
Inferior to any of them;
He has risen like a mighty plane-tree banner
And taken command of all the realm.
Everyone whose ears have heard
And whose eyes have seen him
Has claimed him as their inheritance and refuge;
Those worthless people
Have taken him as their protector.
They say that Kenen has arisen,
And that Kenen’s army stands ready;
His wisdom is great and his wrath is mighty,
And his cunning surpasses that of all men.
The two khans Ebegey and Kögöy
Have risen with spears in hand,
Becoming friends in the saddle;
They have begun to share together
The pleasures of this deceitful world until death,
To die when fate overtakes them,
And, digging a deep grave and stamping it firm,
For one to bury the other.
They have united and begun their revels,
And all the wealth they gathered
They have heaped together in one place.
They have made great Talas a fortress
And hero Kenen its lord.
If we do not go and crush
The crowns of those men,
If we do not drive out the ancient fury
Of Ebegey and Kögöy,
Of hero Akjoltoy and Kenen—
Those four guardians of the people—
If we do not strike first,
And find a way to settle the score,
If we do not block their traveled roads
And drive out every last one of their people,
Breaking their steadfast backs
And stilling their unruly hearts,
Driving spears into their farthest reaches,
Destroying all who resist,
And making their mighty men burn;
If we do not enslave that wretched nation
And divide them by measure of the lash—
The messenger has come saying Kenen is renowned,
Bringing word
That an enemy ready to fight you
Now stands prepared.
If we do not advance and block his road,
Scatter all he has acquired,
And overrun his settlements,
Then slaughter the thoroughbreds bred in Beijing—
This is the secret your lord now declares.
Braid the thoroughbreds’ manes.
If you take off your boots, stretch out your legs,
And lie at ease in careless peace,
Then within five or six years
The renowned gray wolf Kenen—
