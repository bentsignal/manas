Into a mouth that once would not admit a grain of wheat,
A bull’s head now fit;
A crushing hardship befell him;
Alas, into a mouth that once would not admit a millet seed,
A yearling calf’s head now fit.
Behold the appearance
Of your champion uncle Bakay:
His back had lost its strength and stooped,
His eyes bulged and lost their sight,
The sockets of his eyes festered,
And his chest was left wheezing.
Strength had departed from both arms;
All human dignity was gone.
Kanchoro inflicted humiliation on Bakay,
Beyond even that shared by all in death;
Many wrestlers supported
That wrathful Kanchoro.
Making Bakay their kökbörü carcass,
Kanchoro raised a great tumult.
Kanchoro raged,
And Kanchoro spoke these words:
“I have become a man of sacred power,
Bald head!
I have become a club-fisted khan
Who gathers the people and maintains the realm,
Bald head!
I will ferment poison for your appointed day, bloody head;
Thus will I reach your root, bloody head!
I will tear out your very roots,
Bald head;
I will heap many torments upon you,
Bald head;
I will take my revenge while you live, bald head!”
So saying, Kanchoro
Had sixty women and ten old crones
Beat your uncle;
He had seventy wrestler gatekeepers
And the wrestlers guarding the gate
Maltreat your uncle.
Day and night he imposed
Countless torments and afflictions;
He had Bakay’s arms and legs
Stretched like those of a blue-gray kid,
Had him flayed like a mottled sheep,
And set him up as a target
To be shot with blank charges.
He made your swift uncle Bakay
Endure this affliction.
Your aged uncle Bakay’s
Suffering and misery were great;
Bakay Khan’s dwelling
Became a grass hut.
In his old age Bakay
Watched with all the power of both eyes;
In his old age Bakay Khan
Was brought level with the black earth.
Raging, Kanchoro,
Having destroyed the dear lion,
Inflicted violence upon Talas.
In Bakay’s old age
He brought him level with the black earth
And subjected him to humiliation.
At evening he brought Bakay out,
And had old women beat him;
Twice each week
He had wrestlers maul him;
Not once but a thousand times,
He humiliated Bakay Khan.
There, at the dark eye of the spring,
Beside the very straight plane tree,
Along the Üch-Koshoy,
On the plain of great Tört-Kül,
Stood Kanykey’s white palace.
Its door was solid iron and tin,
Its thickness was immense;
Its entrance was a gate of steel.
The keepers at that gate
Numbered seventy executioners.
