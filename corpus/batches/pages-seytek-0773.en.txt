And I brought turmoil upon the slave Sarybay.
I began the fierce struggle
And unleashed a savage conflict.
I rescued the poor common people
From death’s waiting arrow
And from the hands of Sarybay,
Whose venom was strong and heart was mean;
I delivered them from him.
I took the gray purse with its jeweled crescent
And fastened it around my waist.
Making my army of six divisions obey,
I pursued Sarybay.
I overcame his giant champion;
Though crammed full of courage,
I stretched him lengthwise on the ground.
Whoever opposed me,
I split open upon the road.
I made the shooting furious
And the clash of battle fierce;
I exposed the champions
Of the infidel Sarybay’s host
And slaughtered their entire band.
I annihilated them completely,
All his many kinds of companions;
I bent his giants double with my spear
And drove the villain’s
Far-famed rage out of him.
Felling his forest, I made a road,
And brought his whole world to disgrace.
Hewing them down with the sword
And battering them with the spear,
I brought dreadful havoc upon them.
When the infidel, the monster’s son,
Stood there lamenting,
Saying, “My strength cannot overcome a woman,”
He seemed as though his father had died that day;
He burned with shame,
And his lion nature was revealed.
He began to scream and shriek,
Having lost his finest army.
I made my woman’s head a warrior’s
And tightly girded my waist.
I chased the earth-crushing swine
Away from Zhelpinish.
Saying, “This is how the conflict stood,”
I waged such a battle.
I felled his heroes,
Made his renowned men crawl,
And opened a road through the dead end.
I slaughtered every villain
Who boasted of being a champion, leaving them corpses;
I trampled beneath my feet
And humiliated his champion who charged the banner.
I seized the man he had posted as sentry
And made him a slave.
Without letting him turn his courser’s head
Or rise before the world,
I poured volleys upon him.
I crippled his champion
And struck him down in bewilderment;
Then I wheeled around and renewed the battle.
Saying, “This is the way of combat,”
I made my horse’s flanks stream with sweat.
Around all four corners of the world
I drove the villain who had descended upon you,
Circling him four times.
For six full days, without letting his back touch earth,
I gave him no freedom,
Allowed him no room upon the land,
And denied him his own will.
For two days I did not let him settle on the ground;
Such boundless havoc did I wreak.
I opened Zhelzhetpes to full speed,
And drove the earth-crushing swine
Out of Zhelpinish.
I tightly girded my waist
And leveled my spear along the ridge.
Neither by day nor by night did I cease;
I pursued the swine who had conquered China
And made sport of the chase.
