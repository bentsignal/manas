“This Kuutülkü standing here
Is the watchman himself, they say!
If we catch and do away with his fox,
It means God has granted him to us;
But if that bastard Kuutülkü
Escapes onto the open plain,
It means God has struck us down!
Here beside me is Kumayyk,
My beloved creature,
Encountered through the unseen,
Found by the favor of God.
When Bakai led the way,
In the desert of Median
A blue vulture bore him and left him.
They raised Kumayyk and made him a hound;
God Most High ordained it
And brought him to the Bloodthirsty One.
He was cherished more than a child;
While my mighty Manas lived,
My beloved Kumayyk
Was kept in a place of honor.
When Kumayyk had grown,
A tiger-skin covering
Was spread across his back,
And a collar of steel was placed around his neck!
When he stretched into his stride, he never rushed;
My beloved Kumayyk
Never retreated from a tiger.
When they loosed him on a bear and cried, ‘Attack!’
He mangled the place he bit,
And made the master who loosed him laugh.
A tested champion among beasts,
An heirloom left by the noble lord.
I will take hold of him by the collar;
Now that I, Bakai, am old,
I will loose my beloved Kumayyk
Upon the sly fox!
Today Kumayyk
Will rekindle aged Bakai’s
Extinguished fire,
And revive his lifeless soul.
In Bakai’s old age,
He will fatten me when I have grown lean,
Fill my empty stomach,
And satisfy my longing.
That Kumayyk is a champion,
I, a poor old soul, know well.
I have longed to loose Kumayyk,
To satisfy my desire fully,
And make myself young once more—
I have yearned for it.
In his old age, your khan and uncle
Will show what was not known,
Make you perceive what went unnoticed,
And have the straight road reconnoitered.
A gorge of towering black rock,
With birch and honeysuckle mingled within—
Facing that black cliff,
Cutting straight across the bend in the road,
To loose him upon the sly fox,
To have him sink his teeth in once,
Lion Bakai then went,
Leading Kumayyk.
The learned elder Bakai—
A sage mighty in what he knew—
Onto a rugged saddle ridge,
Hero Bakai led him up.
Fastened and secured it once again:
The high-voiced doolbas drum,
Made from the hide of a bil;
When struck once with the axe,
The opposite mountain slope groaned.
When he came up onto the ridge,
Do not ask how the valiant man fared;
Your valiant uncle Bakai
Grasped the golden-cheeked battle-axe
And swung it back,
Making the doolbas ring out, ‘Kang!’
