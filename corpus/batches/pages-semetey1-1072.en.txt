He is as large as half a mountain.
"Meaning to take a wife from Afghanistan,
He had his two choros
Slaughtered together."
Do not go toward the jezail;
If God created you as a living soul,
Do not bring disgrace!
Do not reject my words.
I rely for companionship
Upon this lanky only boy;
O God, do not put me to shame.
Wearing his splendid motley coat,
The hero, son of Manas,
Paced along the road;
He slung around his neck
The Gray Purse left by Manas,
Preparing to give Külchoro
His battle dress.
When the gray-maned lion left Talas,
Your dear mother Kanykey
Had folded away upon a prayer rug
A form no person had yet seen,
Had it loaded upon Artelki:
A splendid white saddlebag.
Kanchoro flew swift as an arrow
And carried it in.
The white saddlebag bore no ornament;
Inside that saddlebag,
What was there, and what was not?
When they brought it and looked,
There was a helmet of blue iron,
Its inside lined with cotton and silk,
Its outside assembled from hardened steel,
Commissioned from Bölökbay,
Who had beaten smooth every flawed place;
Your mother who built the fortress
Had made every part fit perfectly:
"When the white banner comes,
When the shouting tumult comes,
The enemy will advance head-on,
And an axe may unexpectedly
Strike one of the boys."
Semetey paced with a rolling stride,
Showing that he was a hero;
He dressed Külchoro
In the charayna his mother had made.
He then took out another object;
Unless the Creator himself knew it,
Its workmanship was hard to comprehend.
Your dear mother Kanykey—
Could any woman be like this?
Could any woman prosper so?
Inside a single oak
She had hidden an object.
When they looked at that object,
Its collar was gold, its sleeves copper,
Double mail with lark-eye links.
Your heroic mother Kanykey
Had lined it inside with leather and silk,
Had iron assembled over its outside,
Had master after master test it,
Had its inner plates cast from hardened iron,
With double ribs of iron
And twin trailing straps, the remarkable thing,
Gathered together like slender rods.
"A carbine bullet will not cut it,"
She had written in a note at its collar.
She had given that coat itself
The name "Badana."
Badana, which no bullet pierced—
Semetey strode proudly,
Showing that he was a hero,
And dressed Külchoro in it.
Again there was a wondrous object;
At the pommel of his saddle,
Level with his navel,
Your blood-taking prince fastened on him
The weapon called Kabyrga.
