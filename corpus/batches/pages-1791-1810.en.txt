Imposing tribute, their daughters—
When I kissed them, I did not die.
Besieging the city of Beijing,
In the year I took it, I did not die.
When I laid Ay Jang-Jung flat,
On that day, I did not die.
Gathering gold from the Chinese,
Imposing much tribute and tax,
In the year I took it, I did not die!”
Bakai wept bitterly.
When your ruler saw him,
“Elder Bakai, do not weep.
I am journeying to the world beyond;
Do not torment my entrusted soul!
Once Allah has commanded,
Once the angel who takes souls
Stands stretching out his five hands,
Must not one whose appointed hour has come die?
To what the Creator has written,
Must not his wretched servant submit?
Will the dug grave not admit me?
Will the black earth not hold me?
Do not grieve, Bakai, you are my khan.
Do not go settling along the valley.
Do not behave as though Manas were still here.
When the khan dies, an old man
Has no life apart from sleep,
Has no livestock apart from camels.
Girding your waist snugly,
Do not stir up trouble in assemblies.
As though your ruler Manas were still here,
My hero, do not go when there is a gathering.
When you, my elder, go to a gathering,
Let them not say, ‘That wretched-bearded Bakai!’
Hearing the words ‘wretched beard,’
With your companion Manas no longer here,
Let Khan Bakai not die that very day!
Khan Bakai, heed my charge about assemblies.
Jelmayan and Bozbuura,
And Kuu Ingen, I entrust to you!”
After Manas had spoken his last wishes,
After the whole people had suffered grief,
What is seen today is gone tomorrow.
Alas, such is this unruly world!
Between his back and neck,
It had become inflamed and painful:
That wound where the battle-axe had struck.
And in the hero’s ribs, too,
A sickness that would take him had appeared.
Again it had drawn blood and laid him low;
At the ridge of his backbone,
By Alooke’s fierce-faced warrior
He had been speared through the lung.
The tip of the steel is bent,
Playing many games together,
It had passed beyond the spinal cord.
And with this, the hero’s
Appointed death had truly come.
Once the claim upon his life fell due,
The ruler coughed in cracking bursts.
Then Manas, the dear lion,
Lifted his head from the pillow.
Rising, when he cried, ‘Oh!’
Blood came in heaving gushes.
Coughing in deep bursts,
Raising his shovel-like head,
Gathering force, when he cried, ‘Ah!’
From your hero’s mouth
Blood poured out in shovelfuls.
Despairing of the ruler,
All your khans lost their strength.
His fine mustache lay flattened;
He set out toward the world beyond.
His wide eyes closed,
