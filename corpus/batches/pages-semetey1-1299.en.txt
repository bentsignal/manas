God had struck him in this way.
When he turned Arbuudan’s head,
The lion prince then
Stood sunk in grief.
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Had cares too many for his breast:
“Where has my gray wolf gone?” he cried,
Tears from his eyes flooding ten plains.
His face paled and grew thin;
Tears flowing from both eyes
Poured down over his breast.
“If I am parted from my khan and uncle,
To what grave shall I go?” he cried.
“My leopard prince and uncle—
Where can I find him?” he cried.
His breast could not hold his many cares;
Until he found his gray wolf,
From the eyes of Choro,
That gray-wolf prince, tears flooded ten plains.
The noble son of Almambet,
The only child of his peri-born mother,
Aruuke,
Raised in tender affection,
The amulet around her neck,
The young champion Külchoro,
Who had fought others for her breast.
Looking to both sides,
Licking his lips and nose,
When he reached his uncle,
He shouted, “Babedin!”
That spirited young son
Called out, “Uncle!”
The young man hurried over;
Külchoro, lion-like,
Pleaded with his uncle:
“My uncle with silver eyes and sun-bright face,
My thundering uncle!
My uncle with golden eyes and moon-bright face,
My beloved uncle!
Is there a pit dug before you?
Let me be sacrificed for you,
My beloved uncle;
Is there an army surging toward you?
Is there a fire burning without turning back?
My dear uncle,
Is there a host lying unbroken?
Is there a bullet that strikes without turning?
Is there a cliff that grants no pass?
Has some enemy pressed you hard
And robbed you of all recourse?
If there is an enemy, I shall destroy it;
Whoever I meet, I shall slaughter.
My tiger uncle, tell me the truth!”
When his Choro spoke this way,
His prince stood and said:
“My banner fastened to the spear,
My beloved Külchoro,
A gift I inherited
From my lion sworn father.
My prop when I climb upward,
My support when I descend,
My good road when sent before me,
My countless host when you follow behind,
My ravine where the hawthorn grows,
My tempered steel.
My ravine where the sorrel grows,
My fitted blade of steel!
Your uncle has words to say
And the skill to offer counsel.
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Khojo, resolute in his deeds—
We shared one camp from the beginning,
And grew up together from youth.
An enemy who takes up the cudgel is bad;
An enemy who shared your camp is bad;
Worst is the dog Khojo who knows your secrets.
He escaped after coming out to shoot;
Khojo, a proven enemy, is the worst.
