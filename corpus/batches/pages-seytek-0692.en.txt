They drew their mighty bows,
And shot at one another with thudding blows,
Standing amid battle cries,
Fighting without sparing their lives.
All their spears shattered.
Every coat of mail was torn apart,
And each lion’s valor was revealed;
All their robes were ripped,
All their gear burst asunder.
They seized each other by the collar,
And sank waist-deep into the earth.
They took no rest by day,
Nor any rest by night.
They fought through days and nights—
Who had ever seen such a deed?
Both armies stood motionless,
Unable to advance, as though paralyzed.
Their poor heads, once full of life,
Became buckets of crimson blood.
Axe-bearing Sarybai
Came upon him in fury.
He swung his cast-iron mace, huge as a house,
Pouring all his rage into it,
Gripped it in both hands and let it fly,
Turning his warhorse’s head,
And struck with all his might.
The shield burned and became an ember;
What could poor Choro do?
His eyes blazed like fire.
The gray racer’s back was broken,
All its bones cracking,
Its fly-like soul shrieking.
Choro collapsed unconscious,
His two eyes rolling,
His fine mustache twisted,
His flashing eyes staring wide.
Stricken by grievous pain,
His insides seethed like embers,
His two eyes became fire,
And his whole body trembled.
When he faced forward and leveled his spear,
His back had failed and he stooped,
His eyes bulging and staring.
His eyes flashed like a lion’s,
And against the infidel son of a monster,
Before a word could be spoken,
In the blink of an eye,
He arrived faster than an arrow.
As he reached the infidel,
Thinking, “Here is the face of the ribs,
Here is the very center of the lungs,
Here is where this raging swine
Will fall,”
Külchoro thrust his spear.
Such is the way of heroes:
They stood facing one another.
Thinking, “Here is the core of the fleshly heart,
The place between lung and heart,
The measured eye of death,
The very place where he must die,
Where he will wheel like a blue vulture
And his soul take flight,”
Sarybai thrust his spear.
The four-legged chargers
Collapsed side by side.
The spears that struck together
Shattered into fragments.
Such is the way of warriors:
They stood face-to-face.
Firing guns and drawing bows,
They fought with crashing blows.
They struck with battle-axes,
Until the axes shattered
And only the hafts remained in their hands.
They fought with swords,
Until every sword shattered
And only the hilts remained in their hands.
They stood on opposing slopes,
Fighting with battle-axes—
Who had ever seen such a deed?
