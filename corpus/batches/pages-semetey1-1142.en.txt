When he migrated from Altay,
The Kalmyks who fought him were laid low.
The tiger gathered forty companions
And became a wolf-khan in Talas.
The mortal who struggled with him
Became bread.
Yesterday, tiger Manas
Destroyed the Kyzylbash people;
Falcon Manas, the tiger,
Made every place he glanced toward weep.
No person dared look
And advance against Manas.
His fortune was complete;
Looking into his heart,
It was broader than the face of the earth.
Your magnanimous lion-like lord,
The youngest of six lions,
Your gray wolf, the mortal loved by God,
Was the hero Zayypburush;
His breadth was a boundless lake.
At that time Manas
Had a hat large as a cauldron;
Over infidel and Muslim alike,
He had freedom to do as he wished.
Combing his black mustache,
Looking to both sides,
The war banner floated in his hand,
And Akkelte flashed on his back.
A gray cartridge belt large as a foot-sack
Rang against his right hip;
Zulpukor too appeared,
And the enemy's spirit failed.
At the hero's right side,
With mighty Bakay at their head,
Ready to swarm upon the enemy,
They appeared without flinching,
Ready to arrive before an arrow—
Twenty fierce close companions.
Looking to his left,
Wearing a robe of yellow gold,
Mounted on the racehorse Sarala,
With tiger Almang at their head,
Were the twenty on his left.
Mounted on blue-mottled horses,
Wearing blue rapis coats—
Chubak, son of Akbaltay;
Syrgak, son of Ulakkan,
Who subdued the moon and world;
Yrchyuul, son of Yraman;
Hero Shuutu, whose cap was broad as a felt panel;
Kyrgylchal, head of the forty;
The formidable hero Tazbaymat;
Men more skillful than intriguers,
Mighty men who could shatter the world.
Holding a banner with a golden finial,
Your uncle Bakay rode in front,
Making Kartküröng prance;
Khan Azhybay rode in front
Of the tiger, gray-wolf lord.
Nine supported him;
His supporters appeared.
The dark-piebald tiger
Raged at his flank.
Zulpukor fastened at his waist,
His wrath spread across his face;
Those who saw him despaired,
And his thirst for blood showed.
The horns boomed,
The trumpets shrilled;
All the forty companions,
Their elder Kyrgyl at their head,
Had a majestic bearing,
Each a companion worth a countless army.
The horses were firmly tethered,
And all the gray wolves filled the place.
Finding no enemy to meet in battle,
All forty companions grew pent-up,
Finding no ridge upon which to clash,
Finding no born hero with whom to contend,
