Deprived of my gray horse,
With not a blade of grass to nibble,
Not a sip of water to swallow,
I am doomed to die in the desert.
With no famed enemy to blame,
I am doomed to die among my own people;
With mist covering the earth,
I am doomed to be left upon the pass,
Burning and blazing here,
Abandoned in the wilderness.
My valiant mother Kanykei
Had warned me with anguished cries:
‘At heart, Kanchoro
Will recite the prophecies you sang,
Reveal your secrets to the enemy,
And join them in bringing about your end;
All your nurturing of him is in vain.
Will he reveal the foe within his heart
And abandon you?
Will he begin
A terrible affliction and great strife?
When you see his monstrous deceit,
You will blaze and burn like fire,
And then you will know
That your poor mother spoke the truth.
You will know the great prophetic gift
Of your hapless, valiant mother,
That sacred, unfortunate woman.’
Those were my mother’s words;
She said he was an enemy at heart.
Her prophecy has come true—
My fierce, mighty mother’s
Words, my foal, have proved true.
My dear mother Kanykei,
When he was still an infant,
Before he had begun to grow,
Told me to cut off his head with a sword,
To finish him off;
She said it not once, but a thousand times.
So I was fated to witness
This musket-tailed villainy;
So I was fated to die
At the hands of a close kinsman and warrior.
Shamed despite my swift racing stallion,
Abused by my own kin,
I am doomed to die a wretched death,
Burning to ashes and embers,
Doomed to die an unclean death.
Damn you, Kanchoro!
My Külchoro and my Kanchoro,
My two nest-mates, my equal warriors—
Yesterday, on the plain of Jylgynduu,
Along the broad Talas,
In the marshes of Jylgynduu and Keng-Kol,
When we reveled in delight,
If anyone laid hands upon your horses,
Though he said, ‘That is my only horse,’
We would slaughter it and eat it.
If my two lions remained loyal,
We said, where could calamity come from?
Did God hear our arrogant boast?
Has the gasping enemy
Who tears out lungs and severs heads
Sprung from my own hearth?”
So Semetey spoke,
Leaning upon
His golden-bladed battle-axe;
Grieving, he folded his hands,
His heart filled with bitter anguish,
And the gray wolf stood lamenting.
Your hero, renowned as a hero,
Shed tears from his dear falcon eyes
Without even turning them aside.
His heart burned and his eyes blazed;
His poor soul could not be contained in a fist,
And he seethed in torment.
What is here today is gone tomorrow;
His heart was aflame, his breast an ember.
