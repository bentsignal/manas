Trouble arose from a place without trouble;
Alas, Khojo emerged as an enemy.
He began the havoc
And slaughtered your uncle,
Mountainous as Opol-Too.
My dear Külchoro,
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Has set my sorrow boiling,
And truly brought upon my head
This marvel that will be my death.
The moon is dark; night has come;
My wise Choro, hear this:
The day of death has come.
Dear Khojo has grown mighty,
Overpowering honored men.
Of Buurul beneath me,
Only a spoonful of blood remains,
His flesh a blue, battered pulp.
My powder has spilled from its flask;
I have despaired of this grieving life.
No host remains for close combat,
No bullet remains for shooting—
Can God strike us so?
Let us dress him in Akolpok,
Mount him upon Aktelki,
And offer Aichürök as tribute;
Safe and sound to Talas,
Let us turn back now!
Let us speak words of conciliation,
Saying, “Here, take Chürök,”
And before Toltoi
Let us offer Aichürök,
Then return safely to our camp!
Perhaps we should agree to his demand,
Saying, “We were kin—pardon us,”
And offer Arbuurul and Akkelte
As tribute to Khojo.
Perhaps we should give tribute and withdraw,
Without crushing battle with the dog,
And return safely to Talas.”
When the prince spoke thus,
As though stabbed in the eye,
Külchoro sprang up:
“Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Khojo, whose path is strife!
Worse than a fire burning the meadow
Is an enemy sprung from one’s own;
Worse is an illness sprung from the core,
Worse is Khojo, the enemy who fights to the death.
My dear uncle,
Who would listen to these words of yours?
Let some black night listen;
Let the dug black grave listen.
Who else would listen to them!
Let the uncrossable black water listen;
Who else would listen!
Do not dig my deep grave,
My dear uncle;
Do not set my sorrow boiling!
How could I bow my head
And hand my sister-in-law to Khojo,
With whom we have shed blood?
How could I turn my neck,
And offer a heroic soul,
Saying, ‘Let him be’?
Rather than see such suffering,
I shall die fighting.
Akkelte was treasure left by my father—
How could I give it away?
The hell that has come upon my head
I shall face with my own eyelashes!
While my strong head remains,
How could I give my swift horse
To the blustering Khojo?
Let what I have gathered remain in my hand,
Let my faith be my companion;
I shall not withdraw where death has not come.
Until the soft reeds ripen,
