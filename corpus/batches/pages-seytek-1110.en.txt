Modest and fair-browed,
No mortal being could compare
With the unfortunate noble-born Bermet,
Created unlike all others.
As she lay sleeping that night,
She dreamed a wondrous dream,
And saw this magnificent portent.
She saw her dream clearly,
And beheld the full moon;
To her right she saw the sun,
And they were immersed in great delight.
Then, as her dream continued,
A fierce lion and panther
Came into Seytek’s possession.
They mauled whatever attacked,
And destroyed whatever they encountered;
No creature withstood their ferocity,
And the earth burned wherever they passed.
Seytek seized them by their collars,
While every living creature
Found no grave in which to hide,
And no earth into which to enter.
Scorched, the sand curled into drifts,
And wolves fled in panic, howling;
All creatures despaired of life.
A black eagle seized its prey,
And a collared sighthound followed the trail;
He built a fortress at Kakan
And slaughtered everything he unleashed them upon.
That eagle he had flown
Had eyes like opened pits,
And a beak like a steel auger;
Its beak gaped wide,
Its talons spread broad,
And blood flowed wherever it struck.
All the fanged beasts—
Lions and wolves that devoured—
Burned in helplessness
And fled into the gorges.
Through the four corners of the world
He made four complete circuits,
Hunting game with hawk and hound,
And reveled in this great delight.
Through the five quarters of Medina
He made five complete circuits,
And in every known place
He brought all things to completion.
“The warrior is Seytek,” they said,
And the whole wide world learned his name.
He challenged the panther
And grappled with the tiger.
Whatever he encountered he bent beneath him,
And that eagle carried it off.
Rejoicing in the deeds
And pleasures of his hunt,
He wearied his horse and surveyed the land,
Hooding and then flying his bird,
Yet never reached his intended destination.
Before this Seytek
No one remained who dared object.
Then, as she slept, she dreamed again:
The valiant panther Seytek
Found two blades of steel.
The sign of those blades was this:
When swung, they never recoiled from stone,
And from any place struck against an enemy
They never returned without shedding blood.
Their sheaths were beautifully fashioned;
Seeing how keen the steel was,
The great smith and forefather David
Looked upon it and praised it.
Roving on reconnaissance in high spirits,
He encountered a dragon
As huge as Mount Kap-Too.
The matched pair of keen swords
Could not prevail against the dragon,
And fire could not pierce its breath;
They fought but could not overcome it.
With a roar it swept from the east
And came to rest in the east.
