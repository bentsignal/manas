As though taking vengeance on a corpse,
Will he subject her to this humiliation?
Will God strike down the choro
Whose conduct is strange and whose salt is accursed?”
So spoke Aychürök.
Some people say
That renowned Chürök
Was a daughter of the peri.
Wise in counsel, honored as night,
My wondrous sister-in-law—
After Kanchoro heaped disgrace on her,
After the massed black Jediger
Surrounded the palace,
Poor Chürök pondered
Her many grievous cares:
“If the lion born as son
Of my mother’s golden womb,
My ship that sailed the river,
Should suddenly vanish from sight
And by chance lie unburied,
Ravens will croak and peck out
Those two eyes unlike any human eyes;
Crows will foul
His created golden head;
My mother will thereby
Become dung for beasts.”
Without letting anyone know,
Without allowing a single person near,
The learned woman Aychürök,
Agile beyond measure,
Hollowed out the edge of the hearth
Of the clamorous infidel.
She folded her mother Chyyyrdy at the waist,
Placed her within a burial wrapping,
And laid her there.
Her mother Chyyyrdy was dead;
Aychürök, the moonlike woman,
Buried her with her own hands.
Then Aychürök,
Grieving in mourning,
Ran to Kanykey;
Speaking softly but firmly,
She began addressing mother Kanykey:
“Ah, beauty among the dark-haired,
Foremost among women,
Purest in the flowing gown,
Most fitting of the long-haired,
Strongest among all womankind!
My mother who knows the worth
Of suffering and death,
Of paradise and hell,
Of plenty and want,
Of hunger and fullness—
My precious blessing!
You joined what was severed,
Gathered what was scattered,
Kindled the extinguished fire,
And brought dead souls back to life;
You raised bowed heads as banners
And made heroes of heads in mourning.
My mother who has known the weight
Of bitter suffering and affliction,
Who has seen the ways
Of men who are both kin and foe—
Your spring flowing from the valley,
Your brave son whom you bore near death,
Cannot be caught by the eye;
Your only one, whom you bore near death—
Whether he is dead or alive,
No one knows where he is.
When I follow the lore of tradition
And ask those who know,
He was your noble hero’s companion,
The sworn mate of his entrusted life;
When laid beneath him, he was his sure road,
When he rode for months, his countless host;
When foes made war, they shared the dust,
Shared one life and one death.
