From the horses left desolate,
Take whichever lets itself be caught and ride.
Keep your word and return;
You reeking, stinking slave,
Do not stray from anything I have said!”
Saying this, Karagul
Unleashed his wrath.
Külchoro, favored by fortune,
Was struck by bitter misfortune;
The noble hero who once rode chargers
Now grieved, unable even to find a mare.
As he turned back,
Tears streamed from his two eyes
And fell down his breast.
His joints weakened as evening fell;
Sobbing bitterly, longing to die,
His two eyes strained in desperation.
“Do not die, my noble lionhearted uncle,” he cried;
“Never inflict such grief
Upon any mortal.”
His ribs seemed to split apart,
And then Külchoro
Lifted his black felt cloak
And turned toward the horses;
Wailing in earnest,
He went sobbing toward the herd.
When he drew near,
The whole vast herd
Took fright and fled, neighing.
Have you left me wandering, neither dead nor gone,
Keeping me alive without taking me?
O almighty power,
Have I, who am no beast but a man,
Been made a mockery even among animals?
Too humiliated to live, yet unable to die,
Unable to take his own life,
Unable to walk willingly
Toward accursed death,
As the entire herd thundered away in fright,
The horses of that prosperous man,
“I, Külchoro, meant to take one—
I shall avenge my father without killing you.
I shattered your caravan from Talas;
This is precisely how I shall take revenge.
You set the grassy land ablaze
And burned the meadowland;
You poured blood into the black waters,
Made years of misery across the ridges,
And brought terrible disgrace upon us.
You have burned my very being,
You swaggering, mad slave;
This is how I shall gouge out your eyes!
He says he will go before the khan
And ask the khan’s wife, Aichürök,
To give him clothing—
See how this fawning, cunning slave,
This man, this slave, has run mad!
If he goes, let your slave go;
If his fate has come, let him die,
And at the hands of Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
Let him suffer like a sheep!
“Go then, slave, and return;
Bring no token or gift,
And if you value your life,
Return before a mare can be milked!
If you are later than that,
You cringing madman,
I shall kill you myself
And gouge out your eyes while you live!
If you chance to be late,
If by mischance you fail to return,
I shall destroy your whole line;
When I charge in fury,
In the end I shall cut off your head!
Go swiftly and return swiftly;
Now you shall see my valor.
If you are late, madman,
I shall strike without warning
And kill you on the spot.
Put on your felt cloak and go.
