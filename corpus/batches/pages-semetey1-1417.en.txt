Such was the nature of Buurul.
When he reached his fifth year,
They cut his testicles and made him a gelding;
When the wound had healed,
He curved his neck like a stag.
When he came to a ditch a shrub’s height,
He bounded over without hesitation.
Offering a foot-measure of gold and ser,
The khan of Bukhara asked for him;
But he was not given to those who asked.
Far from giving away the thoroughbred,
No ritually unclean person even knew of him.
He curved his neck like an argali;
When a ditch was a rope-length across,
He bounded over it without swerving.
When they learned of his excellence,
Offering tribute of gold and ser,
Khans from every land asked for him.
But he was not given to those who asked;
No cold-hearted stranger
Ever saw your sultan’s Buurul.
When he took on his summer coat,
He looked dark roan, with a low mane;
When he took on his winter coat,
He appeared bright red-roan.
He never flinched when ridden into battle,
Nor suffered when ridden with the baggage train.
He would not tire if ridden for six months;
Until he reached sixty years,
He would not age or loosen his fangs.
Your uncle, old Bakai,
Scraped the ever-ready Buurul
Again and again with the grooming blade,
And wiped the sweat clean
From the rangy courser.
Old Bakai arranged everything neatly
And tethered Buurul
With a long, loose line.
He prepared him to go out scouting,
To turn back the patrol
Of hero Khan Kongurbai,
And reconnoiter the side road
Used by hero Khan Kongurbai.
Breaking ranks amid crossing spears,
Falling to a bullet if death arrived,
Steeping themselves in bloody gore,
Fighting for six months without fleeing,
Dying if fate should come—
And, if the one God granted honor,
Raiding and seizing the livestock
Of the full-strength infidel,
Hero Khan Kongurbai;
Wreaking spectacular slaughter
Upon the bastard Chinese;
And, as a son, avenging
His lord who had gone to Judgment Day—
With these aims he prepared Buurul.
Starting awake from sleep,
He rolled onto his right side,
Gathered himself like a lion,
And strained like a blue tiger.
Preparing to face Beijing,
The splendid hero, the valiant man’s son,
Prayed to the one God.
Ah, the broad and splendid lion,
The unshakable noble hero,
Rose awake from sleep,
Pondering the affairs of Beijing.
He dipped his hands in water
And performed the ablution of his hands;
He put his feet into the water
And performed the ablution of his feet.
Facing the qibla,
His champion uncle Bakai
Now called the azan in a resounding voice.
He hurriedly performed the prayer,
And saddled the choice thoroughbred,
