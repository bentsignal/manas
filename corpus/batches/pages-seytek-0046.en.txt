To plunge into festivities,
To play on without tiring,
Traveling by night and resting by day,
The Jediger of the Seven Valleys
Endured bitter hardship.
Upon forty red-tailed camels
They loaded red gold,
Delighting the moon-bright heart.
A white plume broad as a tent
Was displayed upon the forelock
Of a swift steed, stately as a beast.
Driving livestock for the marriage alliance,
They paid homage and feasted on rich butter.
Karachach, daughter of Karakhan,
Poured out wealth and scattered livestock;
She was the wife I chose and took,
Yet from her I saw no offspring.
Snow has passed along the black mountain;
Many kinds of people have passed away,
Beaming as they cherished children.
I have not even a daughter, much less a son;
Longing for a child has tormented me!
Slaughter ninety beasts for the feast.
My wife whom I won in battle,
That graceful infidel,
The fairy-born Aichürök,
Has borne my hero son.
Name my child for me!
Let ninety beasts be slaughtered for his feast,
And let the child be given his name!”
Saying this, hero Kyyaz
Slaughtered ninety fat mares,
And sheep in great abundance,
As well as many fine colts.
Without further talk, they plunged into revelry,
And the people rose in an uproar:
“The hero has fathered a son!”
If you had seen the countless Jediger,
They all rose in jubilation.
The nation gathered and the people surged;
By this reckoning of days,
Since I took the unfortunate woman,
Eleven months have passed.
The child born today—
My people, consider it well—
Has proved to be my own son!
My joyful heart has turned to celebration;
I shall scatter my wealth and hold a feast
For my newborn child.
Think it right, O Jediger;
Joy has entered my heart.
My heart surges and my eyes shine
At the child revealed to me today.
My people, since Kyyaz became Kyyaz,
Since Kyyaz mounted a horse,
Since he grew to manhood as a hero
And became the equal of heroes’ sons,
Since he donned the white coat of mail
And sat proudly upon his horse,
Since he mounted the bay Tootoru
And rode out on raids,
When yesterday’s battle raged fiercely,
Akbermet, daughter of Kaiypkhan—
While nations gathered and peoples were thrown into turmoil,
And many heroes died in the clash—
Was the wife I captured.
I hung an amulet around her neck,
And painted rouge upon her face;
Never speaking words that might wound her heart,
I cherished her tenderly.
Yet from her I saw no child
And never heard an infant cry “maa.”
Karakhan, khan of the Künjüt,
Had a lissome, sun-bright beauty
Who came from that land.
To win that sunlike beauty,
That lovely woman of sweet and fitting speech,
That woman with lustrous black eyes,
To take such a beauty as my wife
And satisfy my longing once,
