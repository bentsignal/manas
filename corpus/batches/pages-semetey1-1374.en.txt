It is a place that dispels sorrow.
There are different grasses,
It has lands unlike any others.
My uncle Bakai, my old one,
My beloved saint!
While your gray wolf was alive,
While the wide world remained whole,
While your worthy one was alive,
While your fortune remained whole,
There was no people you had not fought,
No land you had not subdued.
When you humiliated Kokon,
When you alone brought turmoil
Upon the face of the earth,
While your bloodthirsty hero lived,
Kara-Shaar, Kambyl
When you besieged them,
When you beat down
The kalday and the Soloons,
When you caused true upheaval,
When you seized the khanate
When you seized the khanate,
Why didn't you know this place?!
Why, uncle,
Did you not travel through this place?!
Uncle Bakai, my gray wolf,
You failed to see this land—
What a cause for regret!
The world's delight,
A paradise* unseen around the bend,
Has remained here!
Could it be a land where people
Would scatter through the valley and not camp?
Could it be a land where every man
Would not become a hero!
Could it be a land where old women
Would not slice and chew rump-fat?
Could it be a land where brave men
Would not drive a thousand horses?
Could it be a land where men would not strut
In pairs of sable caps!
Could our people camp in neighboring corrals
And fail to prosper!"
Aryslan Bakay started the journey,
At Kongur-Ölöng and Kopo,
He had a sheep slaughtered there;
A gentle breeze, a cool summer,
He pitched the wide screen,
And removed the saddles
From all the fine thoroughbreds.
He rested the coursers overnight,
Keeping them tethered until dawn.
Using Akkangky as a pillow,
All the valiant men
Lay with their heads toward the qibla,*
Resting upon their elbows,
And took a brief sleep.
Not much time had passed
When true dawn arrived
And daybreak turned yellow.
The rim of dawn swelled;
When the cold wind blows,
The boys awoke from sleep.
They let all the fine coursers
Drink their fill of water,
And graze great mouthfuls
Of one fescue among eighty grasses.
From a gushing, pure spring,
Without letting the coursers rise,
They quenched their thirst.
Leading them about for a long while,
They filled the stomachs
Of the fourteen thoroughbred horses with grass.
When the sun reached midmorning,
The companions stood
Serving their prince;
All were champions bound for China.
Such was the condition of the boys.
