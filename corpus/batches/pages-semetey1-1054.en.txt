It is also true that I did not give
Khan Akun's Chürök.
The reason I have not given her is this:
At a known place she has one who girded his waist,
A husband lying ready and resolved.
The conquering leopard Manas—
His son Semetey
Is her husband of terrible wrath.
If Semetey comes tomorrow,
Raging and thundering,
'Where have you given my wife?' he will demand,
And he too will come roaring.
Descendant of Dümpök,
Who blazes whenever extinguished;
Descendant of the fire-marked one,
Who multiplies whenever killed;
The leopard himself,
Who poured blood into black water.
His father Manas was a lion,
A hero who made
The whole world tremble.
His father was the leopard Manas,
Who drove white deer and made them cattle,
And threw the whole world into turmoil.
Have you heard the news
That wrathful Manas was born a khan,
And souls fled before his courage?
While heroic Manas lived,
No one who faced him remained alive;
When they saw his heroism,
The khan of Beijing wept.
The news of it still remains:
Manas devastated the age,
And slaughtered and destroyed
Enemies who acted crookedly.
He hewed down those he fought
And straightened every crooked thing.
The whole world gathered,
Yet against falcon Manas the conqueror
None could speak a word of outrage.
Wherever he spent the night,
A deep-rooted shrine arose.
Descendant of the deathless one,
Supported from his roots by Khydyr.
One of my ears has heard this:
He blazes whenever extinguished,
And multiplies whenever killed.
He turned away toward Bukhara
And there recovered;
Until he reached ten years,
He remained lost in Bukhara.
When he reached twelve,
He migrated from Bukhara and took the road,
And, they say, occupied broad Talas.
They say he scattered the wealth
Of Abyke and Köbösh,
And took the place of Manas.
I hear travelers say:
'From his great-hearted father Manas,
The lion-child Semetey
Emerged greater,' I hear.
Saying, 'We shall cause trouble beneath him,'
Abyke and Köbösh died, they say;
Through lack of wisdom, those dogs
Saw the face of the other world, they say.
Look upon the great devilment
Of that fierce mad youth.
They say he slaughtered his grandfather, great Zhakyp,
And drank his fill of his blood;
They say he slaughtered
Everyone who challenged him.
When he migrated onward from Bukhara,
From this side of Kokand,
At the overturned edge
Of a steppe broad as the moon,
All Kokand gathered,
All six thousand champions,
Led by Zhanaaly and Karach,
