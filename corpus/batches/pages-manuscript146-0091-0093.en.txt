He never abandoned his greed,
When he spoke to the accursed one,
He unfolded the words that had befallen them,
Each time one died, they multiplied,
Fortune itself followed him,
The formidable Kyrgyz lineage,
The fallen Kyrgyz, fellow people of the land,
To his command and his word,
Tell Jeljetpes that they must submit,
Their treasure and daughter to Tengir,
Tell Jaljetpes they must give,
I continued from what was handed down,
I asked those who knew,
I did not stray from the truth-tellers,
I did not take from embellishments.
I will not let them range freely and reap,
I will not let his people sow grain,
I will not let his people summer in the mountains,
Kenen’s daughter Kermekash,
Your poor beauty, fairer than one behind a screen,
Dress her in a ring-mail coat,
Mount her upon Kertulpar,
Tell her to ride the moon-swift racer,
Without being deprived of games and gatherings,
A lifeless man will deliver her,
Taking four iron hands,
Stretching around, he will carry her away,
A man left sprawling will be a pitiful sight,
A thousand arrows will be like white frost,
Tell him to submit to the words spoken.
When Chyngteñir mounts and stands ready,
Across the whole moonlit world,
To rule as sovereign,
Tell them to surrender every villain.
If he starts trouble from the outset,
Tell him Kermekash must remain in the land.
He who turned back the black night,
Who spoke eloquently before the people,
A master of words about prosperity,
He was a broad-hearted hero,
I am Chyng, the Great Tengir,
Giving this command to you,
Hear clearly the words I speak.
For the khan to take what is due,
Let a thousand tracts remain and let him yield his share,
To surrender the daughters to Chyng,
Let him bow his neck as far as required,
Let him pay levy and tribute in money,
Let him give gold and treasure by the pud,
Let him give ten thousand remount horses,
If he scowls and sets himself in defiance,
Such is the khan’s dominion,
Let no Kalmak speak against it.
This is the padishah’s dominion,
Let him quickly submit to the profitable word spoken,
Let him promptly give what the envoys demand.
If he does not heed my worthy words,
Every proper remedy will be exhausted,
The people will be slaughtered and scattered,
Across the ridges, alas,
Let him not expose them to ruin.
Women and young children alike,
Great and small, old and young,
Women and men together,
Their land and resolve will be conquered,
They met with Jetpes,
Let no blood-guilt remain upon them.
Let every girded man return without exception,
Bring back the message itself,
O sons of the white-kalpak Kyrgyz,
