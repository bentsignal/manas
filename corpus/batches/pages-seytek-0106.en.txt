You shattered hills and opened roads;
You broke into Beijing and attacked.
You shattered stone and opened roads;
You assailed the whole wide world.
You were a lion of exalted spirit;
While my son Choro lived,
I used to say, ‘What calamity could touch us?’
None who fled you escaped alive;
You were a blue-maned panther.
I was your wailing sister-in-law,
A royal treasure, wealth that never diminishes.
May this false world come to an end!
You overthrew Afghanistan to its roots;
What ancient nation remained unharmed?
You conquered Künjüt and Khotan
And attacked Hindustan.
No land remained whole
Where your stallion had not wheeled;
No nation remained whole
Where Külchoro had not thrust his spear.
You were both warrior and lion;
A vast river-lake
That could never be emptied by drawing;
A broad and noble valley
Whose grass could never be exhausted by grazing.
You were a gray-winged hunting bird
That let no bustard settle on the steppe;
A boundlessly sharp blade of steel
Destined to meet the warrior sultan.
You were a forest fortress in a sheltered bend,
Untouched by wind when storms arose;
You never bowed to an enemy,
But were a ravenous lion.
Saying, ‘When will the lion’s son return?’
I was the sister-in-law who prayed for you.
As Kyyaz’s captive,
I endured his cruelty for long.
You never left my thoughts, my son;
I lived wondering when you would return.
It seems God has opened the door to my fortune:
The lost champion has been found,
The man I lacked has returned,
What departed has been restored,
My grief has been covered over—
It seems God has granted this before my eyes!
The one who vanished without a trace,
My blue-maned wolf of a son, seems to have returned.
I prayed for you with bitter cries.
Knowing that you had gone,
I took your elder brother’s bulletproof armor—
The precious thing, all of it together—
And put it in a great saddlebag.
I wrapped every piece together,
Thinking, ‘When my son comes, let him wear it;
Let him strike the enemy without tiring;
Let him make merry havoc;
Let him lay Kyyaz of the Jediger
Full length upon the ground;
Let him avenge his unavenged wrong;
Let him drag the Jediger of Jeti-Özön
Along the road
And make a spectacle of them before the Danggits.’
I have this precious marvel;
Listen, my fiery son:
I had a coat specially made—
Its back of iron, its sleeves of copper,
Its thickness half a cubit.
I proofed it against direct arrow shots;
It was intended for head-on battle,
And it greatly depleted the treasury.
Anything unfit was rejected with curses;
Steel was filed down and glued,
Then poured across every part.
With awls made of steel,
Immeasurably mighty champions
Stitched it with pounding blows;
When struck with a spear,
The spearhead bent and flew aside.
