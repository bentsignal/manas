Wiser than all who came before,
When foes arrived in countless thousands,
He would ride forth to meet them.
His guardian was the gray tiger;
No such soul existed beneath a saddle girth.
Brave Semetey, the great sultan,
Surpassed all others in valor.
The infidel son of the ogress,
The warrior who made the earth tremble,
He resisted without rest for seven days.
At Echoing Jar-Kyya,
On a path fit for only one horse,
In a gorge no steed could pass,
In a foul place of bitter winds,
He grappled with Sarybai,
Enduring grim hardship and night.
He saw venomous courage and harsh days,
The hero saw ridges and trials,
And met a notorious warrior of hell.
“So this is the son of the ogress;
My direst foe has emerged,
And my true skill has been revealed.
My armor is torn and my arms are spent—
O hell, can such a thing be?
Does this wrathful madman
Spare anyone who grapples with him?
My spear is shattered, my strength gone,
My power exhausted, my day finished;
The ogress’s son has worn me down.
My appointed time has come.
You who first shaped creation,
You made me a son to Khan Manas,
And made me slaughter armies and leave widows.
You made me crush stones into roads,
And made me a mighty man of fame
To whom the whole world granted no mercy.
I set fire to river valleys,
And kindled the grass-covered plains,
I poured blood from black mouths,
And crushed all who confronted me.
Yet unable to draw my blade from its sheath,
Tormented and outraged,
Hard-pressed by a loathsome foe,
Külchoro, though his soul was buried in grief,
Could not kill him himself.
With nowhere to flee,
And no grave in which to lie,
O famous land of Talas, your hills are gold and plains are orchards!
O my renowned land of Talas!
Your young are playful and your people golden,
O my beloved Kyrgyz people!
My people, how have I come to see this?” he cried.
“The hero’s strength has left my arms;
Sarybai has slain me.”
His burning soul became an ember,
And Külchoro groaned,
Halting because he could endure no more.
That hero born greater
Than his father, fierce Manas,
That spirit-aided lion,
Had faced many wrestlers.
Against the infidel Sarybai
He had unleashed a mighty conflict.
They stood face-to-face,
Fighting by turns.
When they struck with iron-headed clubs,
Fire flew from their helmets,
And sparks sprang from their maces.
Spears shattered and heroes died,
As they stood face-to-face,
Firing muskets and loosing arrows,
Battering one another to a pulp.
Weary of wielding spears,
They drove their aimless weapons
Into whatever place they struck.
All the mountains became hollows,
And every body was bruised and mangled.
The enraged infidel,
The warrior who devastated nations—
His strength and lionhearted courage
