I bloodied the deep-flowing river;
When my quarrel came upon me,
I laid low my own father.
I bloodied my white blade;
When my anger truly rose,
I laid Abyke low.
I bloodied my blue-steel blade;
When my great fury came upon me,
I laid Khan Köbösh low.
I slaughtered the forty choros,
And slaughtered them for what they had done.
I burned the rolling country,
And set the grassy land aflame.
I poured blood into the dark water—
Where then was my death?”
The dear lion raged,
The renowned hero left as an heir
By the blue-maned champion Manas;
His anger now rose unchecked,
And he tore the falcon from Kulchoro’s hand.
When the precious leash braided by Kanykey
Was pulled taut as the level sea,
One half of that leash snapped
And remained with Kulchoro;
Your swaggering madman
Wrenched the falcon away.
He swept off its hood,
And showed it the white swan;
Water streamed from its nostrils,
Its throat feathers bristled,
It toppled and cried harshly,
Struggled and flashed,
And would not remain upon the hand.
Your madman’s falcon
Surged forward, unaware it might die;
And your prince himself as well,
Before it reached the intended place,
Had made it distraught,
Waving it toward one side,
Pushing it toward the other.
His temples tightened with fury,
And Semetey, brave son of Manas,
Struggled with Akshunkar.
“It has reached the very height of its form,” he said;
“It has stood pent up for many days
And wholly passed beyond its proper form,” he said.
Driving it onward without tiring,
Thinking it might strike a stone,
He pulled the mouth of tireless Buurul
And turned the horse around.
“Go, wondrous creature,” he said;
“Put all your strength into it,” he said,
And raised the bucket-sized falcon
Before hurling it aloft.
Your noble Akshunkar
Shot from the glove like an arrow;
The blue-maned hero’s falcon
Rose and set itself on course,
Soon seeming lost from sight.
It circled and hovered,
Banked backward in flight,
And beat its wings.
When the madman’s falcon
Caught sight of the swan,
It thundered till ears went deaf,
And roared as though no bigger than a fist.
It reached the tent-sized white swan
Before an arrow could,
Drove that swan down against the earth,
And struck it as it swept past.
Dust flew and feathers billowed;
When Akshunkar struck it,
Its cry was heard in the heavens.
The tent-sized white swan’s
Head plunged into the mud;
Its strength failed as it staggered,
And it crawled toward the gray reeds,
Calling “Kukulook!” as it floundered,
