“Where is your heroic courage?”
“Drive your steel-tipped spear
Into the very heart
Of the swine confronting you!
This is the hour to show your courage!”
He had mounted Akkula;
The gray-maned lion, warrior Manas,
Rode beside him as his companion.
Seytek, lion of the bloodthirsty hero,
Seytek, whose fortune was complete,
Dark blue as the sea itself,
Rode toward the clash,
Having received the great host’s blessing.
O almighty Creator,
The cherished young boy
Was riding away to battle;
The sole hero granted as a gift
Was riding away to the fray;
He was going forth to face
Evil and affliction.
“Creator, sustain him Yourself;
Almighty Creator, prosper him;
When the people behold the boy,
May God prosper his deed.”
As the boy rode against the enemy,
The people wailed and wept,
Crying, “Protect, we pray, this boy,
Small as a crow!”
The broad Chatkal of rugged hills
Was no mountain to cross without heed;
Sarybai, oppressor of the age,
Was no foe for a hero to trust lightly.
His champion’s might was well known,
Yet the boy rode straight against him,
Going to the place of cruel calamity
And mighty strife.
With matchless courage and potent wrath,
He rode against the mighty warrior.
When the crow-sized boy set forth,
The people wailed and wept,
Their souls tormented by grief,
Their hearts distracted by care.
Behold the warrior Seytek:
He stood apart beyond all the world,
Trained beyond all the world;
He rode forth like a lion.
Seizing the black-thonged whip,
He swung it around,
And struck Kunansur’s flank
So that it rang aloud.
Kulansur turned his neck.
With a steel shaft and keen point,
The socketed enchanted spear
Was leveled at Sarybai, who had come to single combat,
And now thrust toward the sky.
He roared as he faced the foe,
Raising the battle cry of “Manas!”
As he charged the enemy,
The fearsome youth
Showed the aspect of a tiger.
When he came face-to-face with Sarybai,
With a piercing cry and shrill battle shout
The hero launched his charge.
At one glance he seemed one man,
At another, a thousand men;
At one glance, a radiant chosen steed;
His terrible majesty stood apart.
At another glance, behind the mighty warrior,
A dark-maned black leopard,
The spirit of Khan Manas,
Could be seen.
It had come to ride beside him;
The Forty Holy Ones and Khizr Ilyas
Had become his companions.
Abiding beneath blessed fortune,
They charged in ranks of forty.
Against that infidel Sarybai
He charged and thrust his spear.
When Seytek bore down upon him,
The infidel’s fury redoubled—
