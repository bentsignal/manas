I shall utterly give it all away.
For this woman's wedding,
I shall stake a yellow velvet fur coat;
I shall stake livestock in tens of thousands.
Instead of one, I shall stake a thousand;
I shall stake a camel that carries gold.
I shall stake splendid livestock;
As prizes for you, multitude,
I shall stake red-tailed riding camels.
I shall quickly stake my wealth;
Thousands of horses with fathom-long tails,
Mounts for champions, I shall stake.
If these prizes are too little,
Stake all the myriad Zhediger people!
If they do not suffice, I shall stake giants.
Without breaking up the herds on pasture,
I have staked all the abundant livestock.
Let wrestlers grapple and heroes joust;
Let there be lung-fighting and bald men's contests.
Let the spectacle surpass all others.
When the people depart roaring with laughter,
Is this not the mark of khanship?
Is this not the deed
Of a mighty, sacred soul?
To govern the people and know the nation—
Is this not the mark of khanship?
To wear a bare sword at one's side—
Is this not lordly power?
To govern the people and sustain the realm—
Come, my people, is this not lordly power?
For Lady Churok's wedding,
I shall spend all my strength,
Lavish all my countless wealth,
And scatter everything I possess.
Long ago, beside the waters of Jeti-Ozon,
At the wedding where Kiyaz took a wife,
At the feast he gave the people,
Let them say, 'Such was the skill displayed,
And thus his country encamped.'
Let the populace clamor and speak of it;
To every soul who hears, may this feast
Sit as smoothly as butter.
Let the victor become rich;
Let the fallen remain empty-handed.
Let them say, 'Such was the skill,
Such was the example,
Such was the gracious khan's design,
Such was the pattern;
Such was the great clamor he gave the people,
Such was their entertainment.'
Tell what you saw
To old men sunk into their beds,
And to stumbling children
Who have not yet gathered their wits.
Let the people bustle and raise a din;
Rejoice, my people,
For the dear lady's wedding.
For this unfortunate noble peri,
How could I spare my livestock?
Not merely my livestock—for Churok,
How could I spare my life?
If these prizes are too little,
Stake the mighty winged steed,
My horse Tootoru;
Stake a land like Jeti-Ozon.
If these prizes are to be opened,
Stake the people, beginning with the Zhediger!
At such a splendid, great wedding,
Would stinginess bring any good?
Will one who withholds a gracious word
Ever prosper?
When their khan issued his decree,
The old men grew excited;
As though massacre had struck,
Every one of them grew excited.
"The khan's decree has come;
He ordered, 'Let none remain behind,'"
