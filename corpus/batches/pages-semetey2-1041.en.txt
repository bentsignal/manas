Ayneke, whom Teke married;
Oyumkan, whom Ormonbek married;
And Kanykey, whom Manas married—
There were twelve of us girls.
We were foals of one stallion,
Children of one father.
We were gentle souls.
The power to discern from afar
Was Akerkech’s gift.
While his father Köksö lived,
It was ever the Kazakh custom
To bluster loudly.
Ah, my river’s fountainhead,
My champion, seen only in my old age!
I was a forlorn soul in my bridal youth,
And late indeed to braid my hair.
From the imposing Manas,
My foal, I bore you when I was near death.
You who stirred turmoil throughout the world—
Semetey and Ümötöy,
You two maternal cousins
Were born on the same day.
Ninety beasts were slaughtered at your feasts,
And you were given matching names.
You were maternal cousins;
Kinship bound you.
No good comes from striking one’s kin;
Two true men should not oppose each other.
One should never attack a close relation;
No good comes from spilling kindred blood.
Accept what he says;
Give him whatever he desires—
Abundant wealth and countless herds!
Send him peacefully on his way;
Make peace without hostility
And remain on friendly terms!
The reason you must do this is that
My elder sister Akerkech
Was a woman revered by all,
A blessed lady.
She was one for whom the white felt was spread,
And among unfortunate womankind
She possessed sacred power.
With lambent, sheep-like eyes,
A fair face without a freckle,
A stately neck and radiant eyes,
Sweet in speech, with a honeyed tongue—
No woman like her exists in all the world.
Full and radiant as the full moon,
Plump and sleek as a white swan,
My poor noble sister
Possessed such great honor.
Known to every listening ear and seeing eye,
Her fame spread across the earth;
She was a woman of sacred power.
From her husband, gray-maned Köksö,
She received as a gift
That valiant son.
That woman Akerkech,
That radiant forceful soul,
Had a guardian spirit supporting her.
She made her son
Her refuge in Köksö’s place.
Köksö had no many sons;
Ümötöy was his only child.
Akerkech, who bore Ümötöy,
Was a woman of sacred power.
My dear, formidable sister
Had a mole upon her lip;
Her simplest word could bring misfortune,
For there was a seal upon her tongue.
Her curse never missed its mark,
Her malediction never fell in vain;
None prospered when she spoke against them,
And her curse never failed.
Such a lady was his mother,
And terrible indeed was her curse.
There was a beauty mark upon her lip.
