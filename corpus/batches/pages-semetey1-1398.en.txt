My champion left as support!
Black-faced Beijing
Has a pass on which it relies,
And a place where it lies fortified.
That creature posted as its sentry—
At every moment, the swine
Has a khan who guards it!
It has a pass where it lies fortified,
And a place on which it relies.
It has a securely built stronghold,
And a fortified city
No mortal can destroy!
It has Kechil and its established order,
And people of forty tribes.
Gold comes from its land,
And it has countless kinds of wealth.
Here the infidels
Have a fox posted as sentinel.
There is the face of Kara-Kyya;
Here is the dark peril!
It is no pass to cross in sport;
Think upon it, my only one,
The Chinese are no easy people!
While bloodthirsty Manas lived,
While my champion Almambet was safe,
Syrgak, swift as a stray kid,
Was the companion
Of my precious elder Ala.
While my whole world, Almam, lived,
Yesterday, when he reconnoitered the road to Beijing,
Foremost among sixty,
The mightiest of the forty companions,
The champion boy Syrgak—
When he took him as companion,
When they scattered the Chinese herds,
And opened the road of the faith of Islam.
At the frontier lookout,
Here on this pass, its fox—
Yesterday, when Almam happened to shoot it,
Across the moon-yellow plain,
Pursuing it with Kökchebich,
When Syrgak reached and slew it,
When the champions caught the fox,
At the lookout farther on,
Champion lion Almam
Wrought slaughter there.
What use is telling or withholding it?
If told, the bastard's tale
Can never be exhausted.
There, at the spur of the ending mountain,
At the blunt projecting headland,
On the back of the black cliff,
Almam had dealt out grief.
On the guarded road no man trod,
A cub of the fox remained alive.
That orphan fox grew,
And became fat and vigorous, they say;
Its fire-red fur
Lengthened to forty fathoms.
This year, at the golden-poled tent,
For hero Kongurbai,
It has served faithfully, they say;
That fox now stands watch there, they say.
From seven days' journey away,
It knows a scent, I have heard.
The mighty Kalcha, hero Kongurbai,
Posted it firmly as a sentinel, they say,
And himself became khan of Beijing, they say!
If we let the fox suspect us,
Swifter than a flying bird,
It races across the plain
And in a moment gives word
To hero Khan Kongurbai.
No wind can match its running;
I hear it is a perfect fox,
One that raises alarm in Beijing,
The black eye of calamity, they say.
