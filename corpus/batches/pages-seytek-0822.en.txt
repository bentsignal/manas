His war cry became a bullet,
And wherever that doomed man faced,
A red flame rose waist-high.
His form was strange, his nature cruel;
Such wrath, it seems, is never born.
He thirstily swallowed the Black Khan,
Who had committed no crime and bore no feud,
Who had done not the slightest wrong,
Yet he began a grievous conflict.
Your dear people of forty forefathers—
Against them he began a grievous conflict.
The field filled with black battle standards,
The people were shattered in an uproar,
And he began a violent war.
Regarding the Kyrgyz as less than a hair,
He slaughtered them without a backward glance.
From Sarybai’s venom,
From the visible fury of hell,
The peaceful people who had lived in joy
He slaughtered and plunged into misery.
The vigorous, swift and peerless hero
Was brought down by the swine Sarybai.
Fierce in menace and bitter in venom,
He had nations of enemies he had fought;
No such being can come from humankind.
No falcon had struck the wild swan,
Yet not one swan remained whole.
None whom the madman’s hand had touched
Remained unharmed among the people.
Some upon the borders fled aside;
He burned many lands.
He poured blood into the black waters,
Planted his banner on the grassy ground,
And began strife among kindred peoples.
He devoured everyone he encountered,
Destroying the entire nation;
All who had committed no crime,
The young and the old alike—
He extinguished their burning hearths
And drove every living soul away.
Many dear souls perished.
Lions who defended their people came forth,
And warriors from many nations came forth;
Many people beheld
The true land from which none returns.
The people were shattered and the nation cried out;
He began a violent conflict.
Their whole population was slaughtered;
Saying, “We shall all perish together,
For Sarybai has encountered us
And utterly sucked us dry,”
The people summoned their courage.
Anyone who resisted him,
Sarybai left none alive,
But severed their heads like sheep.
He split their bellies like melons;
Destroying the people and slaughtering the nation,
He brought such catastrophe upon them.
Because of the calamity that had passed,
Because of this swine,
They scattered all they had gathered
And fled, stripped of fortune.
Leaving not a single group behind,
They departed in one desperate flight
Toward the defile of Jan-Baspas.
In the desert of Jangeldik,
Of all who openly came before him,
He slew a multitude.
Anyone who displeased him,
He beheaded with his sword.
Countless guiltless souls
He gave over to massacre.
Wailing and weeping bitterly,
Lamenting and crying aloud,
Truly burning with grief,
Despairing of their fly-like lives,
The people lost their senses,
Moaning as if about to die,
Because of Sarybai’s venom,
Because of the infidel who had turned depraved,
