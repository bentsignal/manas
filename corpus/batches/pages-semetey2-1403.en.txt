For noble men, Churok
Truly was a fitting companion.
This woman truly possessed
A power to ruin peoples
And slaughter nations.
Good heavens, if blood fell upon black earth,
Behold her flesh, whiter than snow;
If blood fell upon snow,
Behold her face, redder than blood.
Supple as an ermine,
When Aichurok smiled,
Her broad white teeth gleamed.
Seeing Churok's form,
All the khans raised a clamor.
Many a man, meaning to carve meat,
Cut his own hand along with it.
The people bustled in amazement;
The nation rejoiced and proclaimed
That the peerless, unfortunate lady
Had astonished the Zhediger people.
They marveled at her beauty:
The noble, luckless woman
Was indeed a renowned beauty.
"Our people were disrupted and strife arose;
It was fitting that hero Kojo and hero Toltoy
Were slain,"
They said of the events gone by.
Because of that radiant, luckless woman,
Because of renowned Churok,
Gray-maned Toltoy the lion died;
Chynkojo and Tolok died together,
And many souls died in grief.
Because of this overwhelming woman,
Many khans who met her died;
Because of this Churok,
They took up spear and sword,
Fired guns and drew bows,
And fought in bloody clashes.
Look—the people died;
See how many giants died,
One alone and many together;
Heroes died in suffering.
The people were slaughtered amid clamor;
The heavenly people fell into turmoil.
The nation split into hostile camps,
Opposing and attacking one another,
Ripping lungs and piercing gallbladders,
Killing and cutting off heads;
Many peoples suffered massacre.
It was a God-struck affair;
That the multitude suffered torment
Was true without a lie.
It was a blessed and wondrous night.
Ah, that radiant, daring woman
Was a heavenly noble lady,
The very firebrand
Who had peoples raided and heroes slain.
Her name was beautiful Churok,
Yet she was turmoil, not mere Churok.
She was an infidel woman
Who shook nations and disrupted peoples,
The very night of divine power,
The very firebrand herself.
Whoever saw her burned like an ember;
Seeing the fierce woman's beauty,
What soul could look and remain unmoved?
"If only she were my wife,"
Thought every soul who saw her and burned.
Ah, that radiant, luckless woman
Was herself a noble peri,
The dark eye at the heart of turmoil,
One of the truest beauties.
The noble, unfortunate woman
Had ranged over the breadth of the earth;
When this shapeshifting woman transformed,
She flew and wandered
Through the four corners of the world,
Fanning a tail like a parrot's.
