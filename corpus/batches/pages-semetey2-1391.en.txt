If only I could see her, my heart would be at peace;
Apart from my mother, whom I took as my companion,
I have no one.
My insides burn, my liver is aflame;
I have burned and blazed to ashes.
My mature self has become spoil,
And I am going to the Jediger;
I have no mother to care for me!
Whom shall I hold as my Mecca?
The mainstay of the whole world—
Could my lion have come to this?
My blessed fortune, the White Matriarch—
Could my mother have come to this?”
Thus Aichürök,
Speaking sorrowfully, said these words.
Your sister-in-law whitened the dawn like an argali,
Speaking softly, saying this:
What recourse is there against humiliation?
Does not everyone have to submit
To the violence of the powerful?
Countless wrestlers surrounded her;
Against this Kyyaz, who fancied himself a god,
Even if a host encircled him, it could not prevail.
What could the helpless woman do?
Their khan issued his decree;
When the khan gave his command,
His wrestlers closed in.
Kyyaz’s violence
Surpassed all others in the world.
His demons had risen;
Good heavens, how hideous they looked!
Black-omened Kyyaz’s
Teeth were whetted,
And his power had grown immense.
He had diverted the waters of the Chüy;
Abandoning not merely his livestock
But even his own life,
The Jediger surged back again
And carried out savage plunder.
They slaughtered with fierce resolve;
Everything the eye could see
They gave over wholly to pillage.
The Jediger who descended upon you
Entered the white palace,
Dragging their whips behind them.
They spoke taunting words
And provoked the anger
Of Chürök, the formidable woman.
For the wretched prince’s wife
They dug a pit in the place of honor;
They scattered all four kinds of livestock.
The Jediger poured in without restraint
And broke open and searched the bundles;
Thus they stirred up needless strife.
They raided the mares from the hills
And cut off the head
Of the owner who came for his mare.
They forced out all the world’s bile,
Beating his brains from his skull.
They seized his camels as booty
And spilled the contents of his belly;
Thus did the Jediger act.
Crimson cloth in red patterns
They folded on the bias
And loaded upon red camels;
Crimson cloth mottled green
They folded beside the blood-red goods.
Pure gold and ruby stones
They loaded upon swift camels;
Loading each pack saddle with treasure,
They set out with all the wealth.
They dressed her in loose-flowing silk
And mounted her on an ambling pacer;
They dressed her in patterned silk—
Poor Chürök—
And mounted her on a sturdy pacer.
With horns and trumpets sounding,
Six thousand camels and forty pack saddles
