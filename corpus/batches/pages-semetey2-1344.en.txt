“Go forward, every last one of you,
Young and old alike!
Fire muskets and draw bows;
All you black Jediger,
Attack together as one!
Overwhelm and plunder
The helpless women and young children.
Break the stronghold, take the spoils,
Seize the swaying beauties—
Fight the battle in this way!
You massed black Jediger,
From ancient times until now
You have lived by conquest;
Why do you retreat like this
From gatekeepers and women?
What will you do if the lost gray wolf is found
And Semetey returns?
Jediger, divide among yourselves
The property left to the women!
Rather than flee the gatekeepers,
Rather than lie here facing death,
You should die, Jediger!
Rather than the courageous Kanchoro
Flying into a rage
And starting this calamity outright,
Charging with a battle cry
And having the Jediger slaughtered.
Rather than return without breaking the gate,
Rather than stand frightened of women—
Jediger, lower than women—
Rather than flee the gatekeepers,
May Kyyaz your leader die;
You massed black Jediger,
You snarling curs, may all of you die!
You witless black Jediger,
Could a hero be like you?
All of you are a wretched, cowardly people;
Is it any wonder you are called cowards?
Had I not been here, Jediger,
Külchoro would have entered alone
And destroyed your people
In your free land of Jeti-Özön;
He would have cooled your ardor,
Broken your backs,
And hacked your lion,
Kyyaz your khan, to pieces.”
He began his wrathful tirade;
With piercing cries and rallying shouts,
Raising a furious clamor,
He sent the massed black Jediger
Charging to their slaughter.
Again and again he spoke;
The treacherous kinsman Kanchoro—
May he never prosper or multiply,
May he never thrive—
Roused the Jediger.
Every last man thundered;
The entire Jediger host
Launched its roaring assault.
Kanchoro drove in
The skillful spearmen,
The strongest of the Jediger,
And hemmed in the palace.
They wrought havoc at the palace;
The eye could not follow them,
Nor tell which man was which,
Like tumbleweed flying in the wind.
May God spare us the sight—
They stood surrounding the gate.
The executioners barring the gate
Fought without rest for seven days;
Who had ever seen such a struggle?
Talas rose like a mountain before their departure;
Her own son born from her body—
To tell it fully is a great calamity—
Kanchoro had become an infidel foe.
A destroyer had arisen within,
A man who brought ruin upon the people.
