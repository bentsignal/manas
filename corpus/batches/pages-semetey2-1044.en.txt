In the treasury is a yellow key—
A key with its tassel hanging.
I shall unlock and open its mouth,
Saying, “Here it is, unruly Kazakh,”
And among all his people,
Young and old alike,
I shall scatter gold by the sackful.
If Ümötöy will listen to reason,
Let us accept his terms.
On the hills are sixty thousand horses,
Thoroughbreds mingled with three-year-olds;
Let us drive them forth in a train,
And accept his terms.
If he treats his cousin with honor,
Let us give this Ümötöy
Thoroughbreds mingled with swift racers.
Give him robes to wear;
If Ümötöy will make peace,
Give him whatever force he asks,
And grant him abundant herds in great number.
Let us eat meat from a single platter,
Meet face-to-face and behold one another,
Speak again of former times,
Clasp hands and become friends.
Let us bind our spears together
And make this pledge:
To fight the enemy together.
Our bravest and our elders,
As cousins bound until Judgment Day,
Let us all meet together.
To fight the enemy together,
If Ümötöy can be reconciled,
To pursue our claims together,
And scout the enemy together.
If massacre overtakes us in one place,
Let us gather and die together.
Make a firm peace and return;
Say, “Your hero spoke thus,”
And deliver these entrusted words of mine.
If he grows furious and obstinate,
Seize and subdue the slave,
And bring him with both hands bound.
Gouge out both his eyes,
And drive him before your uncle Semetey.
If Ümötöy will come to terms,
Bring him to me.
So that he may become my closest kinsman,
Show him before my eyes.
Let us camp in the same valley
And become true brothers.
Companions gathered in this world,
Sharing death and sharing faith,
Let us become the closest of kin.
If Ümötöy will come to terms,
I shall withhold none of my inheritance.
Broad Talas, left by your father,
And my land of reedy Keng-Kol—
I shall give all this to Ümötöy,
Together with the people left by my father.
I shall give it to him outright;
I shall submit to whatever
That hero Ümötöy asks.
Let us send word among the people;
If he desires to be khan,
Let us spread word throughout the world,
Across the lands of the Kyzylbash.
Calling him “Khan Ümötöy the hero,”
Before all the peoples in our sight,
Let us raise him as khan.
If Ümötöy refuses to listen
And sets the Kazakhs against us,
Bring torment upon the Kazakhs;
Bring ruin upon his people,
And sate yourself at Kök-Muroo.
Saying, “These are the Kazakhs you have seen,”
Bring back many captives roped at the waist.
You were a hero and a lion;
Your words give me joy.
