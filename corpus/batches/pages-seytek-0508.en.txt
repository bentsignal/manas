He called them to comradeship.
Speaking softly but with firm resolve,
He addressed the gray-wolf warrior:
“My heroic gray wolf, my champion,
Do you know what I know?
Do you sense what I sense?
His forefather on his father’s side—
His father was the warrior Kutu.
He was inscribed by true good fortune
As the rightful heir.
He rekindled the extinguished fire
And brought the dead soul back to life.
He joined what had been severed
And gathered what had been scattered;
He was a noble son of his father.
He bore burdens vast as a river
And was a source of life to others.
God took his father,
And after many passing years,
The inheritance came to him.
He scouted around, grieving for the land,
And went out to reconnoiter.
While on that scouting expedition,
Sarybay, son of the monster,
Appeared directly before him.
Our unyielding and faithful
Black Giant
Met him in battle,
And hostile death overtook him.
His loyal surviving comrades
Lost heart and strength,
And came fleeing in ruin.
Your true champion, the Black Giant,
Was killed by Sarybay.
Now the roaring Sarybay,
Loosing his raiders and swaggering,
Means to drive and pursue
The Kyrgyz of Ala-Too to destruction,
Allowing none of their princes to stand
And trampling us underfoot.
As though merely visiting his in-laws,
As though he were a prince ruling the people,
He grins, laughs, and rejoices.
His eighty-four kinds of armor are donned,
And his journey has taken an evil turn.
Your hero stands impatient,
His two eyes become four with watching.
The falcon son of Almambet,
Your blessed champion,
Who never bowed his neck to mortal man,
Your renowned and stubborn warrior,
Grieves at the delay.
As though going to see the bride he has won,
Your hero is upheld by fortune.
He stirs, laughs, and rejoices.
“Sarybay, son of the monster,
Has far-reaching power and potent venom;
Oh, what will become of us?” they say,
And the suffering Kyrgyz grieve.
But at the mention of death he laughs aloud:
This gray-wolf kinsman Külchoro
Stands visibly rejoicing.
In feverish haste, unable to stop,
He dons his bulletproof coat,
Arranges all his weapons,
And girds on his flashing sword.
As though a black fog were descending,
Fury spreads across his face.
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
He has thrown the whole world into turmoil.
Tall and broad-shouldered,
An unrivaled wrestler and a lion,
He never retreats before death—
Ah, what a steadfast, precious warrior!
Calling the renowned men to him,
He drew his arm behind him
And sighed as though weary.
Semetey in the encampment
Called loudly to his lion-like elder,
And to Seytek, khan of the people:
