A saint unlike ordinary men.
Go slaughter the gray beast,
And at the funeral feast, my orphan,
Eat until you are fully satisfied,
Then lay him in a place of honor.
Remember these solemn words;
My hero, my son, heed my counsel.
Your grandmother Kanykey
Was a great and holy woman;
That we were parted from the saint
Was a fate ordained by God.
Weeping and grieving in disgrace,
My darling, we were left behind,
Torn away from our motherland,
From my black-walled city with its gates,
From my village pitched upon the river terrace,
From the homeland that sustained me,
And from the angelic purity
Of my heroic mother.
I wailed aloud in grief,
And while my frail life yet clung sweetly,
I was parted from my true mother.
I screamed and wept till the pasture rang,
Enduring disgrace and bondage.
They dealt me a cruel fate
And slashed my breast open.
My two eyes rolled wildly,
My arms and legs hung limp;
Your fierce mother was bound,
And my two eyes wept a pasture of tears.
My blood drained away as I choked,
My poor coat was torn apart.
As I screamed in grief,
I was parted from my towering protector.
While I suffered plunder,
They cut down my banner with swords,
Hewed down my fruit with axes,
Trampled my living body underfoot,
Struck me heedless of my fury,
And reduced my days to a mere handful.
No hero could withstand that blade,
Nor could any man equal it;
Its steel nature was plain to see,
And two foes who faced it were cleft apart.
When enemies came face-to-face with it,
Their blood was spilled.
Know that such was Zulpukor:
When it unleashed tumult,
Its very blade became invisible,
While the hostile enemy’s coats and robes
Were taken away as plunder.
Upon the broad ridges of Talas,
The keen steel blade was left behind
In the hands of accursed Kanchoro.
Go gather and bring back your inheritance;
Say, “These are the possessions left by my father,”
And demand them from that treacherous dog.
If he will not heed your words,
If he refuses what you are to receive,
Come and tell wretched me,
“Mother, he refused to give it,”
And return without trading insults.
If you have the strength, go
To your aged grandsire Bakai,
And saying, “You are my inherited legacy,”
Bring your poor aged grandsire
Back here with you.
If by chance he has died,
If he has suddenly left this world,
If your grandsire has passed away,
Your elder, the hero Bakai,
Bend him gently, holding him at the waist,
And load him upon the gray beast.
If the chance is yours,
If you find a solitary place,
Choose a fitting spot and dig his grave,
Dedicate and slaughter the gray beast,
And with your own hands
Mark your grandsire’s grave and lay him there.
Your grandsire, the aged Bakai—
