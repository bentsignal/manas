You made me a lion who slaughters Chinese;
If I lose my valiant friends,
Divine Power, what have I done to You?”
Striking Toruchaar once on the thigh,
Pitching forward like a mountain,
Whirling his spear into his hand,
Into the surging Chinese,
Manas attacked without stopping.
Manas arrived with a bound;
Into the host that filled the earth,
He plunged among them.
Their men who cast lassos,
Their champions who swung battle-axes,
Those with nets of iron rope,
Those all armed with daggers—
Your ocean-like Manas reached them.
All of them where he arrived,
He struck down and passed.
Their giants no horse could carry,
He speared down from their horses.
Their courageous men, their warriors,
He speared down, stretched out flat.
Bellowing in his wrath,
The Manchus, like black insects,
He drove out in a sweeping rout.
Can a mortal challenge and defeat him?
Against the champion born bloodthirsty,
Can one who grapples be his equal!
Girding his waist in a frenzy,
Into the unbelievers who filled the earth,
Raging Zholoy and Ezkara,
Against heroes such as these,
He entered alone and attacked.
Those with nets of iron rope,
Those all armed with daggers,
Khan Manas slaughtered as he went.
The strong men clad in jackets,
When they saw Manas's bearing,
Their heroes were thrown into panic.
“That bloodthirsty one—may your house burn—
He will kill us and finish us,” they said.
“Slaughtering every one of you,
He will shatter and slay you,” they said.
They broke from the army and fled.
The reason they fled:
The hearts of those who saw him faltered;
Your wolf-man, one person,
Appeared like many men.
When one looked from the front,
In your champion himself
Was the terror of five lions.
When one looked from the side,
He had the shape of a dragon.
His war cry split the rocks;
He had come driving them onward.
Letting blood flow, spilling stomach contents,
The Black Kalmyks and Manchus,
He had come slaughtering them.
Your accomplished one reached them and thrust his spear,
Using abundant, surpassing skill.
The champions his spear reached
Fell from their horses, sprawling.
Their champions like camels and rocks
Were slaughtered, stretched out flat.
Those who saw had no regret.
Because of Kutubiy,
Facing the Chinese,
Illustrious Manas's
Strife was wrought upon the Kalmyks.
Whirling his spear without stopping,
The mass of Black Manchus,
He broke apart their ranks.
Those who came close to grapple,
He beat with his battle-axe.
The champions skilled at riding,
Unable to find their wits,
Fled, jolting along.
Shattering seven hundred,
The champions farther away,
He chased from behind with his spear.
Galloping on the courser Toruchaar,
All their giants and monsters,
Your magnanimous one entered, bringing them down.
The spur of broad Altai—
They fled to the spur and sought shelter.
Those who escaped alive, not dead,
Were drenched in crimson blood.
Blue haze, burning dust,
Their threadlike mustaches brushed smooth,
Many Chinese souls were slain.
Fit to be felt throughout the world,
Your magnanimous one wrought strife.
Fortunate Kutubiy,
Your valiant, hapless Manas
Rescued him.
The unbelievers like dark willows,
He speared again and pursued,
Driving them toward the emperor.
Through their middle, the wolf-warrior
Bored his way.
Around them, Kutubiy
Cut his way through in slaughter.
On the governors' side, Khan Manas
Closed them in.
Right through their middle, Kutubiy
Beat his way.
Through their middle, Kutubiy
Came reaping his path.
From behind, the Kalmyks,
Lion Manas, the leopard,
Leaving not one alive,
Came sucking their lives away.
Their governors floundered;
They had taken to flight.
Not letting the fleeing enemy escape,
Lion Manas and Kutubiy
Had overtaken them.
Whatever they chose, to the utmost,
They had done.
The swarming Chinese,
Valiant Manas then
