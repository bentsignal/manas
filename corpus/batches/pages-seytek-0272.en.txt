“Crossing forty mountain passes,
Through terrible hardship and great turmoil,
The gray horse has fled.”
Thus Külchoro spoke,
His heart sinking and breaking,
His heart cleft into many ravines,
His cherished hopes shattered.
Aichürök and Külchoro
Both wailed and wept.
Dark clouds covered the sky;
This was a dreadful thing to behold:
The bloodthirsty hero’s noble gray
Plainly escaped and fled.
The gray began this calamity,
Appearing, then vanishing again.
Your gray-maned kinsman Külchoro,
Unable to endure such grief,
Was near to dying.
Brave Seytek grew impassioned
And set out upon Kylküröң.
The young prince’s fine son
Was himself a valiant hero.
Then brave Seytek said:
“My mother and my uncle—
If fate decrees, let me die,
But let me see where
My valiant mother Kanykei is.
If the cruel enemy Kanchoro
Has laid hands upon my mother,
I shall fight him and spill his blood.
I shall discover the truth.
If I do not return to my lands of Jylgyn and Keң-Kol,
To my renowned Kyrgyz people,
And settle in their dwelling place;
If I do not make my homeland
The ancestral land my father left,
Go and honor his tomb,
And send word to my people—
Ancient Talas and Keң-Koshoy
Are lands left by my forefathers.
He has none of his possessions left,
Nor the gray horse he cherished
In place of an elder brother.
Parted from the gray horse,
Külchoro has no fortune.
Ah, my valiant uncle, may you live;
May Külchoro never know misery.
May God, who creates the soul,
Never visit affliction like this
Upon His mortal servant.
Who has not tilted jauntily
The sable cap upon his head?
While fortune crowned his head,
Who has not gone about in thunderous pride?
When a valiant hero set his purpose,
What enemy before him did not scatter?
I fell captive and grieved,
Parted from my khan and uncle,
Whom I had held most dear.
While my valiant uncle lived,
I towered higher than the hills;
Parted from my hero,
I became more wretched than any man.
My head, once surrounded by followers,
Now reels as I grieve.
By my khan-uncle’s last command,
I have lost the noble steed.
While my fortune remained whole,
Ah, my dear one, I became a burdened soul.
Parted from my uncle,
Having lost the noble gray,
I have camped beside the river.
I have become embers from sobbing,
As though my fire had gone out.
May my gray-maned lord not die;
May Külchoro never know anguish.
We wailed and grieved;
Today we lost the gray.
Stricken and sorrowing,
Parted from the noble gray,
