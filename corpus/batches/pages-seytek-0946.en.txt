These were Khan Seytek’s words.
Sarybay came forth to face him;
Such were the heroes.
Wrapped in blue, bullet-proof armor,
Raging like a gray-blue tiger,
That infidel Sarybay cried:
“Wretched Seytek,
If a host comes crowding behind you,
And overwhelms your precious life,
You shall see in the blink of an eye
How readily you turn back!
You will see such calamity;
You call yourself a lion born of heroes!”
Then, poised like a black eagle,
Raging in fury,
Khan Sarybay stood there,
Appearing like a colossal giant.
Before he could turn his horse’s mouth,
When the madman flew into rage,
While Khan Sarybay stood there,
No man dared face him,
No mortal could approach him.
He thundered like a mountain,
Mustering courage in his grim wrath,
And when he loomed before them,
The great host split ten ways.
Eagerly bracing himself,
Swaying with menace,
Sparks flew from his mace,
Fire streamed from his fury,
Blood issued from his black eyes,
And death breathed from his menace.
Glowering wrathfully,
With a black purse at his belt,
His voice made the space
Between earth and blue sky tremble.
His surpassing courage surged,
All his might was in his hands;
As though devouring mankind,
He darkened and scowled,
Spitting blood and chewing stones.

His deeds were those of a tiger;
Such was his looming form.
Unhurried, unflustered, unconcerned,
This scion of a mighty people
Had been endowed beyond measure
By Almighty God the Creator.
Sarybay, son of the ogre,
Was a sight for mortals to behold;
Any mortal who encountered
His appearance and form
Might die before his appointed death—
Such was his aspect.
And if you looked at the man himself,
He resembled the naked taker of souls.
He was tall as a plane tree;
At the villain’s appearance
No man could stand unmoved.
He charged like a boar,
And pursued like the wind;
The way he faced his foe
Was like a bolt of lightning.
His strength was fearsome, his venom fierce;
Such was the one who matched him.
He gnawed whoever crossed his path,
And seemed ready to take the life of any challenger;
Such was he when his measure was taken.
Then Sarybay
Raised his steel-bladed Zulfiqar,
A mighty weapon that shattered combat.
When one whose true death had arrived
Fell afoul of the wrathful man,
Sarybay’s sweeping arm
Brought down foes too heavy for horses;
On every field where he fought,
He was manifestly swift and skillful.
The wisest and strongest,
All who entered
The fury of the melee—
