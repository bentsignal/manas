It is my time to strike.
If he does not submit to my words,
If he does not give his old maid,
I shall bring splendid slaughter
Upon accursed Akun;
If Azhibai and Tümönbai appear,
I shall take their heads."
Drinking vodka and panting,
Lion Toltoi spoke thus,
Growing excited by himself
And speaking to himself.
He girded his waist firmly,
Mounted Surkoyon,
And raced across the steppe.
Calamitous Toltoi
Set out and spurred onto the road.
Looming like Ala-Too,
He raced along the road,
Roaring in excitement.
He rode a sleek horse like water,
Speaking to himself,
Settling into his wrath,
And girding on his unsheathed sword;
Like a cloud about to rain again,
He turned in anger.
Gray-wolf Toltoi's abundance
Was complete,
But your mountainlike hero
Was deficient in fortune.
Had his fortune been whole,
He would have been a mountainlike lion.
His right shoulder was broad;
He was a hero who shattered the Oirats.
He knew the people through wise thought,
And possessed command.
He subdued the whole world
And possessed wrestling strength.
Hero Toltoi came onward,
Standing erect and untroubled;
The drunkard who had swallowed vodka
Reckoned an enemy appearing on the road
As less than the hem of his robe.
If an enemy appeared before him,
What would dear Toltoi do?
He looked ready to carve them with his sword;
If an enemy encountered him,
He looked ready to strike them all down
And slaughter every one.
Surkoyon beneath him
Ran beyond all bounds, leaping;
Hero Toltoi above him
Came onward then,
Holding his battle-axe ready.
Hero Toltoi of the Zhediger
Was born an accomplished khan;
He was the khan who gathered and ruled
The Zhediger of Zheti-Özön.
Toltoi's heart desired
To lie in Aichürök's embrace,
The daughter of Akun.
His inlaid spear with a hollow socket
Came bobbing into view.
In his own heart then,
He came onward rejoicing.
Saying of Akun's daughter Chürök,
"I shall raise her white dress
And mount her white breast,"
No matter how many enemies appeared before him,
He intended to enter among them alone.
To take as a wife,
There was no woman but Chürök;
If ten thousand enemies appeared before him,
There was no hero but himself.
Drunk and rejoicing,
Beastly Toltoi boasted,
Beat the doolbas once,
And raced upon Surkoyon,
His inlaid spear leveled,
