Her heart boiled like fire:
“Can an aged soul live forever?
Has not the mischief
Of wretched Chachykey gone far enough?
Despairing of her fly-like life,
Behind Aichürök
Kanykey came running.
“Kanchoro has always been fierce.
My dear son Külchoro
Has always been tenderhearted.
If they stand face-to-face,
Thrusting spears and striking with axes,
Battering each other in combat,
Taking up Zulfiqar-like spears,
Driving them into living hearts,
And fighting without measure;
If they begin a dreadful conflict
And stake lives frail as flies
Upon a single hair;
If they begin a slaughtering battle,
When blades are drawn aslant,
The sword will blaze with heat
As they grapple till they are cherry-red—
Such is the ruin of furious war.
If by chance the fatal hour arrives
In a struggle such as this,
Kanchoro, who guarded the horde and ruled the people,
Who mounted and held the golden throne,
Who never suffered torment or humiliation
But grazed freely on broad pastures,
Whose five fingers never lacked grease,
Whose marrow no arrow could pierce—
Black-hearted Kanchoro
Will wage the clash savagely.
By what means will he seize
My only son, my sole protector?
Only lately recovered from slavery,
Grown strong after years of misery,
His shoulders only newly filled out,
My lion was pure steel.
Her darkening eyes became clouded;
The two servants who kept her alive
Stiffened like stakes, palsied,
Their chests rasping,
Their entire bodies
Shuddering beyond control.
Her twelve bones and limbs
Wasted helplessly, wheezing;
Her ten vertebrae and eighty joints
Crackled, swollen with yellow fluid.
Her waist sank and her back bent,
Her stricken body withered.
The old lady remained there
With her body weakened and diminished,
Brought level with the black earth,
Left stooped and bent.
Gripping the doorpost, she ran,
Burning and grieving
At Kanchoro’s wrath.
While divinely protected Külchoro,
Her precious son, still lived,
Kanchoro had humiliated her.
She came to slash him to pieces,
To torture him and bring calamity,
To spare not a hair of the villain,
But torment him and take his life;
To face whatever fate might bring,
And stand as a protecting presence
Beside her son, the falcon-warrior.
Your mother Kanykey ran,
Flying like an arrow in her joy,
Toward Khan Kanchoro’s horde,
Ready as a hawk.
Inspiration came to her from the unseen;
She weighed her wits and pondered:
“I shall seize and slaughter Kanchoro,
And roast the bitch Chachykey
For my feast,
Then put an end to the grievous misery
And all the humiliations I have suffered.”
