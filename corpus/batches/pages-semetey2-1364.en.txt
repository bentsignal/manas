She took the sword in hand.
Against the wrestlers surging in,
Against the men who meant to seize her,
The blustering, broad-chested men,
The men rushing in with battle-cries,
Matchless in her swiftness,
Aichürök of manly bearing
Took the sword in hand.
As the clamoring Jediger
Were about to turn and flee,
She took the sword in hand.
Before the wrestlers could scatter,
Before they could block the gate-mouth,
She drew the sword and stabbed,
Striking this way and that,
Whirling everything into dust,
Filling the gate-mouth with blood,
And laying their wrestlers low.
Aichürök fought with courage,
Refusing to yield to their force,
And your sister-in-law would not surrender
Her dear head to plunder.
They kept her tightly surrounded
And fought without pause many times.
The heedless Jediger rushed in;
The champions who had taken gold,
Who had plundered women and made them wail,
The heroes swollen with pride,
Were struck across the neck by her sword,
Blood pouring down their breasts.
Many men reeled in panic;
A tumult rose as they stampeded,
And many wrestlers fled.
Of the many wrestlers who met her,
Men lay dead.
The lions who boasted of strength
Were bodies without heads;
Some lay stretched on their backs,
And many wrestlers, many men,
Lay sprawled upon the ground.
Your sister-in-law had taken up the sword;
Of the fine champions who entered combat,
Each one a chosen steed among men,
All of them renowned heroes,
Aichürök slaughtered about a hundred
And cut off their heads.
They could not endure the heat
Of your grieving sister-in-law Chürök;
They could not approach her brow,
Could not seize her in the palace,
Could not make her their spoil,
Could not face and capture
The renowned khan’s fierce wife.
When they saw the ill-fated woman,
They lost their wits;
Having badly misjudged their plan,
They let the luckless woman
Cut down their warriors and khans.
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Went to Khan Kanchoro:
“This woman Aichürök,
This quivering infidel,
This wayward-minded woman—
Her silk dress cannot be torn,
And she shows no sign of making peace.
She is a woman of another kind,
With a form unlike other women;
She skims along the white sash,
And has flown and traveled
Across the whole wide world.
Your unlucky sister-in-law
Clearly bears the mark of a champion;
She is hostile to men
And shows another nature.
Fair of face but treacherous within—
