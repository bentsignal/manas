The wrestler with mighty arms,
Kazhby, who would strike the foe,
Broad-chested and stern-tempered,
The swaggering, proud-hearted one,
Had come without staying behind.
His warriors riding mules,
Who sped like beasts along the road,
His heroes accustomed to going on foot.
They dismounted on the open ground and shouted,
Calling out, asking where the enemy was;
His warriors had come,
When the Kalmyk sent word.
Of the Chinese clad in jackets,
Not one of the strongest stayed behind;
Of the Kalmyks clad in armor,
Not even their khans stayed behind.
They raised a tumult like a raiding host,
Struck their trumpets with a crash,
The drums boomed,
The brass pipes clattered,
They raised their dappled banners,
Asking, “Where is the foe we are to grapple?”
They raised a clamor for the khagan,
They were not few but many,
An army thronged through Beijing.
From the city of the khagan,
From the plains beyond it,
All those on this side stirred,
And around the gold-pillared pavilion
Forces of many kinds massed,
Not a few, but a multitude;
They had come crowding together,
Ready to serve the khan of the Kechil.
They had come prepared.
Some of them stood chafing,
The wrestlers were kept tethered;
If one should happen to break loose,
The man who tried to restrain him
Would have his shoulder torn out.
If he rushed in and struck with his fist,
Should the poor wretch be caught by it,
His life would leave him.
At the tower in Kendeme
They all came crowding together.
While his wrestlers stood like this,
He sent word and hurried them.
Algara, whom he rode to battle,
To tell how the battle had gone,
The accursed one made him prance,
Pacing him back and forth,
And hurried six thousand Chinese and countless kaldays,
Shouting aloud:
“Hear me, nobles; hear me, khan;
All my people here, listen!
He has girded his waist tight;
That orphaned slave who dwells in Beijing,
He seems to be a mighty hero,
And has pursued vengeance for his father Manas.
While we lay without posting scouts,
He unleashed havoc;
From the great racehorse Algara,
The offspring of that accursed one
He tore out the tail.
Algara has come back alive,
But he has become a sorry horse,
A stumpy black horse.
He has been disgraced.
Have you heard the news?
My black horse that I rode into combat
Had at his flanks
The wings of a mighty eagle.
The bloodthirsty man’s son tore out those wings.
When I look closely at Algara,
Forty calamities have struck him.
His dark haunches, which no whip could lash hard,
The haunches of Algara,
Have now been struck by a whip;
Unless he grows fat and recovers,
