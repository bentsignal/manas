Külchoro, upheld by the spirits,
Was tall and broad-shouldered,
Both champion and lion,
A seasoned hero who knew
The enemy’s ways exceedingly well.
He struck Kulansur once upon the flank,
Took up his fine steel spear,
And rode straight toward Sarybai.
Against brave Sarybai
He charged alone:
“Sarybai, son of the monster,
If you are brave Sarybai,
And I am brave Külchoro,
Let us face each other with leveled spears
And meet in single combat;
If death comes, let us die!
Let us stand face-to-face,
Fight without sparing our lives,
And bring this to its final end.
Let our spears begin their work;
Let turmoil seize the earth
Until it crumbles beneath us.
If I am the son of Almambet
And you the monster’s son,
Let me hear your vaunted fury!
Though born of a monster,
Even if you possessed seven thousand lives,
I would kill and bury you in the earth!
I shall spread slaughter to your farthest bounds,
Leaving not one of you,
But putting you all to the sword.
Let me hear of your courage!
Though we fight for months and years,
I shall kill and bury you in the earth!
I shall trample your encampment
And sweep away your champions.
Without sparing my dark life,
I shall pour out my fiercest wrath.
You infidel, son of the monster,
I shall tear apart your ribs!”
Toward the lone marauder of deadly venom,
Toward the giant Sarybai,
Toward death itself,
Toward the true devourer’s eye,
He pressed on to test himself.
Along the stony ridge,
The tireless lone wolf arrived
At the place where they would crush each other.
At the white ravine of the reedy slope,
They faced each other on the battlefield.
Cutting a road through the army,
Külchoro, upheld by the spirits,
Assailed Sarybai.
From this nearer side,
At the champions’ outer edge,
He tightened his well-fitted belt
And charged straight in.
Spearing all he encountered,
He sought everywhere
For Sarybai, the monster’s son.
Such was his lion-hearted valor:
Those within reach of his spear
He struck down with his spear;
Those within reach of his battle-axe
He hacked and felled;
Those who pressed hard upon his sword
He hewed down;
Those who came before him
He laid waste and struck aside.
The sky was clear, but the earth grew dark;
The ground churned as night descended.
The armies on either side
Mingled together.
They fought amid battle cries—
Who had ever seen such a struggle?
The fainthearted withdrew,
While every champion confronted the foe.
Spears shattered and straps fell,
Horses fell as though mountains collapsed;
A merciless battle raged.
