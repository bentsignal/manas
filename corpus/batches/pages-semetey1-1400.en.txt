He struck it toward the middle.
From the spur of Bozala-Borchuk,
From the gray, knobby promontory,
From the ridge of Kara-Kyr,
From the place where he stood watch,
The fox darted forty fathoms.
Kumayyk went after him;
The fox fled, shimmering like a mirage,
And Kumayyk followed,
Running close upon his heels.
Behind him Kumayyk
Drew near, almost at his tail.
Seeing it, Bakai and Semetey
Shouted out in joy;
Your poor uncle Bakai
Raised the battle cry, “Manas!”
When they reached the downslope,
That bastard Kuutülkü
Dug in both his feet;
When Kumayyk braced like a warrior,
He seemed ready to take flight,
Driving himself straight down.
At the white peak by Ailanysh Spring,
Kuutülkü’s stratagems ran out,
And he fled six circuits around it.
At Zheti-Bulak in Zherge-Tal,
Kuutülkü proved resourceful
And fled seven circuits around it.
Kumayyk ranged over the ridges,
Pressing Kuutülkü hard.
The fox kept on,
Watching for a gully where he could escape.
When he saw how the fox fled,
Fearing it would reach its lair at once,
Fearing it might somehow escape,
Bakai shouted in fury,
Raising the Bloodthirsty One’s battle cry.
He lashed Kökcholok beneath him
From side to side with his whip,
Spurred him on both flanks,
And shouted in fury.
Sacred Khan Bakai
Bent down the tops of the birches,
Startling the sly fox,
Making his horse pound along,
Stretching him out in pursuit;
Your uncle Lord Bakai
Sent him galloping with great bounds.
The ground crumbled under his tread;
Your accomplished uncle Bakai
Drove straight at the fox.
The place toward which the fox fled
Was a reedy ravine and gorge.
To slip inside it,
That accursed Kuutülkü
Strove with all his might
To mislead Kumayyk behind him
And get past.
Hurrying in alarm and panting,
Your uncle Bakai lamented:
“God surely exists, Kumayyk!
Why do you run with such stiff joints—
Has God struck you down?!
Unable to understand your fitful condition,
Now that I am old, Kumayyk,
Or has God struck me down?!
When my lord, the gray wolf, died,
I was the one who herded camels,
And you were the one left grieving,
Your ribs battered open!
If you do not heed the tears in my eyes,
If you do not bite and bring me the fox,
I will club your head open;
I, Bakai, will not remain alive,
But will strike myself down.
I, this wretched bastard, will die
And behold the face of Judgment Day!”
