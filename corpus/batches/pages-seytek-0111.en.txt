He bristled like a tiger,
Raged like a wild boar,
And charged like the wind.
Look at his swaggering lionhood,
Inherited from father and forefather;
See what a lion he was!
The spring flowing from the terrace,
The young hero born to surpass
His father, the gray-maned warrior—
His aunt took him by the hand
And led him toward Choro,
Who stood there lamenting.
She brought him in like a prince who ruled a people.
His ear matched his father’s ear,
As though cast from the same mold;
His nose matched his father’s nose,
As though fashioned in its likeness;
His very scalp matched his father’s scalp,
As though one had been fitted over the other.
Broad-mouthed and cliff-browed,
His upper lip jutted wide
Like a Kalmyk’s bag.
His lashes were flame, his eyes were embers;
No mortal ever held such wonder.
His beauty was singular, his gaze direct;
Among the multitude, his speech was forthright.
His brows were dense as thickets;
The formidable pale boy
Looked able to swallow alive anyone who grappled with him.
He was like a champion who ruled a nation;
God Most High fashions
His servants in every manner.
If he gathered himself and sprang,
He looked ready truly to conquer the foe.
His brow was high, his menace fierce;
He would flatten anyone who challenged him.
Seeing the boy’s valor,
Abandon hope of a peaceful end.
His stature and bearing were extraordinary;
From afar, he looked like a mountain.
He had been named Seytek,
And from his mother’s words he learned
That his people were the Kyrgyz,
That renowned Talas was their land,
And that the Kyrgyz who inhabited Talas
Had never lost it to a disputing foe,
Never yielded their companion to another,
And were a people unlike all others.
He heard that Üch-Koshoy, Talas, and broad Chatkal
Were their lands;
He heard that the dear folk dwelling there
Were his people.
When stirred, they hunted deer;
When they were not weary, they simply
Played freely at their ease.
He heard that those dear folk, the nation’s mainstay,
Were his people;
He heard that patterned carpets and ten kinds of cloth
Adorned their tethering lines.
He heard that Ala-Too, fairest of lands,
Was their mountain,
And that the fruit of this Ala-Too—
Aichürök, the woman taken as spoil—
Was his mother.
He heard the black giant’s account
That his people were Kyrgyz;
He heard the rough giant’s words
That he was fashioned differently.
The hero Manas was his grandfather,
And Semetey his own father.
He heard that in hilly Talas, in broad Keñ-Kol,
His village stood.
Never sated no matter how much he drank,
Never abandoning his ravenous desire,
When ridden through darkness
He prowled like a leopard;
Entered in a shooting contest, he brought good fortune;
Launched into battle, he equaled countless troops;
Once roused, he never missed his mark,
And none he struck survived.
