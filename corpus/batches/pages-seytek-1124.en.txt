Your wish has been fulfilled,
Your enemy has fallen into your hands,
My tiger Seytek, now ride forth,
Good fortune has attended your campaign.
Your vanished enemy has returned,
This alone was your desire,
What you watched for has been granted”—
So saying, Akjoltoy
Mounted the swift steed Külansur,
Knotting up its flowing tail,
Revealing himself as a true hero,
Hearing news of the infidel,
He laughed aloud in delight,
And donned his battle gear.
That noble hero, wolf of war,
Rejoiced at the thought of death,
Through a broad ravine where no hawk flew,
Through a broad ravine where no bird flew,
The warriors raised a ringing cry
From their brass spears,
Speaking these words in earnest.
Our tiger, forefather Jолтой,
Our elder took to the road,
Fiercely raging,
Wearing a helmet of blue steel,
Its crown crested with tassels,
Its cheek guards fitted tight,
Contrived in every way
Never to fall from his head,
Its fastenings crossed,
And knotted beneath his throat.
Upon your impetuous elder Joltoy
Was armor no bullet could pierce.
He made Külansur prance,
And rode along broad Talas,
His stirrups clattering,
While the hard black earth beneath him
Thundered without pause,
Like the surging current of a river.
Kutubiy’s Akjoltoy
Was Seytek’s inheritance,
Himself a keen-eyed falcon;
If calamity befell his noble head,
He would stand beneath the banner
Of the honored Kyrgyz people.
Akjoltoy rode shouting,
A wondrous figure to behold,
Alone worth an army of thousands.
As he departed broad Talas,
His bearing and grandeur
Seemed unlike anyone among the people,
His stature tall as a minaret;
He could not rest without meeting the foe.
Since this Akjoltoy arose,
The enemy had not yet felt his wrath,
Nor had anyone traversed and subdued
Talas with all its multitude.
No foe had yet advanced against it,
No mortal had yet called it disaster.
The enemy who coveted Talas,
A grim and woeful host,
Had not yet cast its watery lasso.
Since Akjoltoy and Seytek arose,
They had not yet entered battle together.
To crush the raiders from the Kalmyks
And break their marching ranks,
Joltoy set out, dark with wrath.
Traveling by night, not by day,
He left the Kyrgyz people behind;
His strength and valor,
When observed, were beyond compare.
He roared in wounded pride,
Raising the battle cry without fail,
Raging and surging,
While the beast flew like a whirlwind,
And he spoke to Külansur:
“Since I became Akjoltoy,
Since Akjoltoy first mounted a horse,
I have not ridden on a distant campaign,
Nor charged the enemy with a shout.
