I shall grapple with him and spill his blood.
If my lion father supports me,
I shall begin the holy war in earnest!
Against the grim Black Chinese,
I shall enter directly and scout the road;
For my godfather Almambet,
I shall at last take revenge.
My godfather Almambet—
All twelve parts of our bodies were alike;
Your gray wolf gone to the next world
Was a lion born as my true brother.
Against the teeming infidels,
When he unleashed utter turmoil,
That restless, unruly faith—
He was the lion who opened its gate;
Of the surging Black Chinese,
He was the hero who broke their host.
If I do not gird my waist for hardship,
If I do not cry aloud and seek
The blood-price of my blessed godfather,
If I do not bring slaughter upon the world,
From Chinese as numerous as ants
If I do not take Almambet's blood-price!”
When Semetey said this,
Bakai stood and answered:
“Ah, my foal, Semetey,
If you enter Beijing, there are many roads;
If we take plunder, that too is abundant.
If we slaughter them and take revenge,
The blood-price is enough to be remembered for an age.
There are many black, low hills and cliffs,
And many enemies to grapple.
To your left are the Chinese,
And many foes to fight.
To your right are the Russians;
Before you are firearms,
And many foes to oppose.
There are pits dug and waiting,
And roads upon which to grapple.
There are many costly passes to cross,
And many heroes lying in ambush.
There are many horses to ride on holy war;
My foal, my son Semetey,
If I keep speaking, there are many grievances.
If you set your face toward China,
My wise one who will bring slaughter,
If you campaign against the infidels,
What will you do, my son Semetey?
You have only now become blood-footed;
Your soul has not yet found rest,
And your blood has not reached full strength!
Without grieving your uncle Bakai,
Heed him this year.
Without grieving your uncle Bakai,
Go in the coming year!
Carefully scout the road
To Beijing beyond a saint's reach.
If you wage holy war against Kakan-Chyn,
The Chinese are numerous as black worms.
Against infidels like black worms,
You have chosen to grapple;
Against hero Khan Kongurbai,
He is no foe to rush upon thoughtlessly.
Sacred Great Beijing—
None who grapple it returns unharmed.
From radiance-pouring Beijing,
Of Beijing's forty gates,
I have seen one gate.
Closed by day, by day it stood open;
Its mouth opened toward the qibla.
At the mouth of that gate,
The Bismillah verse was written.
The followers of Muhammad—
When they came into Muslim hands,
Lest a devil strike them,
God's light was scattered there.
They have pride, but no faith;
That is the land pouring forth radiance.
I am that battered Bakai
Who devoured its great lords!
