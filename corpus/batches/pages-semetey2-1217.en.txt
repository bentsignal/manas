Many were already crossing beyond,
Dropping their spears to the ground,
Unable to stoop and pick them up,
Many were fleeing onward.
Their saddle-cruppers snapped,
And many a fleet steed
Had its saddle slip onto its neck;
Many a valiant warrior,
Thinking, “Now we are doomed to die,”
Was fleeing in terror.
In their frantic haste,
Many trampled one another.
Many a swift racing steed
Had its saddle slip beneath its belly
And wandered about unharnessed.
“Father! Alas, my dear brother
Died at such-and-such a place;
Where shall I ever see him again?”
Many wandered, wailing so.
Barefoot and bareheaded,
Unable to die though wishing for death,
Straining their wits for some escape,
Many hid their precious lives
In scattered places across the open steppe,
And fled away.
When Külchoro wrought slaughter,
There was no respite day or night;
As the carnage raged,
Many people fell sick with fever,
And many were scattered before me.
Unable to find their bearings,
Many panicked, helpless and distraught;
Despairing of their wretched lives,
Lamenting, “Now we are dead,”
The warrior began the massacre.
All the common folk of Jediger,
Suffering punishment in their very lives,
Abandoned all hope of survival.
The dreadful battle and turmoil
Were begun by the lone warrior;
The Jediger, one and all,
Found no refuge anywhere—
Such was the fury of their clash—
And found no place to hide.
The Jediger were seized by panic.
Their heroes Kyyaz and Kanchoro,
Raising a tumult together,
Fled, trampling over the people.
The terrified Jediger
Fled, trampling even those two.
Seeing the Jediger, Kanchoro’s
Hair bristled at the nape;
As though ready to slaughter them,
He twisted about, his body bristling,
And roared in fury.
He turned the great fleeing host back
And drove them together once more;
Rumbling and working himself up,
He addressed the Jediger:
“Damn you, Jediger!
You came saying you would fight,
But fled and disgraced yourselves!
After consulting two by two,
What do you say now?
Forgetting all your promises—
Damn you, Jediger—
You have proved yourselves heathens!
Panicking in fear of Külchoro—
Damn you, Jediger—
You have proved yourselves faithless!
Külchoro is one, while you are thousands;
You Jediger are no men,
But women, every one!
You vast host of Jediger,
You are no people, but heathens!
While in the Seven Valleys you blustered,
Posturing as though you would fight alone;
Now you reel about, Jediger—
