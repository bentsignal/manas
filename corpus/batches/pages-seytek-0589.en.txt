The army surged in a seething mass,
Let the fine muskets fire fiercely.
Those whose hour has come, whose days are done,
Let them swiftly meet their death,
That the Kyrgyz cause may prosper,
And Sarybai, not a hair left of him,
May vanish from this world.
May broad Talas, left by our fathers,
Remain an inheritance to their descendants,
And dwelling in the Ala-Too,
May the noble Kyrgyz be a nation.
May Sarybai, who has set his enmity upon us,
Perish and become black earth!
Let blood be shed and people die;
All you people who fight Sarybai,
This infidel son of a monster,
Make the tyrant bleed,
Lay all his people low,
To the delight of our bitter enemy,
He has ravaged those with royal camps,
This swine, the monster’s son,
This infidel who has brought down doom,
This cruel and calamitous warrior,
Has made it known to all the people,
Abandoning broad Talas for the Ala-Too,
He has laid waste to all the land,
Slain every leader,
And brought the dear people to ruin.
How can we stand and watch
One ravaging dog become khan?
How can we go on living?
If the raging Sarybai
Should suddenly seize you in his hands,
If you are caught in his iron net,
If you fall into his pit,
If you yourself are snared in disaster—
His heart is foul, his nature mean;
Few such creatures are ever born.
He will leave your locks shorn in mourning,
So, Kyrgyz who ride straight against him,
Show your keenest valor,
Against the imperious foe Sarybai,
Who rains down fire and stones;
Upon that accursed incendiary himself,
Bring death and calamity!
Break his despotic power;
You are sons of the sovereign Kyrgyz—
Extinguish him like a fire!
How can we submit to the tyranny
Of the infidel monster’s son?
How can we, undying yet unavenged, live in shame,
And let the ancestral Kyrgyz land
Become another’s spoil?
Whatever Sarybai may say,
He is fierce in spirit, a great calamity;
How can we submit to his rule?
If we make Sarybai khan,
Let him devastate and slaughter the people,
What inheritance shall we ever gain?
It seems this swine longs
To leave not a hair of the Kyrgyz—
Then let us perish in battle!
Rather than let Sarybai reign supreme,
Rather than let the swine master the Kyrgyz,
What good is it to stir and linger?
Let every Kyrgyz, not a hair remaining,
Perish in slaughter
At the oppressor’s hands.
Let every hillside turn to dust,
Let every hollow fill with blood,
Let every slope be veiled in dust,
Let armies gather with a roar!
Let the black earth tremble,
Let these people wade in blood.
Let war cries swell into a din;
Here is the place of combat—
Let the tumult split the sky!
Let the earth crack and part,
Let the doomed shed their blood,
Let dust fly up to heaven,
