Making Khan Kökötöy's funeral honors
Known to the people,
He had baggage loaded on gelded camels
And had some sixty women
All sing the lament.
Boys and old men drove the livestock,
Rushing in from every side.
At the head of the caravan was Külayym;
Sixty women lamenters
All wailed and wept.
Her trailing hem swept the ground,
And her solitary lament split heads.
They migrated, covering the land,
Tethering foals and yearlings together,
And tying mares as they moved.
All the camel calves roared;
All the foals whinnied.
When one looked at the festive caravan,
Kökötöy's migration
Was a spectacle to all who saw it.
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Departed without seeking counsel
From imposing Manas.
He passed Aulie-Ata and Talas
One after the other.
There was no mountain as small as an ankle bone,
No hollow where a saddle blanket could sink;
The whole caravan spread across the land
And entered the broad valley.
The earth grew dim and clouded;
Kökötöy's countless livestock
Became one with the face of the earth.
The grass growing upon the ground
Was erased beneath their hooves;
His wealth surpassed all men's,
And his tusked beasts fed their fill.
Livestock of every kind streamed along;
People of every kind streamed along.
At the head of the caravan, red camels,
Their throats like leather buckets,
Their tusks thrust outward,
Their lower lips drooping,
Striding ponderously,
Their manes covered in dust—
Many camels streamed along.
Their foals whinnying,
Their bellies sweeping the ground,
Many mares streamed along.
Skimming over the earth,
Roaming along the cool valley,
Their mighty horns thrust outward,
Many cattle streamed along.
At Kozu-Bashy and Kopo,
They fattened sheep there;
They crossed every lovely place
And passed along the mountain passes.
The caravan's qibla side
Went along Kegen,
And Bokmurun reached
The broad plain of Karkyra.
He enclosed it from end to end,
And Kökötöy's great royal pavilion
He brought to the plain of Karkyra,
Raised it, and set it in place.
Calling it Khan Kökötöy's feast,
Calling it the banks of Karkyra,
He covered the earth in dust
And made it renowned among the people.
He had all his wealth made ready;
Kökötöy's many young men
Prepared firewood without pause.
At Kara-Saz in Karkyra,
Becoming friends with Kalday,
He boiled salt and obtained it.
Along Great Karkyra,
His surging people now made camp.
For Khan Kökötöy's memorial feast,
Infidels and Muslims alike—
