His bearing is rare, his spirit serene,
A fire that burns unlike any mortal.
His lashes are flame, his eyes are embers,
Among men there is no hero like him.
The companion who was speaking
Was utterly bewildered,
When the falcon surpassing all men
Set his face toward Talas.
Your poor, fierce mother,
The swift mare that seemed to fly,
The finest among all women,
The noble-born Aichürök,
Stately and majestic,
Fair-faced, with flashing eyes,
With black eyes like twin morning stars,
Shed tears from those dark eyes,
Grieving truly, beating her breast,
And came pleading to Seytek,
The hero born in truth.
Clutching his bridle, she screamed,
That wretched, afflicted woman,
Answering with sobs and bitter weeping.
Tormented by sorrow,
She grasped the young man's bridle
And cried aloud in supplication:
“O river valley, my flowing spring,
My young hero found when death was near,
My banner borne upon the spear,
While I remained behind in Altai,
My keepsake left by a noble man!
My deep-rooted sacred plane tree,
Whose sight lifts the gloom from my brow,
My steadfast poplar and plane tree,
From the gray-maned hero gone to the next world,
You are the breath left to sustain me.
My very breath, I have words for you:
You are going to your land,
To Jylgyn, broad Keng-Kol, and spacious Talas.
You are going to your people,
The joyful Kyrgyz and the few Argyn.
Is there a raging Chinese enemy
That requires us both to go together?
Is there some dispute to argue,
Sweating brow and wearing out the tongue?
Alas, brother, what are you saying?
Who could listen to these words of yours?
Let only a dark night hear them,
Let the dug grave hear them,
Let the dark earth hiding all things hear them—
Who else could bear to hear?
‘I shall go alone to my land,
I myself shall go to my people,’”
So spoke brave Seytek,
While Külchoro, born of noble stock—
Külchoro remained behind and halted.
Now look as he wheeled about,
His eyes reddening into embers,
Poised like a striking serpent,
Preparing like a seasoned warrior,
Wisdom coming to him as if from the unseen,
He readied himself in wrath,
And girded on a sword without a sheath.
At the sight, every heart was stirred;
He seemed a towering blue mountain,
A dark ridge above surging waves,
A lion of surpassing majesty,
A hero perfect in his might.
He thundered, spreading dread,
And every mortal who beheld him
Felt his whole body shudder.
Such was Seytek’s state:
Waking in urgent haste,
The valiant man, like Surayyl,
Crouched like a gray tiger ready to spring.
He hurriedly performed his devotions,
Saddled his dark bay horse,
Prepared all his arms and gear,
And girded on his naked sword.
He was the image of his forefathers—
Behold his lionhearted courage!
