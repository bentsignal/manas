Settle your quarrel, Jediger?
Will you not slaughter
The enemy fallen into your hands, Jediger?
Will you not inflict
A singular, exacting calamity, Jediger?
Will you not avenge
The true grievance of the dead, Jediger?
Your chance has come into your hands—
Do you not see it, Jediger?
Will you not divide among yourselves
The bloodthirsty lord’s wives and children, Jediger?
Will you not give Kanchoro
Whomever he desires, Jediger?
Will you not submit to the decree
Spoken by your khan, Jediger?
Will you not find
Countless riches and abundant spoils, Jediger?
Will you not raid
Semetey Khan’s stronghold, Jediger?
Will you not beat out
The Kyrgyz madness, Jediger?
Will you not unleash
A dreadful affliction and great slaughter, Jediger?
And the fierce woman Aychürök—
Will you not take that proud woman as spoil,
Jediger?
Will you not boast throughout your lands,
Raise a clamor, and hold
A great feast for this, Jediger?
Will you not gather every nation
From Jeti-Özön, Jediger?
Will you not slaughter countless livestock
For the feast, Jediger?
Will you not cut her in two
And leave her for Kyyaz, Jediger?
Your khojas in white turbans,
Your mullahs who recite their lessons,
Your elders who can fly,
And all who know their crafts—
Will you not gather them, Jediger?
For your lord Kyyaz,
Cutting shares in this way,
Will you not carve them, Jediger?
Chürök, beautiful as the sun—
Grief and calamity follow behind her;
Such is Chürök’s nature.
With her white breasts exposed,
Her dawn pale as a mountain sheep,
Her eyes shining like a camel calf’s,
Will you not drive her
Among sixty wrestlers and eighty men,
Making a spectacle for all
That ears may hear and eyes may see,
And heap on Aychürök’s head
Affliction and strife no nation has witnessed,
Jediger?
Beautiful Aychürök is your captive;
That is Kanchoro’s counsel.
Brave Kyyaz, struck by a man’s curse,
If you wish, she is your wife;
If her widowhood is truly disputed,
She is in truth your own spouse,
The wife from your village in Jeti-Özön,
Won through contention in Talas,
Whom you carried off as your wife.
How is it, Kyyaz, that you sank
Into merry games and great feasts?
How is it, Kyyaz, that you lie there,
Regarding Chürök as a maiden?
How is it, Kyyaz, that you raised Kanchoro
And made him khan?
How is it, Kyyaz, that you feast
On merry games and entertainment?
Having won the stronghold and mounted the throne,
Swelling with great fortune,
How is it, Kyyaz, that you sank into it?
How is it, Kyyaz, that you lie
In broad pleasure and great amusement?
