Without drifting downstream, he swam straight on;
A creature wiser than a mere beast,
He swam strongly, neither hurrying nor faltering.
A creature wiser than a mere beast,
He took his course straight toward Churok,
The daughter of Akun.
At the place called Bugu-Kechuu,
Your steed did not turn aside
But drew close to the bank.
He curved his neck like a stag,
And without delay sprang from that bank,
Leaping up onto the high ground.
He emerged from the water, sleek and gleaming;
From the corners of the leather saddle cloth
And the blue felt pad, water squelched.
Ah, that creature, the horse Buurul,
Stepped forward and began to amble;
From the corners of the broad saddle cloth
Water poured and streamed.
Sleek as a hunting hound,
With golden shoes and silver nails,
The true courser Buurul's hooves
Flashed beneath him.
His wet hair lay divided into channels;
The water flowing from mane and tail
Fell away in separate streams.
Each vertebra and rib,
Each separate bone, stood visible.
The splendid, well-fleshed creature,
Whose calf was as thick as a bull's waist,
Whose stride was like the wind of the steppe,
When he drew near the maidens,
Moved sleekly at an amble,
Showing off his spirited ways.
The creature assumed every kind of bearing,
Tossing his forelock toward the sky,
Driving his hooves against the earth;
The black-steel bit
Rattled against his throat.
His tail, like willow silk,
Lashed against his spreading thighs.
Every strand of hair stood apart,
And to the people watching,
The marks of a true courser showed plainly.
Those who watched him thought,
"If only that were my horse";
Whoever saw him burned with longing.
Ah, that creature, the horse Buurul—
If you saw his forelock, so beautiful,
You would measure it against your eye and knot it there.
The rider mounted upon him
Was Kulchoro, upheld by holy spirits.
If seven hundred thousand black-clad troops came,
He could plunge among them alone.
