His forefathers were khans, every one,
Blessed by Kydyr from the beginning;
His earliest ancestors were all khans,
Blessed by Kydyr from the first.
Where he had spent the night,
An enduring shrine arose;
God had favored him from the beginning.
Where he had set foot,
A town with a market arose;
God had favored him from the first.
He pledged his hand to twelve spiritual guides,
Received a letter from the caliph,
And so was called an illustrious hero.
His forefather was Böyön Khan;
From Böyön Khan came Chayan Khan,
From Chayan Khan came Ogoy Khan.
How could Ogoy be an easy foe?
Anyone who faced him
Was wiped out.
He dwelt along the Somduk River;
Those who fought Ogoy were left crawling.
His fathers and forefathers
Were the offspring of lions,
Never sated by the blood they drank.
From Ogoy Khan came Balakan.
Balakan’s valor
Spread through many lands.
Anyone who defied him
He seized and beat.
No man could make him bow;
No mortal dared confront him.
From Balakan came Karakan,
Resolute, mighty, fierce in wrath;
He too laid low those who challenged him.
Born of the Kyrgyz people,
His might took men’s lives
And threw the Chinese into turmoil.
Resolute, mighty, fierce in wrath,
He too laid low those who challenged him.
While Karakan held sway,
Five palaces housed his guests.
Do not ask about that valiant man:
There was much for which he was renowned.
Thick of arm and hard of heart,
He bore the name Karakan,
A powerfully built, fearless warrior,
A hero surpassing other spearmen.
He too laid low those who challenged him.
Towering in majesty, fierce in wrath,
He too stirred strife throughout the world.
When that Karakan died,
When the true abode from which none returns
Was revealed to that man,
Of the Argyn and the Kyrgyz,
Shattering lattice walls and roof poles,
Molto Khan arose from among the Chinese,
Making their brides and daughters weep.
His strife afflicted the whole world;
One named Alöökö also arose.
They chose their champions,
Made their strong champions ready.
When Argyn and Kyrgyz together
Were camped along the river,
When Karakan passed from this world,
It was as though a fire had gone out.
No one remained to stand against them;
To utter a word of defiance,
The multitude had no strength left.
Molto Khan of the Chinese
Unleashed the havoc.
Those who defied him
He slaughtered, sparing none.
He levied excessive tribute;
Anyone who answered back
He drenched in crimson blood.
He spared not a single life.
When he brought his strength to bear,
The blood of the many Kyrgyz and Argyn
Overflowed like water.
Remembering Karakan,
The multitude shed tears.
The Black Kalmyks and the Manchu people
Vent their wrath and seize the craftswomen;
Taking them as plunder, the wretches
Seize the girls with five braids.
They fell every willow,
They pull down every house.
Now they have plundered everything,
A time like the end of the world
They have brought upon the people now.
He levied a tax on every hearth;
That swine, ablaze with fury,
Collected it all, leaving nothing.
