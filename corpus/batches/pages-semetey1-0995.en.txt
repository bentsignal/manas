To a distance branching far away,
To a trackless place where horses had not gone,
Upon the pass of Agynek,
He rode his pacing horse for diversion,
Wishing to see the city of Afghanistan.
The tiger lord surged with eagerness;
That poor lion, never stirred before,
Young Semetey yearned
To catch sight of Churok,
As though he had gone mad.
Growing restless late in the day, the gray wolf
Took the great spyglass left by his father Manas
To cast his gaze across the face of the earth,
Of Aychurok, daughter of Akun,
And to obtain news from there.
He climbed the pass with purpose,
Swaying like a noble maiden.
When he came out upon the pass,
Ask nothing of the hero's bearing.
Before him stood a black rock,
Surrounded by mingled barberry and honeysuckle,
A distant forked black rock.
The noble gray wolf turned about;
Your poor hero, born to surpass others,
Composed himself like a lion.
To tell every detail would be difficult:
Of the many possessions left as a legacy
By Manas, who had gone to the next world,
The most renowned among those objects
Was the great spyglass.
It was a broad spyglass with a hoe-wide mouth,
A great spyglass a full fathom long.
When extended, it lengthened by a fathom;
Its interior glowed red as fire.
It had a handle made of gold,
And your noble mother Kanykey
Had said, "Carry it with you on journeys,"
And made a slanting case worn like a body amulet.
Its interior darkened into clarity;
The tiger's great spyglass
Delighted anyone who looked through it.
Have you heard the tale?
When his khan-father, Khan Manas,
Long ago, the khoja of Kokon,
When he was holding him in humiliation,
And the afflicted ruler in Bukhara,
When he had overthrown him,
When no soul remained to answer him back
And he strode about unopposed,
When Khan Manas held dominion
Over the multitude,
Beyond Andijan
And this side of Namangan,
In the shelter of the Kokon khan,
Upon a yellow plain bright as the moon,
He seized it in a raid.
Its lens was cut from ruby stone
And fitted into its frame;
Its handle was overlaid with gold.
When a small part needed repair,
He granted Bolokbay
A thousand mares from the herd.
When its tube was turned four times,
Its lenses separated into place;
When one aimed and looked with care,
An object seven days distant
Appeared only seven rope-lengths away.
The tiger's great spyglass—
He turned its focusing wheel,
Set it to his keen eye,
Turned its adjusting screw,
And held it unwaveringly to his eye.
