To the open-hearted bloodthirsty man,
Saying, ‘My horse is feeble,’
Saying, ‘I myself am alone,’
Let us make a jest of it.
If the bloodthirsty man can be reasoned with,
Let us scatter the food and provisions,
Mount our horses,
And flee in every direction.
Why should we test ourselves
Against the teeming Chinese?
If Khan Manas brings them hell,
Let us wait and then depart.”
With elder Koshoy as their leader,
All the Muslims then
Voiced their grievance.
“If we fight during the feast,
It will be a disgrace upon us.
This is no time fit for combat,
No hour for waging war.”
They wept before Khan Manas
And cried out their grievance.
One bloodthirsty man said
That his horses were thin;
Another said
That he himself was alone.
Another spoke,
Saying his father was old.
Raging and shouting,
He began the great turmoil.
Khan Manas then
Slaughtered and left
All the assembled people.
“Curse your father's graves, wretched people;
Do not say, ‘My horse is thin,’
And eat shame on account of your horse.
Whoever says, ‘My horse is thin,’
I shall slaughter and eat his horse now.
Whoever says, ‘I myself am alone,’
I shall slaughter him now.
Akkula's prize—
How can I fail to take it?
How can I fail to bring
One slaughter upon China?
His anger rising, he shouted;
Some he confined,
Some he beat,
Some he cursed,
And some he ordered onward.
All the Muslims,
Unable to posture in resentment,
Said, “Is it not Manas
Who will slaughter every Muslim,
Who will do to us
Whatever he himself wishes,
Who will somehow reach
Your very root!”
The great black Muslim people
Wept and spoke from the heart.
As they stood exactly thus,
Your fortunate lion Manas,
Against the teeming infidel,
Against hero Khan Kongurbai,
Girded the jekene at his waist;
Against the infidels bending the earth,
Manas attacked alone.
Raging, he shouted,
Calling out to Kongurbai:
“Chinese people of forty khans,
You have come from your farthest bounds;
I have given you abundant food.
The host of ninety black infidels—
You came without breaking formation;
The countless slaughter animals,
I gave in a mountain-like heap.
I gave red gold and livestock;
I gave red-tailed camels.
Black Chinese and many Manchus—
Akkula's prize—
