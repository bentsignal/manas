And had them draped over the fourteen horses,
Each tethered separately.
All fourteen horses
She had strain beyond measure;
At the edge of the fourteen horses,
She had the towering Buurul
Toss like one whose master had died.
The coverings of the fourteen horses—
Hoof matched to hoof,
Made exactly like a tiger;
Ear matched to ear,
Made wholly like a tiger.
On this side of the horses,
"Let no person see," she said,
And had a splendid screen erected,
With a white curtain drawn.
The luckless daughter of Akun,
Displaying her distinction,
Presented surpassing games and revelry
To the gray-maned hero.
"My precious prince has come,
And God has granted my wish," she said.
Some sixty unbred mares
She had slaughtered for his food,
And upon gilded platters
Had carved meat set out.
Your dear sister-in-law Chürök
Had a curtain made from pure silk,
Held and stretched from its sides,
Raising her honor above the people
And satisfying the affection of all who saw.
From silk quilts of every kind,
Thirty young women and thirty maidens
Had luxurious bedding spread.
Thirty young women and thirty maidens
She had open their saddlebags,
And for the custom called zhengetay,
From pounds of golden embroidery thread,
She had gifts scattered to them all,
Winning their agreement with her wishes
And duly giving the customary gift.
If you look upon these games,
Aychürök, whom you have seen,
Now staged the revelry.
Her dear head beheld joy,
With maidens and young men mingled together.
Yesterday, your honored sister-in-law Aychürök,
Hearing words from those who had come,
Heard that, "If he sees kumis and a maiden,
The princely leopard and falcon
Will die of delight."
She prepared things in every fashion;
Of all that could be prepared,
Nothing at all was lacking with Chürök.
Eighty young women and eighty maidens,
Eighty young men and eighty lads
Sprang about and held their games.
They twisted their kerchiefs
Into clubs,
Women and men mingled,
Striking one another
As they stood like this.
Women and men mingled,
Taking turns with the kerchief-club,
Striking the one each loved,
And satisfying their desire in this way.
"These are Aychürök's games,
The wedding feast of Semetey," they said.
One woman and one man,
Embracing, met as lovers,
And kissed to ease their longing.
The men sang,
The women listened,
And played without tiring.
They went to those they loved,
Drank kumis and grew merry,
Their very bones growing warm;
