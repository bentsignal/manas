All our scouts have been exhausted,
And we stand driven from our course.
We are guarding the place where we shall die,
Standing as though about to depart.
At Kara-Buura in Chatkal,
Along the winding Kyrgyz road,
Grime rained down upon our heads,
And all four kinds of livestock scattered;
A fierce battle began.
…………………………….
He slaughtered everyone without exception.
He chased the deer until they became cattle;
He cut all of us down.
Would a prince’s descendant,
Whose heart feared combat,
The only son of a noble house,
Be thrown into confusion by a hero
And possess a spirit like mine?
My opponent is Sarybai;
I am riding out against the enemy,
And shall reach him and strike with my spear.
If I do not fight this slave,
May I sink unborn into oblivion;
May I die rather than live.
This swine seems to have run mad;
Let me test myself against him.
When Sarybai pours out his venom,
Has he no bullet aimed at his brow,
No ancestral bullet
Among the heroic Kyrgyz people?
Did we break through the turmoil and clear a road,
Attacking a people trapped without escape?
Did we leave him trembling in torment,
With his spear driven into the earth,
That he should wreak such slaughter
And create this battle?
Did we steal away his piebald mare,
Or scatter the white-fleeced wealth
Of that dishonored swine?
Did we drive off his gray mare,
As though scattering all his gathered possessions?
Calling ourselves his lord and khan,
Did we disgrace him in public
And scatter all he had amassed?
Did we ravage his outskirts and drive him away?
Did we taste the flesh
Of that maddened swine?
Did we seize all his possessions?
Did the swine’s suffering overwhelm him,
Or did we leave him looking behind?
Did Kuyaly break faith and become embroiled,
His thoughts disturbed and brooding,
Hiding himself as something precious?
Did I steal the plunder away,
Or seize his camel stallion?
Unless we destroyed the crops
Of his people spread across the land,
Unless we killed warriors and slaughtered nations
And became foolish bloodthirsty men,
Why did he inflict such tyranny and shame?
He made us cry out like a nation rich in pacers,
Quenching the fires we had lit
And sending souls fleeing in droves;
He seized and cruelly humiliated the innocent.
God sees his tyranny,
Yet he showed no regard for high station.
He wounded those bound by oath.
He brought evil upon my people,
Broke my back again and again,
And threw my nation into confusion.
We endured a dog’s humiliation,
The enormity of grievous disgrace.
Will this accursed swine never mend?
Can the world not sate him?
Will he keep this nation beneath a cloud of dust
Until he has slaughtered them all together?
Under Sarybai’s venom,
Under the calamity of his wrath,
Countless armies of the people died;
Everyone old enough to know reason died.
