“Make the roadside a battlefield,
And drive them against the Danggits!
Turn back the riverlands
Of that overbearing swine!
Wage war until death
Against the swine who attacked you!
Drive them, children, drive!” he cried,
“Overturn and destroy them,
And finish Sarybay!”
His brow was grim, his venom strong;
He leveled any who assailed him.
When his raging fury seized him,
The gray-maned hero was no easy foe.
He could spread slaughter through the world,
And when his wrath grew fierce,
He would leave not one Chinese soul,
But slaughter them outright.
Black-hearted and blue-gray without,
He was an unpredictable leopard.
At the sight, the heart was shaken:
The spirit of gray-maned Manas
Appeared as though alive.
The whole world stirred;
His silhouette seemed a boundless lake,
His eyes burned like embers,
His shout was fire,
His figure vast beyond measure.
Gray-maned Manas, the leopard,
Joined those three
As their companion, that magnanimous lion,
And himself pursued the foe.
“Drive them, children, drive!” he cried,
“Seize the monster’s son!
He will not heed my pleas—
Strike your spears into his waist!
Wage true war,
Cut him down and finish him!
Stand face-to-face,
Raise the battle cry of ‘Manas!’
And unleash a dreadful battle!
Drive them, children, drive!” he cried,
And noble Manas, the beloved lion,
Himself shouted encouragement.
They seemed an army beyond counting;
Your heroic father, brave Manas,
Joined the three tempestuous warriors
As their companion.
Behind them the beloved lion
Appeared in human form,
And the mind of anyone who looked closely
Reeled in astonishment.
From the edge of the northern quarter came one
Who spared no life in battle
And never drank his fill of blood;
Who broke through dead ends and opened roads,
And when countless enemies came,
Charged straight into their midst;
Who never recoiled from glowing embers
Nor flinched and fled from flame;
A master horseman, watchful on the road—
Syrgak, warrior of the magnanimous lion,
Who never drew back once he thrust,
Never lost the trail of the dejected,
And in darkness never mistook
Or missed a shadow’s track.
Broad-bodied and short in stature,
His whole frame was black stone;
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
A sturdy, well-built giant.
He raised his socketed spear
High above him,
And from hosts that closed in combat
He never stirred or retreated.
Whoever came within his reach,
He crushed into the earth like a sheep.
When black muskets cracked
And fierce matchlocks snapped,
He never started or drew back.
Before enemies pouring upon him,
He never retreated in fear.
