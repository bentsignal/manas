Give me Maaniker, Burut!
If you do not give me Maaniker,
If you do not heed what I say,
I will brook no excuses,
I will not eat the meat you have served!
The place I came from is Bakburchun;
I have heard that Kokotoy's Maaniker
Is a peerless racehorse.
I have my Manchu and Chinese people;
If you do not give me Maaniker,
Burut, I have a score to settle with you.
Clad in mail,
He is a steed for heroes to ride.
Wearing a wrist guard,
He is a steed for commanders to ride.
To load him with gold and silver,
To present him to Esen Khan,
Maaniker is a fitting racehorse.
To deck him in precious stones,
To ride him proudly,
Maaniker is a matchless racehorse.
He is a steed worthy
Of the Great Khan of Beijing.
Of the Kalmyks clad in jackets,
He is a steed for the strongest to ride.
Of the stone-bedecked Chinese,
He is a steed for the finest to ride.
This is Kokotoy's memorial feast,
The foremost of great gatherings.
If you do not give me Maaniker,
I have a judgment formed by testing,
And a snare I mean to set.
I will pay no heed to your feast;
Damn you, Burut slave,
I will not rest until I take Maaniker!
Now I will plunder your wealth,
And lay your people waste.
I will spare none of you,
Now I will beat the fury out of you!
On the road through Toorakty-Chylan,
At the hand of mountain-like Joloy,
You inflicted cruel torment,
Destroyed a thousand champions,
And took tea worth a thousand camels.
You committed such a deed;
This outrage of yours remains,
And you cast warriors down dead;
This tyranny of yours remains too.
Agree to these words, Burut,
Without quarreling or making strife;
For the offense committed before,
Give me Maaniker, Burut.
I have words to say;
Burut, I have a score to settle with you.
When, before the Great Khan,
I had received his command,
And loaded gold upon a thousand camels,
Because of you, Burut,
As I went to trade,
You stirred up strife on the steppe,
Slaughtered a thousand champions,
And seized as plunder
The riches borne by a thousand camels.
You committed a grave deed against the Khan;
By doing this too, Burut,
You have become guilty.
To unleash a great tumult,
I set out prepared from Beijing
To plunder you, Burut.
Know my readiness from this:
I have iron armor no bullet can pierce.
To plunder you,
I have an army of twelve tumens,
And a mighty man named Kongurbay.
You swaggering Burut,
I have an answer to exact from you.
If you bristle and strut,
I will seize you at once,
