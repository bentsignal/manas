In the blink of an eye,
Brave Toltoy did whatever he wished
To Afghanistan.
Poor Toltoy then
Began the turmoil,
And utterly slaughtered
Afghanistan's warriors.
He unleashed a fierce calamity;
Poor Toltoy then
Lay there taking lives.
When the people were being slaughtered,
Was he not born a lion?
Dear Choro arrived.
Against Toltoy once again
He unleashed the melee.
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Turned Akborchuk's head,
And at Toltoy of the Jediger
He now shouted and leveled his spear.
When he saw Külchoro,
Mountain-like hero Toltoy's
Ribs began to tremble,
And his entrails quivered.
The brute he had seen before—
Could Toltoy remain without fleeing,
Having barely escaped death?
Could Toltoy stand there calmly?
Külchoro like Gabriel,
Making the earth split and thunder,
Came at that moment,
Roaring toward Toltoy.
His eyes reddened like a wolf's,
His neck stretched unusually long;
There was no man like Külchoro.
When Toltoy looked back,
A gray tiger was coming behind him.
With his tail raised high,
He made for Khan Toltoy,
Shouting a mighty cry.
The blue invincible one, never sated with blood,
As though about to sweep him away,
Came up beneath him.
Before Toltoy's eyes
He began the melee.
From that invincible one's wrath,
Poor Toltoy then
Was nearly flung
From atop Surkoyon.
His heart began pounding,
Despairing of his precious life,
Toltoy burned and trembled.
When he saw Külchoro,
Toltoy looked and grieved;
Thinking he would die,
He despaired of his fly-like life.
As though about to leave this world,
He lost his wits.
Lion Toltoy then
Fled away from his people.
He fled like a woman—away he went,
Toltoy of the Jediger, their noble
lion.
Roaring in fury,
When he saw Khan Toltoy,
Lion-prince Külchoro
Shouted the battle cry “Manas!”
“Do not flee like a woman,
Toltoy!” he shouted.
Toltoy came crossing
A saddle-shaped ridge.
Glancing about in fear,
Toltoy came fleeing.
Without looking around,
With no care for his own life,
Külchoro pursued him.
Brave Toltoy's Surkoyon
Was no beast that could let him catch up,
