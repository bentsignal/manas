As Akshumkar did this,
Warriors wept and khans wept,
Everyone who saw him wept;
Stone-hearted lions wept,
Countless tireless heroes wept.
An uproar rose and the people wept—
All who wept,
Young and old,
Bad and good alike,
Wept, consumed by their own grief.
“Now that the bloodstained warrior is gone,
What age and what days
Will come upon us?” they wept.
The Greathearted One was gone from sight.
“How will the camps
Of the Argyn and Kyrgyz settle?
What will become of us?
How will our people make camp?”
The people's master was lost.
“What fate now awaits
The Argyn and Kyrgyz?”
The people wailed in tumult,
Their clamor split the sky.
While the heroic sultan lived,
The people cried and spoke of this,
Telling horrors clear to all.
While the warrior still lived,
We ate the barren mare's foal;
If the Chinese shouted from the ridge
And stirred a fierce battle,
We used to ask, “Where is the danger?”
There in Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
When the Kyrgyz lay in proud abundance,
Red haze brightened the land,
Fine mares with foals stood tethered,
And kumis flowed by day, spirits by night.
In those flourishing days,
In those days we wore sable caps
With paired trim, cocked jauntily.
When restless, we raided the foe;
While our hero still lived,
If Chinese armies met us head-on,
We drove them all the way to Beijing.
We hunted deer in the hills
And lived safe, untouched by sorrow.
Ours was a people whose daughters-in-law were lovely,
With broad headdresses and long hair ornaments.
While the bloodstained hero lived,
We asked, “How could disaster reach us?”
Our land was hilly Talas;
Now our rocky ridge has collapsed.
Parted from the warrior lion,
Our backs seem broken,
Our people seem to writhe in pain,
Our fortune seems bound fast,
And our hopes seem shattered.
There are argali on its hills,
Silver on its sunny slopes,
Wild asses on its pale plains,
Ibex on its terraces,
Snow upon its mountains,
Game within its forests.
In Jylgyndy, Keng-Kol, and Talas
All these delights abound.
Its springs boil up and overflow;
Nightingales sing there,
And hearing their songs,
Young men soften with longing.
Talas was a spacious land,
And these noble people here
Were the free Kyrgyz nation.
No enemy advancing could defeat them,
No host could prevail in combat.
These few Kyrgyz here
Thrived upon the finest land;
Before every doorway
A thousand mares were tethered.
We were a people living at liberty,
