My lion-hearted uncle, khan of the age—
Is he any less than your Kyyaz?!
Who would listen to these words of yours?
Let some dark night listen,
Let a deep-dug pit listen!
You kept praising him, blustering away;
Let the army of Chochko’s Jediger hear,
Let the pampered bird hear!
Let the barking dog hear—
Who else would listen?
You base-born Kanchoro,
You black devil of a warrior,
There is insolence in your words
And treachery in your eyes.
If you have the strength, you rabid cur,
You mean to bring me down;
You speak words that make one gnaw the reins,
Foretelling a time of true calamity,
And swear by the realm of spirits—
Has God struck you mad?
If I do not gird my waist firmly,
If I do not drive the Jediger of the Seven Valleys
Back to every corner of their lands,
If I do not spill their blood,
If I do not slaughter all their people,
Then may the spirit of noble Manas
Hunt me down and strike me!
If not, may Choro, who consorts with demons,
Strike you down!
Damn you, Kanchoro,
I have seen what these people do—
So this is what you intended.
You base-born, crooked slave,
So you meant to reach me at last!
You meant to forsake this gray wolf
Blessed by Kyzır,
And find some cunning way
To bring about my end.
You sent a letter to the Jediger,
Summoned Kyyaz to your side,
And meant to take him with you;
You meant to plunge my head
Into grievous hardship.
Your true champion Kyyaz,
With six swift horses
And a letter no bigger than a palm—
You meant to bring him here
And stir up a mighty tumult,
A great conflict.
I have seen many strange people—
So this was what you meant to do,
You accursed bastard!
By finding the chance,
You meant to bring about my end.
Making Kyyaz your holy protector,
You bastard swine,
You meant to abandon me.
From the first, there was deceit in your words,
And treachery shone in your eyes;
If you had the strength, you rabid cur,
You meant to bring me down.
There was crookedness in your speech,
And guile in your eyes;
Watching for your chance and plotting,
You rabid cur, you meant to harm me.
You shameless bastard,
You meant to bring down upon me
A black day, misery, and great strife;
You meant to seize
The bloodthirsty hero’s wives.
You meant to reveal yourself midway
And go over, you dog,
To Kyyaz, with whom we are at war.
You meant to bring conflict
Upon the hero.
