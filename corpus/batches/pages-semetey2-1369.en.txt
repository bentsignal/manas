He was as slippery as a snake,
His strength like an unbroken cliff.
This ill-starred, cunning warrior
Seemed ready to swallow anyone who grappled with him.
He made every preparation,
Strapping on his battle-axe,
With the chest of a hungry lion
And the gaze of a hunting eagle.
To look upon Kanchoro’s bearing,
He was like a predator that could fell a bear,
Like a crag that could devour a full-grown camel—
Such was Kanchoro’s wrath.
At the frenzy of that ill-starred wretch,
Many stood dumbfounded,
Marvelling at his cunning.
Kyyaz Khan, hero of the Jediger,
Saw he was commanding and lionhearted,
A man trained by a gray-maned hero
Who had witnessed many things.
This one was calamity incarnate,
A son who had learned from a warrior
And grown accustomed to his ways.
Such was Kanchoro,
The villain who came forth corrupted.
Such was your prince to behold;
When he spoke, his words flowed like a pacer.
Once he began speaking,
He was like a mullah delivering a lesson.
He met others like a tiger,
Broad-armed and stonehearted;
When you regarded Kanchoro,
He was a sturdy, well-built brute,
Who unleashed great havoc when he armed himself.
Who else ever lived like this?
Kanchoro, the maker of mischief,
Brought great terror upon a woman.
He killed his own elder kinsman,
Winning infamy among the people;
Imperious Kanchoro
Now stirred great strife against the widow.
The reason he discovered her secret was this:
Would he turn against his own
And shoot worse than an enemy?
Among all sons ever born,
Could any faithless betrayer,
Any outright, ill-starred villain
Equal Kanchoro, who betrayed the salt he had eaten?
Kanchoro burned with fury,
This scorched and blackened warrior,
Who murdered his own elder brother—
A villain surpassing all the world.
Its socket was stout and its point was sharp,
The gaping battle-axe;
Its broad blade was a handspan wide,
And wherever it swung, blood spread.
Its socket was as thick as a mace-head—
Such was the battle-axe.
It never recoiled from stone;
When swung tirelessly,
It never turned aside from iron.
Its edge was joined with steel,
And no one it struck was left unharmed;
So that none might depart in peace,
It had been tempered with human tears
And rejoiced when pursuing the foe.
When swung in the dark of night,
It glowed red like an ember;
Only when sated with blood did it grow still,
And if not fully glutted with blood,
It quivered like hardened sinew,
While fury streamed from its face.
Its haft was wrapped in gold;
Without even fighting, it terrified the enemy.
Indeed, the magnanimous gray-maned hero
Had it fashioned to suit himself.
While this weapon was being made,
No small portion of the bloodthirsty hero’s treasury
Was consumed.
