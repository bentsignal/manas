Saying, “They may overrun us.”
It was an army that had come to the feast.
“If they happen to take offense,
They will destroy and devour us;
If they scowl and grow angry,
Perhaps they will seize us and leave,
Perhaps they will plunder their way
To Beijing in Chynmachyn.
Perhaps these Kalmyks will do
Whatever they please.
If a quarrel happens by chance,
If they happen to leave in anger,
Perhaps they will slaughter us.
Perhaps they will lay low
Every hero who raises his head.
Perhaps they will bind the weak and feeble
And take them away
To the seething Kalmyks.”
When the Kalmyk vanguard arrived,
The great people ran in fear,
All despairing of life.
What could the wretched few people do,
Stamping the ground in agitation?
What recourse had those people
As they blamed Bokmurun?
Before much time had passed—
May God never show us such a sight!—
A din came that split the earth;
Seven standard-banners came;
An army spreading across the land came.
The spear points glittered;
The people's heads swayed.
At the sight, the Muslims'
Hearts throbbed without cease.
As though splitting the mountains, a multitude came;
An army like a surging sea came.
Mountain-like Manas was not there;
Tormented souls were stricken with anguish.
When they asked whose army it was,
It was Khan Zholoy's army.
Bokmurun's arrogance
Was the Muslim people's misfortune.
Khan Zholoy of the Kalmyks—
His appearance and grandeur,
His figure mounted on his horse,
Looked as vast as Ala-Too.
Khan Zholoy of the Kalmyks
Put on his gauntlet,
Raging like a boar.
If you were to see Zholoy,
He was the summit of a snowy mountain,
The prince of the Black Kalmyks.
When Khan Zholoy entered,
The mere handful of Muslims
Split apart and scattered,
All running without saying,
“Welcome, guest, welcome.”
Flying like arrows as they ran,
Kökötöy's wrestlers
Took Zholoy's horse;
The mountain-like giant Zholoy
Dismounted, swelling with pride.
Behind him his Kalmyks,
Massed like hairs on a cow,
Also dismounted together.
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun—
The torment he brought upon the people!
The great hardship that now arose
Was the punishment of the Kalmyks.
Saying, “Have you arrived, honored guest?”
Bokmurun walked forward
Before Great Zholoy.
Bokmurun had exactly thirty thousand
Wrestlers serving him;
For Zholoy of the Kalmyks,
They prepared the food he would eat.
The mere handful of Muslims wondered,
“Good Lord, what will he do?”
