Holds perils in many places.
When your uncle Bakai speaks,
He has words of countless kinds.
If we journey farther,
I have a place where I can leave
My dear boys with the baggage train!
When I count the days of old,
My insides burn and my liver is aflame;
What does this Bakai hold within him,
And what does he not!
When I tell the tale of my earlier days,
I seem to remember them.
When this wretched soul counts his companion,
I wither and wither away!
When I speak of my former days,
With my beard white as a swan,
At the time I leave on a journey,
I seem to boast!
There is the land of Beijing to strike,
And a marvelous people for us to fight!
If we leave the baggage train for six months,
There is a place mortals long to see!
There is a pass made secure,
There is a place mortals do not see;
It is the pass on which I have set my resolve.
If we travel onward from here,
Let me tell you the nature of the land:
Pass follows pass,
And low, rolling hills appear!
Many a rounded mountain appears;
If we travel along the mountain foot,
We come to the lands of Kök-Suu and Kushtai.
We shall pass beyond those two;
The twin ravines of Echki-Ölbös
Rise with their ridges in view,
And your mighty uncle Bakai's
Heart will then be torn!
When we climb that ridge,
Its corners appear dim,
And its peaks rise hazy!
Bulging beyond the reach of sight,
The mountain summit appears.
My dear foals,
My heart is torn here!
On a jutting mountain spur,
Upon a hanging promontory,
Are rugged black ridges—
Here is a wondrous tale!
There are Üch-Jyrgalang and Köngüz;
If you see this place,
It holds a whole, level world!
There are wild boars in its forest,
Red deer at its bends,
Argali upon its hills,
Foxes upon its plains,
Tigers upon its ridges.
Lofty mountains rise jagged;
Upon those heights dwell
Wild goats and ibex!
There are bears upon its slopes,
And how many wondrous creatures.
There is snow upon its mountains,
And rooks within its forests;
Every living creature is there!
What a wonder it is to behold:
Its birds hatch in the birches;
If you do not die, boys, you will see
How they lash the air in flight.
Its birds hatch in the poplars,
Driving the air with their wings!
The breeds of game are scattered there;
A brave man's heart opens,
And an eager youth rejoices.
When bright dawn breaks,
When light falls upon the earth,
Geese, cranes, and demoiselle cranes
Cry out loudly enough to split the ears!
There are swans among its reeds;
Ripe walnuts fill the ravines,
And its apples lie in dark heaps;
