My ancestral home is mighty Kakanchyn;
My anger has risen and I stand enraged.
Bloodthirsty warrior, I shall slaughter you—
How could I leave you alive?
Lest people say that mighty Semetey,
Whose support was like iron,
Had become mute;
Lest people say the bloodthirsty lion,
Once the just one, had become witless;
Lest, because he speaks no words and utters no speech,
They say that he has gone mad—
If I do not truly gouge out your eyes,
If I do not slaughter you, my true lord!
I endured hellish torment like this;
I endured so much grief for you—
Alas, my lord, if I do not slaughter you!
May the heavens strike me before I can cry “Ah,”
And may the spirit of my father Manas
Strike me squarely upon the crown.
May the sealed blue sky strike me,
May the broad-breasted earth strike me,
May the spark of the white matchlock strike me,
May the bullet of the white musket strike me.
My lord, I shall slaughter you
And put an end to you.
My anger rising as I stand enraged,
I shall take keen steel in hand;
My mighty elder brother, brave gray-maned lion,
I shall bring calamity upon you.
I have endured so much torment because of you.
If I do not slaughter you, my elder brother,
If I do not put an end to you!
I shall throw the people below into tumult
And delight every soul who hears:
‘My mighty elder brother has died,’
‘The dear man has gone
To the true realm from which none returns.’
Every army will gather in a throng,
And dust will soar into the sky.
‘My mighty elder brother has died,’ they will say;
If it falls upon my head, I shall see.
I shall spill my noble blood,
Valuing it no more than a fly;
In truth I shall sacrifice
My golden life, slight as a fly.
Though I implore you, you will not speak;
Though I speak again, my brother, you will not mend.
You twist and struggle to flee like a spirit,
Preparing your way of escape.
Broad, level Sary-Adyrmak,
The people’s autumn pasture beneath the hail—
I have no livestock left to drive there.
My own elder brother has become my enemy;
I have no life I would spare from death.
I sing my song in earnest,
And strengthen my powerful resolve;
Do not call forth my infidel wrath.
The reason I spare you not is true:
When I was about to die,
I was a slave in a sheepskin;
While I remained alive,
I was the son of a dogskin slave.
I am one who has tightly girded his waist;
I know well where
You and I were born together,
Sharing every affliction.
My dear mother Kanykei—
We kicked our way out, wounding her womb.
Great Manas, towering like a cliff—
We sprang from his mighty loins.
We entered one delight together,
Grew together and lay in blood,
And lay within a single womb—
When was I ever a son of Manas?
If called a slave, then a slave I am;
From this surging host of Kakan,
I was the son of your wretched elder brother.
My first father was Chylaba,
My ancestral city is Bakburchun,
My lineage is the Chinese of Chynmachyn.
