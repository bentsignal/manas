Headfirst into a pit.”
So saying, Karadöö
Turned the gray champion steed's head.
“The face of the elbow,
The vulnerable dark ribs,
The middle between them—
That is the place to spear.
Unless Küzhürmön is a mountain,
He cannot withstand the spear;
This is the moment he will tumble.”
With speed beyond reckoning,
He reached him in a flash;
Karadöö struck Küzhürmön
And drove past with a booming blow.
From the place he had struck,
Strips of fine cloth streamed out.
The people cried out in tumult
And rejoiced for Karadöö.
The boastful Küzhürmön,
Like a mountain falling,
Was hurled from his champion steed
And lay stretched along the earth.
He fell sprawling headlong
And drove into the arena floor.
When Küzhürmön came off his horse,
The people cried, “Karadöö has won!”
The crowd roared in uproar,
A tumult that split the sky.
Karadöö, son of the hero Kutu,
Made his wrestling strength known;
The people despaired for their lives,
Saying he was a formidable hero.
“A broad-trousered, wide-chested
Wrestler has come;
He is a wrestler who brings slaughter
Upon anyone who fights him,
A wrestler who brings torment
Upon anyone who faces him.
When his rib bones mature,
When the flesh of his calves fills out,
When the weakness of childhood departs
And he turns fully toward heroism,
When Karadöö becomes such a man,
He will maintain a court and become khan,
Become a man of standing,
A man forged of steel.
He will rule the people and sustain a nation—
Ah... he will become a true man and wrestler!”
He had staked and won the prize.
With your elder Kutman at their head,
They began dividing the countless treasure,
The myriad livestock,
And every one of the many prizes.
When they divided the winnings,
They took red-tailed camels
And, what a wonder, many kinds of livestock.
The most skillful found their share:
They took dark woolly camels;
The finest young champions
Took black-blazed mares.
Their hearts were at ease;
Those who won prizes became rich.
But the hero Karadöö,
Without letting a coin touch his person,
Did not tie even a single mare,
Calling it his booty, to his lead rope.
He took no goods from the spoils.
The sign of his divination came true;
Their hearts were at ease—
Karadöö had won the contest.
