My mountain-lion father, Khan Manas,
His bulletproof armor billowing—
If only that destroyer had stood watching!
Then let these Jediger here
Have shamed the suffering warrior,
Have inflicted this disgrace,
And then taken his life!
Wearing a blue-lynx cap,
Wielding a mottled blue shield,
When ten thousand foes appeared before him,
He gave them no longer than meat takes to cook.
His eyes saw into the distance;
He stood alone and unmatched in every contest.
The consummate sage Bakay,
Our forefather, mighty as a hero—
Our poor uncle Bakay
Was not watching from the ridge.
So the treacherous Kanchoro
Did not then commit his outrage,
Nor did he take my life.
He knew seventy winding tongues;
He traveled roads that led toward death.
He whetted his eloquence,
And never shrank or stayed behind in war.
He spoke before the people;
No enemy who faced him prospered,
And never was his word in vain.
A six-month ordeal
He foresaw from afar.
Descendant of Kök-Terek,
Eloquent spokesman of gray-maned Manas,
Achybay, son of the Argyn khan—
If only that cunning man had stood watching!
Then let the Jediger
Have inflicted this disgrace!
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
Stout and powerfully built,
A scabbardless sword at his waist,
Like a cloud returning with rain,
His wrath spread across his face.
He broke through seven lines and opened a road;
If seventy thousand foes came,
He charged alone into their midst.
Foremost among the sixty,
Fiercest of the falcons,
Best of the spearmen,
Best of the warriors,
Black steel was his blade—
Syrgak of my father Khan Manas.
At the sight of an enemy he readied himself,
And steeped his steel in blood.
Though countless thousands of foes came,
Even facing death, he never fled.
In the finest years of his strength,
He was Syrgak, warrior of Manas,
Agile as a sparrowhawk,
A star by which to steer in the heavens,
Companion to my father Almambet.
If only brave Syrgak had stood watching!
Then let black-hearted Kanchoro
Have inflicted this disgrace!
With a fierce cry and a shrill battle shout,
He never turned back from combat,
But faced the enemy head-on.
He dyed his spear dark with blood
And struck down the formidable.
Of noble, golden aspect,
The foremost champion of the Kyrgyz,
Chubak, son of Khan Akbaltay,
Was himself a lion.
He wore a blue, arrow-proof coat
And rode a dappled blue charger.
When ten thousand foes appeared before him,
He struck without blinking.
If only your gray-maned hero Chubak
Had stood openly watching!
Then let Kanchoro
Have inflicted this disgrace!
