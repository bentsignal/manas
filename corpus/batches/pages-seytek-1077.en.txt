A fortress wall encircled it.
In its center stood a golden throne,
With a garden of fortune and wealth,
And a keeper tending it.
There were trained warriors there,
And a thousand greater than those warriors,
There were men armed with heavy muskets,
And if an order came from Bermet,
There were marksmen who never missed.
There were those who answered the instant she spoke,
Born and bred for that purpose.
Gripping clubs as large as houses,
Giants guarded her gate.
Her champions stood ready,
And she had peoples of many kinds.
The ill-fated fairy maiden
Had fortified herself thus.
She had not stood idly by,
But gathered champions from among the fairies.
The reason she gathered champions
Was that if he were a hero and a lion,
One who could subdue the whole world,
If he were truly a gray-maned hero,
If his fortune were complete,
If his heart were broad as a lake,
If he were an unflinching lion,
Then all the champions
He would trample underfoot like charcoal.
But if he were a man without courage,
With a cowardly, feeble heart,
Then at the sight of the champions,
He would find nowhere to hide,
And no grave into which to crawl,
He would squander all his riches,
And flee back in panic.
If he were a lion of supreme courage,
He would stride on unconcerned,
Regarding not merely the champions, but the world itself
As no more than the hem of his robe,
And would march straight in.
The prudent, ill-fated Bermet
Thought deeply and devised a plan,
And arranged many wondrous trials.
From the city of the fairies
She gathered champions and brave men,
Heroes and lions,
And fortified herself securely;
She had not remained idle.
She thus tested the valor
Of Seytek, who would come to face her.
When he saw the champions,
He did not rein aside his steed,
Nor falter and halt,
But his eyes blazed like a lion’s,
And he fiercely ground
His broad, gate-like teeth.
He loaded his white musket,
And when he showed his wrath, he thundered,
Until the black earth, hairy-breasted,
Seemed shaken to its depths.
Six grooms brought up their horses,
And these six, led by Akjoltoy,
Rode freely at the very front.
When they saw these six,
The champions and guards,
Those who watched the approaches,
Despaired of their precious lives.
Those valiant lions upheld by the saints
Appeared before the simpletons’ eyes
Like angels of death.
The guards scattered in a frantic rush,
Bursting away in every direction,
Separating one by one instead of massing together.
Then it became clear that lion Seytek,
The gray-maned hero, was truly a lion.
The champion guards at the gate
Each seemed worth a thousand, they said,
Yet they were left utterly bewildered.
All the champions crowded together,
Clasping their hands to their breasts,
