Carried Aichürök’s countless possessions;
They loaded all her many treasures.
Red and green banners flew;
Such was the spectacle to behold.
A clamor split the sky,
And the wrestlers shouted;
The Jediger rejoiced,
Raising the battle cry “Kyyaz!”
Seeing the abundance of wealth,
The great riches left by the gray-maned hero,
The Jediger bustled about
And divided it by whip-lengths.
Taking much gold and many possessions,
They shared it out in celebration;
They took gold and ornaments by the pud,
And carried away great wealth.
“Kyyaz has taken Chürök,” they said;
The people clamored, and his prestige rose.
The Jediger, glutted with riches,
Could scarcely open their eyes.
Then warrior Kyyaz
Reached the broad expanse of Jeti-Özön,
At the wooded slope of Jerge-Talduu,
And pitched his royal camp.
At the spur where the mountain divides,
Where Toltoi kept watch,
Upon a rounded ridge,
Across the broken, rolling hills,
On the far-facing slope,
Beside the hillock
With forty birches and twin poplars,
He reached the place and pitched his royal camp.
Kyyaz, warrior of the Jediger,
Brought Aichürök’s white yurt,
Its crown-ring golden and its ropes of brass;
Thus it came into warrior Kyyaz’s hands.
He became a man of imposing rank;
Warrior Kyyaz struck fear into hearts
And became khan over all the people.
In warrior Kyyaz’s hands
Lay immeasurable wealth and myriad goods;
He became their boundless owner,
A giant who ruled the people,
A khan who maintained a royal court,
A man of exceptional power—
Such was warrior Kyyaz.
He became lord of the people he ruled;
Such was warrior Kyyaz’s station.
Dear hero Kyyaz—
Dust rose wherever he rode,
And souls fled before his might.
He became a renowned man;
Among the Jediger of Jeti-Özön,
Accomplished Kyyaz became khan.
“In Talas I, Kyyaz of the Argyn,
Utterly destroyed the people,” he said;
“I slaughtered them without leaving one.
Only Kanchoro, my sworn brother for the life to come,
Did I leave alive.
I stripped the country bare with plunder;
Not merely the bloodthirsty hero’s livestock—
I seized his wife as well.
In fertile Keng-Köl and broad Talas
I strangled every settled man.
I avenged the blood-price not of one brother but seven,
Pursuing their vengeance to the full;
Before Allah I have no regret.
I brought death upon his people;
Not merely his ordinary livestock—
I seized the bloodthirsty hero’s wife.”
Among the Jediger of Jeti-Özön,
“Let all my settled people come,” he said.
“Let them see my wedding feast,
And what I intend for my people;
Let all who wish behold it.
Let them call me a hero unequaled in the world;
Let my people witness my valor.”
