At the mouth of the gate, Kanchoro
Struck her in the lung
With his spear and flung her aside.
Her battle cry split the stones
As she bore down upon Kanchoro,
Just as she was about to reach the warrior
And destroy him utterly.
But Kanchoro was well prepared:
He had fitted his weapon with black horsehair,
Sighting and balancing it against the foe,
Had forged grooves into its iron,
And honed it like a wolf’s tongue.
Lest it slip from the hand in haste,
He had wound it with yellow brass;
When struck by a switch it rang aloud,
Singing like a war drum.
From an irgay shrub at the foot of Opol Mountain,
Torn out by the roots
And twisted by wrestlers,
He had made its sturdy shaft.
Where the grooves lay open,
He had wrapped it with sinew,
And to ensure a firmer grip
Had soaked it through with glue.
He had covered it in camel-hide
And fitted the shaft for war.
Such was Khan Kanchoro’s spear—
So strong and cunningly fashioned.
The seasoned slave, that wolf of war,
Had entered many battles before;
In the days of the gray-maned leopard
The hardened slave had seen much.
He was skilled in the handling of spears,
And in every circumstance
The sturdy slave found the surest stratagem.
Holding the iron-and-brass spear
Short beneath its blade,
Aiming this side of the ribs,
At the edge of lung and liver,
The raging swine struck.
When the practiced swine struck,
The gray horse crumpled and fell,
And poor Akbala plunged straight down.
“She is truly my enemy,”
Thought Khan Kanchoro,
And an incurable hatred lodged in his heart.
His dagger-bearing wrestlers
Closed fiercely around
That woman Akbala.
Before the white dagger in her hand
Could rise and stab,
Before she could settle the uproar,
Before she could slaughter five or six,
The ready Kanchoro
Found yet another way:
Taking his golden-bladed battle-axe,
The practiced Kanchoro
Struck from Akbala’s hand
The white dagger.
Deprived of her white dagger,
Akbala fell into the snare.
Her ribs felt torn apart,
Tears poured from her eyes,
Her nose ran like a spring,
And all around the young woman
A clamor rose to split the sky—
Akbala had been captured.
Into a misery no struggling could escape,
Akbala had fallen.
Then Akbala,
At the gateway before Kanchoro,
Spoke these words to him,
Words of dreadful grief to those who understood:
“You warrior-slave who slaughtered your lord,
You vile slave who sets himself up as a prince!
What has come over you, infidel slave,
That you would massacre us all without heirs—
Helpless women and little children—
