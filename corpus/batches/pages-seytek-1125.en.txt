They fed you rice as grain,
Saying, ‘Let him ride when he longs for holy war,’
And you came to me from the spirit realm.
You have never spread your flanks in a ravine,
Nor traveled a hard and distant road,
Nor learned how far it may be.
Ah, noble beast Külansur,
You are my comrade, sharing death;
You will lower your head to the earth
And boldly lengthen your stride.
A whip is the rider’s one support,
But when disaster befalls a precious life,
A hero’s purpose is something else.
Now I descend into the open steppe;
May my livestock be sacrificed for my life.
They have raided the borders of my people,
So I have mounted you in mastery, Külansur,
Saying, ‘My true beast is my comrade’;
I have mounted you in ardor, Külansur.”
So saying, Akjoltoy
Began to shout;
Rage seized his wretched soul,
And for pride and honor
He staked his life upon a hair.
Chonbilgich, who had come from afar,
Relentlessly began his oppression.
To behold Chonbilgich,
To fight without tiring,
To contend with him in open combat—
It was already too late to hesitate.
Against that warrior of perfect strength,
That infidel raider Bilgich,
Who stole horses and ruined nations,
He would unleash his deadly wrath;
If he found a way, he would
Win the horses back,
But if Akjoltoy could not,
He would take the road of no return,
Never again to live in health,
But simply surrender his life;
Though they were thousands, he would attack
And test his weight against them.
He traveled along the hills,
His anger hot enough to burn stone,
His surging force enough to topple mountains,
Straining onward without pause.
Alone from all his host, Akjoltoy
Quarreled with Külansur.
Here was the place for the whip,
The black-edged buldursun.
Its grip was smooth as glass,
Its wrist loop richly tasseled;
The hide of a sturdy three-year-old bull
Was stretched along its core.
The hide of a yellow-hoofed young buck
Was tightly braided around it.
A gray wolf’s sinew was set in its tip,
The braider had tightened and finished it,
Designing it for a foe in combat.
The black-edged buldursun,
When it struck, deafened the ears.
He seized it and swung,
Then drew up Külansur, who had never felt a whip,
And lashed him behind the thigh.
At that moment, when the sharp whip
Struck the racehorse,
Its flesh parted like a sheep’s,
Its back straightened like a lamb’s,
And it opened its mouth with a roar;
Behind it, a great cloud of dust
Spread out like a house along the road.
It flew forward roaring,
While the black earth rolled over
And shimmered behind it.
They drew near to Bilgich,
Whose multitude made the earth sag.
When he saw Akjoltoy’s tumult,
Bilgich roared, pouring out his wrath,
