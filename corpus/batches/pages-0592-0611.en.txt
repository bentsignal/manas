Your spirit lofty, your name weighty,
You are the lion I honor!
You are my mighty one, my khan,
The youngest of the six lions,
Allah's beloved mortal,
Generous Manas, you are my soul!
You are my one whose fortune is complete,
The youngest of the six lions,
Allah's beloved mortal,
You are my hero without equal.
Many years before this,
I discerned and knew you would come.
‘The dark, blue-maned leopard
Will come,’ I kept saying.
To meet you upon your road,
Created by Allah Most High,
To give you what was entrusted for you,
Receiving word from Allah,
‘His name is Manas, the lion,’
I burned with longing in so many places.
‘The lion has come at last,’
I went about yearning for you.
I searched the world;
Lion, for your sake,
I went to many places.
The matter is difficult to tell.
If God ordains it as a blessing,
I will entrust one thing to you.
Holding hands and seeing face to face,
I stand here stirred with excitement.
Whom my Allah created precious,
My precious one, with you yourself
I stand here to meet.
Longing to see your face,
I came to behold it.
The six blades beloved of God
Came down from heaven expressly.
Lion, to you yourself
I came to give them.
Those who know this will speak of it.
When you put them in their sheaths, the six blades
Are your companions close to your life;
When you draw them from their sheaths, the six blades
Are your tigers that split what you strike.
When you draw them deliberately,
Will men come to face you?
If you swing them in fury,
Will any enemy remain unsplit?
When you enter a campaign, they are your guide.
When you enter combat, the six blades
Are companions stronger than a countless army.
If you draw the sword to face them,
Will any man approach?
If you brace yourself and swing,
Will any man remain unsplit?
Allah gave the six blades,
All are steel, all are sharp.
Their precious sharpness is proven.
If you face and attack an enemy,
If you cut a way through dense woodland,
The six blades are exceptional helpers.
Nothing like this comes from the unseen.
Anywhere ears have heard or eyes have seen,
For a round-headed mortal,
Descending from heaven to meet him,
Nothing like this was ever found.
Do not think my words idle.
Heed my words, my child.
Meeting those who desire him,
I am the elder from the unseen himself.
Those touched by my hem prospered;
I am the very presence of your Kydyr.
If you campaign and fight,
These blades are the companions you need.
For a round-headed mortal,
My child Manas, consider:
This is a treasure that cannot be found.
When your proud, precious back
Bends, draw them forth.
When your wretched, fly-like life
Is hard pressed, draw them forth.
If you swing to right and left,
Dark blood flows like a spring.
If you shout and draw them from the sheath,
Unless they feast their fill on blood,
They will never go back into the sheath.
If eagerly you draw them forth,
They never remain idle without drinking blood.
The six blades that fell from heaven,
The sword found from the unseen—
Such a thing does not happen among people.
Among the six blades,
The well-fitted sword in its sheath,
The best of the six blades,
The sharpest of them all,
God specially ordained

The one named Zulpukor.

Came this inscribed writing.
Look, Manas, my colt,
(The many people of the dark faith)

The high pass of Kara-Buura,

He crossed back into Talas.
Dear Manas, becoming a lion,
Now arrived in high excitement.
The dear people, worn and scattered,
Now gathered together.
At Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
Along broad Talas,

Such had become their days.
Taking Manas as their support,
They made Talas their homeland.
When bored, they hunted roe deer;
When not bored, the Kyrgyz
Played freely at their ease.

They laid it out on the yellow plain.
Fattened lambs, yearlings from barren mares,
They boiled until tender.
Wearing double marten-fur caps,
They lay swaying at ease, they say.
Gathering close, the dear people
Lived in joy, they say...

Making the dun horse prance,
Destructive Manas then
Called out for his kin.

I scout on all four sides,
Unless I raid the people who boast of giant strength,
Unless I find my kin,

Let me sink away unborn!
Traversing the whole earth,
All the wide world,

My close paternal kin.

Among my sleek herds,

My kin who were humiliated,

Akkula, the horse for scouting—
Unless I ride without halting,
Those kinsmen who were scattered,

Unless I turn and find my kin,

Let me sink away unborn!

Let me wage war on their nation.
Those kinsmen who went wandering,
If I live, let me find one!

How can I remain here uselessly?

How can I keep it all alone!
Akkula, swift horse from the unseen,
Unless I turn his head straight into the fray,
For those kin who have perished,
Unless I bring slaughter on the world!
Clashing with those I encounter,
Exchanging shots with those I meet,
The tulpar whose burden is precious,
Unless I turn his head straight onward,
Of my kin who have perished,

Unless I pursue their blood-price to the end,
Against the many people who hold themselves high,
Unless I fire and wage war,
My helpless, wretched kin,
Unless I seek and find them!
I was born while we lived in Altay.
Growing to maturity, becoming a hero,
I became equal to sons of heroes.
When I remember, my heart turns to embers.
I was wholly deprived of kin.
My size is half a mountain;
‘This Manas was left without kin;
Let him sink away unborn!’—let none say that.
The sustaining Creator
Had inscribed it on our brows,
Scattering us all.
For those kinsmen driven away,
Unless I turn my neck toward the Truth,
My people who have wandered,
Unless I gather all, leaving none,
Those who humiliated my people,
Unless I slaughter all, leaving none,
Let me die unworthy of Manas's name;
Let me sink away unborn!”
The blue-maned one raged without stopping.
His eyes blazed like fire.
The great lurching terror
Was seized by his thirst for blood.
When he braced himself, strength surged.
If you saw the warrior's face,
Hair sprouted enough for five felt stockings.
Distressed, the dear lion
Now stood in fury,
Now hardened his resolve.
“Mighty Allah, grant a way!”
This was what he now asked.
Rousing himself like a lion,
His sight shook the heart.
If you saw the blue-maned one then,
His blue-maned nature showed.
Preparing himself like a goshawk,
The exceptional blood-drinking leopard,
Wisdom coming from the unseen,
Unleashing his fury, burning within,
The falcon, blue-maned one prepared:

Unless I charge and raid his people,
My kin who were separated,
Unless I seek and find the living,
Unless I turn my neck toward death,
My kin who have gone into the depths,
Unless I pursue them all, leaving none!

They plundered the khan's possessions.
Destroying so many,
They slaughtered his champions.
To the four corners of the world,
Peoples ruled by princes took booty

Many, living in captivity,

My kin who bore themselves as warriors,
They stormed in and bound them.
Their champions who claimed heroism,

Ruining my people,
They took as booty,

Unless I free my land,

Thrusting spears, breaking the ranks,

Ah, blue-maned lion Manas—

Nothing like it happens in an age!

Behaved differently from all men.

Wrath spreading over his face,

Your blue-maned Manas rode now.

Seemed the choicest of lands.

A breeze came caressing the face.
Ah, dear one, this land
Was a place a mortal would long for.

Wild asses raced at play.

Springs flowed and overflowed.

Held gray hares and foxes.

Was the treasure of all the earth.

Oluya-Ata.

The blue-maned leopard grew excited.
The blue-maned one, the outstanding hero,
To the yellow, uneven hills,
To Zhangyryk's Zhar-Kyya,

His blue-maned courage shows.

He is a hero surpassing mortals.

Could he be a man who would not join me!”

Is he one of the Muslims?

His prowess is immense.

No such son arises in the world.
His figure and his bearing,
The way he sits his horse—
He has a tulpar beneath him;
A mighty fellow, a skilled spearman.
He is keen, he is a lion;
He is an exceedingly brave hero.
Choosing a tulpar beneath him,
Perhaps he is one of the heroes who ride,
Traversing the whole earth,
Roaming all the world,
Finding no mountain to crash against,
Finding no enemy to fight,
Perhaps he is one of the heroes who roam.
Taking the road on a six-month journey,
Never growing weary beneath me,
If only he could become my swift steed!
This person who has come before me,
To generous Lord Manas,
A friend into the next world—
If only he could become my kinsman!
As a kinsman, by my side,
Will he stand close and true?
If by chance he is an enemy,
Will he shout and thrust his spear?
Jousting midway between us,
Will God utterly strike us down?
