The people cried out, lending courage,
While the warriors stood in this plight.
The born hero Seytek
Spoke softly, yet with firm resolve,
Addressing the gathered multitude,
And beginning his speech to the people:
“Sarybay, son of the monster,
Has enslaved the falcons,
Has enslaved noble heroes.
He drove off all he encountered,
He began a savage conflict,
Subjugating everyone he caught,
And slaughtering them without exception.
His fame has traveled far;
Do not ask what that madman is like—
His outrages are many.
He grappled, proclaiming himself a man;
For many days they fought for victory.
None who attacked could bring him down,
None could wrestle the dog to defeat.
His cunning is formidable, his venom strong;
When I recall it, the swine seems invincible.
For seven days I fought without rest;
Wounded, my strength has left me,
And I can find no peace.
My heart burns with care,
My people, I stand before you wounded.
All the warriors have grown distraught;
The lion-hearted Külchoro
Was defeated by Sarybay.
Every ear strained in alarm,
And the ordeal seemed endless.
Sarybay, whom I opposed,
Shows himself a bristling savage.
His prowess is beyond measure,
His warrior’s strength beyond reckoning,
And such is his merciless wrath.
Sarybay has wrought a fearful marvel;
Do not despise my words—
This was a desperate deed,
And it has reduced my speech to pleading.
He bent my back low;
Against that infidel Sarybay,
I fought, but lacked the power;
I wrestled, but lacked the strength.
After grappling, no might remained in me;
If I die, I shall have no regret.
My youth has shown itself;
This poor soul despaired of life.
I had drunk my fill of liquor,
And had little sense within me.
Now I am burdened with worry;
I had been drunk on liquor.
The child grieved, his joints going slack,
The poor wretch despairing of life.
The infidel son of the monster
Revealed his savage ferocity;
Like one who had heard his fearsome name,
I despaired of my entrusted life.
Against the grunting boar,
Standing on opposing sides,
For nine full days,
We fought with sodden blows.
Through all nine days,
We wounded every fleshy part.
My ribs were shattered flat,
My breast filled with darkening blood.
This bright world is deeply cherished;
While alive, one cannot help loving life.
‘If only each of us survives’—
Such a longing fills these words.
I have not yet reached my destined prime;
The milk of my mother Chürök’s breast
Has not yet left my palate.
My boundless strength has not yet matured;
My time for wrestling has not yet come.
I am truly still an unripe youth,
A child newly turned fifteen.
If you provoke conflict without fighting,
I, an untried child, will fall into endless woe.
