You will now see, Ümötöy,
The true abode from which none return!
You will truly learn your lesson;
If I gather my strength, you will die!
If I regard you as kin,
Wretched Ümötöy,
You will seem no different from those Chinese.
If you are a champion, if you are a lion,
If, finding no mountain to assault,
You think I am someone easily dealt with
And stand there girded for battle,
Then tighten your belt securely.
Seek from the Chinese the blood-price
For your father, heroic Köksö, whom they slew!
Zhylgynduu, Keng-Kol, and broad Talas
Are not the lands of Bakburchun;
The few Argyn and Kyrgyz people
Are not the forty tribes of China.
You shout and level your spear—
This conduct of yours is no innocent thing!
If I rise in wrath,
You will die now, cousin!
Your guilt is on your own head;
Now your deeds will fall upon you!
You will learn what strife is
If you still refuse to listen:
You will wear the very robe of the Chinese.
Beneath the battle standard, your blood
And your lives will spill forth now!
If I join battle fiercely,
Your people will lie slaughtered;
You men who boast of prowess
Will be consumed by grief and care.
Ümötöy, who began this war,
Do not say your teeming people have submitted!”
So saying, Külchoro
Braced himself in anger
And settled firmly in the hollow.
When the mighty warrior grew wrathful,
His fury spread across his face.
Terrifying everyone in sight,
He urged forward his gray horse,
Which flew like a cloud.
He donned his blue mail;
To behold that mighty man,
He seemed ready for any deed,
Preparing himself at an angle.
“If I happen to strike back,
If I unexpectedly join battle,
Will disaster then befall
These wretched Kazakhs?
Will my dear lord Semetey
Shame me, saying, ‘You slaughtered them needlessly’?”
So he wondered.
Thus did Külchoro,
That peerless man, consider what lay ahead,
A man unlike all other men;
So Külchoro stood there that day.
Thinking many thoughts beforehand,
He called out sternly to Ümötöy.
But Ümötöy would not heed restraint,
And there was no reforming him.
“Level your spears with a shout
At this wretched slave of mine!
Though he possess eighty lives,
Strike this accursed warrior
From every side!
You came to fight and now stand idle—
Damn you, people of the Kazakhs,
Such conduct brings you shame.
Aim at the head above
His tightly belted waist and cut it off!
This lion, who trusts
In bloodthirsty Manas’s son,
Who enters battle when commanded
And drives every foe before him—
Cut off the head
Of this rabid warrior!
