Kept fighting in the turmoil,
Refusing to surrender her hapless head as booty,
And battled in that accursed place.
When the Creator decrees a grievous fate,
There is no choice but to bear it.
O plundered and shattered one,
Such is this worthless world!
There was no dark ridge to shelter her;
She struggled and grappled,
With the accursed men seeking booty,
Wrestling those dishonorable wretches.
As the struggle raged,
She had no refuge left in Keng-Kol,
Ah, no proud Kyrgyz remained;
Ah, she had no refuge left in Talas,
Ah, no fortunate Kyrgyz remained!
Your heroic sister-in-law Aichürök
Lost her senses in grief,
And, sobbing in misery, was parted
From both her husband and her people.
When the Jediger, covering the earth,
Swarmed around her in frenzy,
And made your sister-in-law their spoil,
She stood alone against that multitude.
Khan Kyyaz, afraid of the woman,
In the fury of combat,
Seized the hem
Of the foolish bloodstained choro.
Your sister-in-law unleashed a mighty battle,
And many warriors came between them;
She sliced away
One skirt of the wastrel’s robe.
“Let the wrestlers seize her!”
“The shameless shrew who turned the people
Against us has devoured us!”
“A wounded boar will never recover!”
So Kanchoro went about shouting:
“She will not leave you alive!”
The valiant Kanchoro
Rallied the warriors and made ready;
From her flank, brave Kyyaz
Stood raging and poised to spring.
Your sister-in-law stood alone against that multitude—
What could the poor woman do?
How could she prevail against them?
Will seven wolves leave
Even the carcass of a single sheep?
When the Jediger swarm upon a woman
And beat her, will they leave even her remains?
They plundered her camp and disgraced her;
They brought your prince, brave Semetey,
To a wretched end,
Before your sister-in-law, a woman all alone.
She charged in fury
And was reaching for Zulpukor,
About to bring upon the warriors
Yet another slaughter,
When Kyyaz, whose battle-axe had a steel haft,
In the blink of an eye,
Before anyone could say a word,
Reached her with practiced skill
And hewed straight through the steel
At the middle of its haft.
The moon-faced woman Aichürök
Fought without rest for six days,
And suffered wounds in seven places;
She fought without rest for seven days,
And suffered wounds in seven places.
Blood flowed from her white breast;
The strength drained away
From fair Chürök, and she grew faint.
Her heroic arm, once quick to strike,
Hung powerless and numb;
Her crimson dress and fine robe
Were soaked in dark, clotted blood.
This dog-born, faithless world
Grew too narrow to contain her soul.
