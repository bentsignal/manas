From atop that tower
She called out to all the assembled people.
“Surging hosts of the Jediger,
Come, all of you!” she cried.
“Great dark host of the Jediger,
The khan’s doorway is like Judgment Day;
We need a way to pass through it.
The khan’s heart is massive iron;
We need a way to melt it.
Let the renowned Jediger people
Take counsel two by two.
Now, what do you say?
Behold this dreadful matter:
The tormented, ill-fated woman
Has fallen into grievous labor;
Come and judge her suffering.
Come, elders grown old among the people,
All you long-eared men of wide experience
Who understand the words you hear.
Come, you who calculate the months,
You who reckon the lunar count;
Come, men skilled in calculation,
And hear the question I shall pose.
If Aichürök, Akun’s daughter,
Should suddenly bear a child,
Whose will it be? Consider!
If moonlike Chürök
Bears a child today,
Whose will it be? Consider!”
When the Jediger heard this,
They set down the days in writing
And made their reckoning of them.
Then they set down the months
And calculated their count.
The shrewd and skillful elders,
Like men struck by slaughter,
The hard-pressed Jediger,
All seethed with agitation.
In the marsh of Üyrülmö Hill,
They held council for three days.
A great reverberation struck the earth;
Stars appeared one by one overhead,
And light quivered upon the ground.
Stars flashed in the heavens,
Cries rang toward the qibla,
The bitter wind before dawn whistled,
The reed tops rattled,
And fledgling larks clamored.
Full daylight touched the earth,
And the radiant dawn advanced.
Every beast and living creature
Raised a clamor from the earth;
Upon every mountaintop,
Radiance poured as the sun rose.
From the palace in the tower,
Holding a golden staff,
Waving her white kerchief,
Wrapped in a loose fur coat,
The unfortunate woman walked with measured grace,
Imploring the Creator.
Within the tower with its threshold,
The helpless, ill-fated woman
Stood in true labor.
Mountainous Kyyaz now saw
Your sister-in-law’s travail.
Seeing it, brave Kyyaz
Prepared himself like a hawk;
An idea from the unseen
Came to brave Kyyaz.
Rumbling in fury,
He addressed the great dark host of the Jediger:
“Assemble before the khan!”
Khan Kyyaz began to rage.
When they saw Khan Kyyaz’s wrath,
The Jediger acted as though an enemy had come;
Crying, “May our people’s road be fortunate!”
They scattered in fright.
The tower was the emblem of the khan—
Ask no more about that tower:
Its full height was magnificent.
