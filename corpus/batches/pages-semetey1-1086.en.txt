The Creator would surely never grant another.
Where the racehorse Akkula had not trodden,
Nothing on earth remained whole.
Where Manas's hand had not reached,
No people remained whole.
Was it not Manas whose every deed
Pleased mortal men?
Did Manas not raise
The tiny band of Muslims
Among infidels of another father?
The one he pinched was Bakburchun;
The foe he fought in earnest was Kakan-Chyn.
When many saints spread their hands in prayer,
Hero Manas was their blessing.
When the Chinese swept over the land,
To the Muslim people
Hero Manas was a father.
He was called the hero Manas;
I have seen what he was
Upon the face of the world.
I have seen that he was a fortress
Through which no wind reached the Muslims.
Yesterday—was there such a deed in your tidings?
Dear Kozho, listen!
Yesterday, at Kökötöy's funeral feast,
Before the great gathering,
Koshoy went, and I went.
Chynkozho, son of Shygay,
When did you go with them?
Yesterday, when Khan Kökötöy passed away,
When he went without turning back
To the land from which none return,
There was a man named Kökötöy;
In the world, Kökötöy
Had been a wealthy pillar.
In old Tashken and Chyyrchyk
He dwelt as a wealthy man.
Of all the peoples of the age
That ears had heard and eyes had seen,
Kökötöy was the wealthiest.
He was a khan with forty rooms
Filled with red coins and yellow gold.
So there was one named Kökötöy.
To all four quarters,
Kökötöy's wealth
Spread and brimmed like a lake.
He tended all four kinds of livestock;
Upon the hairy-breasted black earth,
Kökötöy's worldly herds
Had sprung from the strongest stock.
Kökötöy, father of the people,
Had grown very old;
His wealth was complete,
Yet Kökötöy, father of the people,
Had remained childless.
Trusting in his wealth, Kökötöy
Had taken seven wives;
Until he reached seventy-five,
He had remained without seeing a child.
When he reached seventy-five,
He took seventy young men as attendants,
Hunting wild asses and pursuing gazelles,
And went on a great hunt.
