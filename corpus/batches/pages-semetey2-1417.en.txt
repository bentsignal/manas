When the great multitude of Zhediger
Looks upon your spectacle,
May the spear you thrust shatter;
May the champion horse you strike squarely
Fall stretched upon its belly.
When you advance and strike firmly,
May the spear be hung up for measuring.
Let your fame spread openly
Through the people, my foal:
‘Kutu's son is mighty!’
Let old men and all who see him
Despair of their lives,
Saying, ‘Küzhürmön is a wrestler!’
You are alone—be fierce;
Let the multitude despair of life.
Though poor, be generous;
Be sudden enough to scorch stone,
And let the rich despair of life.
If you are a hero, be a wrestler;
Let the khan despair of life.
When you thrust, thrust firmly;
Take aim and spear Küzhürmön.
Let him spend his wrathful strength;
Let Küzhürmön, who entered for the prize,
Cast away all care for life.
Küzhürmön, who boasts of courage—
That champion famous among the people,
Bull-chested and rash-tempered,
That swaggering man swollen with pride—
Spear him squarely and strike true;
Take aim, seize him, and strike firmly!
Let them shout, ‘Küzhürmön has flown from his horse!’
Let the Kyrgyz carry this tale,
The people shouting in excitement;
Let your strength and courage
Please the gathered nation.
Let it become a tale among all the people.
Extend your spear and hold your guard;
Do not flounder, my foal,
Hold your body firmly and do not hurry.
May Küzhürmön's strength depart
And his clothes and armor come apart;
Let Kutu's son be revealed,
And Küzhürmön called a clumsy wrestler!
Hero, add the gray kulan to the herd;
In your father's old age,
Let him be counted among men
By being called the father of a wrestler!”
Kutu came and spoke these words,
A wondrous marvel to those who understood,
And placed the spear in his hand.
Karadöö set out on his course.
When his father Kutu had spoken,
Delivered his words and turned back,
Karadöö inherited those words.
Such was Karadöö's way:
Without looking around,
Before the teeming Zhediger people,
He spared his life for nothing.
Karadöö gathered his strength;
Sparks flew from his great nostrils,
And fire flashed from both eyes.
“Will this wretched soul not see
The decree written upon its brow?
When its days and water are exhausted,
Does a person not die in the end?
Against thundering Küzhürmön
I shall thrust my spear with all my might;
Against the wrestler who boasts of strength,
I shall clash and fight with the spear
And win one prize from him.
If I cannot do that, this wretched soul
Shall face a wrestler like Küzhürmön
And be cast down.
I shall dart like a merlin,
Come out along his flank,
