When I drove them toward the sunrise,
When they crossed by trackless ground,
Crying out like crows,
When the Black Kalmyks fled,
Pursuing them from behind,
When I chased them relentlessly and crushed them,
The Kalmyks' khan, Molto Khan—
When I caught Molto and slew him,
When I made such sport,
Molto's daughter Karabork
Was the wife I seized.
I had no child by her.
In those days, loading gold coins on seventy camels,
Loading elephants chosen for their strength,
Driving livestock to arrange the marriage,
Making promises and chewing fat,
Gold by the pood and red copper
Given as gifts to the bride's elder kinswomen,
Thus meeting face to face,
Temir Khan's daughter Kanykei—
I had no child by her.
Elder Koshoi, hear my words!
They said a man does not pass without an heir;
I am going without an heir.
They said a man does not depart without a child;
I am going without a child.
If you take the hide trousers,
And give a blessing held in honor,” he said.
As Khan Manas stood weeping,
Koshoi gave his blessing.
Unbelievers and Muslims,
Raising their hands and crying out,
Gave their blessings together.
Semetey, conceived through that blessing,
The lamp of your unfortunate one—
In Khan Manas's place
I look to him for support.
When this orphan was born,
The sorrow in his heart
Manas banished, leaving none,
When he named him warrior Semetey,
The Russians ate at his feast and departed,
Saying, “If this boy lives,
He will overthrow the Oirats.”
The Chinese ate at his feast and departed,
Saying, “If this boy lives,
He will overturn the age.”
River and spring flowing through valleys,
The dear child won only at death's door—
You thrust a spear right through him,
You pursued him to the point of death.
Upon Telkurong you saddled
A hide lining beneath the saddlecloth.
Pursuing my orphan,
You showed your valor today!
As Bakai spoke thus,
Poor Kardigach
Wept piercingly, wept bitterly,
Screaming, wept hard and loud,
“From Manas, my very own brother,
The child he left behind!” she wept.
Kardigach stood and spoke,
Speaking a terrible thing no one knew:
“Elder Bakai, Semetey,
Do not bar my way today.
If you let go of my hand,
Let me mount Telkurong properly.
The six who know no ancestry,
Let me drive them all before me.
Making every one of them stagger,
Those old men Abyke and Kobosh,
Let me gouge out their eyes in torment.
Upon those dogs of evil aspect,
Let me slaughter every one, leaving none.
Like things seized by a lion,
Let me wipe out those dogs completely.
Let me trample their heads,
Beat and crush them like swine.
An evil spirit must have possessed them.
Casting a harmful spell upon those dogs,
Let me open their eyes wide.
Those six who have gone mad—
Let's get angry.
The scoundrels gorged on liquor,
Let me quell their roaring fury.
The old ones, with Kyrgyl at their head,
All forty companions,
For Manas in the world to come,
Let me slaughter them as a sacrifice.
Upon the dogs who attacked first,
Let me take revenge like this.
The scoundrels gorged on liquor,
Let me trample their heads,
Scatter their four kinds of livestock,
Raid and seize their mares.
If they come near, striking to scatter their brains,
Let me knock them out of those dogs,
Some of those who have gone mad.”
When Kardigach said this,
Semetey spoke these words:
“Alas, elder sister, what are you saying?
Who should hear these words of yours?
Let the night that blinds the eyes hear them;
Let the dark-bottomed water hear them.
My wretched sister, who should hear them?
‘When he grew angry,
He slaughtered your elder brothers,’
Would not everyone gossip so?
Would God not strike me to the core?
Killing one's own kin—
Is there precedent for such a deed?
Devouring one's own relatives—
Let alone among humans, elder sister,
Is there such a deed among beasts?
What is not on people's tongues?
‘Manas's mad orphan son
Slaughtered his elder brother,’ would they not say?
If my people heard this openly,
Would they not devour me with mockery?
When people gathered to talk,
‘They were a legacy from his father Manas;
He slaughtered his companions,’ would they not say?
All the gathered people,
Coming together and gossiping,
Would they not all devour me?
‘In Temir Khan's city,
He wandered away and grew up;
This son of Manas
Turned out rootless,’ would they not say?
Jilgynduu, Keng-Kol, this Talas
Are lands left by my father.
All the Kyrgyz and the many Argyn
Are people left by my blood-drinking father.
This broad Talas here
Is no city of the Chinese khakan.
Who split lungs and take gall—
My two elder brothers, Abyke and Kobosh,
Are no Chinese enemy Konurbai in Beijing.
On this dreadful day my sister is not herself.
Consider these words, elder sister:
Is there a hollow on the dark ridge?
Ah, my dear elder sister,
To slaughter and leave wailing,
Is there an enemy come from the Kalmyks?
Is there a hollow on the red ridge?
To destroy and slaughter,
Is there an enemy come from China?
My dear sister, hear my words.
A warrior longs for a swift steed.
I will never harbor ill intent
Toward kin who share my woven warp.
My dear sister, hear my words.
You are of the kind whose hem hangs low;
You bear the name of woman.
Turning back from the land of Jelpinish,
You will not settle here as our neighbor.
Your threshold is elsewhere, dear one;
You will never be kin to us.
Go on, sister, to your people,
To your land of Jelpinish.
Do not grieve, elder sister.
If the gelded camel dies, a young camel remains;
Will its pack saddle lie on the ground?
If the father dies, there is a child;
Will his place stand empty?
Once I have settled in Talas,
Driving seven hundred red hybrid camels,
Driving livestock that furrow the earth,
If I do not braid my horse's tail,
My renowned brother-in-law Jarmanas—
If I do not go and see his face,
My accomplished elder, warrior Bakai—
If I do not take him to lead the way,
Against the battling Ak Doo and Kok Doo,
If I do not make sport of slaughter,
My sister Kardigach and my brother-in-law,
If I do not rescue them,
The enemies who confront you—
Wait, brave sister—
If I do not slaughter them all,
May I die as though never born of Manas;
Cursed be the hour of my birth!
Against Kok Doo, who fights you,
Let me grapple and shed his blood.
Jelpinish, where you dwell—
Reaching it and camping nearby,
Standing by you, elder sister,
If I do not come and wage war,
From the monster's hands,
If I do not rescue you,
May my name Semetey perish!
Making the promise firm,
Swearing an oath upon our lives,
Let us put the day in writing,
Let us reckon the days.
This is the promise, these are the words;
Elder sister, now go back.
‘Semetey has promised to come,’
Go and tell my khan-brother-in-law.
Acting as a wife, elder sister,
Do not turn back to me.
Go out of my sight.
When I go to my kin,
If you come before my eyes,
My precious sister, you will die;
You will face Judgment.
If you appear before me again,
Sister, I will slaughter you.
Beside Manas, gone to the other world,
I will lay you foot to foot with him.
I will slaughter and eat Telkurong, then leave.
To both my mothers,
‘My sister Kardigach has died,’ I will say and leave.
Farewell then, sister,” he said.
They shook hands;
Holding his hand, she wept bitterly.
Old Bakai, the spiritual protector, wept;
On that dreadful day the grieving one wept again.
Speaking her lament as she strode,
To Almighty God,
The orphan found only at death's door,
She entrusted as a sacred charge.
With her dark-brown horse,
With her black battle coat,
Parting from Semetey,
Kardigach turned and rode away.
Through deep depressions,
Kardigach rode along the hollow.
Her heart sorely troubled,
Tears streaming from her eyes,
‘The only child left by Manas—
What will those six scoundrels do to him?’ she said.
