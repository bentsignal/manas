If even a crow appeared from afar,
They would carry word to the city.
They stood throughout the land,
To learn the truth
About kinsman and enemy,
Serving as messengers between them.
What the hollows were like, what the mountains were like—
They were guides who returned at a run.
At the slightest stirring they turned back;
At the command to speak, they instantly
Reported whatever they saw
To lion-maned Seytek,
Traveling under concealment,
And whenever anything occurred,
Bringing word of what appeared.
The diviner at the watchpost,
An inheritance from the forefathers,
A keen eye handed down by heroes,
Spoke his words clearly
And lived openly among men.
When Sarybai, son of a monster,
Rode out for single combat,
The brave diviner saw him,
And at once brought the lion-maned hero
News for Seytek.
Then the diviner
Spoke these words:
“Lion-maned Seytek, hear me;
I have seen a marvel such as this.
In all the world I have never seen
A man of such surpassing terror.
Do not refuse what I say:
To the ravaging monster,
Hero, you granted a term of seven days;
The enraged Sarybai
Is a fearsome calamity such as this.
His lion eyes are blazing,
His overflowing rage is boiling;
He is swollen like a blue tick—
Such is his appearance.
Armed and furious,
Draped in black velvet,
Khan Sarybai rages;
His stare is like a tiger’s.
No man could face
That foul-natured boar;
His roar is like a lion’s.
The ravager who drives the people mad
Seems able to grind them into sand beneath his feet.
No living soul can withstand his wrestling;
Those he meets seem to fall dead.
His cry is like a lion’s;
His strength seems to have overflowed.
Unable to withstand his might,
Khans, common folk, and living creatures
Seem to flee in terrified haste.
Sarybai has ridden out for single combat—
Such is your monster.
He looks ready to slaughter his opponent,
To gnaw apart whoever meets him;
His baleful venom is so great
That he seems ready to swallow anyone he encounters.
Such is the hero’s ferocity.
His figure is like a mountain ridge,
His blue-black hardness is like iron.
When you behold his wrath and venom,
He seems ready to devour the living—
Such is his valor.
Brave Sarybai has come as a warrior;
From the infidel’s fury,
The black earth itself shook.
His destructiveness was beyond counting;
I saw him arrive.
He became an ember burning with spite;
Before the infidel’s imposing form,
The speechless flowing river
Ceased its course and stood still.
No common multitude can stand
Against the enraged infidel.
When he bellows, he kills;
