Will his day of triumph not dawn?
Will he not pursue us as far as Köykap?
If that warrior is enraged,
Will he not make a spectacle of slaughter?
Will he not leave not one of you
And massacre you all?
Will he not utterly plunder
The Jediger’s women and girls?
When Külchoro meets an army,
He grinds it down like sand and flour.
He makes them drink his furious venom,
Surrounds, slaughters, and batters them,
And finishes them within two days.
He will raise red slaughter and turmoil,
And bring great misery and uproar.
He will empty the bullets from his cartridge pouch;
When his wild fury seizes him,
He will wipe out many of you.
You are a lord, hero Kyyaz—
Will he not trample over our heads?
Will he not crush the lords,
Dig a well in the place of honor,
Ransack all your bundles,
And scatter your four kinds of livestock?
Will he not dig a well in the place of honor,
Turn upon us and wreak havoc,
And avenge every ancient grudge?
Once that warrior has recovered,
Will he not rip your belly open like a donkey’s?
Foolish Kyyaz, may ruin take you!
Should the two of us be left alone,
Will he not dig your road to doom,
Bind your hands behind your back,
Strip off your coat as booty,
Break through your gorge and make a road,
And disgrace your bloodied head?
Good fortune to you, hero Kyyaz—
Will he not seize you by the scruff?
Will he not bind us together
And exhibit us to the Dångyt?
Will he not cut off your head like a sheep’s?
If you fall into Külchoro’s hands,
Will he not split your belly like a melon?
He has ground the rugged mountains into grease—
Külchoro, whose father was Chinese,
Who laid his own elder brother low,
Who never shrank from a face-to-face fight
And never turned back from shedding blood.
Such is the warrior’s nature.
He drinks and is never sated with blood;
The man who fights him is left crawling.
He never retreats from combat,
And when he charges in fury,
He never turns back before drinking blood.
How can you make that black-hearted warrior
Your kinsman?
By counting an enemy as kin,
Now and at every turn,
You have proved a witless Kyyaz,
One who never dares a great risk,
Who cannot step outdoors at night—
You are a coward, Kyyaz,
Without a shred of sense;
A cowardly Kyyaz indeed.
You are a Kyyaz who brings
Disaster upon his own life,
A Kyyaz who will ravage
And destroy his own people,
A Kyyaz who will drive an arrow
Into his own body,
A Kyyaz who will surrender
His own life to death.
If you mean to deliver him as bloody spoil to the slaughterer,
Speak the truth;
If you mean to take the formidable woman Aichürök,
Speak the truth about that as well.”
The utterly wicked Kanchoro
Made his cruel wrath known,
