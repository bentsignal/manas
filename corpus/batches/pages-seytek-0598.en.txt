To consume every foe he faced,
To sacrifice himself for his people,
To die if death had come,
And settle the account in earnest
With the savage, treacherous swine,
He rode out alone from the Kyrgyz,
His silhouette looming like a mountain.
He threw his dreadful enemy into confusion,
Making his peerless racer
Stride with a stately gait.
He brought it toward the tumult of the host;
Upon the steed huge as Ala-Too,
His father, the noble falcon and lion,
Rode at a rolling gallop.
Buurul sensed the enemy,
And raised his antler-like head.
He turned toward the direction
From which the mighty infidel would come,
Lengthening his stride like a wild ram.
The superb battle charger
Glared with eyes like a mountain ram’s,
Pinned back his ears like a wild ass,
And, truly destined for the unseen,
Swept the earth like a whirlwind.
No livestock could match
The pace of Buurul beneath him,
Nor could the wind match his gallop.
He drew tight the long reins,
And his hooves furrowed the earth,
As he raised his steel spear
And headed straight toward Sarybay.
From Sary’s curving ridge,
From the edge of Kötörmö,
He covered his awesome form
In armor no bullet could pierce;
Grieving in bitter wrath,
He fastened its nine clasps
And donned his battle coat.
He threw all before him into panic;
Behold the towering, mountain-like figure,
And the manifest prince’s
Fearsome aspect—
The hair on one’s socks would stand on end.
His eyes blazed like a hungry lion’s,
And he ground his teeth as he charged,
Roused like a starving lion;
The lionhearted hero’s majesty was unmatched,
And he roared in fury:
“Through all my years I have seen lions,
And I have seen hosts of warriors,
Yet it seems I had never truly seen one;
Alas, Sarybay, wretched soul,
It seems I have lived without dying.”
The bloodthirsty lion flew into a rage,
And readied all his weapons;
Crackling like lightning,
He prepared himself against Sarybay.
Sarybay was the one he would face,
He who had brought misery upon his descendants;
Such was the mighty clash.
The warrior’s keen edge
Was like well-tempered steel;
When a day of desperate battle dawns,
All true heroes are thus.
From Kanykey’s fortress,
From Aichürök’s encampment,
From the plains of Great Talas,
From the banks of Jylgyndy,
He stirred all the people into alarm,
Making the matchless charger
Stride with a proud, rolling gait.
Taking the wise and accomplished lion,
And the warrior companion as his support,
He went to wage single combat,
To drive the monstrous infidel’s son
All the way back to his own land,
To unleash a fearsome slaughter.
The hero girded himself in wrath
To deliver the Kyrgyz people
From calamity.
