He opened the ammunition pouch,
In desperate need then,
And scattered shots into the sky.
This was why he scattered them:
Chynkojo could not be seen,
So he fired toward an unknown place.
At the edge of a swirling cloud,
Semey fired a hundred shots,
And shot away the mane and tail
Of Chynkojo's Black Horse.
As if he would leave no humiliation unpaid,
The crouching lion
Seemed about to seize Kojo.
Upon Buurul beneath him,
He lashed from side to side,
Spurring right and left,
Your mighty prince grew ardent,
And raged like a lion.
They exchanged fire, sparing no life,
They exchanged fire in furious assault.
The Buurul horse ran without stopping,
Crossing rounded mountains,
Passing many bulging valleys,
One in the sky and one on earth,
They stood their ground, sparing no life,
And the two gray wolves fought.
The ammunition in the saddlebag
Had all been shaken out,
And Semetey, the son of Manas,
Despaired of his doomed life.
The powder shook out of the powder flask,
And he despaired of his anguished life.
There was no bullet in his cartridge pouch,
And your mighty prince and sultan
Had no ammunition together.
Black within and fiery without,
There was no one who knew his secret.
His greatness was beyond compare;
As he went on fighting,
There was no companion here
To bring him ammunition.
When the gray wolf stood hemmed in,
When he turned Buurul's head,
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Kojo whose deeds were formidable,
Drew his bow like pouring rain,
And fired bullets like hail.
Black mist billowed from the sky,
His bullets screamed through the air,
A thousand at a time he fired and passed,
A hundred at a time he fired and passed,
Then the Alengir bow and steel-tipped arrows,
With those he shot forty times;
Chynkojo had begun the battle,
And went on thus without regret.
In his bow and gun
Not half a shot remained.
He pondered and spoke,
And devised every grave calamity.
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Kojo whose deeds were formidable.
Then Kojo thought this,
Kojo, a hero clear in mind,
Conceived an evil no man could know:
“If I descend and fight on earth,
Semetey the swine will spare no life.
I shall turn back,
I shall go to Jeti-Özön.
Beside Jerge-Tal,
