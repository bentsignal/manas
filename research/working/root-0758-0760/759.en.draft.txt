You struck her down, screaming.
You struck my mother's swaddled child away from her.
The treasury left by Manas,
You plundered it utterly.
Thus you did just as you pleased.
The wealth left by my father,
You took as booty and rested content.
Until I reached twelve years of age,
Because of you, my elder kinsmen,
Because of your foolish deeds,
Into Temir Khan's city
I wandered and made a living;
Into Kara Khan's city
I fled and made a living.
When we wandered to Temir Khan,
As for me, still in swaddling clothes,
Saying, “Someone will bring suffering
Upon my child while he is young,”
My mother Kanykei
She cut through my twig,
[HOLD]
Made me step over a dog's bowl,
Made me step across a sword's edge.
Thus she suffered humiliation;
She gave me to her own brother Ysmayyl.
When holy sages spread their hands,
I knew nothing of blessings;
Of Manas, gone to the other world,
I knew nothing of his being my father.
A wretched soul born to such a fate,
I grew up wandering.
[HOLD]
Until I reached twelve,
Temir Khan's little camel,
Ysmayyl's son—
That was what I was, poor soul.
When I reached twelve years of age,
Finding no mountain to hurl myself against,
Finding no foe to fight,
Finding no head to wrench off,
Finding no stone to bite,
I locked up the wandering dervishes,
I beat the mendicants.
If I saw a khoja, I kicked him
And locked him in his mosque.
When they saw my wild behavior,
All the people of Bukhara grew weary.
Temir Khan took fright;
His mind alarmed, he pondered,
And gathered the people of eight khans.
Then the widow Kanykei,
In his sixtieth adult year,
Entered Taitoru in the race.
Kanykei's Taitoru
To the great city of Bukhara
Came far out in front.
The many prizes in Bukhara,
I gathered them all.
Not taking half a coin's worth of goods,
Not taking half a penny's worth of hair,
To all the wrestlers
I gave them as booty.
Those watching crowded round;
The people looked on in wonder.
When the crowd had gathered,
Offering a thousand dark camels,
Offering a thousand black mares with white blazes,
Temir Khan proclaimed:
“Let the wrestlers wrestle on foot;
Let the loser go empty-handed!”
Making it known to the people,
He made his proclamation
And gave his stern command.
From Kokand came Too-Balban;
From Bukhara came Ak-Balban.
The champions wrestled on foot,
Clashing like gray bulls.
