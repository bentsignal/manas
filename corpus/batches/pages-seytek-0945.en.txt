The infidel’s fury mounted,
And he stirred a savage battle;
His wrath rose time and again.
Great hosts, sparing no blood,
Clashed and fought,
While I, in black mourning veil,
Amid the bloodshed of battle,
Stand locked in combat.
If my strength allows, I shall strike
That troublesome swine,
And lay him on his back;
I shall drag him along the road,
And defeat him if he comes to battle;
I shall drink like liquor the blood
Of that invincible boar.
If death seizes me and my day ends,
I shall yield my precious life.
Against that fierce swine of a foe
I shall try my strength in combat!
When fate arrives, must one not die?
Must not every mortal born
Meet the Creator’s decree?
When the naked taker of souls,
Who reaches out amid the strife,
Draws near,
The speaking tongue is stilled—
What soul can escape?
…………………………….
Subduing the whole wide world,
The black earth, barren of fat—
“Do not grieve, my people,”
Said Sarybay, son of the ogre.
His white-barreled gun was enormous;
Nothing like it existed in the world.
As they advanced to meet,
They fell upon each other with war cries;
Before countless gathered peoples,
Such was the shape of this conflict,
Such was the venom the accursed one spoke.
This was the fate of the city-dwelling folk:
From Sarybay’s venom,
From the violence of his wrath,
Unable to endure his oppression,
The noble, wealthy people
Burned and smoldered into embers.
The red-necked falcons,
Champions surpassing ordinary men,
Vanished entirely from among my people.
Many warriors fought;
Then at one fateful time
He made widows of those with husbands,
And drove living people into slavery.
Not once, but a thousand times he did it.
He leveled hills into plains,
And used force against noble heads.
He bound and ruined much of my fortune,
…………………………….
And shattered many of my hopes;
He made my people groan
And utterly broke my back.
Hear my truthful words:
You have waged war many times,
And still I have not taken my revenge.
Among round-headed mortals,
Among peoples armed with every weapon,
His doom is hell and his venom strong;
No horror like this exists.
When he rode out from Zhelpinish,
He came leading ten thousand troops,
Driving the entire host together;
When they saw wild rams, they chased and ate them,
When they saw argali, they shot and ate them,
When they saw ibex, they gathered and ate them—
Every living soul suffered there.
Their heads ensnared in torment,
He shot and destroyed them all.
He came to exterminate
The Kyrgyz line to its very root,
Saying, “I shall sweep them all away.”
