They faced each other and fought
Until their strength was exhausted.
Akolpok, which no spear could pierce,
Was torn apart like scattered ash.
The troops cried out and recoiled.
Sable mail with lark-eye links,
With a jewel-studded collar and copper sleeves,
And costly crimson
Bulletproof coats were shredded.
Many champions and many lions,
Mighty, wise strongmen—
So many heroes were slaughtered.
So many conflicts had occurred;
So many heroes and lionhearts
Had been swallowed by the swine Sarybai.
They struck but could not prevail;
They could not defeat the son of Zhelmoguz.
The fierce champions and lionhearted warriors who came
Fought but could not defeat him.
They clutched their collars in grief,
Despairing of their fly-sized lives.
The overturning world’s
Deceit became plain.
They stood face-to-face,
Having ridden out to single combat.
The three fierce warriors, one after another,
Fought him in turn.
On the great battlefield where the people suffered,
In a deserted ravine where no living soul passed,
Stood noble gray-wolf Semetey,
Külchoro on his Karala,
The spring of the hill country,
Brave Seytek, born after his father’s death,
Semetey’s valiant son.
They stood face-to-face,
Shouting as they thrust their spears,
Until strength failed and power departed,
Fighting day and night.
Who had ever seen such a deed?
The two arms that had served them
Were paralyzed and could no longer rise.
Their precious heads, needed while alive,
Were drenched like crimson buckets of blood.
Their precious lives, alluring to those doomed to die,
Longed for a single glimpse of deliverance.
Unable to overcome Sarybai,
Each day they endured felt bound to a span.
Death, from which no traveler returns,
Circled close before their brows.
Their bulletproof coats of armor were pierced,
Their many layers falling away,
Every lining torn loose.
They fought but could not prevail.
That infidel son of Zhelmoguz
Kept demanding battle in this way.
The one destined to grapple with him was Kuyaly,
The champion maiden who would oppose him.
She took the cast-iron-headed weapon in hand.
At the very sight of the hero Sarybai,
Take fright and flee far away!
Unable to prevail in combat,
Those golden heads suffered torment.
He turned toward the battlefield,
And raised in his hand
The steel-hafted battle-axe.
With a ringing clang, utterly unconcerned,
He vowed to break the banner
Of Manas’s heroic descendants and slaughter them,
To wipe out their entire line.
The champion warriors who clashed with him,
The lions upheld by divine Shay,
He valued no more than his boot heels.
Strength and valor, sport and battlecraft—
God Almighty had granted that swine
Without measure.
True champions and lionhearted warriors
He valued no more than a belt-string.
He neither swerved nor flinched,
Neither faltered nor stirred,
But stood like a forest lion.
