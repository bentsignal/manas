He aligned and wheeled his spear,
Thundered menacingly,
And spared no effort.
Its shaft was mounted with silver,
Its edge was faced with steel—
A steel-headed battle-axe.
Gripping it firmly, he braced himself,
Drew in his frame and made ready.
Fire poured from his mouth,
And his courage showed in full;
God had smitten him with fury.
Blood streamed from his charging steed,
And blood flowed along the path of his charge.
Growling in rising passion,
He thundered words to himself,
Raging and lunging,
Writhing with violent force;
His brows and lashes flared apart,
And venom poured from his mouth.
Toward the dauntless son of the hero
He turned and braced himself,
Straining and glaring with courage.
A mountain-like surge came to a halt,
Like a horse tethered to a post.
At the swine’s thunderous approach,
The accursed veteran
Readied his fire pit.
The black earth, without overturning,
Held firm beneath the massive weight.
The boar was rich in strength;
The skillful enemy Sarybay
Opened his mouth like a wolf,
And fire scattered before him.
He assumed a form of savage courage
And stirred grievous strife.
The mountain-like Tookyzyl
Broke the ground beneath its hooves;
He made Tookyzyl bound,
Spurring it right and left,
And lashing from side to side.
Pouring out his wrath, he shouted,
Raising the battle cry, “Where is he?”
His violent fury burst forth,
And he appeared like a mountain of black rock.
Sarybay was indeed a warrior;
Such was his conduct in combat.
Vision dimmed amid the tumult;
The battle, if seen, was a roaring storm.
Young Seytek, brave gray-maned wolf,
As Sarybay faced him
And began the savage slaughter,
Before an arrow could arrive,
Measured the place he must strike.
As the man rode to thrust at the boy,
“I have met my enemy—
May God prosper what follows;
May my forefather Manas uphold me,” he said.
Against the menacing villain,
Who swept away all he encountered,
The boar, warrior Sarybay,
The descendant of the brave gray wolf
Turned Kulansur’s head.
The father’s son
Did not fluster or hurry;
A descendant of his line
Never retreated from a foe,
Never fled after yielding ground,
Never thought, “I shall die,”
And never surrendered his courage to another.
The black-gray wolf, the peerless lion,
With strength compounded like packed sand,
Fought there in battle.
He measured and thrust toward the jaw:
“Just beneath the breast,
Right through the lung,
That is the place of death.”
Sarybay now leveled his spear:
“Right at the point of the jaw,
Right through the heart,
That is the measured spot.”
