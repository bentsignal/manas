Holding each other's reins,
Fighting face-to-face,
Shouting and striking with axes,
Grappling from the very hem,
Until they exhaust one another—
It would be vain if I failed to foresee it.
If I failed to foresee this affair,
An axe or sword might pass through,
And needless death might overtake him.
I had its inside lined with silk;
Its four sides were wondrous—
I had them reinforced with sharp steel,
Cast from yellow gold
And placed within its middle.
For seven years I watched and repaired it,
Intending it for the battle
Of my renowned son.
A deep-blue iron helmet
And coat of mail, for him to wear together;
So he might cross their fastenings
And tie them beneath his throat;
So he might know this service.
It is indeed true that possessions
Are the protection of a life held in trust;
It is also true that a sharp weapon
Shatters when struck against it.
I have been making these things;
Hear of this labor of mine!
Know the value of it when you go,
And say, ‘My mother suffered hardship!’
A true helmet and coat of mail—
My valiant son, wear them when you go!
I, your unfortunate mother,
Have many kinds of armor;
To give you, my son,
I have many kinds of wonders.
My heart is shut in grief, my soul aflame;
In this mother's hands,
What is there, and what is not!
That you would grapple with the khagan,
And stand unleashing turmoil,
Bringing fierce upheaval,
That you would campaign
Against Kakan-Chyn's Beijing—
I knew it long ago!
So my orphan with his noble calling might wear it,
I had prepared many pieces!
As I stood pondering,
On account of my youthful son,
God's sun darkened in the sky;
Sixty worries came upon my head at once.
My hair twisted like reins;
Yesterday, unable to stand from true grief,
I summoned the lame smith Bölökbai
And forty Kalcha craftsmen.
I had them wear the jekeybos coat;
I, wretched and resourceful,
Mounted them on ambling horses that did not gallop,
And had them search the whole earth.
I had heard much from those who had seen
The ibex of Jetik-Too,
And from all who knew
That nowhere was there any hide
Like its hide.
I loaded provisions on camels,
And had the mountain ibex of Jetkileng
Shot after they went to Jet-Kait.
Keeping it from the eye of the sun,
I laid it in the shade;
I, wretched woman, seasoned its hide,
Resting neither by day
Nor sleeping at night.
I had it worked by my own hand;
Lest its curing remain incomplete,
I had exactly eighty-five strong men
Work it without pause for six months,
Bringing its curing to perfection,
And had skilled men cut it,
