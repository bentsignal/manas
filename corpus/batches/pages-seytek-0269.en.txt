For blood-red slaughter and battle,
The noble steed was perfectly fitted.
In grievous, desperate conflict,
The noble steed had stood firm.
When given barley, he crunched it,
His eyes shining like an argali’s;
He cracked wheat between his teeth,
His eyes shining like a stag’s.
When ridden into battle, he never flinched,
The blessed, broad-chested courser.
Through heat that seemed to burn the brain,
Through stench reeking of dung,
Across forty-one high mountain passes,
Across deserts forty days wide,
You were a beast that endured it all,
For you possessed the nature of a tulpar.
You were a soul dearer than a father.”
So Külchoro lamented,
Grasping the forelock and breathing in its scent,
Sniffing him deeply, saying, “My inherited Buurul.”
His reason fled and his heart burned,
His heart aflame with grief.
Having found the camel-like gray steed,
He sobbed and wept all the harder.
God forbid anyone should behold it:
When he saw Külchoro,
The noble steed, massive as a camel,
Neighed and snorted,
Pawed and pounded the black earth,
Flung his forelock toward the sky,
And hammered the ground with his hooves.
He clattered his bit,
And strained, dragging at the earth;
Noble Buurul stood leaning close,
And Taybuurul kept sniffing
At Külchoro’s neck.
His mane and tail streamed apart;
The animal heaved deep sighs,
And tears poured from his eyes.
As Buurul stood weeping,
His noble elder brother Külchoro,
Before he could cry out the glad tidings,
Külchoro, upheld by divine favor,
Saw Buurul before his eyes,
And tears poured from them.
He pressed his face to noble Buurul’s forelock,
Breathing in its scent,
Clasped him and sniffed him deeply,
Saying, “You remain to me from my father.”
Though he was a beast, he had a soul;
Your swift horse was a trusted companion,
An inheritance left behind
By my gray-maned father, Khan Manas.
We came together when I was no bigger than a louse,
We joined as one when I was no bigger than a thumb;
He carried me across ravines,
Guarding me like a father,
A peerless steed renowned among the people,
My inheritance, dearer than my father.
Broad-chested and shallow-maned,
He never flinched when ridden into battle,
Never tired when sent scouting,
His parrot-like tail trailing behind.
When a desert ninety days wide arose,
When people entered a land of suffering,
He endured every hardship.
My beloved Buurul,
I had him tended in a darkened stall
And had an amulet fastened around his neck;
When a dubious thing appeared,
The brass-beaked one tested and approved it.
Taybuurul, massive as a camel—
On high passes and sheer cliffs,
Buurul was the horse ridden over the heights;
In dire hardship and great battle,
Buurul was the horse ridden over the earth;
In blood-red slaughter and swirling chaos,
Buurul was the horse ridden on the flank.
Where lungs were split and heads were hewn,
Buurul was the horse ridden into the fray.
