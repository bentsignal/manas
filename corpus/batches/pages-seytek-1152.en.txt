“In Beijing, where wrath cannot penetrate,
His people swarm like black beetles.
This is the great nation of China,
A place where men settle only for battle,
Where your first forefather led the charge
And the land sucked dry all who fought.
There, parted from his lions,
Your first forefather met his end.
It is no cheap land like an open ridge,
Nor is Bilgich a common warrior.
If we go any farther,
There are iron-walled enclosures
And deeply excavated pits.
There are armies of that madman,
So dense the sun cannot be seen.
Anyone who falls in cannot leap back out;
Their stubborn traps are hell itself.
That senseless scourge
Commands a well-arrayed host of ten thousand.
Once locked in combat, they never retreat;
It is a radiant and exalted land,
Where the people of Chin-Machin dwell.
Their warriors butcher any who provoke them;
They are a people who ask, ‘Where is strife?’
Whenever one among them dies,
They roast and eat another.
When their giant rage possesses them,
They strike down and eat any crow they see,
And surround and devour any wild ass.
They flare up anew whenever diminished;
No living being has ever reached their root.
Though slaughter lasts for years,
Their baleful power never fades,
And no fearsome enemy has overcome them.
Seeking to contend with them,
Long ago your father, Khan Manas, died;
This is that calamitous, prolific nation.
The greatest sorcerer and schemer,
A horse-master, swift even on foot,
The one China sends forth when turmoil comes,
The great expert guarding the throne,
The strategist of the watchman khan,
The shepherd of broad Beijing,
Where even a wolf cannot slip inside—
Great Bilgich, son of Kojozhash,
Knows more than his father knew.
His fortune and skill are perfectly matched;
He will not yield to an equal foe.
He never submits to a commanding word;
Though mighty Möngübö came swarming,
Even rebellious giants could not defeat him,
And he never yielded his measure to anyone.
Will he not lure you onward by feigning flight?
Will he not deliver you to his champions?
If you encounter them, those champions will spill blood;
When enraged, they will cleave you into a thousand pieces.
Once roused, will they not slaughter you
And bring about your death?
You gathered booty from every land
And loaded it upon the swift camel;
At one time or another,
You have made us suffer for your deeds.
Does not every man die from a single shot?
Does not this soul endure hardship?
You are a foolish man;
By refusing all human counsel,
Will you plunge your uncle,
The valiant Joltoy, into misery?”
As Uncle Joltoy spoke these words,
Kulunsur raced up beside him;
When that swift Kulunsur
Had drawn a bridle’s length ahead,
Your uncle Akjoltoy
Seized him by the cheek strap.
His bellow split heads,
His shout shattered stones;
His charger caught up in a pounding gallop,
And he rebuked brave Seytek,
Revealing countless truths,
And telling how one may die before one’s destined hour,
