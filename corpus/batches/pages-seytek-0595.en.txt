The hero Seytek faced him,
The monstrous giant’s warrior son,
The infidel who would swallow the world;
They grappled and fought,
Exhausting every stratagem.
Almambet’s lion,
Külchoro, upheld by the spirit Shay,
Shouted and thrust his spear,
And with the steel-shafted spear
He shattered sixty.
Against the monstrous infidel’s son,
When the cannon fired, no flame pierced him;
When the gun fired, no bullet pierced him.
He tried the White Musket’s shot,
But the full strength of the nobles
Was steadily diminished.
His cunning was unmatched, his venom strong—
Could there be another like him in the world?
Boasting of his strength and lion-heart,
He deemed himself a hero capable
Of overturning the pillar of the universe.
Sparks flew from his dark eyes,
Fire flew from his mace,
Dust rose wherever he advanced,
And when he charged into battle,
He made dust rise among the warriors.
Raging to slaughter the people,
To wreak all the havoc he knew,
To bind and seize the common folk,
To crush rebellion forever,
Not leaving a single soul,
To pass through, slaughtering the Kyrgyz,
And roast like salt the descendants
Of Manas, blessed by Kyzır,
Eradicating them to the very root.
Leaving no root of the Kyrgyz,
He meant to carry them away as spoil,
To spread turmoil to their farthest bounds,
To wipe them out without a remnant,
The descendants of hero Manas;
This marauding swine was like
A flood sweeping across a river crossing.
The mad brute would charge,
And reach you in the blink of an eye;
His looming form was like a whole mountain,
And if he attacked a thousand men,
He seemed ready to devour them at once.
He was both flame and burning ember;
You wretch whose soul has fled,
Even hell held none like him.
At the sight of Sarybay,
Those he toppled would plunge headlong,
And those who challenged him would be consumed.
When roused, the mad brute
Seemed to be the sole
Pillar of the earth.
Clad in armor no bullet could pierce,
Prepared for the stony battlefield,
He settled grimly into his wrath;
The cast-iron head, huge as Mount Opol,
Had bound up his iron head,
His eyes blazing like embers—
Sarybay, the monstrous giant’s son,
Had no equal beneath the heavens.
He made all his weapons ready,
And strapped his sword at an angle;
His measureless form stood apart,
And if a Kyrgyz fell into his hands,
He looked as though he would never turn aside.
When roused, the mad brute
Seemed able to turn back a flood;
When he charged in fury,
Fixing his hatred upon his foe,
He seemed able to grind a mountain to dust.
When the swine surged forward,
No mountain could withstand him;
When the mighty giant charged,
No one could force him to bow,
And none who met him remained alive.
Semetey came to face him.
