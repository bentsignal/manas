When we lived in Kokon,
We were the ones who led the people,
We came from Kokon,
We did not devour your people,
We had seen the designs of Moscow.
Whatever day befell us,
We saw the enemy’s wrath,
Unable to withstand the cold wind,
Sickness pierced us to the marrow,
There was no remedy for our staggering,
Taking the relic into our midst,
With no solution left, we panicked,
Unable to find a place to sustain life,
We fled without finding rest.
When we lived in Kokon,
We were Kyrgyz wearing quilted coats,
We were pure people who fielded cannon,
With great courage when facing the enemy,
We had been khans for forty years.
Like a river flowing from the core,
We were blood against the enemy,
Like water scattered abroad,
We were souls under oppression,
The matter returned to its beginning,
If this affair falls upon our brow,
We will bow our necks to death.
We were humiliated by the empire,
Fleeing from God, we became desperate,
We became full from so much migration,
We now come before you.
From another crown and throne,
Who has not shaken and burned?
While the empire held power,
Who has not lived under confinement?
This is the affair in which we broke our pledge,
Saying Padishah Jeljetpes would come,
In truth, you did not become Kokon.
The people of this city,
When that khan still stood at their head,
A blue-gray steed with a sweeping tail,
We had defeated many and grown strong,
Our horses raced upon the open steppe,
We used to range across the mountains,
When fortune stood over our heads,
Even youths would answer us with words.
We are warriors who guard the people,
We are of Kalmak blood and lineage,
Beaten by the wrathful one,
We are wretches who fled the khagan.
We are a people of trusted companions,
Against the dark enemy outside,
We came with our arms opened wide.
We are fighters with dun-colored manes,
Binding bullets into our wounds,
We are poor men who have been wounded.
Ebegei and Kogoi, the two lions,
Dragging their tails, raising an uproar,
We are coursers that win the prize,
Even untrained, when loosed we took the wolf,
We are dark gyrfalcons.
We are heroes equal to you,
Having encountered the khan’s wrath,
Driven about, unable to find a resting place,
We are diminished men whose people are gone.
We have gone about saying, “Receive us,”
If you choose to break and take our fortune,
We are among the honored ones.
Turning from the dark path toward the light,
Guarding the world by a hair’s breadth,
We have been struck down by the khan’s wrath,
Led astray by dark belief,
Wandering without finding a people,
We are heroes who fled the khagan,
Ebegei and Kogoi, the two giants,
If you send us from the host, we are capable,
We will bring back his tulpar.
We are twelve heroic champions,
Breaking a horde and taking a horde,
We will move like a dark-maned stallion,
The blessed Ala-Too will not be clouded,
If we do not wait, we shall die.
Without seeing humiliation, hardship, or quarrel,
We shall return and see it from our camp.
If you accept us, blessed one,
If the enemy is a ruined soul,
We will serve as wings for you.
When I lived in Kokon,
The black Chinese settled in splendor,
I was khan over the black people,
In Talas I was padishah over the people,
In Chüy I was their horde,
Among the Jalal people I was their realm.
Pearl and coral, precious stones,
I alone wore them outwardly.
Asking about affairs among the Chinese,
I was a son who knew them,
Riding the cloud and cloaked in frost,
I was one of the heroes who roamed.
A true Kyrgyz man among the four,
A turn lasting six months,
Since then the Kyrgyz have not taken it.
We are becoming men,
My people remain without lawful union,
Before the khan, the eye of the horde,
Among the horde’s maidens,
As in former times last year,
In the city of conquest,
My ancestors gave birth to me.
Whatever kind of khan my ancestor was,
He was one who led the people.
