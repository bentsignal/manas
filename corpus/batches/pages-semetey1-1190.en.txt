Drawing until others were frightened.
The coarse-headed, stone-skulled man
Stood forth upon the field.
All the watching Muslims
Started and grew afraid.
Algara beneath him,
When it chased a three-year-old, never let it escape;
Its tail was sparse and its mane was scant,
And it was practiced against a fighting foe.
The foot of its tether stood on blue stone.
The people marveled and roared;
The Black Chinese and many Manchus
Raised a clamorous shout.
The Muslim people lamented;
The Black Chinese and many Manchus
Raised a crying shout.
When they saw Kongurbay's form,
Khan Koshoy of Katagan
And saintly hero Bakay
Saw the monstrous size
Of hero Kongurbay the infidel.
"Kongurbay of the Chinese
Has perfect fortune;
He is a hero who knows
The enemy's devices perfectly.
Kongurbay, son of Alooke,
Has all twelve limbs in proportion.
He is a hero of towering stature.
Whoever grapples with him cannot survive;
He is at the height of his power.
The renowned hero Kalcha—
If a hero comes out to fight him,
Will he arrive before an arrow?
Will he reach and crush him?
Will he offer him as a sacrifice?
Will he take his ancient revenge?"
Lord Koshoy and Khan Bakay
Saw him and despaired of life.
When they saw Kongurbay's form,
Golden-crested, with tasseled belt,
Only son of Khan Azoozil,
Wearing a coat of yellow gold
And mounted on a piebald horse,
With a decorated belt around his neck,
Pleaded with the Muslims,
Saying, "Let me enter the joust.
My uncle, old Koshoy,
You elders stand here;
You palmful of Muslims,
All of you stand here.
What I amassed remains in Kakanchyn;
What I have chosen is my faith.
What I amassed remains in Beijing;
What I pledged is my faith.
My Prophet is true, my religion is true;
The golden throne was left in Beijing.
When I renounced it and scattered it,
Turned from the black faith to the true one,
And fled as a Muslim,
The Chinese were a multitude and I was alone.
What deed have I done before God?
Barely escaping death,
Leaving my martyred mother behind,
All the Chinese and many Manchus
Came out to block me and fought.
From the anguish formed in my breast,
I prayed to God:
May I find support at the appointed place;
May I find a hero among the Muslims.
Against Kongurbay, son of Alooke,
I will unleash a great tumult;
I will turn and attack the swine
And take my ancient revenge.
So I have long prayed.
If I meet him in a lonely place,
I will offer the mother-forsaken Manchu
As a sacrifice,"
