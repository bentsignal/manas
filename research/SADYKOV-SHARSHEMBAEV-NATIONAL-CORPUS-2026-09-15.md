# Sadykov–Sharshembaev national-corpus lead

Search date: 2026-09-15. This note records only evidence newly established while tracing the corpus, concordance, software, and K1–K5 addressing described by Tashpolot Sadykov and Bakyt Sharshembaev.

## Finding

No online query interface, downloadable text corpus, database export, source repository, or archived public distribution of the Sadykov–Sharshembaev Sayakbay data was located. The evidence instead identifies two related products:

1. a 2011 printed concordance, *Manas Destanı: Kırgızca-Türkçe Büyük Dizin* (TDK publication 1030, 1,647 pages, ISBN 9789751623836); and
2. a separately registered Windows/MS Access electronic word-index application for the Sayakbay Karalaev variant (Kyrgyzpatent certificate 470).

The underlying Sayakbay texts are described as previously published editions, not manuscript transcriptions. Therefore the K1–K5-style word addresses can locate occurrences inside the input publications, but current evidence gives no basis for using them to recover lines absent from those editions.

## Exact evidence

- The original national-corpus paper states that, because the academic edition was unfinished, the project ingested previously published texts. For Sayakbay it names *Manas* I–II (1984/1986), *Semetey* I–II (1987/1989), and *Seytek* (1991):

  <https://bvsis.manas.edu.kg/bakyt.sharshembaev>

- The downloadable Manas University CV repeats the Sayakbay registration and certificate 470, again without an attached program or dataset:
  <https://avesis.manas.edu.kg/download/bakyt.sharshembaev>

## Addressing and missing-line assessment

The project’s location codes appear to function as bibliographic word addresses into its five named source units rather than manuscript shelfmarks. This is an inference from the concordance description and the paper’s explicit five-item Sayakbay source list: the 1984, 1986, 1987, 1989, and 1991 publications. Consequently:

- K1–K5 can help resolve spelling, word occurrence, and page/line placement in those publications if the 1,647-page concordance or certificate-470 database is obtained.
- They cannot independently demonstrate manuscript-level material beyond the published witnesses.
- They cannot supply the known missing Sayakbay lines unless the unlocated certificate-470 database contains data beyond the source list, for which no public evidence was found.
- The MS Access file would still be useful for exact crosswalks and checking whether the corpus designers silently normalized, omitted, or added rows.
