Hurrying in great agitation,
Putting on Akolpok,
And readying his arms and armor.
Yesterday—have you heard of this?
While bloodthirsty Manas lived,
It was encountered through the unseen,
Found upon the open plain,
And, by God’s decree, came
To Sayypkurush, the hero and lion.
The six-edged blade that fell from the sky,
The finest of all sword blades—
Have you heard of it?
Zulpukor, which remained from Manas.
Zulpukor, left as a gift
By valiant Manas, the gray wolf—
When he attacked the shouting foe,
When he drew it from its sheath
And entered the enemy without looking back,
It would bring the end of days.
Until it had drunk its fill of blood,
It would not return to the sheath when put away;
When swung this way and that,
It never went idle without taking a head.
When grasped in the hand with a shout,
It went wherever it was swung;
When its bearer entered the grappling foe,
It drenched them in bright-red blood.
Zulpukor, inherited as a legacy
From Manas, the gray wolf of old—
That was his treasure when he rode on campaign.
The splendid champion, son of the Bloodthirsty One,
Girded on the sword Zulpukor,
And like a cloud about to rain again,
His face turned wrathful.
He buckled the gray kise left by Manas
Around his slender waist.
The object held in the gray wolf’s hand
When he stood leaning upon it
Was a golden-cheeked battle-axe;
On the gray wolf’s left side
Was a side-pouch made of gold.
The sight arrested the heart;
When one looked upon
The handsome-born champion’s other side,
An okchontoi and quiver could be seen.
On a level upland at a forked pass,
Upon a ridge no horse could cross,
He gathered himself in anger;
The splendid hero, son of the Bloodthirsty One,
Leaned upon Akkelte.
Though he had come to spread alarm,
The splendid hero, son of Manas—
That broad and noble hero—
Was not yet one to stir.
The surpassing lion, son of Manas,
Arrested the heart when seen;
He appeared to have been born surpassing
His father Manas, the gray wolf.
