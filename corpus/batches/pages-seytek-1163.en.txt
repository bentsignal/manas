With the dignity of a princely pasha,
He entered the palace—
Your uncle Akjoltay.
With uncommon foresight he made his plan;
Turning toward his right,
Without letting anyone know,
Without alerting a single soul,
He leaned aside,
Filled out his robe,
Slipped his hand into his pouch,
And drew out a fist-sized object.
Disregard it and shame will follow:
“My steadfast child Akbermet,
This pale walnut, large as a fist,
The holy wanderer Khizr
Has entrusted to you.
Eat it, my little one;
Your grime-covered heart will open,
And your frenzy will subside.
My child Bermet, heed me:
I have become your healer.
May you be mother of the nation
That bears a lionhearted champion.
Accept your trust, my child,
Purest among women in flowing skirts,
Foremost among women in white kerchiefs.
Eat it, and it will quench your thirst;
Give your great blessing, my child.
Say, ‘My uncle has left me an inheritance’;
Regard this service of mine, my child.”
When Akbermet heard this,
She walked forward glowing,
Shining like the risen moon,
Blooming like a parrot’s plumage,
Revealing the graces of ninety tribes,
That hapless fairy princess.
Her sacred virtue became manifest.
Before dear uncle Joltay
She sprang like a bow and bowed,
With prayer beads hung about her neck,
And pleaded beyond all measure:
“My uncle Joltay, peerless khan,
You who join what has been severed,
Who gathered all these people,
Who mended what was broken,
My undeserved good fortune,
My sheltering presence, my destiny.”
Speaking thus, Akbermet
Bent her neck and trembled,
Imploring her uncle,
Her whole being melting,
Her flesh and nails reaching out.
When he had told her the true tidings,
Her tiny soul cried out,
And she wept in sincere supplication.
As soon as she ate that walnut,
Her frenzy subsided,
Her mind cleared in contemplation,
The grime upon her heart fell away,
And strength entered and filled her.
Overcome with joy, she trembled:
“I have repented ninetyfold,” she said,
Bowing her dear head down to the earth.
“Uncle Joltay, may you live long;
I have always looked to you for shelter.
I will not assail you with harsh words;
You are the spiritual guide I humbly implore,
And I will never cease to hope in you.”
Thus spoke Akbermet.
As she stood there in this wondrous state,
And finished speaking
To her uncle Joltay, the gray wolf,
Seytek readied himself like a hawk;
Into the lion Seytek’s mind
An inspiration came from the unseen.
In a dream, his forefather hero Manas
Revealed an auspicious vision.
It was his forefather Manas himself,
The radiant one supporting and sustaining him,
Who had come to visit him then.
