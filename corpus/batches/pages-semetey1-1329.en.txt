If my death has come, let me die;
While I remain alive and undying,
With the Black Kalmak and the Manchus,
Let me grapple once and test myself!
I shall bring them mighty alarm;
I shall face them and fight,
And avenge my father’s blood-price!
I shall gird my waist firmly;
From the Kalcha there in Beijing,
I shall pursue my father’s blood-price!
Against Beijing of Chinmachyn,
I shall truly level my spear;
For my sworn father Almambet,
I shall truly pursue his blood-price!
In the original city of Kakan-Beijing,
I shall dig out its very stump;
Charging straight in and spearing,
Fighting them to exhaustion,
I shall satisfy your longing!
Kongurbai, son of Alööke—
I shall fight him and spill his blood.
If my destined end arrives, sprawled out,
I shall die in Beijing itself!
Against Beijing, beyond my strength,
I shall lean back and level my spear;
Clashing with the Black Kalmak,
If my strength suffices, this poor soul
Will slaughter every one of them.
Toward the tent with golden guy ropes,
I shall go on holy campaign,
Against hero Kongurbai;
If my power suffices, the Kalcha
I shall slaughter as a sacrifice.
If God grants it, to Beijing
I shall go alone myself!
What desire would I attain
By forcing the multitude?
Against the seething Chinese,
I shall go alone myself!
Against the Chinese and Manchu people,
If I do not shout and level my spear,
If I do not shatter
Forty-doored Beijing into chaff,
Spilling blood and breaking their ranks,
And, if God wills, fall to a bullet;
If I do not oppose that swine,
The khan of the Kechil, and make him suffer,
Making the swaggering Black Kalmak say,
“He is the son of the Bloodletter!”
If I do not start conflict and raise dust,
Enter battle and take blood,
And heap tumult upon tumult;
If I do not go alone to face
Kongurbai there in Beijing,
If I do not avenge my valiant father
Upon the Kalcha there in Beijing!
Against the Chinese, countless as cow hairs,
I shall wage relentless war;
To famed Beijing
I myself shall reach!
How could I shamelessly gather
The many great men among the Muslims?
Let them say, “He is a great man’s son,”
While I alone pursue the blood-price.
Against the infidels, swarming like black worms,
Against hero Khan Kongurbai,
Into their original city, Great Beijing,
I shall enter straight and level my spear;
Those deep-rooted infidels,
Hero Kongurbai himself—
I shall slaughter them all, leaving none.
Spearing and breaking their ranks,
And, if fate arrives, falling to a bullet.
The writing of the Creator—
If it is written upon my brow, I shall meet it;
Making them say, “He is the Boundless One’s son,”
