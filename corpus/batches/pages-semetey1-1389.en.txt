Who grew up as equals among the people's sons,
Whose right shoulders grew broad,
Who grew into heroes that shattered the Oirats!
So that your hearts would be rich with courage,
I fed you blood-streaked horse-rib fat;
So that you would become inseparable kin,
I had Kanykei nurse you,
And among the Argyn and Kyrgyz,
When did I ever let anyone call you mere boys!
You two scoundrels did not guard the horses;
This deed of yours is very bad.
When I take you double, you will not fit,
And when I drive you on foot, you will not walk!
You two scoundrels would not find a way
To remain and guard the horses!
You do not understand the words spoken,
And when I drive you, you will not take the road!
You brought hardship upon our reconnaissance
And made us carry your saddles and tack!
You took off your coats upon the steppe;
Parting us from every tulpar,
Boys, you have brought a marvel upon my head.
When we were to reconnoiter the main road,
When we were to drive off
The Chinese people's countless herds,
Even in sleep one would not dream such a dream—
Has any clan ever seen such a deed!
The road to Beijing is exceedingly long;
How shall we set our faces and go?
How shall we now reconnoiter
Fortified Beijing?!
When placed in front, our auspicious,
Resourceful uncle
Has the majesty of a countless host.
You found words in sudden danger;
If you are truly a khan,
My dear uncle,
Find sight in the darkness.
Join together what is broken;
Gather together what is scattered!
Find a road where there is none;
If you are truly a lion—
Lion uncle Bakai,
Find a place where the horseless boys
Can be left in safekeeping!
If the horse dies from carrying two,
And we die from hardship,
What desire shall we attain?
Better that we leave the boys
With the baggage train!”
After Semetey said this,
His uncle, old Bakai—
The holy clairvoyant,
The revered kinsman and saint—said:
“Very well, my son, very well;
What you say is wise.
If we take the boys double,
Toward what goal can we proceed?
Better that we entrust them
To God in some place
And leave them with the baggage train!
Kakanchin's Beijing is a formidable city;
May gracious Allah grant a road—
How many places hold peril!
The Chinese are no feeble people;
When their jang-jun issues a decree,
They are not a people who spare their lives.
Beijing is no feeble land;
You will fight the Kakanchin,
No people for mortals to assail!
An earthen rampart encircles it;
Within that rampart
Dwell the unruly Chinese people.
God knows whether we shall reach it;
God knows whether we shall remain alive
And not die in Beijing!
That is a people of painful calamity;
That is the pass of Beijing we must fight!
It has guards and a khan;
Dear Beijing of Zuntung
