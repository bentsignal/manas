In your prime, splendid in sable,
You used to wear it proudly;
When black night fell upon the Kyrgyz,
You plunged into the clash.
Overcoming all who grappled with you,
How many peoples we used to rout!
His fortune was complete,
He was a lion upheld by the spirits;
While ancestral power and honor remain,
A man never truly dies.
He gathered the wealth of the world;
Across the whole universe
He sought ancestral lands—
Bakai was a lion among heroes.
Clad in blue lynx fur,
Bearing a blue-mottled shield,
He blinded those he marked
And ruled the whole world.
No enemy reached his frontier,
Nor did he live off the treasury.
Great was your dignity, fierce your wrath,
Alas, our forefather Bakai.
One day is light and one day night—
This accursed, fleeting world:
Behold how false it is,
How it parts one from one’s companion
And leaves one behind.
His beard had whitened like a swan;
When the day of his journey came,
Behold how he was parted
From Manas, his companion in time.
Behold how it heaped
Toil and misery upon his head.
What is seen today is gone tomorrow;
This accursed world is filth—
Behold how false it is.
Behold how the golden throne
On which he sat slipped away.
Behold how he was parted
From the fortune in his hands.
Though wishing to die, he could not die,
Nor could he take his own life;
Along the road of death and fate
He could not walk by himself.
He could not lay his noble head,
Still living, in the grave.
Stripped of his fortune,
Parted from his peers,
His time had come to waste away,
To tear at
And pluck out this beard.
To the race he once would enter,
His time had come not to go.
Though nearing the true world,
His time had come when he could do nothing.
Parted from his pacing steed,
His time had come to trudge on foot.
Parted from his imposing presence,
His time had come to be pitied.
His sable cap became mere dust,
His suffering became idle gossip:
His time had come to decline.
Though Bakai was eighty,
He still had deeds he could perform,
And a road leading to Judgment Day;
He still had wondrous words to speak.
Though mortal, his true self remained;
If you took Bakai’s measure,
His speech was as it had been before.
Heard throughout the universe,
My father Bakai possessed great renown.
When he was born into the world,
He came alone, yet was worth a nation.
His eyes saw from afar,
His heart was utterly clear—
Gray-maned Bakai was a sage.
From amid a vast assembly,
He emerged alone to win the race.
As the people bowed and made way,
Their hearts divided in countless thoughts.
