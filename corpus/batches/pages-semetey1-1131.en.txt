Say all the Muslims
Have put on black headdresses in mourning!
Say he must gird his waist firmly;
If bloodthirsty Manas the leopard
Is truly a valiant man,
Say he must pursue the seething Chinese
All the way to Beijing!
The measured time is six days;
Let him not delay beyond six days.
Go and say they have been left needing his aid.
Say the elders led by Koshoy
Have plundered
All the meat at the feast.
Say they have exerted their grieving strength;
The mighty Koshoy and Kongurbai,
From the hands of the Muslims,
Have seized every fine racehorse,
Leaving none at all.
Say they made the palace maidens who went strolling
Into captive women.
Say the servants who cooked the meat
Beat them with fire pokers,
And the witless Black Kalmyks
Slaughtered two thousand of them.
Say Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Gathered the multitude
And held many festivities.
Without consulting Manas—
Say this was Bokmurun's appointed hour!
Without taking offense at that hour,
Say he must make the spectacle;
Dear brave Manas the leopard,
From the Black Kalmyks and Manchus,
The mere handful of Muslims—
Say he must rescue them.”
Brave Koshoy voiced his grief,
Giving six swift horses
And a palm-sized letter.
When the sun sank trembling,
When the Black Kalmyks and many Manchus
Lay gathered in a mass,
Thrown into confusion,
When they all dismounted together,
When all their chiefs
Lay there without making a sound,
Saying that no man would now travel,
The brazen Chinese
Fired their bellowing cannon.
Counting them less than his boot heel,
The Chinese lay there resounding.
Saying, “We have taken the Burut as plunder;
At the very feast we plundered the Burut
And have now put an end to them,”
They played their horns and trumpets.
With old Koshoy at their head,
I saw where they grieved;
I saw where the Black Kalmyks
And many Manchus rejoiced.
The mere handful of Muslims
Gathered together,
Saying the great Chinese would devour them on sight.
The Muslims stumbled about
And fled together,
Terrified and alarmed.
They saddled Maaniker
And put on the mail coat.
Without letting one person know,
Without making one person aware,
They mounted him on Maaniker.
Before the Black Kalmyks and Manchus,
I saw the great mass of Muslims
Cower in fear.
Sending him out like thieves,
To princely Manas the lion,
By night they sent Aydar—
I saw them send him.
With Khan Koshoy at their head,
With heroic Töshtük among them,
