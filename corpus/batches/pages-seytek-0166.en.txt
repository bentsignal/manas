His entire body was strength;
He was renowned, a pillar,
A champion and a lion.
He was a dark mountain crowned with crags,
A towering, lofty peak,
A steel bullet against the enemy,
Among all mortal sons ever born,
An undying crimson ember.
Among all the sons of men,
No champion like him had ever lived.
He was truly a servant of God,
A valiant eagle of bloodthirsty Manas,
A mighty lion’s son.
Wearing a sword without a sheath,
He roamed the whole wide world,
Broke through dead ends and opened roads,
And struck in every direction.
Hardened and sated with blood,
Among all the people
He was famed as the leopard Choro.
He was an undying beacon-fire;
Among mortal humankind
No other wrought such devastation.
Since his gray-wolf lord had died,
He had suffered bitter torment.
His two hands were gouged,
He was flayed and brought to disgrace,
His days narrowed to a handbreadth,
And all he saw was bloody misery.
The boy had inflicted horrors upon him,
His joints had gone soft and trembled,
And yellow fluid had flooded them.
“How much strength has he left?” they wondered,
And the milling crowd pondered;
All the people stood like this.
The lion Choro, son of Almambet,
The hero Choro, favored by the spirits,
Had wearied horses while seeing distant lands,
And wearied nations while seeing many peoples.
His heroism was beyond measure—
“Now take your vengeance thus!”
So spoke the hero Seytek.
The descendant of swift-footed lions
Turned his swift steed’s head,
And tested the strength
Of his mighty uncle Choro.
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Blazed like a crimson ember;
None like him had existed since creation.
Tall and broad-shouldered,
Both wrestler and lion,
No such hero comes from humankind.
Broad-breasted and wide-chested,
Handsome in form, with dark eyes,
The born champion Külchoro
Before Kyzylsart and Kara-Döö,
And many honored choros,
Set ninety mattocks
Together in a row.
Fat oozed from the hero’s thumb,
And blood came from his throat;
The bones of his arms bent,
His ribs warped apart,
And his joint bones crackled.
His broad front teeth, like a gate,
Teeth unlike those of common men,
Cracked each time he clenched them.
For the poor man favored by the spirits,
Ninety formidable mattocks
Had been set out in a row.
They witnessed the heroism
Of the poor man favored by the spirits;
The gathered people watched and proclaimed it,
Marveling at my champion.
The people cried out in an uproar,
Their clamor splitting the sky;
The great multitude acclaimed him,
And all the people marveled
At the renowned gray-wolf champion.
All his remaining strength was ready
