“Saying, ‘The Chinese raided the horses,’
How can we turn back without attacking the enemy
And show our faces to the people?
In Kakanchin's Beijing,
Even if we are on foot, my foal,
We shall fight until we die!
While this gray head blessed by Khizr remains,
How can we return to the Kyrgyz, saying,
‘We let them be slaughtered on the road’!
Let us turn and enter among the Manchu
And bring splendid slaughter!”
Your prince would rather die for honor
Than fail to go and make war
Upon Kakanchin's Beijing!
He and Khan Bakai together
Took courage, those dear men.
They placed the horseless boys before them
And led them onward;
Lion Bakai led the way,
Taking a direct road toward Beijing.
They crossed rugged ground,
Passed over high, difficult mountain saddles,
And traversed all manner of wondrous places.
Straight-flowing springs and clear water—
He did not wish to stop here.
Uncle Bakai led them onward
Through lands he himself had seen,
Past valleys with springs and streams,
And over rugged open plains—
Taking a direct path across the open plain,
Khan Bakai went
To the ridge of Kara-Kyr,
Along the Üch-Karkyra.
At Kapka-Tash by the Sary-Jaz,
He halted at Kapka-Tash.
His weary heart distressed,
Your dear uncle Bakai
Lamented the days of old!
The reason for his lament was this:
Had Bakai not fought enemies from childhood
And grown accustomed to war?
The marshy ground of Kara-Kyya—
My dear foals,
Yesterday, while lion Manas was alive,
This was where the great host encamped!
Thick with feather grass and without stones,
They called it the Sary-Jaz valley.
If you ask about this place,
It was where horses fattened and grew full;
While lion Manas was alive,
This was where six hundred mares were slaughtered
To feed the army!
Two men shared each portion,
Carving off strips of rich horse-rib fat;
This was a place of abundant delights!
While my thoroughbred Manas was alive,
Horse fat flowed murmuring
Down along the spring!
When we journeyed to Kakan,
This was where the great host
Made merry after wreaking turmoil!
While that Manas was alive,
While our fortune remained whole,
This accursed Bakai did not die
And did not behold judgment!
When the past comes to mind,
My heart is painfully torn;
This accursed, barren world
Seems to have deceived Bakai
As the whim took it!
My heart is grieved, my liver burns;
My companion Manas,
Who knew my inmost secrets, is gone!
Parted from his Manas,
Bakai has not a thread of fortune!
Was it not Bakai who cast
The world behind him?
