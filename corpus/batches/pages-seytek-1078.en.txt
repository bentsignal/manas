And marveled at brave Seytek.
Unable to withstand his fury,
With no soul able to resist his courage,
The guards dared not face him;
They could not come near
The face of valiant Seytek, the gray-maned hero.
His terrible presence threw every soul into panic.
Upon two thoroughbred steeds,
Seytek and Akjoltoy
Made their horses trample
The splendid carpets spread before them.
The six grooms who tended the horses
And the companions beside them
Were tigers, each worth a thousand men.
By the splendor of their array,
They were all keen-eyed warriors
Fit to subdue the whole world.
Each bore a spear,
And if you looked at some of them,
They wore unsheathed swords at their sides.
The tigers formed a ring,
All keen-eyed warrior companions,
Men who would not flee
When black muskets cracked.
If an overwhelming enemy host came,
They were warriors who never panicked.
When a fierce gun barked,
They served without flinching.
If some other disaster came,
If their frail lives were endangered,
They stood beneath the banner.
They were steadfast gray-maned heroes,
Fierce in presence and mighty in wrath,
Who laid every opponent low.
The guards beheld their terrible presence,
And from the force of their wrath
Several met their doom and died,
Merely on seeing the gray-maned hero’s form.
Their minds reeled.
The guards standing watch
Ran about in confusion,
Despairing of their precious lives,
Losing their senses and growing weak,
Overwhelmed by his terrible presence.
They rushed about in panic
And beat the great war drum.
To the hapless fairy maiden,
Bewildered and nearly senseless,
They now brought the news:
“I have seen men of surpassing splendor;
I could not go and face them—
Every one is a champion, a crimson flame.
If roused, they could tear the soul from the body,
And if they drew a breath, it seemed
They could suck down all eighteen thousand worlds.
I saw a lion of magnificent stature,
A hero beyond all measure.
I despaired when I saw his terrible presence;
All the guards were thrown into turmoil,
And his true courage was revealed.
Some champions could not endure it,
And their appointed death overtook them.
They ride steeds swift as bullets,
And every one bears a steel spear.
They wear black hats as broad as cauldrons,
And look like angels of death.
No mortal dares approach them,
Yet they do not torment the world without cause.
But any mortal who provokes them
Does not escape death alive.”
The prudent maiden Külayim,
Her envoy at her side,
Who lived and rose together with her
And devised wise counsel for her,
Her most trusted attendant,
The strongest in finding a way,
Upon hearing this news,
Sprang up and darted like a doe,
Showing her graceful skill,
