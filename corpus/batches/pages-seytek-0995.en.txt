They pitched a royal encampment,
With kumiss by day and maidens by night,
And held revels through the night.
That was a land of summer pastures,
And he was the lion who brought his people joy.
The people’s faces brightened;
After suffering great humiliation,
Being held as though doomed to misery,
And grieving without consolation,
They were delivered from Sarybai.
All the assembled people—
Their venerable elders,
Their sages and eloquent speakers,
And their many capable men—
Conceived a radiant plan
And held a feast in honor
Of Kuyaly and the hero Seytek.
“That infidel Sarybai
Plundered brides at his pleasure,” they said.
When you looked to the farthest reaches,
The clouds had vanished,
The night was departing and dawn breaking.
He had no voice left to utter a sound,
No hand left to stretch forth.
“We have given Sarybai his due,” they said;
He had vanished from this world.
“God has granted what we desired,” they said,
And the blessed Night of Power descended.
Allah Most High, the one God,
Had now granted it to them.
The oppressor of the people was subdued,
Light poured down upon mankind,
And the heavens shone with radiance.
The grievous calamity and great turmoil
Subsided when Sarybai died.
Ruinous fury disappeared,
The people prospered in exultation,
And order among the folk was restored.
Their thoughts turned toward progress,
And the times encountered prosperity.
They scattered his ashes to the sky,
And extinguished the scattered brands.
From this world they cleansed away
The infidel entirely.
Their great desire was fulfilled,
And death came to the great villain.
Goodness entered the world;
The incurable sickness that had struck,
The myriad arrows lodged in their ribs,
Was healed and subsided.
Resentful brows relaxed,
Their wretched anguish abated,
And their moon-bright faces cleared.
The people clamored and held a feast;
The wicked swine was gone,
And order among the people was restored.
Sarybai had waged war
For many months and many years,
And they had suffered, held in misery.
On that day the multitude,
From the dire and savage affliction,
Through the support of Seytek
And the heroism of Kuyaly,
Were delivered from Sarybai.
The people settled down in peace,
Lying at ease upon their well-fed flanks.
They migrated freely to mountain pastures,
And settled upon the foothills.
They lived according to their will,
Hunting deer whenever they grew bored.
When the multitude was not weary,
They played freely together,
Rode young horses and flew hunting birds,
Marked out the ordo field and cast their knucklebones,
And won surpassing honor.
Amid grand and lively festivities,
They raced horses and young steeds,
Along the banks of broad Talas.
