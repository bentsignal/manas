The one who reads the stars in the heavens—
Could it happen that
The old scourge Bakai is his companion?
May they not reach the very end together,
May they not drive away entirely
Every last thoroughbred horse.
Since I am Karagul,
I shall unleash my wrath fiercely.
The thoroughbreds ridden into battle—
I shall cut them off and keep every one,
Then I shall address my words
To the foolish son of Manas.
There is no boat on the black water;
While Algara stands beneath me,
There is no creature who can oppose me.
I shall avenge myself for all the past.
Saying, "I put an end
To the infidel son of the bloodthirsty man,"
To the khan of Kakan,
Before Khan Kongurbai,
I shall go to claim my glad tidings.
If he grants it to me,
I myself shall take the khanship of Chet-Beijing.
Today I have no equal upon the open ridge;
While I still have my falcon's head,
There is no death for wretched me!"
So saying, Karagul
Broke away along the narrow track,
And when he reached the narrow track,
His aspect appeared like a tiger's.
"If he does not consent to these words of mine,
If this year that swine does not give me
The khanship of Chet-Beijing,
I have rendered much service already—
Why should I go on living here?
I shall start a war and stir up strife,
And go over to the Burut.
I shall gather up the credit for my valor,
Saying, 'This here is my valor,'
I shall set it before the eyes
Of the bastard Kalcha.
If the fools simply grant it,
At the very least, I shall ask for
The khanship of the Torgout.
While I possess this valor,
Why should I herd horses?
I shall clash with the Kalcha;
If he does not recognize this service of mine,
I shall go wherever my face is turned!"
Karagul swelled with menace,
And this man gave a battle cry.
His courage was strong, his venom fierce—
Give up all hope in this infidel.
Heaping up dust as high as a mountain,
He made Algara bound onward,
Toward the spur of a towering mountain,
Toward a jutting headland.
Before one could say a word,
In the time it takes to open and close the eyes,
He arrived swifter than an arrow.
Flying like a cloud, Algara
Among the horses before him
Plunged straight in.
All the stallions and geldings
Fled into the herd in agitation,
And every thoroughbred horse
He took, striking them into line.
"Where is my own excited herd,
May it burn, going?" he cried.
Enraged and grief-stricken,
Karagul rode ahead,
Leaving not a single one behind;
Every choice swift horse in the valley
Karagul drove into confusion.
Along the separated foothill road
Karagul went,
Cutting the thoroughbreds from the herd,
Karagul drove them on.
