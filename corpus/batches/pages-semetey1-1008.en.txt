Manas did not give him to those who asked;
Far from giving Buurul away,
He let no ritually impure person ride him.
He curved his neck like an argali;
If game a rope-length high appeared,
He leaped without fail.
Once his heroic quality was known,
Offering white ingots and costly cloth,
Khans from every land asked for him.
Unlike every other beast,
Buurul possessed wings;
Which of his feats shall we tell?
If the road was forty days long,
He would not so much as sip water;
If there was a dangerous deep pit,
He never stepped astray and fell.
Kulchoro drove Buurul standing in the line,
Making him leap with a roar.
God creates every living soul;
That young man Kulchoro
Was traveling onward to Urgench.
Kulchoro hurried breathlessly;
Early words seemed late,
So eager was he to arrive early,
Watching with all four eyes.
When he came out upon the pass,
Ask nothing of the hero's bearing.
Toward spirit-like Buurul,
The black-jawed white whip
He took up and swung;
Kulchoro made it ring aloud,
Striking the horse's ankle.
When the whip struck without warning,
For a target-shot's distance Buurul
Dragged his feet, unable to walk;
Once he had passed the target-shot's distance,
He revealed that he was a courser,
Set his feet against the ground,
And no wind could match his trot.
No livestock could match his walk.
When he ran down a slope,
His forelegs extended like a roe deer's;
When he began to climb,
His mane and tail streamed wide.
When broken, uneven ground appeared,
His feet seemed not to touch the earth;
When he began to climb,
No one could discern
Which gait the imperious courser used.
He flattened his ears like a kulan
And churned the earth like a whirlwind.
When rough and rolling ground appeared,
A flood of dust followed behind him;
The world seemed uprooted and wandering,
And your companion upon his back
Marveled whether the imperious courser
Had taken flight.
His soles clattered against the earth;
The black-steel bit
Cracked against his throat.
The places over which he passed
Were hollowed out like kilns;
Where his sliding hoof struck,
The earth was cut into embankments.
The ground crumbled beneath his steps,
And clods as large as a house
Shrieked above the head
Of the young man whose road lay hard.
Steam issuing from his nostrils
Billowed like the smoke of an army.
Stones struck squarely by his hooves
Split apart with ringing cracks,
And shattered with shrill cries.
Lengthening his stride like an argali,
Ah, that creature, the horse Buurul,
Reddened at the eyes like a ram,
Flung his forelock toward the sky,
Hammered his hooves into the earth,
And the ground wheeled in the mirage,
