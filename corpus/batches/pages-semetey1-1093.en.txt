I shall levy a tax upon every hearth;
I shall discipline and beat them all,
And gather everything, leaving nothing.
Uncle Koshoy, hear my words!
Among the sheep is a sacred he-goat
That never stirs from the flock.
Maintained by the treasury,
In my dear father's palace
Are two hundred pampered maidens.
Their bodies are richly adorned;
I intend to stake them all
As prizes."
Then, as though pierced in a gland,
Hero Koshoy sprang up:
"My child, he who gives a feast incurs blame;
He who gives a feast incurs sin.
Steel will strike on the descent,
And we Kyrgyz will be few.
The black Chinese and Manchu people
Will be enemies to our people.
There is the teeming black Chinese
People of Kakan-Chyn;
If they attack, they have the custom
Of drinking Kyrgyz blood.
There is a crooked people;
To straighten their perversity,
There is the hellish wolf Zholoy.
There are black Chinese and many Manchus,
Infidels seething in multitudes;
Over all of them
There is the hero Khan Kongurbay.
When his wrath seizes him,
Might he not unleash havoc?
The reason he would unleash havoc is this:
Bloodthirsty Manas killed
His father, Khan Alööke.
Might he not begin battle at the feast
And inflict a single calamity?
There is the capable Boroonchu,
Who went and joined the Kakan;
And Bozkertik of Tokushker—
Those three are brothers.
Seeking to attack
The tiger, bloodthirsty Manas,
They too lost their father.
The steadfast black Chinese
Have an unending vengeance.
If you summon the peoples
To your khan-father Kökötöy's feast,
They have lions who will fight us.
There is the elder Khan Kökötöy;
Here are all
The Kyrgyz khans' sons.
When Karakan arose from the Kyrgyz,
Reigned as khan, and died,
When he passed to the other world,
All eight of Karakan's sons
Were left powerless and bewildered,
Left as crawling children.
The struggle for the khanate is a hard affair.
When they brought havoc upon
These many Kyrgyz people
And scattered those who resisted,
When the Chinese day dawned,
They pursued with gunfire
Those who resisted.
When the fighting people
Died enduring humiliation,
Whoever fell into the Chinese hands—
They drove one away to a hero
And cast another into a ravine.
They drove one into captivity
And another to Altay.
Zhakyp was exiled to Altay;
The lion Manas, the bloodthirsty beast,
Was born while they wandered in exile.
