Sarybay struck with his spear.
The accursed one’s nature was thus:
His fury kept mounting.
In the blink of an eye,
Before a word could be uttered,
He kept changing form.
Beyond human comprehension,
He was gaining the upper hand.
Warriors and whole armies
He kept overwhelming.
Overturning valleys and mountains,
The infidel’s furious demon
Kept surging forth.
A thousand times worse than before,
The rabid dog’s great fury
Kept overflowing.
Raging in a thousand forms,
As though preparing to drain
The whole world dry,
Such was Sarybay’s nature.
Whoever witnessed his warrior’s might
Despaired of his precious life,
Feeling the age close tightly in,
His hair and beard bristling,
His burning eyes blazed,
As Sarybay unleashed his fiercest rage.
Külchoro, born of noble Kül,
With Külchoro at their head,
He shook off and scattered
A great host of Kül’s people.
He began speaking in wrath,
And his words, more deadly than poison,
Struck them down and destroyed them.
Sarybay stood there, raging,
And addressed the vast host:
“When my wrath rises and I rear up,
I shall unleash a savage battle.
I shall spare none I can seize,
Not leave a spoonful of blood,
But slaughter every last one;
He too is a hero born apart.
Külchoro is indeed a valiant man.”
Sparing neither life,
They fought in desperate fury.
The sprawling battlefield filled with standards,
And an uproar split the sky,
As they grappled on and on.
The hero Sarybay and Külchoro
Kept fighting on and on.
Sarybay, son of the Monster,
Was one no mortal could overcome,
A swine that smeared the earth,
Whom no living man could withstand.
He seemed as vast as a gorge,
As though he would swallow any soul he met.
When he came face-to-face,
It was as if he demanded, “Who are you?”
As though he would seize every soul he met,
Suck it dry, and devour it.
Like a fiend who brought nations to ruin,
Like a champion who ruled the world.
He roared, looming huge,
And bristled in anger,
Ringing out without concern.
Though struck by spear and axe,
He reckoned the gray wolf favored by fortune
No greater than his ankle.
With every blow, Sarybay
Cracked like lightning,
Clashing with mounting courage,
Bearing the standard across the dark battlefield,
His eyes blazing like a hungry wolf’s.
He girded himself and strained,
Swaying as though about to strike,
Without sorrow or distress,
Without even knitting his brow,
Never crying, “Alas, I am slain!”
Never fearing death,
He barred the road of escape,
Closing it off as he advanced.
