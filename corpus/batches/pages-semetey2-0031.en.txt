“Have you heard of the deed
Of the man who came on patrol and split my head?”
Turning wrathful and transformed,
Ready to tear Karagul apart if he caught him,
He spoke threats against him.
Breaking the ground beneath his tread,
The noble lord mounted Buurul bare-backed
And raced away.
Such was the nature of Buurul:
His destiny was to be ridden into battle;
That bastard horse Buurul
Was Semetey’s wing.
It was his custom to chase down
An enemy who fled before him.
There was no hillock as large as an ankle-bone,
No hollow as broad as a saddlecloth,
No furrow touched by a plow,
No burrow dug by a marmot;
Whenever he came upon such terrain,
It was his custom to run all the harder.
If the desert was forty days across,
If the country was harsh and difficult,
It was his custom to endure.
When he saw the hero’s bearing
And reached the rolling ridges,
Buurul lengthened his stride;
His legs drove down like an argali’s.
When he began to climb uphill,
His mane and tail spread wide.
Kongurbai’s Algara
Thundered his hooves upon the ground;
In Semetey’s horse Buurul’s throat,
The black-steel bit
Clattered against his palate.
When they entered the downslope,
Kongurbai’s Algara
Ran far ahead, shimmering like a mirage.
When they began to climb uphill,
Semetey’s Buurul
Closed upon his heels and hemmed him in.
Buurul ran in haste;
When Karagul, son of Katal,
Saw how he ran,
“What has happened to that dog?” he said.
Despairing of his splendid life,
He crossed rough, hummocked ground,
Passed over rugged, rolling ridges,
And the mighty man fled before him.
Such was the precious sight:
The two went like bride and groom.
They traveled on through haze
Over rugged ridges;
The coursers dug up
The black earth as they went.
Algara and Karagul
Hurried onward.
They traveled through a hazy land
Beyond the reach of sight.
They traveled through the desert,
Ending in a dim and hazy point.
They went along the far side
Of rugged ridges
And level uplands beyond sight.
Such was the nature of the courser;
Karagul, who had deceived the hero,
Put his trust in his racer
And fled in boundless haste.
Where Algara’s and Taibuurul’s
Hooves struck squarely,
The ground was cut like a great canal;
Their precisely planted tracks
Were carved like the hull of a ship.
Such was the nature of the coursers:
Their mouths gaped like saws,
And blood-mingled white foam
From the bereft, ill-fated horse
Scattered across his breast.
