He sniffed him deeply, again and again,
From my father who passed before,
He sniffed him, saying, “You remain as my inheritance,”
And while Seytek stood weeping,
Aichürök came wailing bitterly.
When she saw the dapple-gray steed,
Your ill-fated noble sister-in-law
Clawed her face and shrieked:
“His horse remains, but he is gone,
My noble-hearted lord is nowhere to be seen,
Is this what you are, filthy world?”
So Aichürök lamented,
“My precious one’s noble steed,
Would not tire when ridden on patrol,
Would not weary after six months’ riding,
Until he reached sixty years of age
His fangs would not jut out, nor would he grow old,
He would not falter when ridden into combat.
He would not sicken when ridden with the baggage train;
But you are here, and the hero is gone.
When I think of it, what dreadful misery!
My ruined lord, come and take him!”
So Aichürök
Raised her voice in a piercing cry,
And at her white face, fair as a plane tree,
She tore and tore;
Dark blood flowed where she clawed,
And all strength left your sister-in-law.
Her gray pacer went ambling on,
While the blood streaming down both cheeks
Flowed over the gray pacer’s mane,
Running down a whip-length.
Overcome by grievous affliction,
She threw herself down when she saw him.
Shrieking at the sight of Buurul,
Poor Aichürök
Fell down as though dead.
She had settled at the cliffside camp,
Bereft of her noble-hearted lord,
And had come to bear a wretched name.
Before my lion-hearted father,
If only I might die as a sacrifice!
When even a beast grieves like this,
I am human—I am his son;
How can I endure it,
When the beast comes neighing,
Lamenting thunderously beneath me?
The beast spoke a word
That said, “Let my noble father not be dead.”
It drove a thought into my breast:
“If my lion-hearted father were alive,
Leading the way and fighting foes,
If my bloodthirsty father were here,
I would don my chain-mail cap
And strike the enemy head-on.
With my noble, lion-hearted father,
I did not die as his companion;
I did not crush the approaching enemy,
Nor tear them limb from limb.
My precious heroic father’s
Robe worn on ceremonial occasions—
I did not put on that bulletproof coat.
If my dear father were alive,
I would ask for and mount my Buurul,
The steed no bullet could reach,
And charge the foe with a battle cry.
With my lion-hearted father,
I never rode as his companion.
I am left an ill-fated orphan;
My leopard of a father, come and take me.
Your beast stands neighing,
Your orphan stands crying aloud.
My father is gone—what cruel fate!
My devastated father, to whom all twelve
Pledged their united allegiance, come and take me!
Saying, “My sole beast left from the host,”
And, “Though a beast, he is my very soul,”
He pressed his face to the horse’s chest,
Grasped its forelock and sniffed it sorrowfully,
And clasped the noble steed in his arms.
