My magnanimous uncle, heed my words.
Understand what I am saying;
Uncle of my dark day, heed my words!
I shall make my valor known.
Alööke’s Kongurbay
Has a black horse, that infidel’s mount,
With wings upon its flanks;
Wherever ear has heard or eye has seen,
It races in a frenzy across the earth—
Such has ever been its way.
I shall make my heroism known
And bring that black horse into our hands.
The forty tribes of China are a mighty realm,
A host as countless as black worms.
Do not let the Chinese recover;
Even if they bow like brides,
Do not let them trick you out of the black horse again.
They have marksmen and hosts like floods;
If they engulf us in their wrath,
Remember that ever since your father Manas,
Kongurbay has always
Made deceit his way.
I pretended to be Chinese
And practiced deceit beyond all measure.
Thus shall I bring the haughty Kalcha
Into our hands.
My valiant, lionhearted uncle,
When you confront him, strike hard;
Set things right without being deceived.
The broad Keng-Kol plain and Suusamyr
Lie on the road Kongurbay once took;
It was the army that kept deceiving
Our magnanimous father Manas.
Ever since Bedel, deception
Has been that swine’s way.
He was the son of a mighty sorcerer,
A man unlike other mortals,
A lion whose fame
Spread throughout the world.
My princely uncle, hear this from me;
Hear the cunning deed I mean to do!
I shall saddle Buurul properly.
Is any oath owed to that enemy?
I shall bow down like the Chinese.
When they seize their idol
And turn me against the foe,
Do not surge forward, uncle, thinking,
“The Chinese have broken and fled.”
Do not cast a turmoil no man could endure
Upon Külchoro alone.
Do not think, “He has gone
Into the midst of the stinking Manchus,
And worships as his god
The bronze idol
They fashioned with their own hands.”
Do not mourn my place in the ranks.
Do not rage and bristle,
My lionhearted uncle and sultan,
And make my misery boil over.
Among that Khagan’s forty khans,
I shall spill their blood and crush them
And stage a spectacle.
Understand what I have said:
To deceive and turn the Chinese,
My precious uncle, heed my words.
If you give me Buurul, I shall mount him
And move among them,
Making them call me khan, not slave.
The heirloom musket left by your father—
Now the enemy we must shoot has appeared.
If you give me Akkelte, I shall take it,
Sling it over my broad shoulder,
And then go to the Kalcha.
Making them say, ‘He surpasses his father Manas,’
I shall thus seize the hearts
Of Beijing and China’s forty tribes.
As though some great lord had arrived,
Let the Chinese swarm out and run;
