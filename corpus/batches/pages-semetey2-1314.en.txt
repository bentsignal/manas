Calling quickly upon Baabedin,
The woman glowed with delight:
“My khan Kanchoro will take me!”
All her sorrow disappeared,
All her grief was mended;
The woman brightened and recovered,
All her resentment dissolved,
Her burning heart grew calm.
“Yesterday, while the bloodthirsty lord still lived,
I knew much anguish;
Kanchoro and I
Would whisper together.
We had pledged
To camp freely on the ridge
And become husband and wife.”
The spoiled woman Chachykey—
So much was the shameless one esteemed;
The scoundrel had attained
Her promise and desire.
“Take gold for your glad tidings,
Gold scattered in fist-sized pieces.”
She rejoiced when her lord died—
Such was Chachykey’s nature.
Her wickedness stood revealed;
The woman rejoiced
That she would marry Kanchoro.
That woman Chachykey,
That swaggering infidel,
Slaughtered ninety colts for her feast
And set her wealth against the cost.
She let the Jediger overrun
Semetey’s great stronghold;
She had bright gold and white silver
Scattered in lavish handfuls,
And gathered khojas and mullahs.
That woman Chachykey
Had Kanchoro cut up the meat;
She made the splendid entertainment
Surpass that of all the people.
As for saintly old Bakay,
She had eighty fearsome wrestlers
Drag him about as their kökbörü carcass,
Neither killing nor losing him.
Though not eighty but a thousand
Used brave Bakay for kökbörü,
Your khanly uncle would not yield.
From ancient times until now,
That uncle had endured torment.
All his coat was torn,
All his beard was plucked,
All his armor was stripped,
And every whisker of your aged uncle Bakay
Was torn from his face.
As the wrestlers mauled him,
Behold your ancestor like Bakay,
Your prophet-like uncle,
Parted from those who loved him;
Behold how he was humiliated.
His swan-white beard turned pale;
Your ancestor stood withered.
Bereft of those who loved him,
Behold what that grave-robbing wretch
Did to your father.
Tears from both eyes spread in ten streams;
Unable to die though wishing for death,
Your ancestor went about weeping.
Deprived of those who would sustain him,
Your poor uncle Bakay—
Behold how he wailed.
His face wrinkled and wasted;
Tears flowing from both eyes
Poured across his breast.
The broad world became narrow;
Majestic Bakay became old and gray.
What is seen today is gone tomorrow—
Such filth this world proves to be!
His insides burned, his heart was gone;
He had no strength to exchange blows.
