I saw where they panicked.
Infidels countless as hairs on a cow,
Swarming like ants—
I saw the ground they covered.
Their great city is called Beijing;
The Chinese are a people of forty khans.
At Kökötöy's memorial feast,
I cast my eye over the multitude
And took the measure of their numbers.
My side-locks were upon my head,
And I myself was six years old.
They mounted me on a dappled bay pacer,
Dressed me in a robe of silk,
And led little me pounding along
To the feast.
The people filled the land and seethed;
Infidels and Muslims
Crossed paths and swarmed
Along the banks of Karkyra,
At Khan Kökötöy's feast,
Below Aygyr-Zhal,
In the hollow of Ükürchü,
Along Üch-Kapkak,
Before the assembled people,
At Khan Kökötöy's memorial feast.
The people filled the land in crowds;
Dust flew into the sky.
When I looked over the people,
When I looked toward sunset,
The seething multitude
Was on the heights of Kyzyl-Kyya.
When I looked toward sunrise,
The seething multitude
Was along Chöl-Adyr.
In the hollows of Charyn and Ketmen,
Those of the army who could not fit
Were on the face of Chong-Kegen.
The great army on the rearward side
Was at the upper edge of Teek.
Those of the great army who could not fit
Were among Karkyra's pines,
Upon the pine-clad slopes.
The southern side of the gathering
Was along Aygay-Tash.
Then young Aydar with flowing forelocks,
Wearing a coat like mail,
Riding a horse like Maaniker,
Skimmed over the face of the earth.
Among every son of man who had heard,
He visited each without omission.
He traveled by day and night,
Went far without sparing his horse,
Never stopping overnight on the way,
Never resting or catching his breath.
He reached Talas
And now prepared to deliver the message
To princely Manas the leopard.
Young Aydar with flowing forelocks
Had circled and traveled
The four corners of the whole world;
He was the man who carried the news.
Khan Manas had been left without word;
The burden weighed upon his mortal soul,
And now the messenger arrived.
Manas had been left without news
Because, drunk on his countless livestock,
Ill-fated Bokmurun
Had sent him no message.
Kökötöy's arrogant son Bokmurun,
When Khan Kökötöy died,
Had hastened to gather a multitude.
Receiving no news and hearing no account,
Khan Manas remained offended.
His eyes blazed like fire;
If you had seen the leopard,
His thirst for blood had seized him.
In all the eighteen thousand worlds,
For righteous, bloodthirsty Manas—
