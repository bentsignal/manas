Had the tail feathers of a parrot
Set in ninety different arrangements
Upon her blue lynx-fur hat.
Behold the wisdom of Churok,
And the turmoil so openly born.
Amid thirty daughters-in-law and thirty maidens
Stood Aychurok;
Beside the great flowing river
They gathered and began their games.
When your great lord, fierce by birth,
Saw Aychurok,
He nearly dropped dead.
Taking his spyglass in his hand,
From the ridge of Acha-Tash
He shouted in fury,
Calling by name for his young men,
Kulchoro and Kanchoro.
That poor lion who could not be stirred
Was now a poor hero ready to move.
When they heard his shout,
Kulchoro and Kanchoro
Bent their knees toward the ground.
Without delay, to learn the news,
And to reach their lord,
They put on their arms and armor.
Flying like arrows, the young men
Made ready their several mounts.
The horse one companion rode
Was Kokburchak of Kobosh Khan;
The horse the other companion rode
Was Abyke's Aktelki.
They vaulted on like shepherds,
And rode before the face
Of the wrathful Bloodthirsty One.
Racing until they drew even,
The young men conferred:
"Our khan-slaying elder is a tiger;
His cry rang out loudly.
His cry was terribly harsh
And seemed to devour our ears and noses.
There is haste and alarm
In our elder's voice.
Is there a deeply dug trench?
Is there an army raising an uproar?
Is there a trench dug deep?
Has some formidable enemy
Surrounded him on every side?
Our lord's voice rang loud.
Has something borne down upon his head?
Has our gray-wolf lord taken fright?
If an unceasing clamor has arisen,
If a merciless uproar has broken out,
If a grim, deep-browed foe appears,
If an enemy comes crying havoc,
Let us reach the hero
And crush the danger utterly;
Let us free and recover
The hero-born gray wolf!
If the enemy has come from Mecca,
Let us choose and mount the finest coursers,
Scatter all of them,
And drive them toward their homeland.
If the enemy has come from China,
Let us drive them back into Kakan."
Having settled it thus, the young men
Raced toward their elder;
Their two stirrups struck together.
Spurring in breathless haste,
They turned their horses' heads.
When they reached their elder,
They took their spears in hand;
The companions of fearsome bearing
Stood bristling and alert.
When the young men arrived and stood there,
The young lord Semetey,
As though his fierce mother Kanykey
Had given birth to a man indeed,
Smiled and beamed broadly,
