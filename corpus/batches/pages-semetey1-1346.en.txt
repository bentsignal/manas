Beijing, where no frail old man can tread—
Disgrace if you let yourself be defeated there.
You wild youth who takes refuge,
Your life is entrusted to God!
Against the Chinese who throng the earth,
You have set out alone on holy war;
Upon the toilsome Black Chinese,
You intend to pursue the blood-price.
May your news come from the campaign,
May your journey lead back from Beijing;
A hero’s work is risk itself—
You are going forth to meet it!
The Kakanchin is the foe you will fight;
God knows whether you will die,
God knows whether you will return—
You are going forth to die!
You have begun the journey to Beijing,
Leaving the many Argyn and Kyrgyz people,
Leaving your camp desolate!
They say Beijing is a distant land,
One no one has seen from afar;
For a traveler who goes without tiring,
They say the road is six months long.
If one circles Great Beijing,
It has a lake that takes long to traverse;
If one goes straight, some roads
Cross a desert forty days wide.
Beijing is no cheap and easy land,
No people of Andizhan
To be taken and left gaping.
My beloved one who gives me comfort,
Go safely and return unharmed;
Come back quickly and do not make us pine!
Hero Bölökbai said these words,
Then all the equipment from the treasury
He gave and entrusted to him;
The infidel spoke in lament,
And every weapon of war
He entrusted to Semetey.
Bölökbai, the master smith,
Was the sheath to his sword;
To my father, gone into eternity,
He truly had been a support at the waist.
The dear hero Bölökbai, master smith,
From the treasury palace
Brought out the equipment.
Among all that equipment,
The finest was Akkelte.
When this gun was slung on,
It was a mirror upon the shoulder;
When one entered battle with the foe,
It was a shield for the entrusted life.
Its straight shooting was proven before the people,
An inheritance remaining
From your father, the bloodthirsty leopard.
Almabash, surpassingly sharp,
Its bullet did not waver at long range;
It made no distinction between far and near.
If you must shoot at the Chinese,
This is the treasure left by your father!
Its lamb-liver-shaped core was set firm;
This was the sharp weapon that never shifted.
When one stared and took aim,
Dust rose from the place aimed at;
Among enemies who heard its voice,
Reason fled and life departed.
From my noble hero’s palace,
Yesterday, when the villains seized it,
A clumsy dog who knew no secret
Had taken my champion’s gun
And treated it as an outcast.
Every one of its riflings was ruined;
The living gun, that wondrous creature,
Had gone into mourning.
Saying, “It will not take fire when I shoot,”
The scoundrels—damn your father’s grave—
Gripped the gun by its muzzle
And smashed it against a stone.
