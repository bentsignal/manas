We shall take our revenge without letting it pass.
Akjoltoy, your name rhymes with Seytek’s;
If we camp as one people and become friends,
If angered, let us strike the foe,
And dwell in Ala-Too, caring for the people.
Let us make roads where there are none,
And against the threatening enemy
Raise such turmoil as to halt them,
Charging and striking them down.
If we unite in one place,
Can any eager enemy defeat us?
Would an enemy approach our borders?
Would warriors dare assail us,
Or lay their cold hands upon us?
This is what I say—understand it;
Akjoltoy, heed my fierce words.
Here is Ala-Too, your sustenance;
Here am I, lion Seytek;
If you are burdened, here is one to ease your sorrow;
Here is your steed—the Kyrgyz people.
Father, warrior Akjoltoy,
Listen carefully to my words.
Valiant hero of beks and khans,
The place that has long troubled me—
Where did you find, O leopard,
The daughter of a peri who is my equal?
That hapless peri-born maiden—
Who is the man she has set her heart upon,
And what land does she inhabit?
Who are the people who will arrange
For me to reach her and become her husband?
My valiant mother Aichürök
Once spoke these words distinctly.
The bride I am destined to take
Would be the purest of peri maidens,
The foremost among all women,
Wearing a wondrous flowing gown—
She described her thus.
“She has a radiance like moonlight;
She is unlike all other mortals,” she said.
If what she told me is true,
If the maiden is indeed wondrous,
If that serene poor soul
Truly possesses the nature of a peri,
If she is a genuine peri maiden,
If she firmly undertakes the task,
Accepting whatever my appearance, good or bad,
And never wounding my heart,
If she makes my dwelling a guarded haven,
And among all who hear and see,
Spreads my fame among the people,
And makes my settlement a thousand strong,
If she does not oppose my word,
Nor bring sorrow upon me,
If she keeps my city flourishing
And continually spreads my renown”—
When Seytek had spoken thus,
Akjoltoy paused gravely, then spoke softly,
And the warrior Akjoltoy began his speech,
A man whose eyes discerned things from afar,
A sage who had witnessed much:
“Understand these blessed words;
My precious fierce one, heed my speech.
Whoever has seen the nature
Of that hapless peri-born maiden longs for her.
Unless he were the son of a mighty hero,
Who could ever win such a maiden?
Eager to become your bride,
Enamored of your face,
And yearning for you yourself,
Even for the traces of your passing,
She donned armor woven of iron,
Gathered a thousand peri warriors,
And because of what had happened,
All for the sake of the valiant gray wolf,
She shattered a mighty ruler’s fortune,
Had his champions slaughtered,
And ended his wretched life;
The ill-fated crowned ruler
She broke and crushed like a felt cap.
