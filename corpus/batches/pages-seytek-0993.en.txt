You went to your beloved, you say—
With your imperious battle cry,
I will snuff you out like a dying fire,
Leaving not a single root,
I will tear you out root and all,
And bury you before you sprout.
If you rave, you madman,
I will break your ribs.
If you wriggle without cause,
I will spill your blood like water.
Whatever the hour may bring,
You never cease your defiance.
You fawning madman,
You shameless swaggerer,
Straining upon the spearpoint,
You bristled in fury.
I will bring your death upon you,
Extinguish your burning soul,
And send you from this world.
I will strike you down in battle,
And settle the true account
Of this crackling fool.
You dog of a wolf, Sarybai,
Even a dog is far above you;
Even a dog answers when called.
You are a bird, Sarybai,
Even a bird is far above you;
Even a bird answers a whistle.
You heed no spoken word,
Nor do you go against distant foes.
Brisling in fury,
You never cease your defiance!”
So saying, Kuyaly
Gripped the tempered spear in her hand
And Sarybai upon its point
She shook and violently flung.
Your sin is upon your own head,
Your deeds fall upon your dark crown.
The bare-shafted tempered spear
She clutched firmly,
While Sarybai hung above
And she swung him to and fro.
Thus Sarybai
Was split in two upon the spear
And despaired of his wretched life.
He lost all warrior courage;
His shattered body was hurled,
His backbone split in two,
And jutting from the other side
The tempered spearpoint appeared.
Kuyaly was truly adroit;
She readied the tempered spear,
Rent wide his dark liver,
And split his heart in two.
His liver was sliced like cheese,
And the cur knew death had come.
His courage fled, his strength was gone,
And Sarybai’s blood poured out.
His life departed with his strength;
She stretched him upon the spearpoint,
Overturned him and cast him down,
And to the true abode whence none return
Sarybai had gone.
At the hands of the maiden wrestler
The swine’s appointed death
Had come in just this way.
Sarybai lay dead,
Yet seemed as though the dead man
Might revive and rise again.
They had clashed with thunderous blows,
As though to begin the fight anew.
It seemed the departed man
Might revive and rise again.
As Sarybai lay dead,
Drenched in crimson blood,
They advanced in their turn
And plundered all his wealth.
His gold and possessions
The teeming people seized as spoil,
Precious jewels of red gold—
