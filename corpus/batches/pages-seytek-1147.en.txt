The peerless steed proved a true companion,
Showing the worth of comradeship.
The wounded Akjoltoi
From the medicine for every need
Took two cow-head-sized bowls of black remedy,
And when he crushed and drank them both,
Life stirred visibly within him.
Through his twelve limbs and thousand veins
The medicine coursed everywhere;
His wound closed without pain,
His eyes, sealed like paste, opened,
His vigor returned and strength filled him,
Energy surged and power blazed;
For the gray-wolf hero Joltoi
Good fortune gathered upon his brow.
It was as though he roamed Ala-Too,
With games by day and delight by night;
Life and vigor surged within him,
His belly was full and his blood replenished.
The poor hero could no longer stand watching.
Without setting foot in the iron-soled stirrups,
He leapt astride the thoroughbred Kulunsur,
Mounting him in a single bound.
Embers could not pierce it when tamarisk burned,
Bullets could not pierce it when cannon fired,
Blades could not pierce it in close combat:
The noble Külnaary
Had bitten and worked that wretched armor.
So that in battle against an enemy
No shot would ever stray through,
She had folded it like a serpent’s belly;
The noble Akbermet,
Leading the women with brass thimbles,
Had them stitch it with tiny, even stitches.
Any who worked poorly were rebuked,
Their steel needles were filed anew,
And broken ones cast in the appointed place.
It was stuffed and quilted by champions,
So when a spear was thrust skillfully,
It slid away to one side.
It had nine fastenings,
The blue battle coat worn by champions,
A stout coat no arrow could pierce;
When its wearer entered close combat,
It shepherded his entrusted life,
And when he entered a shooting fight,
It shielded that entrusted life.
Its broad skirt of steel had no opening,
A fitted blue battle coat,
And Joltoi donned it without shaking.
He calmly arranged its buttons,
And tied the fastenings down each side
Without haste or flurry.
Studded with jewels,
It satisfied the heart of all who saw it.
All eyes turned when he girded it on,
And souls rejoiced at the sight;
On a moonless, overcast night,
It shone as brightly as moonlight
When seen upon his waist.
He girded on the blue pouch,
Took up the blue-lacquered spear,
And rode along the edge of the road,
Making the peerless steed Kulunsur
Prance beneath him.
Your hero Joltoi
Rode proudly, braced for battle,
Swaying as though the earth struck toward him,
His silhouette rising like another mountain,
His whole body surging with power.
He shouted from the front,
“Where is my dear Seytek?”
And raised a mighty battle cry.
Seeing strife, battle, and turmoil,
Seeing coats without end,
Joltoi saw the vastness of hell:
Those ninety there, these two here,
Attacking from every side.
