You speak of estrangement
And will not cease your mischief.
You make words blaze like fire,
And turn my brother into an enemy.
You make my own kin a foe,
And do this to me.
You meant to make my lament be sung,
You roaring seers,
And have worsened my wound.
By speaking such vicious words,
You meant to cost me my head.
You seers meant to question me
About some far-off youth of mine.
You meant to rot my treasure;
By committing this boundless deed,
Damn you, diviners,
You meant to bring about my end.
You mounted a swift horse to scout,
And by yelping on, you seers,
You meant to part me from my brother.”
Thus Kongurbay spoke,
Hunched in fury,
Bristling in distress.
As though darkness had fallen upon his wife,
Kongurbay capered wildly,
As though fleeing a pursuer.
He stiffened in wrath,
Looking ready to swallow them alive;
His eyes boiled in every direction,
And he gnashed those formidable teeth,
Chewing them side to side.
His eyes bulged from their sockets,
Huge as the bays of a lake;
Such was the accursed man’s bearing—
He seemed ready to swallow anyone he saw.
He stamped the earth in frenzy,
And the Kalcha hero began to speak.
When they saw Kongurbay’s wrath,
The forty tribes of China
And their vaunted lions
All wailed together,
Abandoning all hope of life.
Then Kongurbay, son of Alööke,
Let out a swine-like roar.
Such was the Kalcha’s state—
A dreadful marvel to behold.
In his wrath, he looked able
To reap the Chinese with his sword;
If he made a move, he looked able
To swallow every last Chinese man.
His gray-wolf ferocity revealed,
Such was Kongurbay to behold.
“Damn you, Chinese people!
Clamorously worshipping stones,
You have set yourselves above me.
Between elder and younger brother,
You Chinese have come
And sought to divide us.
Having made me the Kalcha’s seer,
What is it you intended to do?
You snarling Chinese dogs
Have plainly sought
To bring about my end;
You sought to destroy the Kalcha
And depart, you Chinese,
Toward the Khakan’s realm.
If you provoke my boundless fury,
I will strip off your Chinese robes
And make a splendid slaughter.
If frenzy seizes me,
May I perish if I do not settle all your accounts together.
If I stiffen in rage,
May I perish if I do not crush your distant fury.”
Thus spoke Kongurbay:
“Damn you, men of the Khakan!
Claiming you would set things right, forty seers,
You have plundered my possessions
And brought this discord upon me.
