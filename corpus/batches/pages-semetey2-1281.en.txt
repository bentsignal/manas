Mounted you on a steed no bullet could catch,
And sent you shouting against the foe.
He carved meat for you and fed you,
And made not only the Kyrgyz, but all mankind,
Call you Kanchoro the hero.
He equipped you and sent you forth with your spear,
And spread your fame among the people.
He built you a stately walled dwelling,
And every enemy who challenged you
He fought and laid to rest.
Over the Argyn and the Kyrgyz
He made you governor, sultan, and khan.
Now Semetey proved a mighty lion,
Yet when you grew fat, without so much
As a coal-black fault from him, your heart turned corrupt—
You, Kanchoro, son of Chubak.
How did you grow so powerful?
You defiled your oath and threw the people into turmoil;
You plotted evil against your elder brother.
Chynkojo and you are alike—
You have proved a worthless companion.
May there never be another like you,
A companion who is no true hero.
You turn all before you into prey,
Yet call yourself a heroic companion.
Filled utterly with treachery,
You are an infidel companion.
You covet whatever pleases you
And seize it for yourself.
If that famous rumor is truly true,
To the very depths of hell
You are destined to go.
In the end, consumed by flames,
You are destined to remain there.
Into every calamity and affliction
You are destined to go.
Whoever heeds your words,
Into the torment of true hell
You are destined to cast.
Into the great strife that destroys nations
You are destined to go.
You regard your own kinsman
As though he were an enemy.
Destroying nations and corrupting peoples,
You are a companion who betrays them.
To every grave affliction
You are all too ready to yield.
Those who call you friend
You hand over to punishment
With your own hands, my friend.
In your own mind you strut proudly,
Thinking yourself a mighty friend.
Treacherous in thought and forked of tongue,
You have proved a corrupt friend.
Let me tell of your corruption:
You became the enemy of your own elder brother.
Can one such as you be a hero?
What good is it to call you one?
You are calamity that never passes,
The very black eye of disaster.
You were born, poor wretch,
The very embodiment of one who ruins nations.
You are the black eye of conflict—
Not merely its eye, but conflict itself.
Semetey is the halter of the nation,
His fame known to all the people.
The quarrel that shook nations and ruined peoples
Was caused by that slave Chynkojo.
Had Chynkojo not existed,
The wise and valiant Toltoy,
Would not, because of Chynkojo,
Have stood on opposing sides,
Tearing lungs and kindling fury,
Fighting one another in resentment.
There was no white-ridged mountain between them,
Nor were they enemies destined to slaughter each other.
It was the cruelty of that slave Chynkojo—
And now, Kanchoro, it is your tyranny.
