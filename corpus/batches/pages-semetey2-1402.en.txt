Without looking around him,
Not sparing so much as a hair,
He conceived a plan no man could fathom,
And lavishly spent abundant wealth
And countless livestock upon the feast.
Across the plain of Zherge-Tal,
Along the banks of Jeti-Suu,
He raised loud laughter.
Among the Zhediger of Jeti-Ozon,
He gathered everyone without exception.
The people assembled and packed the place;
The folk of Jeti-Ozon crowded in.
He opened the treasury,
And all the amassed gold and wealth
He scattered lavishly.
He began the games on a boundless scale,
And all the countless goods
He utterly gave away.
He slaughtered stallions with tusk-long teeth,
And specially chosen mares;
Their two humps standing high,
He slaughtered ungainly camels,
Setting out wealth beyond measure.
When the formidable gray wolf had died,
To the palace taken as spoil,
Before the khan's dark-eyed lady,
Before dear Churok herself,
He summoned the people's biys,
Gathering all those rulers,
And hero Kiyaz brought them
Into Aichurok's dwelling.
The khojas in mottled robes,
The mullahs wrapped in turbans,
The eloquent and learned elders—
So they might carefully behold
Aichurok's beauty and diversion,
He gathered all the people.
Those closest to himself,
Kiyaz gathered his khans
Into Aichurok's palace.
To his khans and royal kin,
To everyone close to him,
To his tiger-hearted heroes,
To those who rode beside him,
To his wise and capable lords,
He displayed Aichurok's bearing,
Testing the judgment of them all.
The khan proclaimed the viewing,
And before the noble, unfortunate woman
Those who faced her stood amazed.
Why had she remained apart from the people?
Her waist was sheathed, her face moonlike—
Ah, what a radiant, unfortunate lady!
Supple as an ermine,
Her face glittered like the sun;
She sat in dignity, glowing.
Sixty clasps and forty buttons
Flashed upon her breast.
The Zhediger who saw her
Despaired of every woman they had seen.
At the overwhelming wonder,
Whoever looked upon her trembled.
When Churok whispered,
Her voice was like a brass komuz;
The black hair down her back
Was like the plumage of a parrot.
When she turned, her face flashed;
Great earrings fashioned of gold
Glittered upon her breast.
And then there was Churok—
No, not Churok, but a force of upheaval,
That infidel woman of singular nature,
That graceful lady,
The renowned Lady Churok.
This woman was no mere Churok,
But turmoil that could ruin a people;
That champion, Akun's daughter,
Truly was the moon itself.
