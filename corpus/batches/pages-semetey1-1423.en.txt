Keen-eyed, a judge of warriors—
That is the accursed man himself.
Karagul, doomed to die in flames,
That is the crooked slave himself.
He is the khan of the horse herders;
His wrath is complete, though he is an infidel,
And the cunning of Karagul
Is second to none.
May Karagul, doomed to die in flames,
Not deceive you with some clever trick;
May that swine whose home burned
Not butcher us in this way!
May unmitigated disaster not race
To Chinmachin and Beijing;
May Karagul, doomed to die in flames,
Not send word in every direction.
He was a deceiver, a consummate trickster,
One of the most resourceful slaves.
Be wary of Karagul,
My beloved champion!
If the troublesome slave falls into your hands,
Gouge out his eyes until they are blinded;
If he begs, do not spare his life—
Seize him and kill him.
Put the swine who thwarted your desire
To a complete end!
Karagul, son of Katal—
Here is the dark peril!
He is an obsequious slave;
My colt, my son, listen well:
He is one of our true enemies!
A slave skilled among spearmen,
A foremost slave, ingenious in stratagems!
That is the accursed man himself;
He became khan of the horse herders,
And, unless what I say is false,
This is the time when he lives in luxury.
When one fancy takes him,
He eats the frozen fat of an ox;
When another fancy takes him,
He eats the suckling foal of a barren mare.
This year God must have struck the slave;
He gives no thought to anything,
Growing violent from drinking spirits,
Growing heated from drinking koumiss,
With sweat streaming down to his bones.
Karagul must have become such a creature;
Karagul must have become a khan
As great as a mountain among the horse herders.
Gray must now streak his beard;
That swine, like me,
Must have become a hoary old man.
Once he is parted from his bay stallion,
If you do not destroy his black racer Kyydu,
You will witness
The turmoil Karagul brings!
I have said much—heed my words;
My precious child, understand!
At the edge of the horse herds,
On the face of Kürpüldök,
In the hollow of Kyr-Kayyn,
Along the sickle-shaped ridge,
Karagul’s tent
Can be seen standing right there,
Along with the hero named Karagul.
He makes himself known;
From inside that tent,
The accursed swine
Makes his lordly rank known.
Four or five horse herders at a time
Carry him outside so he can urinate.
Karagul’s life of pleasure
Keeps the accursed dog alive.
Upon his wretched throne,
Whenever he raises his head,
The horse herders feed him
Slices of fatty meat.
That is the accursed man himself—
