Of the spies who came to test him,
Five were swallowed by death.
Zhöökülük must have been an extraordinary man,
For he alone escaped without dying.
His reason failed and his senses fled,
And he despaired of his precious life;
Blazing up like a live ember,
Before one could say a word,
In the blink of an eye,
Before anyone could look,
Zhöökülük had crossed
Six ranges and forty hills.
Trembling like a drawn bow,
Hurrying and rushing wildly,
Zhöökülük pressed onward;
In the blink of an eye,
Before anyone could look,
He rushed breathlessly toward Chongbilgich.
Zhöökülük reached him
And stirred up great turmoil.
He spoke of the mighty giant,
Told lies of every kind,
Ranted and exaggerated,
Making them despair of their precious lives,
And so began that lament.
The cauldron that boils men like meat—
The Chinese cast it aside.
Chongbilgich thundered:
“If you know nothing of the people, crawl into the earth!
Though I called you a hero, you are an infidel,
A worthless wretch who ruins the nation;
You go about destroying the people.
These are the very eyes that saw his forefathers;
Now heed these words of mine:
It was our forefather
Who slaughtered the accursed Kyrgyz.
You went scouting, threw the people into disorder,
And spewed crude, corrupt words;
It was you who started this uproar.
Posing as a hero and boasting like a warrior,
You shake the champions’ resolve.
Why do you begin
Such nation-wrecking strife?”
Chongbilgich was indeed a hero:
Does not a man die when his hour comes?
Does he not live while his days remain?
“When he fled at your mere words,
His noble father, Kojozhash,
Had only just seen this son born before he died.
Will they not say he fled back here,
Without a shadow of courage or a word to speak?
Will they not say he came fleeing in panic,
Scattering all his possessions?
Will they not say all six lost their wits
And perished?
Will they not say you came here
Embroidering your tale with wild exaggeration?
If you are no swift-footed runner, crawl into the earth!
Who should listen to these words of yours?
Let the blinding night hear them;
Let the muddy water hear them,
Let empty shrieks and clamor hear them.
Know now what I tell you:
Bury yourself in the earth along with your words.
As for the measure of this tale of yours,
Let the dog barking at the bөө hear it,
Let the birdlike beast called bөө hear it—
Who else but these should listen?
Yesterday, when your mother labored and bore you,
The people must have gathered and the nation rejoiced,
Saying, ‘A son has been born!’
They must have given countless rams and livestock
As rewards for the glad tidings,
Calling you a man who would uphold honor,
And saying you would defend the people.
Fail to guard the people and be ruined!
Fail to be a man and die!
You have spoken words that kindle panic;
Hey, would that you had sunk away unborn!
You have terrified my people,
