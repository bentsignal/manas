Those who wreak slaughter through the ages,
Who throw not merely women, but noble men,
And even the khagan into panic.
His valor was extraordinary—
Let us tell of that valor:
His bristling moustache
Was like the saber of a Bukharan,
And the breath from his mouth
Like flame from a smithy’s furnace.
Crackling like lightning,
Like a keen black falcon,
He flashed with fury;
His heart grew cold, his innards burned,
And a dreadful conflict began.
That noble soul, once roused, could not be restrained;
Such was the mighty eagle
Whom they bound by a single hair.
“If you call me a slave, a slave I am,
Yet I am the watching black eye
Of my late gray-wolf brother!
I broke through Jet-Kait and opened a road.
I spread strife across the earth,
Hardened by drinking blood,
And won fame as a leopard-warrior.
I knew that I would die,
Yet, pampered woman, my disgrace,
I have burned with devotion to you.
In the splendid palace you were mistress,
While I was an orphan, countless as grains of sand.
You cared for me in my orphanhood;
Though you did not give me milk from your breast,
You gave me doe’s milk.
You filled my life with delightful words;
While my dear brother lived,
You became like a mother to me.
Over your countless horses, withheld when asked,
I shall cast the cold lasso;
The golden throne withheld despite entreaty,
I shall seize without entreating.
It was not darkness—become night itself!
When bloodthirsty Kyyaz was slaughtered,
You alone mourned and veiled yourself.
You who befriended Kyyaz,
Your wrath boiled and arose;
You were a hero versed in many things.
You began this desperate revolt and great turmoil,
Seeking to save your life;
Why then did you leave alive
This warrior doomed to die in madness?
He has begun strife among your multitude;
Unless you kill him and take revenge,
In the end he will destroy you!
Seize your wrestlers and lions firmly
And put him down,” she said;
“Bind the maddened slave!”
The pampered woman Chachykey
Rose raging from the palace,
Haranguing Kanchoro,
Shouting for the wrestlers
And summoning her Kanchoro.
The pampered, vicious shrew
Stirred boundless conflict,
Pressing Kanchoro hard,
So eager was she for strife.
Before the wrestler-guards could swarm,
Before the wrestlers could close in,
Before the queen could give her order,
Before the people could assemble,
The wearer of eighty-four pieces of armor,
Who flared when roused like crimson fire,
Whose courage was stern and venom strong,
A scourge born among mortals—
Külchoro, upheld by the spirit Shay,
Braced himself like a leopard.
Inspiration came to him from the unseen;
His bloodlust seized him fiercely,
He ground his teeth with a crack,
Gripped by valor, his fury boiling;
His thirst for blood possessed him.
