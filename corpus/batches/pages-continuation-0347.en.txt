His raging fury boiled over.
As he was about to strike with his spear,
The hero and lion,
Swift beyond measure,
The most perceptive sage,
Ground his teeth like millstones.
His eyes flashed like a lion's;
He thrust and whirled the sword.
Er Kenen struck the dun horse
At the place the poor hero had chosen,
Drawing the blade across it.
When Kenen performed that feat,
When he brought the sword down true,
The champion Chyntengir's
True stallion toppled.
Its house-sized head fell;
As if a mountain had collapsed,
The dun horse pitched over and lay still.
When Chyntengir was left on foot,
Your father, the dear gray-maned hero,
Hewed with his sword
And shouted as he waged battle.
The sword shattered on the swine,
Leaving only the hilt in his hand.
The Kaldays and chiefs,
Men with iron lariat nets,
All armed in iron,
Fully armed infidels,
Heroes who had come from Kakan,
Mounted Chyntengir again
On another swift stallion.
They put their khan on horseback,
Burning Kenen's soul with anguish.
Then they cast iron lariat nets
Over Kenen,
Surrounded and attacked him,
And truly robbed him of escape.
They firmly bound his hands.
Poor Kenen the leopard,
The champion on whom hopes rested,
The renowned hero of the Kyrgyz,
Sultan of the white-capped people—
Kenen was alone and the Chinese were a thousand.
They tore and ruined his coat,
Displaying their great force,
Tormenting his fly-like soul.
They wound his hands and feet tightly;
Chyntengir bound and enclosed
His entire body in chains.
They jerked him until his back bent,
Loaded him upon a gray mule,
And ordered, “Avenge your forefather!”
They had their champions beat him
And drove him before their khan.
Poor Kenen saw
A cold and merciless black night.
“If you are a hero and a lion,
Descendant of your Kalmyk forefather Kongurbay,
Loosen my hands for a moment.
Let me see your heroism,
And then I will take your head.
Release my hands,
Then hear my answer.
While my eyelashes still move,
How can you bend my back?
How could I surrender my people?”
Hearing this, Chyntengir said:
“The Ala-Too is no land for us;
The old enemy, the vile Burut,
Will never be a people to us.
Inflict harsh torment;
Silence his tongue without delay.
Bring his death upon him now,
Deprive him of every recourse.
Cut strips of hide from his back;
You blood-drinking champions,
Seize him and take his life!
Cut off his sheep-like head;
Rip open his melon-like belly!”
Thus Chyntengir spoke,
Urging and dispatching
His champions and giants,
Rousing and dispatching
His strongest heroes.
His champions and warriors
Now began to trample and beat him,
Inflicting every torture they knew.
They tormented and took his life,
Cutting off his hands and feet.
The champions attacked
The noble gray-maned hero
And took his precious head;
They finished him then and there.
“We slaughtered and destroyed the champion
Whose stars crossed ours.”
Breaking through the dead end and opening a road,
The Chinese army attacked directly
Against the true Black Kyrgyz.
