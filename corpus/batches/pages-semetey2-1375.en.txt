Among Kyyaz’s Jediger
Stood countless warriors prepared;
Yet the mourning woman Aichürök
Unleashed, as you see, a mighty battle,
With swiftness beyond measure.
In one lightning movement, the ill-fated woman
Drew the sword from its sheath.
She charged like a lion,
As if wisdom came from the unseen;
Her eyes flashed in fury,
And a terrible blood-drinking look seized her,
As she bit her lips and ground her teeth.
With swiftness beyond measure,
The ill-fated woman, blade ringing,
Took the monstrous steel Zulpukor
And gripped it properly;
She sprang like a striking snake,
About to cut Kanchoro down.
As she drew the sword across
And was about to deliver the mighty blow,
To strike the crooked, faithless
Scoundrel across the throat,
And put the raging madman
To death,
The mourning woman Aichürök,
Who had begun a true bloodletting,
Was surrounded by many warriors
Of that infidel Kanchoro.
Just as she was about to strike,
And knock off his apple-like head,
The warriors came between them.
Ejibay, son of Emil,
Was an ungainly wrestler;
And beside the black-tongued, fleet-footed
Slave Kanchoro
Was a wrestler called Töö Balban.
Ejibay and Töö Balban—
Those two blocked her path.
She was a man-killing, fearsome woman:
Not merely two, but seven of them
She beheaded with her sword.
God forbid that one should see it—
The palace filled with dust,
And the gateway ran with blood.
Aichürök moved like a whirlwind;
The warriors who pressed in,
Those who obeyed the khan’s command
And dared oppose Chürök,
Fell sprawling to the ground.
Warriors who fancied themselves skillful
Fell stiffly to the ground,
Their bodies without heads.
The widowed Aichürök,
When Jylgyn and Talas lay in turmoil,
When the royal camp was being plundered—
The widowed Aichürök
Made her proud head her banner,
And threw the Jediger people into uproar.
She made a warrior of a woman
And laid the Jediger, who covered the earth,
Level with the black ground.
Many warriors surrounded
The doomed woman Aichürök;
Your sister-in-law began a mighty battle.
Calling her a savage woman,
And losing their wits in panic,
They stabbed your sister-in-law
With spears from afar.
Your poor sister-in-law stood alone
In that battle:
Brave Külchoro and Akjoltoy,
Her two dear blood brothers, were absent;
My ancestor, the sage Bakay, was absent;
My uncle, brave Khan Sary, was absent;
There was no forest into which she could flee,
No Nogoy kin to cherish her.
