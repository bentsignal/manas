The vast black host of Jediger,
Surging in from every side,
Rescued their khan,
The hero Kyyaz.
Sary Khan raged,
As if inspired with wisdom from the unseen.
Having lost his wits, brave Kyyaz
Pleaded with Kanchoro:
“You are both my comrade and my friend;
We are out of options—give me counsel!
The moon has darkened and night has fallen;
This is a day of terrible calamity.
My strength is spent and my power is gone;
My wits have fled and abandoned me.
From the day I became Kyyaz,
From the day I mounted the horse called Kyyaz,
I have never met my death.
Your dear friend Kyyaz
Has never seen such a man among the people.
I have seen an extraordinary warrior,
One who never yields his companion’s life;
His speech is unlike that of other men,
And his eyes are as large as cups.
He is a champion and a lion,
A hero surpassing all mortals.
Beneath him he has a thoroughbred steed,
And the strength to swallow not only Jediger
But the whole world.
Beside Sary Khan,
Black Maar, who can suck up land across six ravines,
Seems an easy foe.
When I see Sary Khan,
My whole body bristles;
Like a charcoal-burner set ablaze,
He roars like a great drum.
No man can face him in combat;
Whoever encounters him cannot survive.
Even if all Jediger rose as one
And swarmed him, they could not prevail;
Whoever engages
His towering hound cannot escape alive.
He is a champion and a lion,
A hero worth the whole world.
Jediger cannot overcome him;
Why did Almighty God
Create mankind weak
And call us His servants?
My dear khan and comrade,
I will not advance against Sary Khan;
If I fight that branded boar,
I will not survive.
I will not face that madman;
If I confront him, I will not live.
Of all men ever born,
His weight and might are greatest.
Can anyone who grapples with him survive?
Can anyone defeat him face-to-face?
Can one confront him and return alive?
He is bald as a slave-boar,
And few men can wrestle him,
For he is like a champion who could wreck the world.
If Sary Khan truly wished,
He could pluck off this head like a sparrow
And tuck it into his purse.
No man can face him;
No one can bring harm upon him.
Let us send word to the strongest
Among the many heroes of the people;
Let us withhold none, but summon them all,
Then lie down before Sary Khan
And surrender.
His speech is unlike other men’s,
His eyes unlike any human eyes.
What manner of warrior was Semetey,
Who subdued such a man
And brought him here?
When Sary Khan charges in fury,
Our warriors lose their strength.
