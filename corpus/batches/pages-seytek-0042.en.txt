Shining like the risen Moon,
Glowing like a bright ornament,
She stepped lightly forward,
Carrying her infant,
Her white breast bared.
Softly she spoke these words;
Her tongue was eloquent, her speech honeyed—
No other soul was like her.
She turned her neck like a young doe;
Aichürök shone as if touched by moonlight.
Your Chürök, daughter of Akun—
Not merely Chürök, but your storm—
Before Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
The resourceful woman Aichürök
Proclaimed the glad tidings:
“Be pleased with my words;
Glad tidings to you, Kyyaz!
Your fortune has filled like a lake,
Your train has grown a fathom long.
Your destiny has truly prospered:
Your moonlike wife Chürök
Has given birth to a son!
Your companion who reads the stars above,
Aichürök, your own consort,
Now stands here to show you
The fulfillment of your mighty hopes,
O warrior Khan Kyyaz—the child is yours.
Hang a talisman around his neck;
Saying, ‘A woman of renown has given birth,’
Raise him, my valiant Kyyaz.
Take the child who has come as your heir
And make him master of the inheritance.
The Seven Valleys are your land,
And the Jediger, who dwell there,
Are your people.
Gather all your people from across the land
And hold a feast of celebration.
You have taken many wives,
Yet never before have you had a child.
Let the road ahead stretch far—
You have not held races and children’s games,
Gladdening all who speak of them;
You have given no feast for the people.
Glad tidings, Kyyaz—heed my words!
The trust entrusted to you,
O lionhearted warrior Kyyaz,
I have brought to you myself!
Your broad road has opened wider;
If the child survives and grows,
Your reach will span the world.
Set your thoughts in good order;
Warrior Kyyaz, take and raise your child!”
After Aichürök spoke thus,
Brave Kyyaz burned inwardly,
But said little and made no reply.
He recognized that the child
Was indeed a true calamity.
Realizing it, brave Kyyaz thought,
“Now true suffering has come;
A destined enemy has arrived.
Almighty God the Creator
Has now given me grief.
A child has been found today,
But more than a child, this affliction
Is a black demon of disaster!
My seven sorceress crones—
Without caring what a horse was worth,
I freely gave them my horse;
Without caring what a robe was worth,
I freely gave them my robe.
Those bribed old women
Have proved worthless:
They could not steal the child,
Could not finish him off!
You fawning, rabid curs—
If I do not round you up,
If I do not loose a glorious slaughter
Upon every one of you,
If I do not take your heads by the sword
And drag you along the road—”
