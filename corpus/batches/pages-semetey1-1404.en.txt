Where the great black horse is tethered,
That infidel, son of the kalday,
Hero Khan Kongurbai—
That is where he himself lies encamped!
That blue-gray narrow gorge
Is where the accursed infidel
Placed his magic!
Within that very enchantment,
I have heard that Beijing's magic
Turned its face elsewhere,
And that they fortified it
Five times more strongly than before!
Within that very enchantment,
In the place they excavated,
Upon the road where they set the magic,
No mortal has traveled;
No challenger remained alive.
Upon that very road
There are sorcerers and a khan;
Among the smooth-skinned swine
There are predators of every kind!
Its tiger bore fifteen cubs;
No mortal ventured near!
There are sixty dragons,
And beyond them
A creature held there by magic:
There is a sword-snouted elephant.
Beijing is no easy road;
My precious son, understand!
Among those seething infidels,
How many places have a khan;
There are countless kinds of enchantments
That speak in human tongues,
And there are guardians too.
There is a beast beyond words,
There is a pass on which they set their resolve,
There is a place where they lie entrenched!
It has a road mortals long to see,
And a ditch beside the road
Where the ignorant fall and die!
It has boiling brine
Into which the ignorant plunge and die;
There is an iron-cable net
That traps those who go reconnoitering.
Those who go there never turn back—
Such are those calamitous Chinese people!
I am the Bakai who demanded Beijing's tribute
And took it!
The place where I passed through and ruled
Has become a wretched land left by Manas!
Blue haze and low mist—
That is the pass of Kaspang-Too!
The haze beyond the reach of sight—
That is the Sary-Suu of Kürpüldök,
That is the bank of the Sary-Suu!
If my young hero remains safe,
Those are the Chinese people he will fight;
I am the Bakai who saw them
In the days of sacred power!
If we ride onward from here,
That is the pale, pebbled pass;
That is where Kongurbai's Kuukulja
Lies on watch!
Yesterday, when Alman struck Kakan,
When your Ala shot
The wild ram standing guard,
That accursed Kuukulja
Fell into terrible rage
And left behind its young.
Its young grew up
And, they say, reached seven years of age;
That Akkulja, all by itself—
That swine, ablaze with rage—
Became the guardian of the land, they say.
I hear it is the chief guardian
Of hero Kongurbai,
Whose tent poles are gold!
