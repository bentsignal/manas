His beard was sparse, hair by hair;
He was beardless.
He was practiced at facing the enemy;
He possessed leadership.
With red spear and Zulpukor,
He was a hero who struck in turn.
In our Kakan and Beijing,
He was a hero who could endure
Forty days of jousting.
He was skilled with the white battle-axe,
An expert in warfare,
A mighty one who found the enemy's stratagem,
He was,
Mottled-bodied and very lean,
A servant fit for a hero.
Without fearing this man alone,
With iron ropes and nets,
With ranks of Kanjarkol men,
I shall surround him
And bring him utter torment.
With sixty sorcerers and a thousand champions,
I, the lion, at their head,
Shall stalk the poor man carefully
And pierce him with one overturning thrust.
Is there a mane upon dark water?
While Khan Kongurbay stands,
Is there a khan a tether can reach?
Against the ill-fated Burut,
Will my Zholoy leave anything undone?
If I send him with an order,
Will hairy-soled Tükübay
Not bind and seize
Every one of the handful of Burut?"
Before swaggering Ezkara
Had withdrawn his words,
The many Muslim khans
Had filed past like sheep.
Ezkara of the infidels
Had thoroughly assessed
The Muslim heroes.
There was Kozubek of the Kokon khan;
When he saw Kozubek,
He called him "the lumbering one."
There was Malabek of Margalang;
When he saw Malabek,
He called him "one who capers
Like a foal at the tethering line."
There was fork-bearded Synzhybek of Anzhiyan;
He called him "one who struts
Like a male black crow."
He now knew that the vast black host
Of Muslims had gathered,
And knew that bloodthirsty Manas
Was not there.
My courageous hero Khan Zholoy
Was like a spur of the black mountain.
If Kongurbay truly attacked,
Having come claiming to hold a true feast,
Let them not say there was slaughter
Among the people of Chynmachyn.
Heed what I say, Burut;
Give me what I demand, Burut.
Kökötöy's Maaniker
Is a deep-rooted black racehorse;
This horse borders on the supernatural,
Its tail and mane set it apart,
And it appears suited to us.
It will not tire when ridden scouting,
Nor grow sore when ridden with the baggage train.
It is a beast with wings,
A creature fit for a khan.
Fastening on his mail,
It could circle Beijing five times;
It is a mount heroes would ride!
It has the marks of a racehorse;
Mounted by striving heroes,
It is a beast that could travel Beijing.
Heed my demand, Burut;
