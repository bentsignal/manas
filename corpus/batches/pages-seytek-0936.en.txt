Those remarkable men who heeded no counsel,
The meek who understood nothing,
Those who plowed grain to sustain life,
And cut hay in preparation for winter,
Young and old alike,
He drove away the entire people.
He bound burdens upon their backs
And threw this nation into distress,
Beginning a terrible conflict.
Pouring out his wrath, Sarybay
Slaughtered them utterly.”
So spoke the people amid their clamor,
And the host cried out in uproar.
Before the descendant of the noble lion,
They submitted to brave Seytek,
And spoke before the people.
The heroes serving as mediators,
Resourceful champions,
Men who whetted their sharp tongues
And spoke eloquently before khans,
The most skillful spearmen,
The strongest of the assembled host,
Men who had seen much,
And wise, eloquent counselors,
Stood with hands folded.
Changdayak, the army’s finest,
Said this to brave Seytek:
“If you are a hero and a lion,
Your first ancestor was Tügöl Khan,
Blessed from the beginning by Khydyr,
Descendant of the primal hero,
Heir to the first holy man.
Sarybay was your enemy;
Do not regard everyone as a foe.
Sarybay was the swine,
So, hero, do not cast your terrible venom upon us.
Hero, you need living souls to tend,
And a hero such as you
Needs a people such as us.
We shall sell our coursers and pay tribute;
Sarybay was the one you opposed.
If you seek vengeance upon Sarybay,
Descendant of the blessed lion,
If you charge in wrath,
Draw your sword from its sheath
And unleash your dreadful fury.
Slaughter us all.
If your daily rage seizes you,
Gouge out our eyes in vengeance.
We are Sarybay’s countless host,
A people ravaged by war.
Here and now,
We are sheep fallen into your hands.
We turn our warhorses’ heads,
Hoping you will shelter us like a poplar.
Here in front of you, Changdayak and all,
We stand with folded hands.
We are captives in your hands,
All of us accounted guilty.
If you are truly a khan,
Do not assail our precious lives,
But let us go safe and sound.
Sire, since you are our khan,
Draw your sword from its sheath.
Without sparing a single soul,
Slaughter us all.
If you ask of former times,
We are men from the Windy Pass;
Beginning struggle, conflict, and strife,
Hero, we came here to fight.
But now, brave Seytek,
We are mortals fallen into your hands.
We bow our heads to truth,
Hoping mercy will come from the hero,
And ask you for one spoonful of blood,
Too little even to fill your mouth.
As a seven-day trust,
Hero, we beg you for our lives.
If you spare us and leave us alive,
Our poplar trees will become standards,
