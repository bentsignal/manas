When he fights, he is never sated with blood,
He who served as the Kyrgyz people’s shield
And pondered all their cares and sorrows.
When he met a foe, he charged,
And drank the blood of the enemy he encountered
As though it were kumis.
Whoever stirred his wrath,
He shed his blood and drove him away,
Driving his black-steel spear
Deep between his ribs.
He was the champion, he was the lion,
Throughout the whole wide world
He was the hero whose every deed prevailed.
His fame reached across the world;
When he girded his waist and roared,
The uproar reached even Beijing.
Will he lie down before Sarybai,
Saying, “He is a wrathful wrestler”?
Will he merely wander through the land,
Without clashing and battling,
Without meeting his foe and dying outright,
With blood spilled and sight extinguished,
Seeing no chance to turn back?
Will God strike down the bloodthirsty one?
Would he, because Sarybai is fierce,
Submit like a coward without courage,
Like a man struck by a curse,
And yield to his tyranny?
His forefather was a peerless warrior and lion.
Would he give the truly beloved Kyrgyz
As plunder into the hands
Of one vicious dog?
While the ill-fated man yet lives,
Would he submit to this conflict?
Would he give the fortified gates and beloved city,
The rightful Kyrgyz heirs,
Without first dying and perishing,
To that bloodthirsty Sarybai?
Let their worthy men be beaten,
And the dimple-mouthed, camel-eyed beauties—
Let this whole people grieve and weep,
Let the nation’s anguish be seen.
For my nation and my people,
Let this dear life be a sacrifice!
Let my bones remain upon the plain;
For my white-kalpak Kyrgyz people,
Let the magnanimous hero’s life be offered!
Our forefathers from whom we sprang
Were never defeated by a wrathful horde,
Never bowed their gaunt frames to a grasping foe.
Such was the people into whom we were born:
Not satisfied even when they drank by asking,
Never putting aside their hunger for more;
When driven through darkness,
They prowled like leopards,
No enemy raided their pastures,
None who opposed them remained alive;
Whenever diminished, they blazed anew,
Whenever destroyed, they grew stronger,
When calamity struck, they flared up,
They summered along the mountain slopes,
And none who grappled with them escaped unharmed.
From ancient times they feasted on fat,
Their cauldron was never without grease,
Their people clamored and ate meat;
How could I permit my valiant Kyrgyz nation
To be slaughtered?
For the people, for the nation,
Let me truly be the sacrifice!”
He couched his steel spear;
The bloodthirsty lion himself,
Burning with fury and grief,
Bound his precious life
To a single hair and cast it away.
When he came in violence,
Like one who would lay waste to hell itself,
What man could withstand
The magnanimous hero, lionlike in might?
He wore armor of blue steel
And charged alone against a host of foes,
