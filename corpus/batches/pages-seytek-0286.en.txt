Almighty God creates living souls
In every different form.
The valleys stretch broad and level,
The wooded mountains rise in grandeur;
Our wondrous Talas
Has snow upon its encircling peaks,
And every kind of living creature.
All creatures endowed with life
Are gathered here in abundance.
Cuckoos sing upon the hills,
And nightingales in the woods
Pour forth their many plaintive songs.
When the mournful nightingale sings,
Those who bear sorrow long to listen;
Many a melancholy soul
Yearns to enter its delight.
Willows bend low,
Argali cluster on the hills.
Deer shelter among the knolls,
And there, upon the terraces, wild goats
Pour across in every direction.
Their ears held sharply upright,
Wolves prowl along the lower slopes.
Their tails trailing behind them,
Foxes frolic on the plain.
Swift bustards and great bustards
Scatter to graze upon the hills;
Every blessing is true delight,
And white waters pour from the uplands.
Our wondrous, spacious Talas,
Its apples are as large as horses’ heads,
Its walnuts like stones in a stream;
Feather grass sways waist-high,
Tulips and sorrel
Twist and trail about the waist.
From the broad, branching valleys
Springs divide and flow.
Perhaps in wonder at Talas,
The boy’s thoughts wandered away.
When he looked closely upon broad Talas,
He rode on without sparing his horse,
Never halting to rest his steed,
Never lying down to satisfy his sleep,
Never leaning on an elbow to rest,
Never dozing even for a moment;
He hurried on in high excitement,
Roused like a lion,
Travelling without pause,
Riding onward without restraint.
How could the hero have foreseen
The mist lying like the back of a saddle?
He made his way toward the haze.
On this side of the mist
Was a valley of surpassing cliffs.
Kara-Buura and great Chatkal
Form a mountain range adjoining Talas;
Its rocky passes are perilously narrow,
Its ridges are sheer precipices,
Its mountains girdle the valley like a rim.
It is called the broad Chatkal range.
He crossed over Chatkal,
And before him lay broad Talas—
He entered spacious Talas.
Our wondrous, spacious Talas
Lay spread out wide,
Its lofty mountains rising grandly.
Its springs surged and overflowed,
Its nightingales sang and trilled,
And sorrowing hearts melted into tears.
Its leaves are broad as hides,
Its beetles large as thumbs,
Its hogweed stalks thick as wrists;
When its maral deer bolt away,
They resemble pale-faced cows.
Its saplings are like plane trees,
Its little birds like snowcocks,
Its lizards like snakes.
Its wild asses are like three-year-old colts,
And its gliding snakes
Are like coiled ropes.
