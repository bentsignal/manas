Including a separate palace
For Buurul, mighty as a camel stallion.
This splendid palace—
Seen from outside, it looked like one palace;
Once inside, it seemed a thousand palaces,
So vast was its magnificence.
It was cast and sculpted as though molded,
And its door was of white tin.
There were six stewards who tended the horses;
Baimyrza and Akjoltoy,
Whenever Seytek had need,
Were keen-witted men who found a solution.
When Akjoltoy had spoken thus,
All the heroes grew excited
And every warrior became animated.
They played chess and won from one another,
And drank kumis from the leather vat;
When they heard the news of the maiden,
They grew merry on kumis,
Until sweat streamed from their brows,
And they burst into clamor.
They drank spirits from horn cups
And joined the celebration.
Then the lions gave themselves
To unending merriment
And plunged into the revelry.
“The one God has freely
Granted us our desire,” they said;
“To Seytek, blessed by Kydyr,
A beauty from the peri folk has come,” they said.
A roaring tumult arose,
And that day the clamor burst forth.
“A beauty has unexpectedly come to him,” they said;
“This Seytek’s fortune
Surpasses that of his father Semetey,” they said.
The reason it surpassed his father’s was this:
His father, the fierce Semetey,
Had won Aichürök,
But suffered anguish and battled enemies;
His ancient foes attacked,
And his head was pledged to death.
The two giants, Chynkojo and Toltoy,
Brought about a terrible slaughter.
When his father took Chürök,
He plunged the people into suffering.
But for Seytek, whose fortune was greater,
The hapless daughter of the peri
Came of her own accord,
Without galling a single steed
Or causing pain to any soul.
When God wills, He grants;
The Night of Destiny has come to us.
The people rejoiced and the nation surged,
Swiftly invoking Baabedin;
In the name of magnanimous Manas,
They dedicated a moon-hoofed horse
And spoke many splendid blessings.
The noble hero, until then unmoved,
Ordered joyous games to begin
And had brought forth a white mare
That had borne no foal for six years.
When the mare was brought,
That noble hero, proud as a rooster,
Grasped both its ears and forelock
Together in his hands.
Without binding the mare’s legs,
He cut off its head.
He sacrificed it to the spirit
Of his grandfather Manas.
When the young man sacrificed the mare,
All the companions of the tiger’s heir
Cried out together:
“O noble leopard Manas,
May your spirit uphold us!”
All his comrades shouted,
“What a boundless deed!”
Every hero grew inspired,
Every swift, armed young horseman
