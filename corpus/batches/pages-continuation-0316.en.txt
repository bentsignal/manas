The noble Gulayym,
The true lady, peer of princes,
The capable lady in all she did,
Purest among those with brass thimbles,
Foremost among the scarf-wearing women,
Said to the old women who were fleeing,
“Fall down and be damned,
May grave dust fill your eyes!”
She scattered earth and chanted,
Performing many different rites,
Invoking Baabedin’s blessing,
Naming a horse for the crescent hoof,
Then slowly turned,
And before the child upon the shield,
The luckless woman repented,
Bowing as young wives do,
And opened her skirts, crying, “God!”
When she looked at Kenen,
Dark within and blue-gray without,
He was one beyond all reckoning,
His courage was singular, his wrath mighty,
Such were the poor child’s black eyes.
He was one of the mightiest heroes,
The lion of the Kyrgyz people.
With a steel razor in her hand,
She swiftly cut his cord.
Saying, “Blessed be the day you saw the light,”
She wrapped him in a robe,
Into his right hand, black blood
Brave Kenen grasped,
Into his left hand, yellow gold
Brave Kenen clutched.
He cried and roared wrathfully,
To the vast multitude,
As if crying, “I shall be the people’s guardian,”
Thus he raised his battle cry,
While still in the birth membrane, the child
Was known to be destined for a khan,
The fierce black-maned leopard
Was known to be a peerless soul.
Against the wrathful foe,
He showed he would stand firm,
To the darkly raging fierce leopard,
He was known to become a fire,
In battle against the enemy,
He was known to stand his ground.
He opened his mouth and roared,
And the black blood in his hand
He flung in a storm.
If a savage, turbulent enemy arose
And came to grapple with his people,
He meant to crush the black foe,
And scatter his blood,
The people’s defending hero
Meant to guard the nation.
Sixty young wives and forty old women
Could find no composure,
Could not endure his countenance,
Could not withstand his majesty,
And in a flustered panic,
They paced back and forth.
The yellow gold in his left hand,
With many different cries,
The roaring child scattered.
By scattering his omen,
He meant that if an equal foe arose,
He would turn him yellow as gold,
Drain all strength from his limbs,
And afflict him with jaundice.
If the foe pressed upon his throat,
He meant to seize his people and possessions.
If a foe came forth to challenge him,
He would pursue vengeance for six generations of fathers,
Turn him yellow as jaundice,
And take revenge.
A mighty bird of prey came to the perch,
The falcon Kenen came into the world,
They devised wonders no man had conceived,
And held a feast that satisfied the world,
The people’s hearts found peace,
At word that Seytek had seen Kenen,
The multitude throughout Ala-Too,
With vast treasure and mighty fortune,
Were overcome with tenderness,
Their living hearts rejoiced,
For at his birth the signs
Showed that he would be a lion,
Without delay they prepared a feast,
And the great host surged with excitement,
With meat heaped like Ala-Too,
And broth spread like Ala-Köl,
The tumultuous revelry of the feast
Subsided after nine days,
The entertainment had been abundant,
And the people’s longing was fulfilled,
He became a giant no horse could bear,
And became lord of all the Kyrgyz,
A glorious khan,
