If we set his blessed road before us,
He would lead countless armies.
The glory of the gated city,
The city of the deep-rooted plane tree—
Brave Külchoro found him,
And his lord was restored to the seat of honor;
Thus the disgraced Choro’s
Honor was restored.
“Is this waking,” he said, “or a dream?
Is this the lord I longed for?
Is this my gray-maned lion himself,
The very eye of the tiger,
Encountered as if by providence,
Found upon the white plain,
Met after vanishing on the road—
Is this my tiger himself?”
He would not release
The bridle of noble Buurul.
Blazing with anguish, he gathered himself,
And the noble warrior pleaded;
Glowing and flaring like an ember,
When he saw his lion of a hero,
How could the sorrow-laden warrior
Possibly stand still?
Ripened and blazing like an ember,
The wretched man who had seen his lord—
How could he endure and stand still?
Külchoro burned with a righteous flame,
A bare sword hung at his side,
And, pleading “Elder brother,”
Külchoro stood there burning.
He remains rigid and takes no heed,
And holds the sobbing Choro
Of no more account than his boot-heel.
No distinct words leave his mouth;
When they wail aloud, he hears no speech.
If called alive, he seems dead;
If called dead, he seems alive;
The lord and the Choro
Cannot come to an understanding.
For the sake of the tens of thousands,
Because the lord was gone from sight,
The Argyn-Kyrgyz people of Talas
Found the Ala-Too passes turned to mere roads,
And this nation was humiliated by all.
They mowed meadow grass as tribute,
And paid heavy taxes and levies;
What they saw was utter darkness,
And what they endured was a day of torment.
When the khan departed from the Kyrgyz,
Food vanished from their cauldron,
And their dear people lost their head.
No one could bear another,
No one heeded another’s words;
They drove away the noble lord with lamentation,
And clamored like plundering ravens.
Grief spread among the people,
And many were beaten and humiliated.
Such was the people’s degradation,
All their warriors became slaves,
And the people’s suffering became a commodity;
This honored hero here
Became a slave to one man after another.
Now this is the state of the people.”
Speaking thus, Külchoro
Seethed as his heart dissolved,
And, meeting the bloodthirsty warrior this way,
Told how the hills held deer and the plains argali,
Explaining the nature of the land;
Then of the walled city and the people at the gates
He told all that had happened,
Standing thus before the gray-maned lion.
And so Külchoro
Found the elder brother who had raised him,
The forebear dearer than a father,
His hobble-cord braided with gold,
The one he esteemed above
Both father and mother,
The fruit of the deep-rooted plane tree,
His lord whose figure loomed like a mountain.
