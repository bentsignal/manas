Perhaps they will bring harsh devastation,
Overrun the village now,
And scatter the white-gold goods.
Perhaps we will all meet our end;
Why did we give this exhausting feast?
Perhaps they will plunder everything.
Here is the feast if you want its spectacle;
Saying, “You have done wrong,”
Here is my head if you cut it off.
They have brought disaster to my house
And will utterly end my line.
It was livestock left by my father;
These Chinese are demanding
The swift horse Maaniker.
If I do not give Maaniker,
They stand ready to cause turmoil.”
Bokmurun came weeping
And presented Manas
Kökötöy's great ingot, large as a bucket.
It was a great ingot.
Manas accepted the gift
And handed it to his uncle Bakai.
His thirst for blood seized him strongly,
And he said to Koshoy:
“Your life has grown long;
You have become querulous.
Strength has left your body,
And you have grown weary.
When the Black Kalmyks pressed you,
You have come to nothing.
Power has left your body;
Your body has lost its power.
You have become useless.
These Chinese act as tyrants;
I have no time to lie in the uplands.
If these Chinese bluster,
I have no life I would spare from death.
While my dear soul lives,
I have no livestock to offer the Chinese,
And no time to give Maaniker.
If your infidels scowl,
I have no soul that will shrink back.
Sense has fled your body;
You have come to nothing.
Your courage has gone with age;
Dear uncle and leopard,
You have become useless.
If the Chinese scowl in anger
And take Maaniker today,
Growing insolent tomorrow,
Will they not take Akkula too?
Will they not add him as well?
The following day they may come
And take in the same way
Many leopard-like racehorses.
Those many swift horses.
Will they not strip away the swift horses?
When we are left on foot and downcast,
Will they not bring hell every day?
If we are parted from our racehorses,
Will the Muslim giants not
Carry their saddles on their backs
And suffer bitter hardship?
Will they not suffer?
Will the Chinese not do as they please?
Will God not strike us thus,
And will they not drive us on foot and kill us?
The seething infidels
Will part you from your horses
And scatter all four kinds of livestock;
Will they not dig a well in your place of honor?
Will they not bind your heroes?
Once left helpless on foot,
Will they not bring calamity?
Will the Chinese not do as they please
And slaughter every one of you?
Will they not cross over your flank
And scatter your great wealth?”
