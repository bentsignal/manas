The land shines with their tribute;
Do you see that their livestock has come?
Out of their wits with fear, from Afghanistan
Envoys have come—do you see!
Had those envoys not come,
They would have suffered
Remarkable humiliation from me—do you see?!
Folding their hands before us,
They intend to give Chürök
As tribute—do you see?!
If they do not give Aychürök,
The many Afghans will be crushed
And die—do you see?
Let us hear the envoys' words;
If they do not give Aychürök,
If they do not accept what was said,
Then let us ride out to shoot!"
When Kozho said this,
Toltoy stood and said:
"The envoys who have come between us,
Tümönbay, son of Tülkü,
And Azhybay, son of Azhy—
I myself do not trust
That eloquent-tongued hero at all,
That infidel-like man
Whose wish has never been fulfilled.
Yesterday, when the people drove uphill,
At the time called At-Kara-Til,
You yourself were there, Kozho;
We took horses by their leads,
And Afghan and Zhediger
Shouted war cries and fought.
We mounted stallions from the tether line,
Took poles from the ground,
And fought for seven days.
A band of men died among the Afghans,
And a band of men died among us;
Because of Aychürök,
We made a merciless slaughter.
Azhybay came between us,
Saying, 'I accept your demand.'
He is surpassingly eloquent by nature,
And has the habit of deceiving.
There is neither Chürök nor even a morsel;
We were left sitting in disappointment,
With necks outstretched like howling dogs.
Swift-tongued, eloquent, and argumentative,
His nature is exceptionally gifted,
And he always has the habit
Of deceiving us.
Know this that I have said;
My lion Kozho, heed my words!
Let us not accept what he says;
Let us not keep babbling
With that over-eloquent dog.
Let us seize their envoys,
And upon Afghanistan lying here,
Let us stage a splendid slaughter;
Let us seize by force
The Chürök they refuse when asked!
My precious Kozho, heed my words;
Let us forge tempered steel
And overrun the accursed Afghans,
Laying them waste!
Let us smash their lattice willows,
Make their young women and maidens weep,
Smash their roof poles,
Make their sons and daughters weep,
And demolish their city.
Let us take entirely as plunder
The children in their hands,
And drive out of the Afghans
All their distant arrogance.
Let us shatter their stone city
And utterly conquer it;
Let us devastate their sandy steppe
And leave all the Afghans
Howling like dogs upon the plain,
