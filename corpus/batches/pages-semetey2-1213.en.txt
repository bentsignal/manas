When the stallion behaved this way—
We do not know the truth of it;
Much is false and much is true,
For no one who saw it has returned.
We made it a tale for diversion,
To recount the deeds
Of heroes long vanished.
Half is invention and half is truth;
No soul has come back from beyond—
Only the Creator Himself knows.
At that moment Bozaiman stood,
His tail and mane streaming apart.
He had no tongue with which to speak,
So the creature neighed,
And tears poured from his eyes.
When they settled in broad Talas
And livestock filled the valley,
Wise Sary Khan
Was out ranging with his herds.
Aimanboz, then an eight-year-old,
When dawn shone clear
And light touched the earth,
Appeared to him from the unseen.
When he reached eight years of age,
Sary Khan found him,
Tied a plume upon his head,
And kept him as a breeding stallion.
Mares covered by that stallion foaled,
And from Bozaiman himself
Came dark bay noble steeds,
With broad lungs and brazen legs,
Horses fit to ride into battle.
His tail was an armful, and full was his mane—
Such was the creature, if you measured him.
When they saw the gray stallion,
The birches wept and the willows wept,
Every living witness wept,
The earth shook and the grass wept,
And poor Külchoro wept without end.
His uncle Sary Khan,
His elder dearer than a father,
Had died while fighting,
Suffering death at the madman’s hands.
He grasped him at the waist and folded him,
Crying, “Do not die, my lionhearted uncle!”
Then he loaded him upon Bozaiman.
By the black springs of Kainar,
Beside the straight plane tree,
Below Tört-Kül,
In the garden planted by noble khans,
Facing Jylgynduu,
On the near side of Keng-Kol,
In the lower hollow of Keng-Shor,
Along Tal-Mazar,
He hacked and hollowed out
A grave-shaped place,
And hiding his face from sight,
He laid Sary Khan to rest.
Leaving none of his grief unspoken,
He tried to drive Bozaiman away.
Whinnying mournfully,
The gray stallion would not obey.
Though beaten, he balked and would not move;
The gray stallion refused.
Unable to turn his noble neck,
The gray stallion stood fast.
Like a wild ass, he turned his neck
Toward the qibla.
Though driven, he stood braying,
And the gray stallion would not move.
“Well then, if the ancient tales
Are true,
My creature, my gray stallion,
Go ahead to await my uncle Sary Khan.
Go as his companion, noble beast;
Go together with my uncle.
If you remain alive and ply the world,
No beast like you will ever leave mortal time.”
