The streaming tassel upon my spear,
From the gray wolf who went to the next world,
The Creator ordained for me alone
This gift I inherited.
In my fervor I send
A playful boy of twelve like you;
Your elder's poor life is yours in sacrifice!
Without argument, to scout the crossing—
How well suited are you, Kulchoro,
To obtain news from the caravan?
To travel as a wayfarer,
And learn what sort of enemy is there;
The red-patterned, bird-pommeled saddle
And a maiden's horse appeared—
To learn who she is?
A caravan has camped at the ford;
If you go without delay, Kulchoro,
Your needed road has appeared."
The companion, son of Almambet,
Girded on a silver belt
And turned his sun-bright face.
He was himself a fierce young man;
When his elder sent him,
How could he stand still?
His brow was high and his wrath strong;
He would never retreat even from death,
And straightened whoever challenged him.
Flying like an arrow through the mirage,
As though about to see his own in-laws,
He approached his elder.
His figure and bearing
Appeared exceptional to the eye;
His heart warmed and rejoiced.
Moreover, Kulchoro
Was a lion sustained by Saint Shay;
Were it not for divine protection,
He was a greater hero than Semetey.
At twelve years he was in his prime;
Ask nothing of the poor man's ardor—
Its heat could strike a stone.
"If you say go, elder, I shall go;
I shall obey your word.
Without refusing the direction you send me,
Elder, for your cause
I shall become a sacrifice.
I shall reach there and obtain news
Of the falcon that flew away.
If a looming division of troops comes out,
I shall attack without fleeing;
If I meet an enemy formed for battle,
I shall fight him to the end.
I shall speak words; understand them,
My gray-wolf elder, heed what I say.
I myself, Kulchoro,
When I am dead,
Am a slave in a dog's hide;
While I walk alive,
I am a son in a sheep's hide.
I was not born from the same womb,
With its belt bound firmly, as you;
I did not spring from the true loins
Of Manas, who went to the next world,
Nor did I break forth
From the womb of my dear mother Kanykey.
Where could I be a son of Manas?
As I walk under my own power,
I am the son of Almambet the Chinese!
May they not discover my servile station;
Those formidable, gossiping maidens—
May they not learn that I am a servant,
And laugh while singing taunts against me.
May your companion not go away in shame
And wander under the blow of God!
Aktelki beneath me
Has crossed many mountain passes;
The poor beast's hooves are worn blunt.
If a mountain with a high pass appears,
May I not fall from it and die!
