I stand ready to bring them low.
The true sovereign of the Khagan,
I stand ready to take his land,
And all the forty tribes
Upon the face of the earth.
For the lionhearted, magnanimous hero
I once vowed to take revenge.
If the Chinese flee in terror,
From the mouth of Keng-Jailak,
From the mountain of the golden mine,
I vowed to seize their land,
And avenge Almambet, Chubak, and Syrgak.
I vowed to settle their blood feud.
I will break through Beijing’s frontier
And proclaim, “This is Kyrgyz land,”
And raise up a khan.
After crushing the Khagan’s realm,
I intend, when Bakai has grown old,
To raise him as khan
Over the countless Chinese and their khagan.
Wheeling about and battling,
Shouting and thrusting spears,
When we turn upon the Khagan
I mean to bring him ruin.
When Kongurbay faces us,
If valiant Külchoro’s aim is true,
I mean to give him a spectacle.
The sands blaze and shimmer there;
If a cauldron is set upon the sand,
It roars and boils over.
No living creature
That encounters it survives.
Haze rises, gathering at the corners,
And dust billows through the hollows and ridges.
I will encircle the Chinese
And seize their land.
From the forty khans of teeming China
I mean to capture their armies.
From among Ulungtu’s sons,
From the mountains of Great Kaspan,
The perilous Kara-Suu—
This is the ill-fated place
Of which the ancients spoke.
From the plain of Kara-Suu,
From the mouth of Tash-Kapchal,
In the mountains of Kaspan-Tau,
There is a treasury of gold mines;
I intend to take its ore.
Kara-Tau, from which the forty Chinese tribes
Of Beijing obtain their tea—
That towering, lofty mountain—
I intend to take its land.
Wearing motley trousers and blue shirts,
Black caps and baggy breeches,
The many Chinese who look like women—
I intend to take their people.
Kara-Tau, where rubies are mined—
This place is called Kökhar-Sai.
I intend to seize
Their mules and camels as booty.
I intend to load up and carry away
The pure gold and white silver
Taken as booty from the Kakan.
I intend to smash his treasury,
Raze his city and take its gold coins,
Take its treasure as booty,
Load the gold coins upon the treasure,
And seize a thousand dark-eyed maidens.
My foe is Kakanchin;
I shall set my sights upon it,
Take it, and lay it waste.
Sary Khan’s bustling city—
I intend to lay it waste.
If I rage and exert my might,
Through the gorge where no people tread,
I shall make a road.
Beijing, beyond even the floodwaters’ reach—
My host shall reach it.
