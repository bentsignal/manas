The people knew the worth of the land;
Was it a place where anyone could remain idle?
Was it a place where men would not compete
In mass games of kökbörü,
Where young men would not immerse themselves
In every kind of pleasure,
Roaming the hills and finding gold,
Knotting their horses’ banner-like tails and racing them,
The honored champion surging ahead,
Taking up white steel and barring the road,
Wearying horses while ranging the land,
Setting loose hounds and flying hawks,
And riding proudly prancing steeds?
Could anyone fail to ride there?
Girding their slender waists,
Born as defenders of the Kyrgyz,
Fastening belts around their trim waists,
And attacking the enemy in battle—
Could they fail to strike there?
Drinking their fill of kumiss and courting girls,
Men reveling amid delights—
Could they fail to enjoy themselves there?
With black hats like cauldrons,
Raising the people’s fortress as their banner,
Could they fail to sway in dance there?
Laughing together in chorus,
Could they fail to rejoice there?
Our wondrous Talas
Has hills and mountain passes,
With countless kinds of grasses;
It has most things people need.
Feather grass and other grasses grow,
And from every mighty broad slope
Springs pour forth like gold.
It is a land where men laugh with delight
And young warriors may repose.
There livestock foal, bear young, and multiply;
It is a land where people live in joy.
Our wondrous, spacious Talas
Has forests and dense woodland,
Appearing equal to paradise.
Snow blocks its mountain passes;
Look carefully, and it possesses
Every earthly delight mankind desires.
Fox-tail grass and woodruff,
Blue wildflowers and white blossoms—
Every kind of herb is there.
Anyone who sees it is fully satisfied,
When beholding such a Talas.
It is as though a richly patterned carpet
Had been spread across Talas.
Every beholder’s heart is content;
Talas is wondrously beautiful.
It is a land where swift horses are stretched at the tether
And kept tied there,
A land of merriment by day and games by night,
Where people spend the summer,
Wearing paired sable hats
And swaying in dance,
Riding three-year-old colts and flying hawks,
Living there in happiness.
When bored, they shoot roe deer,
And play without weariness
At countless delightful games,
Taking keen, baying greyhounds
And dark goshawks—
Could they fail to hunt there?
Could they fail to immerse themselves
In rare games and pleasures?
Could they ever have their fill
Of its many entertainments and games?
Would they not seek tracks on rugged ground
And range across it?
Could they fail to immerse themselves
In rare games and pleasures?
Competing for the true prize between them,
Could they fail to shoot?
Playing freely amid delights,
Could they fail to revel?
The people played freely, and all laughed,
