He urged on Too-Kyzyl beneath him,
Making the horse stride ponderously;
A grievous sight even to contemplate,
He threw the whole world into panic.
Raising the clamor of bloody slaughter,
Enshrouding the wondrous mountains in haze,
Your infidel, vast as Mount Kap,
Armed himself and came out to fight.
The infidel Khan Sarybai
Shot and hacked through the battle;
The people panicked, unable to prevail,
And despaired of their mortal lives;
Unable to find their bearings, they fled.
When strong men’s backs were broken,
When he slaughtered all who faced him,
They fought but could not prevail;
Six times before this
They had battled without defeating him.
The poor people merely watched;
Whenever Sarybai entered the field,
The nation cried out in anguish,
Despairing of their fly-like lives.
Their courage failed and they cringed,
Wondering how many souls would be slaughtered;
Their sight dimmed and their eyes bulged
Whenever Sarybai entered the fray.
The people wailed in misery,
And languished beneath their punishment.
Against Sarybai’s venom
No human strength prevailed;
He was like the wrath of the true God,
The very image of a death-defying man,
Fierce in anger and mighty in venom,
With the black eyes of a fiend.
The people despaired of life;
Already timid, the poor folk
Grew sallow as in a wasting sickness.
They spoke together in whispers;
The poor people were crushed,
Unable to find their bearings.
Against the infidel warrior Sarybai,
Not one soul could venture forth.
The whole world was stricken with fear,
And every counsel was exhausted;
All the warriors stood helpless,
Wondering whence this monster’s son,
This infidel, had sprung.
“Which broad-necked young warrior
Will go out to face him alone?
Who will dare encounter him in battle?
Who can fathom the measure
Of this thousandfold courageous scourge?
Who, experienced and wise among the people,
Will go against this infidel Sarybai
With keen judgment?
Which great champion called a lion
Will answer his challenge with skill?
Murapy, auspicious when he raises the war cry,
Whose shout is like a thousand warriors—
Will he perhaps go?
Whoever faces him will die.”
The heroes despaired of life,
And the people crowded together, lamenting;
The entire army cried aloud.
“Warriors Seytek and Külchoro,
Unless you find some means,
Death has stalked us down;
Every expedient is utterly gone.
Can anyone match him in battle?
Can any man overcome him in combat?
Can anyone advance against him?
Can one who meets him remain alive?
How can we gain deliverance
From this black-fiendish swine?
When he rages in fury,
Even another madman like me
Will meet a death of bitter doom;
Many men who go out singly
Will be encircled and captured.”
The people despaired of life,
