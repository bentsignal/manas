How can I abandon him?"
So saying, Semetey
Pulled hard upon the reins,
But Kyldykara, fit to be slaughtered,
Into the roaring Sary-Suu,
Into the heart of Sary-Suu,
Bolted away and entered.
When he reached the water,
He fell without staying upright.
Semetey, who had deemed himself a hero,
Plunged headfirst into the water.
Enraged and grief-stricken,
There amid the water, from the Black Horse,
He was torn apart and left behind.
He bobbed along like a drifting log,
The gray wolf's head kept dipping down.
When he wrenched his precious head up,
At times it could be seen,
And when it vanished, thinking, "I am dead,"
The gray wolf's spirit faltered.
The moonless night grew dark,
The day when death would come had arrived.
On the farther bank stood the saddle-galled bay,
It was Algara himself,
For that accursed man's Black Horse
Had taken on another form just then.
Karagul, son of Katal,
When he saw Semetey,
Was at that moment wearing
A felt cloak with a golden collar.
With sparse tail and little mane,
That accursed man's Black Horse
Was the mount he rode just then.
Look closely at Karagul's conduct,
Here is the black-hearted man.
He was an exceptionally cunning slave,
A stratagem that men could know,
He was a lad who knew still more.
From the ridge the wind broke loose, glowing red,
In the sky a leaden cloud appeared,
Mist covered the ravine,
And hail struck upon the foothills.
Then Karagul
Said, "There is no living soul but me,"
And prayed to himself.
Upon the Black Horse beneath him,
He struck his whip this way and that,
And spurred him on both sides.
Such was the mischief of the raging slave,
Such was the sport he made of the boy.
Shyta Crossing, Tash Crossing—
For that shrewd Karagul,
It was a crossing perfectly familiar to him.
When Karagul cracked his whip,
Could the Black Horse fail to run?
Crushing the ground beneath his tread,
He sprang through Tash Crossing,
And Karagul came out.
When he had emerged from the water,
That fine son of Katal thought:
"It was I who opened the road
From Kakan to Bakburchun;
His father, foolish Manas,
Was a slave whom I deceived six times.
While my mighty head remains,
How could I give up my swift horse?
This orphan seems to have grown bold;
I shall try trading blows with him!
While my heroic head remains,
How could I give up my livestock?
This orphan has gone mad;
I shall try grappling with him!
Look how the fine son of the bloodthirsty man,
Fixing his hostile gaze upon this place,
Has come here!
Now, before he can so much as speak
Or utter a word before my hand,
Just watch him die!"
