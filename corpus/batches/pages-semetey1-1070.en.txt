Külchoro, left as a gift
Whose father was a lion lord,
Was a man a spear's height tall.
In the palace with iron doors,
In Aychürök's splendidly built
Fortress,
When the pledged day was fulfilled,
When the time came
For Toltoy the bridegroom to arrive,
The vast Afghan host was there,
The common people and their khan;
Khan Akun was there too,
Azhybay, son of Azhy,
Tümönbay, son of Tülkü,
And all those who gave wise counsel—
Every wise man was there.
"Khan Semetey, our son-in-law, has arrived,"
They said;
The many Afghans in the city
Had heard the news,
As had their impetuous Bek Akun.
Saying, "Lion Semetey the bridegroom
Will rescue her
From Toltoy of the Zhediger,
And raise a great tumult,"
All the Afghans gathered
And invoked Bahauddin.
If the prose tale were told on,
The tale of Choro, son of Almambet,
Would never be exhausted.
At twelve years old, that remarkable boy
Asked leave of Semetey,
And bowed as he said:
"Blood-taking elder brother, leopard,
My saint who made me your dependent,
Give me your horse no bullet can catch;
Give me your coat no bullet can pierce.
If you give me both, I shall put them on
And learn what tyranny
Arrogant Toltoy possesses!
If God's written decree
Is inscribed upon my brow, I shall see it;
I shall die while jousting
Against Toltoy of the Zhediger!
That hulking infidel Toltoy,
The hero coming as bridegroom—
Whichever of Kozho and Toltoy
Falls into my hands,
If my death has come, I shall die;
If it lies within my power,
I shall make a spectacle of them for you!
I intend to break their ranks
In a clash of spears and see;
If death comes to me from God,
I intend to die!
Whichever of Toltoy and Kozho
Falls into my hands,
I intend to make him a spectacle
And present him to you.
That consummate prince Toltoy,
That would-be brother-in-law—
Let us be drenched in bloody blood,
Let us stand and fight without fleeing,
Let us grapple from the very skirts,
Descend upon the open plain,
And bring each other to an end."
