Then she spoke these words:
“Turn your horses’ heads around;
Boys, halt at the place ahead.
I have seen a dream,
And have many duties to entrust to you.
Know that I speak with sorrow;
My dear boys, heed my words!
Your prancing prince is no baseborn man;
Do not let your swaggering lord
Set his bird upon the swan,
Or seize the white swan where it rests!
He is riding away in fierce anger;
May he go safely and return sound.
Let my eyes see my prince
Together with his noble falcon;
Bring them safely back to me.
If either one of the two is lost,
Boys, may my eyes never see again!
Do not let him cross by the broad ford,
Or let a caravan carry news of him.
Go in health and return alert;
Come swiftly, before I begin to yearn.
Go in peace and return sound;
Come soon, before I grow weary.
If you commit some wicked deed, it will be shameful;
Badisha’s Akshunkar
Is entrusted to you, boys.
If the noble falcon remains safe
And you bring it before my eyes,
If you return safely to me,
I shall agree to what you ask:
From among the slender beauties
In red dresses and crescent belts,
I shall obtain wives for you!
If you make these words of mine come true,
For the twin boys that you are,
Your poor sister-in-law Chachykey
Would sacrifice her very life!”
Your sister-in-law said this in sorrow,
Raised her voice and uttered her lament.
Semetey, the son of Manas,
Wore out horses and ranged the land;
Would that blue-maned champion heed her?
He loosed his hounds and flew his birds,
Taking fourteen swift horses,
And the two choros who followed him.
Your prince mounted in high spirits,
Crossed ridge after ridge,
And passed over broken, mottled ground.
At Jangyryk’s Jar-Kyya,
Along the winding single track,
The bloodthirsty one now arrived.
At the mouth of that ravine
A mass of white fiber lay like a band of dawn;
Your noble prince now saw it
And turned Buurul’s head toward it.
He rejoiced when he saw the fiber,
Turned toward Kanchoro and Kulchoro,
And joyfully spoke to the two:
“Kulchoro and Kanchoro—
You two are companions alike to me,
My bright stars in the sky,
The sable trim upon my collar,
My steeds that could carry me six months,
You who kicked together like foals,
Who struggled for the same breast,
My two brothers, alike in every way.
If we see the many Black Chinese and Manchu hosts,
They will seem a swarm of black worms;
If we are caught together on campaign,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
If we stand and fight without fleeing,
If we confront them face to face,
If a night of painful hardship falls
And we meet in bitter battle,
We shall need armor to wear in the struggle.
This white fiber we have found
Could be of great use to us, if you agree.”
