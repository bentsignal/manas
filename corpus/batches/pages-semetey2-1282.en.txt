Understand what I have told you;
Oh, dearest friend, heed my words!
Let us not turn aside into rebellion,
Nor raise a hand against this bloodthirsty hero
Whom Khizir has blessed.
Why assail the lionhearted hero
And needlessly become entangled in suffering?
Why remain stubborn?
Come, dearest friend, let us leave.
Let us have no part in quarrel or conflict.
This Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Is a warrior equal to us both;
Let us not attack the poor man.
He is a blazing red ember,
Unmatched among all creation;
He is a steel bullet against the foe,
A fiery champion beyond all others.
His right shoulder is broad,
And all twelve parts of his body are perfectly formed.
When an enemy presses hard upon the Kyrgyz,
He is the lion whose mere presence confronts them.
This formidable hero Külchoro
Is the warrior who receives their assault,
A fortress established for the Kyrgyz,
A defensive stronghold of iron.
Pure in thought and generous in spirit,
He is a lion who spares no effort.
Not only the Kyrgyz, but all mankind
He is one capable of defending.
Let us have no part in musket-fire or conflict;
Heed my words, dearest friend—
Let us leave without attacking Külchoro!
He is the very embodiment of a gray-wolf hero;
Why persist in defying him?
Come, dearest friend, let us leave!
Let us tell the people
That Külchoro is such a man.
Without rebellion or warfare,
Come, dearest friend, let us turn back.
Why contend in anger
With this gray wolf blessed by Khizir?
Lest there be a massacre,
Come, my friend, let us leave!
I have seen Külchoro’s form:
Tall in stature and broad of shoulder,
Both a wrestler and a lionhearted warrior—
No hero like him can be found
Anywhere in the world.
He is blue-gray as tempered steel,
Steadfast in a true massacre.
Let us not stumble into disaster
Or involve ourselves in a terrible conflict.
Let us lead the finest steeds unsaddled
And load them with yellow gold.
Let us present every swift charger
As a gift to Külchoro.
Heed me, my friend; let us turn back!
Let us consent to what he asks;
To the young champion, Almambet’s son,
Let us give whatever he desires.
Gold coins, gold, and these matchless steeds—
Let us hold none back, but offer them all.
Without grappling or fighting,
For mercy’s sake, my friend, let us turn back!
Let us become as a wind from the qibla,
And become one people with Külchoro;
Chewing flour of white wheat together,
Holding the holy book of the creed,
Licking the edge of keen steel,
Sacrificing a gray horse from the white herd,
Let us become friends for the world to come.
Like flour made from white wheat,
Let us mingle into one;
Like hairs from a white antelope,
Let us draw smoothly together.
Like the stars in the heavens,
Let us rise and move as one,
Pressing breast to breast,
