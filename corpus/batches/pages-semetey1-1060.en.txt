This one was fit to govern a people,
That one was fit to remain among them.
This one was a true man.
As the Zhediger stood so,
Raging and straining,
Tümönbay, son of Tülkü,
Jerking his black-blazed thoroughbred
As though its rider had died,
Brandishing his spear and shouting:
"I will make your mother my wife!
Come out now for single combat!"
The black-blazed thoroughbred
He reined in and turned;
Tümönbay, son of Tülkü,
Surged forward holding his spear,
And leveled it straight before him.
Tölök and Chynkozho,
Without letting Tümönbay hear,
Called Azhybay and spoke:
"You are the guardian of human honor,
Azhybay, son of Azhy;
You are one of the wise!
You are skilled in speech,
The best at loosening knots.
Judge it yourself and know yourself:
From now on do not bring near
Tümönbay, son of Tülkü,
That ill-tempered hero,
That infidel with a white devil!
Azhybay, son of Azhy,
Your honesty became clear today.
If you will give Akun's daughter Chürök,
Tell us what troubles you;
Tell us the day
Toltoy may take Chürök!
Stand beneath the banner before you leave,
Make your pledge before you leave;
Say, 'I shall certainly give Chürök!'
And put the day in writing before you leave.
Take the appointed date with you;
Say, 'I shall certainly give Chürök,'
And make the pledge before you leave!
We too will make a pledge;
Swear your dying oath before you leave!
Chew and offer the white wheat,
And hold out the oath-token.
Let brave Toltoy be the bridegroom.
Without letting a single person know,
Without letting a single person learn of it,
I shall give as her bride-price
Eight hundred black horses like hair.
For Aychürök's bride-price,
I am now preparing to bring
Before Khan Akun
One thousand piebald horses like hair.
Aychürök of Khan Akun,
The beauty whose fame has spread,
I shall take the girl through marriage alliance;
I shall take a skilled beauty like her.
If it can be managed, for Chürök
I have long been preparing for Khan Akun
Eighty red pack-loads,
Ready from the beginning;
Upon those loads are placed
Bolts of red silk,
And to set the loads right and tie them on,
I have exactly sixty servants.
The price of a single load
Is the blood-price of ninety men.
I have been preparing the bride-price;
Listen well, dear Azhy.
I too know the worth of people.
When Akun's daughter Aychürök
And Khan Akun, without speaking ill of us,
Come to the summer pasture,
I have been hoping
To surround them and make a marriage alliance.
If you give your daughter Chürök—
