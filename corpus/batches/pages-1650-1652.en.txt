Without fearing death,
Still more were rejoicing.
At the sight of the enemy,
They grew wild and strained forward,
Thrashing like a woman whose husband had died;
Six men to hold one person—
There were many more like that.
With no sense entering their breasts,
Still more were losing heart.
Bent over their spears,
Those making ready were beyond counting.
With eyes blazing like wolves’,
Biting through their tongues,
There were still more champions.
Divided into group upon group,
If you sat and watched their forms,
The varied people of imperial China
Looked as though they would suck you dry.
Riding red-roan horses,
Their long tails tied in tight knots,
Their fiercest men formed separate ranks;
Swaying across the hills,
They too had surged in.
In black robes and broad shirts,
The governors’ army gathered,
And surged upon them all together.
Bearing iron ropes and nets,
Biting through their lips,
Their eyes seething deep within,
Never releasing those they grappled with,
Never sated with human flesh,
Ajgu and Jajojo
Had surged in together.
The Chinese people came with their armies,
Overwhelming to anyone who measured them.
If you looked closely, your Muslims
Could be bound captive by that approaching host.
When you looked at the Chinese,
The earth seemed to turn and split apart.
In that coming battle,
The handful of Muslims
Scattered like frightened sheep.
When the mighty Chinese army,
That formidable host, surged in without measure,
Their bitter brine of misery was about to boil.
Through the red and mottled haze,
Arrows from bows streamed down.
“My banner on a crimson spear,
Dear Alake, keep me safe!” they said,
And sought shelter with your Alake.
Girded in golden iron,
Girded with a belt of gold,
Azizkhan’s Almambet,
Light shining on his moonlike face,
Blood suffusing his dark eyes,
Light shining on his bloody face,
His complexion like oiled wheat,
His eyes like twilight mist,
Without looking round him,
When the Chinese surged upon him,
Your valiant falcon
Did not spare his life at all.
“Against the darkening black unbelievers
I have now waged holy war,” he said.
“If God so wills, here and now
I have offered up my precious life,” he said.
“To Beijing, where the Prophet never trod,
I have ridden on holy war,” he said.
“If God so wills, here and now
I have offered up my one precious life!” he said.
Stake-necked Sarala
The noble lion made rear;
Lean-haunched Sarala
The noble lion sent wheeling.
The black-cheeked battle-axe
Hung at your Alake’s wrist;
The double-edged Zulpuqor
Was drawn ready from its sheath.
The yellow willow he had planted yesterday,
His willow still stood behind;
The throne fashioned of pure gold,
His throne still stood behind.
He scattered what he had gathered,
Seeking faith alone.
A merciless onslaught
Had he not come to unleash?
The forecast was very high
Karykan’s painted musket,
Taken as a gift from Beijing,
My renowned champion Alma
Had he not brought it with him?
When slung, the painted musket
Was the mirror of his moonlike shoulder;
When he entered battle with the shooting foe,
It was a comfort to his entrusted life.
In play, a matchlock;
In earnest, a breaker of foes.
The painted spear swayed in his hand,
The gold-mouthed painted musket
Gleamed upon his moonlike shoulder.
With brown eyes free of pale flecks,
With a fair face free of freckles,
With no dirt upon his eyelashes,
Khan Aziz’s only son,
Blue flame hissing from his mouth,
His mighty teeth, broad as a door,
Crumbling like millet grains,
“This handful of Muslims—
How can I stand and let them be taken?” he said.
“If I can find a way, the Chinese
I shall slaughter all the way to Beijing,” he said.
“Against six myriad black unbelievers
I shall exchange shots and wage war,” he said.
Your Alake entered the battle.
“Without standing by them as support,
How can I remain alive?” he said.
Khan Baltay’s Chubak—
Did you see his blood stir?
Baabedin’s Baychoro,
Warrior Shuutu, his cap as broad as a yurt covering,
Old Kyrgyl, leader of the forty,
Yraman’s son Yrchy,
Akayar, who lifted camels,
Who drank poison when told to drink water,
Who spilled stomach contents when told to shed blood,
Who took heads when told to take caps,
