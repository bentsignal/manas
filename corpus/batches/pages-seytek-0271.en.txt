Külchoro stands there crying out,
Calling for Taibuurul.
His senses gone, his heart aflame,
His whole heart burning as in fire,
He grieved in deep distress.
The dapple-gray horse he cherished
Was lost to him.
Over rugged hills and passes
The noble steed had gone.
Vanishing like a phantom,
The noble steed escaped.
However he looked, it could not be seen;
That wretched, accursed beast—
No one knew where it was.
Enduring hardship, grieving,
Parted from Taibuurul,
The tormented Külchoro
Stopped by a gateway rock
And lamented every sorrow of old.
In the yawning ravine
He poured out his regrets at length:
“When I rode the gray and made him rear,
And charged as though avenging a fallen hero,
When I donned my coat of mail
And leaned back as I struck the foe,
Why did I not die then?
When our vast wealth was safe,
When my gray-maned uncle lived,
When I crushed the Chymchyshkan
And held the frontier,
When I subdued Kakan
And put it to the torch,
Why did I not behold then
My true nature that never retreats?
I am stricken and grieving;
I have lost the one I cherished.
My lion uncle is no more,
And Külchoro has no fortune;
The noble gray he cherished
As dearly as himself is also gone.
When power slips from one’s hands
And beast and man alike wander lost,
When every living soul is seized
By wrath and grievous hardship,
Then days become like this.
When the yellow, hilly, sloping rocks
Become passes such as this,
When bleak days descend upon your head,
Then your people become like this.”
Clutching the reins, he wailed,
Sobbing and weeping in earnest.
“I girded the spear about my waist;
When seventy companies of foes came,
I charged alone among them—
Have I truly come to this?
Your back was made to ache,
Your flanks were rubbed raw,
Yet you never failed when ridden into battle—
Have you truly come to this?”
So Külchoro spoke,
And while he stood sobbing,
Before the words had left his mouth,
His heart cleft into ravines,
Then Taibuurul
Gathered in his belly and bounded,
Pounding the black earth.
The stars in heaven were eclipsed;
A dreadful wonder to behold—
He plainly escaped and fled.
Stretching his stride like an argali,
The noble steed brought shame upon them.
However they looked, he could not be seen;
No one knew where he was.
The noble steed vanished like a spirit.
When he lengthened his argali-like stride,
The creature had gone far away;
Vanishing, he had broken free,
The creature had departed.
However they looked, he could not be seen;
No one knew where he was.
