The ill-fated hide
Folded bellywise like a snake.
I gathered every seamstress
And had it sewn with tiny stitches;
I had skilled workers test it,
And lined its inside with silk.
Forty Kyrgyz daughters-in-law
Bit and tightened its seams;
I had them reject what was unsuitable,
File down steel,
And pour it between the layers.
The sight of it satisfied the eye;
I fitted and repaired it,
So a thrusting spear would not pass.
I intended it for the conflict
With hero Khan Kongurbai!
When Kandagai is gripped and pulled,
It stretches like glue;
When Kandagai is released,
It gathers like a tendon.
The sight of it satisfies the heart;
If struck with a shout,
Even a thrusting spear will not pass,
And the hand gripping it grows weary.
If an Ochogor gun fires, its bullet will not pass,
But lodges within its middle.
With a golden border at its rim,
The ill-fated Kandagai,
All of its wrist guards
Were embroidered in pure gold.
I worked twin spade-shaped ornaments into it;
So its seams would not come apart,
I wrapped them with yellow gold.
Beijing was a formidable place,
Its people wicked and dangerous;
If they stood facing one another,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
And fought with all their might,
Arrowheads would shriek,
Their points of impact burst apart,
And spilled blood gush.
Lest needless death overtake him,
And he leave this world clear-eyed
Yet filled with regret,
Costly Kandagai—
I have been making it.
My precious orphan, listen well!
Many strong men worked and softened it;
If you take it to Beijing, there will be great conflict!
Its opening has a broad border;
If you wear it in battle, my foal,
It is a shield for your entrusted life!
Receiving messages from many places,
My orphan, my support—
Wondering whether he would remain safe,
I specially kept preparing it!
The one you grapple is Kakan-Chyn;
The one you oppose is Bakburchun.
My orphan foal, listen:
Wear it when you go to battle;
The road to Beijing is a perilous road,
Face the foe and strike!”
Weeping bitterly, Kanykey
Now gave him the Kandagai.
When one looked at the Kandagai,
One saw the skill
Of your dear, formidable mother.
Kandagai was exceedingly precious,
With a worth no man could attain;
So her son might prepare and wear it,
So he might strike the foe without tiring,
The mighty queen had made
The Kandagai!
True saint Kanykey—
Most skillful of the long-haired,
The finest of women.
Dear queen Kanykey,
The purest of womankind,
