When he charged, he tore lives apart;
He also had such ferocity
That ten ranks of men could not sate him.
Yet the unreformed Choro
Also had his clumsiness.
Canopy of the shining river,
Hero of humankind,
From the broad plain where cries rang out,
From the broad ravine where no bird flew—
So spoke the hero Seytek,
And set ninety mattocks
Side by side in rows.
“You were a great lord and a lion,
You were such a gray-wolf hero!
Have your flourishing days
Passed away, my princely uncle?
After suffering days of grief and misery,
Meeting the strength of the wrathful,
And enduring so many ordeals,
Has your body weakened and your strength departed,
Has all your power grown weary and spent?
Has it left you, my princely uncle?
If you still have power and are a hero,
Summon your ancient strength.
The foe you must grapple with is Kanchoro,
Like the shoulder of a dark rocky mountain;
The hero you must contend with is Kyyaz,
Like tempered red iron.
Do not let it be said that Külchoro,
His strength gone, was killed by them.
Undertake this mighty ordeal,
My peerless and illustrious uncle,
And put your strength to the test.
If your vigor boils and your strength runs full,
If you are as you were at first,
Then wage battle.”
So spoke the hero Seytek:
“When Kanchoro turned treacherous
And drove your people from Keng-Kol,
We should have set eighty mattocks
Before that swaggering villain
And tested his strength;
Only afterward should that wretched soul
Have gone to fight the enemy.”
When he had spoken thus,
The hero Seytek, son of a champion,
To test his uncle’s power,
To measure him against Kyyaz, the hero’s son,
And send him against the foe,
To avenge his ancient wrong,
Caught and slaughtered forty barren mares
From among the horses,
And, to test Külchoro’s strength,
Set ninety stout mattocks
Together in a row.
Would Külchoro, favored by the spirits,
Shrink from the contest?
He tested his former strength.
The Choro, ready with the spear,
Still possessed his skill.
His name was known throughout the world,
And in Beijing his one name
Was inscribed in the chronicles.
His wrestling strength was extraordinary,
His heroism beyond measure;
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
A sturdy, powerfully built giant,
He raged like a tiger,
And none who fought him survived.
He charged like a wild boar,
And grappled like a snow leopard.
His stride was like a tiger’s,
And none who met him survived.
His sweeping coat measured eighty-four spans;
When he surged forward, he was crimson fire.
If you saw his appearance, it was thus:
A manifest calamity and disaster,
Terrible in majesty and mighty in venom,
A camel-built scourge
Who leveled all who wrestled him.
