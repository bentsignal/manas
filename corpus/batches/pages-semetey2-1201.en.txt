Do you still remember
How they destroyed so many of your young,
And carried your leaders off as captives?
They shattered your nation
And seized your land,
Dear to you as the ancestral mountains.
They demanded the lives of your young,
And divided your leaders among distant lands:
Some they drove to Altai,
Some they drove to Kanghai;
Half of us went to Eyren,
And most of us fell into an abyss.
Many of us went among the Kazakhs,
And most of us fell into misery.
Then his father, the hero Manas, arose
And came yesterday to settle in Talas.
When your Manas became the great Manas,
When he donned his invulnerable coat,
Mounted his horse with legs spread wide,
And rode his steed Akkula,
No land remained where Akkula had not trod.
No nation remained whole
That Khan Manas had not conquered.
That cherished land of Jetisuu
Manas gave you and made your homeland.
Delivering you from the infidel,
Greathearted Manas made you a nation.
How can we, his people, march against
The gray-maned son of Manas?
How can we betray the salt
Of the gray-maned hero now gone?
Have you no shame, Kyrgyz people?
How can we attack his son?
With what face can we now strike
The bloody-handed hero who shielded so many?
Let us cross the foothill ridge;
Come, people, let us divide and flee
Without attacking or assailing the hero!
Here, let us cross the Jorgo Pass;
Come, people, let us flee without approaching him!
Let us cross the Beshtash Pass;
Come, people, let us flee without confronting him.
How can we heed a slave false to his salt
And attack the hero from the side?
How can we heed that arrogant dog
And strike the gray-maned lion?”
The Jediger halted without crossing
The saddle-backed ridge;
The Jediger dared not advance
Against the gray-maned son of Manas.
Though Kanchoro and Kyyaz shouted,
Calling on them to surge forward,
They wondered how they could dishonor
Manas, who had gone to the other world,
And the Jediger would not attack.
While the Jediger still held back,
Before another word could be said,
In the time it takes to blink,
From the meadows of Jylgynduu and Keng-Kol,
From the lower reaches of Talas,
An astonishing cloud of dust billowed up,
With shouting mingled in the dust.
When they listened to the shouting,
The war cry “Manas!” rang out.
The sight stirred every heart:
A figure loomed like a mountain.
Like the depths of a narrow river,
His valor was beyond compare;
He resembled an angel of death.
His brows were like a crouching hound,
His head was as great as a cauldron,
And upon each of his two shoulders
Two giants might have perched.
He looked able to slaughter all he met;
“Manas!” was his battle cry,
And his full eyes were broad as bowls.
His manly strength was immense;
Once he seized a foe, he never let him go,
