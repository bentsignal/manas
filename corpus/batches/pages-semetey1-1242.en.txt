His messenger has come—give an answer!
If you are a man, Khoja, reply!
Leveling spears and shouting,
How many souls came out of Batkal,
Raising the battle cry of Manas.
Riding a red-roan horse,
Its horsehair tail tied tight,
A ruddy young boy
Came shouting and thrust his spear.
When he reached me and thrust,
I pitched backward from my saddle;
My foot slipped from the stirrup.
My eyes darted wildly;
My legs hung loose.
The spear struck my rump;
From the boy who speared me,
The reins fell from his hand—
That alone saved me.
Had his reins not fallen to the ground,
He would have drawn the line,
Made a spectacle,
Spilled my blood,
And taken my life.
Riding a black-roan horse,
Its tail gripped and tied tight,
A dark-complexioned young boy
Suddenly appeared in my path
And brought calamity upon me.
Khan Manas at their head,
Crying, ‘Seize the slave, seize him!’
‘Catch him and bind his hands!
Gouge out both his eyes
And drive him like a donkey!
The vile man who would take a wife—
Seize him, seize him, seize him!
Though the swine begs, do not relent;
Kill him wherever he is caught!’
Bakai urged them onward.
Beyond him,
Among the horses were blaze-faced tulpars;
All forty companions were there,
Almambet and Chubak among them.
The boy beneath him
Had taken up the urging cry;
Chubak of Akbaltai
Raised the shout.
His brow was high, his venom strong;
He set right whoever attacked him.
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
The twelve-year-old Külchoro,
A sturdy, full-limbed stripling,
Whose heroism was unlike another's,
Whose strength was extraordinary.
The deep pit dug for me,
My bitter brine thus brought to the boil—
He showed my eyes
A wonder no man could accomplish.
When we migrated from Talas,
God-struck Chynkojo,
You bore down like a heavy load then.
Your witness taken from Talas—
Saying, ‘I shall seize his wife,’
You nearly reached my root.
When I said I would not go, you refused;
You left me no choice but to go.
You brought corruption upon my head
And meant to show me a spectacle.
You caused accursed Toltoy to be slaughtered;
Saying you would seize the bride
Of blessed lion Manas,
You brought anguish upon my head.
I heeded your words
And went to behold
Humiliation no man had seen.
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Your deed was bold, Khoja.
My blustering hero,
My infidel whose mother is forsworn,
