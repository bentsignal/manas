All that has come down from the forefathers,
He knew with stern attention.
That dark Kyrgyz still rides on.
Upon Beijing’s golden throne,
Where its people were established in splendor,
Saying China would suffer calamity from the Burut,
Saying any man who grappled with him would die.
From the Kyrgyz farther beyond,
It was inscribed under a vanished rule.
Saying, “I shall take the Burut,”
When they heard this news,
The khans rode out from Kaken.
The lion never turned back.
Of the Burut who joined the battle,
From his harsh and grievous lament,
He pondered deeply and took a horse.
The people of Beijing once again,
The dark warrior Kozhojan,
A marksman like you rode out.
Not one soul turned back.
They prepared for the great ordeal,
For the grave face of hardship.
He entered it on that register.
Understand what we have said.
Behind you stood only the marksman.
My strength was no mere child.
You were a champion and a hero.
You were the lamb that turned the flock.
You were the gray-maned hero himself.
No bullet pierced what he wore.
No mane could overtake what he rode.
Those who heard the name Burut,
If the champion committed his people,
The stratagems of mighty Beijing,
The champion would spill blood like embers.
You were livestock standing at the stake.
