He seized the woman by the buttocks,
With her forty daughters-in-law at their head,
And swooped upon her like a hawk.
Kulchoro was a tiger indeed:
Her poor hair, thick as sedge,
He gathered firmly in his hand.
The woman who had answered back
Kulchoro caught at once
And began lashing with resounding blows.
Her dress was torn apart,
Her fat rump laid bare,
And the dark blood driven from her
Poured down her sides.
Chachykey's forty daughters-in-law
All fled away shrieking:
"He has slaughtered our sister-in-law!" they cried,
All of them sobbing bitterly.
Her flesh split in pieces broad as platters;
Once the whip had struck her,
Her blood streamed down in rivulets.
Terrified, that sister-in-law of yours
Cried out and told the truth:
"She changed herself into a white swan,
And carried away the falcon.
Calling her 'the shrew Aychurok,'
I drew the fateful line;
That black-faced Aychurok
Has played this wondrous trick.
The elders know Churok;
She was the one who began the tumult.
Last night, on the holy night of Friday,
On the Thursday evening,
We stood casting stones at one another
And fought without yielding.
Aychurok flew out over the land,
Surely to test the thoroughbred,
And has carried away
Semetey's falcon."
I shall drive seven hundred red camels,
And livestock enough to cover the earth.
If I do not braid every tail,
If this year I do not behold
The face of Akun, to whom I am betrothed;
If I do not drive seven thousand
Bay horses as the sister-in-law's gift,
And well-fed gray camels
Whose trailing breast hair sweeps the ground;
He had a hand strong enough to take her,
And he was himself the khan of Afghanistan.
If I do not give them as her bride-price,
If this year I do not behold
The face of blessed Akun;
If I do not win for my hero Semetey
The noble-born Churok,
That serene beauty—
The knowing speak at length,
Saying, "Churok is the fairy's daughter";
The ignorant repeat tales,
Saying she has stirred turmoil
Throughout the whole wide world.
Those who beheld her beauty
Made claims upon Khan Akun
For Churok from every foreign land.
"Give us your grown daughter," they said;
None who heard the report let the matter rest,
And countless giants pressed their claims.
Even then, it was she
Who kept poor Akun's head unharmed.
Many times we conquered Afghanistan,
That sprawling black-walled city;
The great khan who gave his word
Possessed sacred authority.
Send my greetings to Semetey.
Tell him plainly, without reserve,
That his hero's Akshunkar
Was carried away
To the city of Akyn Khan.
That was Churok herself.
