From Beijing, though it had fallen into ruin,
The mighty could wrest no victory!
There is a teeming Chinese people;
When we ask the elders,
Everyone who challenges them—
They say it is their custom to drink their blood!
Going to confront Beijing,
Many renowned khans have died;
When we ask those who know,
They say all the heroes died!
Beijing is no easy land,
And the Chinese are no people upon whom to take revenge.
They are established as the Kakanchin,
A wing of grievous calamity;
It is their custom to drink the blood
Of everyone who challenges them!
Their chief, Tungsha, lies there;
Great Beijing and its many peoples—
Such is their tumult!
They answer only to themselves,
They say, those imperious Chinese;
Though ears have heard and eyes have seen,
No mortal man has conquered Beijing.
The people of Beijing are forty tribes,
Scattered in forty directions;
Of worldly abundance,
To stand as its pillar,
Almighty Allah the Creator made them!
To make war
Upon radiant Beijing—
I, a wretched and fierce-tempered woman,
Have had my good fortune consumed!
To say you will conquer
Machyn, Kakan, and Bakburchun,
To say you will make war
On the eminent, ancient Chinese people—
That is you consuming my good fortune!
Understand every word I say!
Do not scorn me as a woman;
Lion prince, heed my words!
When I asked about Beijing's nature,
I learned the Creator had made it strong:
It has a fortified city around it.
Of radiant Beijing,
Within every one of its cities
There is also a carpeted garden!
Every kind of blessing
Was spread throughout broad Beijing;
Until the end of the age,
No one shall lay a claw upon it—
Almighty Allah, the one God,
Created it with this promise!
Great Beijing, blessed as paradise—
Can a man conquer it in war?
Look at its multitudes, like black worms!
Esteemed Beijing, land of the Kakanchin,
No mortal man has seen the whole of it;
Gracious Allah created
That teeming nation!
As I have heard from learned tradition,
The Flood's surging waters did not reach it;
Though slaughter lasted ninety years,
Its sacred power did not depart!
Its people were not routed and scattered;
Though famine lasted ninety years,
Its people's bellies did not hunger!
The land you face is Beijing;
My hero, my prince, heed my words.
You have taken the road to a stronghold,
Loading provisions on the finest tulpar!
Let us grieve and suffer;
Though I am a woman, stay awhile,
And let us say all we have to say!
My prince, if you go to Beijing, you will die;
When will you see this wretched woman again?
If you go to Kakan, you will die;
When will you turn and come back,
To the lady who cherishes you—
