He raced along the rugged slope.
The bay horse flew at a gallop,
And Aichürök upon his back
Despaired of her fragile life.
The white reins, knotted short,
Strained rigid in her hands when pulled.
The hostility of the masterless brute
Now plainly revealed itself.
He thundered onward like a flying cloud,
And wherever his hooves struck, the black earth
Burst apart on every side.
Truly, Chürök’s
Fragile soul cried out in terror.
He bolted wildly, snorting,
And raced headlong, bent on mischief;
That great bay horse, a veritable scourge,
Flew like a cloud.
“If I turn him aside, I am dead;
In this relentless place
I have seen what Tootoru can do,”
Thought Chürök, her soul nearly departing.
Tootoru bolted away,
Bringing cruel havoc;
He ran without swerving,
For Tootoru was a beast indeed,
While Aichürök upon his back
Despaired of her precious life.
Er Kyyaz’s bay horse Tootoru
Plainly showed his malice.
“If this bay horse has his way,
He will carry me into a fire-pit
That scorches the feet, and hurl me in;
He will carry me into a misery
No one can cross and escape;
He will carry me into a snare
From which no trapped soul can emerge;
He will carry me where the clear sky is veiled,
Into the enemy host, and cast me down.”
The noble but ill-fated woman
Thought long and hard:
“Those bulging eyes of yours—
I shall kill you myself!
If I cannot settle your account,
You accursed, demon-ridden beast,
If I cannot beat the frenzy out of you,
And dash your hide against the stones!
I shall subdue this woman,
Carry her off, and take her.
I shall bend the back and neck
Of this woman who thinks herself so strong;
I shall bring her untimely death,
And utterly thwart all her schemes;
I shall plunge her into chaos
And take my vengeance at last!”
So thinking, the charger
Lowered his head to the ground
And lengthened his stride beyond all measure.
His soles thundered upon the earth,
The bit of black steel
Clanged deep in his throat;
His mane and tail streamed wide,
His forelegs stabbed the ground,
His forelock parted across his brow,
And in the roaring brute
The marks of a true charger appeared.
His ribs drew taut,
His hooves bit into the ground,
His spine jutted out,
And his great bones bulged
As he raced onward thus.
He meant to twist his neck like a stag,
Swerve toward a pit as deep as a man is tall,
And cast himself into it,
To bring outright death
Upon moon-faced Chürök,
To find some means to destroy her
And wreak his fury at last.
