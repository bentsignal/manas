One glance showed a thousand men.
Toltoi, who had taken up his sword—
The sword came out of its sheath.
When he saw the prowess
Of the child blessed by Kydyr,
Dear hero Toltoi’s
Fly-like soul shriveled.
The Forty Saints and Kydyr Ilyas,
Many mighty warriors,
Surrounded the boy.
When he saw their support,
God bound the hand
Of your mountain-like Khan Toltoi.
To strike with the sword,
His great hand had been raised;
God struck Toltoi,
And with the sword in his right hand,
Fixed it above his head.
However he shook it, the dear hand would not fall—
God had struck Toltoi.
Arbuurul, flying like an arrow,
Ran and vanished;
Behind hero Toltoi,
Young prince Külchoro
Regained his line and pursued.
Toltoi truly burned with grief;
When he saw Choro’s form,
Hero Toltoi started in fear
And despaired of his fly-like life.
“His raised armpit,
The gland at the center of the armpit—
If I can wound that gland,
For Toltoi standing there,
It will be the moment his life departs.”
He turned Buurul’s head,
And at the wild-eyed Toltoi
He leveled his spear once more.
Saying, “At the armpit itself,”
The young leopard prince
Drove his spear again.
Missing the armpit, Syrnaiza
Glanced upward and passed;
It grazed
The blue flesh of his neck.
Just as he was about to spear Toltoi,
Just as he was about to cast him into hell,
He gripped and swung Syrnaiza;
But the white reins soaked in dye
He had let go free—
His youth became apparent.
The reins soaked in dye,
As though misfortune had struck,
Caught around the pastern,
As though hobbling him;
Taibuurul stood still,
Shaking his head, unable to move.
Taibuurul refused to step.
God had struck the boy;
Buurul stood entangled.
Külchoro flew like an arrow,
Turning in fury,
But the oversized great reins
Wrapped around the hock;
Buurul stood unable to move.
Not knowing the enemy’s craft,
God had struck Choro.
Külchoro was twelve;
He had not yet known an enemy’s worth,
Had not yet driven an enemy stumbling.
Before this, Külchoro
Had never ridden out and struck a foe.
Külchoro struggled frantically,
His brow tightening in strain;
Unable to find his bearings,
He fought with Taibuurul.
“A plane tree left without water—
Unable to catch hero Toltoi,
Our fortune has been bound.”
