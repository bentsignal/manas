The seer entered, denouncing the boy
And recounting all he had seen:
“Prince Kong, lord of all the world,
Great and merciless sovereign!
Mightier than all the Khakan’s realm,
We have chosen to bring you a word,
Wondering, my khan, what may come of it;
We have prepared this counsel for you.
If this counsel is good,
Heed it and say, ‘Agreed.’
If this counsel is bad,
Then spare not one of our wretched lives—
Slaughter us all!
Our champion, our lion,
Do not walk into disaster.
Brave Kalcha hero,
Look closely at this accursed one
Whom you call your kinsman.
The stranger shows in his speech,
The outsider shows in his eyes.
Deceit is present in his words,
And distance is there in his gaze;
If this kinsman finds the strength,
He means to strike you down himself;
If this boy finds the means,
He means to cast you aside.
Can anyone from the Kyrgyz be your kin?
Can one who did not spring
From the farthest reaches of your China prosper?
Could this scoundrel from the Kyrgyz
Truly be your kinsman?
He will lay hands upon your treasure;
If this accursed Kyrgyz succeeds,
He will bring about your end.
Hero, will your coat of mail save you?
Heed my words, Kalcha:
Can one raised elsewhere be true kin?
Will a foreign coat ever fit you?
Can an outsider become your brother?
Double mail with lark-eye rings
Will not part when struck by a patterned spear;
This kinsman of yours who came from the Kyrgyz
Does not appear Chinese in form.
Mottled red and dun cloth
Will not part though we tear at every edge;
If by chance he falls into Chinese hands,
This man of yours does not look likely to flinch.
The armpit of double-linked mail
Will not part though we pull it wide;
This kinsman whom you so desire,
If he falls into Kalmyk hands,
Does not look likely to turn back.
If your kinsman is a trickster,
He will deceive you and wear your coat,
Then in the end bring calamity upon you.
This Kyrgyz has grown cunning;
Will he not keep calling himself my kinsman?
My true prince, think this over:
Will he not trick you out of your thoroughbred?
Once your thoroughbred falls into his hands,
Will he not drive you utterly mad?
If your kinsman is genuine,
Will he not still deceive you and wear your coat,
And drive your host downhill?
If your Kyrgyz survives,
He will turn his spear in his hand;
Once he learns the secrets of your camp,
He will destroy your settlement.
He will watch and learn your whereabouts;
If this Kyrgyz survives,
Will he not drive you all the way to Beijing?
If your kinsman learns your secrets,
Will he not raid and scatter your people?
By deceiving us, saying he has become kin,
Will he not settle his score in this way?
Your Kyrgyz will use honeyed words;
Do not let him deceive you into eating your food,
Coming here and saying, “I have become your kinsman,”
