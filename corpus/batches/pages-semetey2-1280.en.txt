And if tomorrow the new generation
Should seek rest, it will stir up strife.
They will bind their pinewood spears
And wage war with hatred fixed in their hearts.
Think on this, Kanchoro:
To our right are the Russians,
On your flank are the Chinese,
Before you stand the riflemen;
Many enemies are waiting
To contend with us.
Why should we fight to the bitter end
Against this deeply rooted gray wolf?
Come, let us leave, Jediger.
If word that the Kyrgyz are slaughtering one another
Should reach the Chinese,
If their moon-crested banners fill the land,
If a clamor rends the sky,
If dust flies into the heavens
As their army comes swarming,
Who will wield the blood-dark, keen-edged sword
Without letting it waver?
Who will lead this great Kyrgyz nation,
Keeping the infidel’s wrath at bay
And sparing the people grief?
That gray wolf Semetey is not dead;
That noble man has not entered
The true abode from which none return.
When the bloodthirsty foe thrust his spear,
I believe Semetey was merely wounded;
Vanishing beyond the eye’s sight,
I believe he passed into the unseen world.
If death has not yet reached him,
If by chance he remains alive,
May the great host of the Kara-Jediger
Not meet utter destruction together;
May he not slaughter us all.
May he not mount his dapple-gray horse in fury,
Nor suddenly turn back
And punish the rebels without mercy;
May he not charge with a shout and lowered spear
And pitilessly slaughter the people.
Jediger of Jeti-Özön,
May the offspring of that enraged lion
Not come down upon our necks;
May he not scatter our four kinds of livestock.
If by chance he grows furious,
If rage should darken his face,
May he not break our ranks.
Great host of the Kara-Jediger,
Let us not assail the wrathful lion,
Become needlessly entangled in disaster,
Be caught in a raging flood,
And have so many of us slaughtered;
Let us not perish in a massacre,
Nor let so many of us become a spectacle;
Let us not die under oppression.
When I consider all that has passed and all to come,
Kanchoro, son of Chubak,
My friend, when I weigh your every intention,
There is something strange in your words,
And something singularly quarrelsome in you.
Your words are evasive;
When I measure and watch your conduct,
I find the quarrel lies within you.
This brave Semey, son of the bloodthirsty hero—
And you, Kanchoro, son of Chubak—
When you were orphaned of your father,
He cherished you more than his own kin.
Kanykey gave you her left breast,
You infidel slave, and nursed you;
Semetey was magnanimous
And regarded you as his own nestmate.
Keeping you beside him, he made you a warrior,
Making you the equal of grown champions,
A lion capable of shaking the world;
He raised your little soul to greatness,
And set your wretched life aright.
He clothed you in armor no bullet could pierce,
