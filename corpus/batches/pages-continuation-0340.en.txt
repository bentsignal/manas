Then Er Kogoy
Bent at the waist from fighting on foot.
His frail life was pressed hard;
His strength failed and calamity came.
It became a night that blinded the eyes,
A day of disaster to behold.
Not letting his wound-up turban fall,
Not letting Er Odu take him,
He fought beyond all measure—
Who had ever seen such a deed!
Blood flowed from his dark eyes;
Poor Kogoy was bewildered.
His two hands that had served him
Could no longer rise and were paralyzed.
His precious head, the lock of life,
Became a red bucket of blood.
He lost consciousness and bled,
His strength gone, his body weak.
His dear eyes seemed about to close,
Drooping and hanging dimly.
Accursed pale Death
Was seen approaching his brow.
Odukan stood there thus.
A prince among his Kaldays,
A cruel prince who had seen much,
A slave-marksman who never missed,
Proud of his marksmanship,
Hid behind him.
“Aim between the forked shoulders;
The face of the skull is the mark,
The very center of the measure.”
He set a bullet as large as a sheep's head
Firmly against the sight,
Bound it to certain death,
And struck, splitting his head.
His soul left before the smoke cleared;
The fatal bullet struck his temple.
Our poor elder Kogoy
Had departed for Mecca.
Seeing this, Ebegey cried:
“Rather than lose my comrade
And let the Kyrgyz be cast into fire,
Rather than lose one of my horns
And remain a hornless beast,
Rather than lose mane and tail
And remain a stunted thing,
May I sink unborn into the deep;
May I die and not remain alive!
My figure before me,
My watchful Kogoy,
My bannered mountain ridge
No enemy caravan could cross,
My greatest lion in time of need—
Let me die for my twin!
My truly created golden soul,
If the Creator truly wills it,
I will give up this fly-like life.”
Thus Ebegey spoke,
Whipping the gray racer Aksutkul
From side to side,
Driving him right and left.
He struck the stallion's flank
And shouted, “Comrade!”
As he attacked Er Odu.
They stood face-to-face,
Loosened the mouths of their steeds,
And without frowning or knitting a brow,
At once thrust their spears.
Their spears shattered against each other;
They wearied through many days.
All the blood was swallowed up;
Their impenetrable armor, impossible to seize,
Was torn into shreds.
They waged a fierce battle.
The two giants struck each other
With iron-headed maces.
They covered the hilltop in blood
And every eyelash in dust.
Unable to overcome each other,
Their strength only increased.
They seized each other's collars,
Wrestled and grappled,
And made an astounding spectacle.
Choyun Alp, worth ten Odukans,
A devastating giant,
A champion of the royal treasury,
With muscles like a bull's back,
Able to devour those who resisted,
Arrived and joined the attack.
He swung his axe wildly
At your ancestor, father of the people,
And clubbed him right and left.
When your heroic elder could find no escape
And was caught between the two,
The swift hero Jeljetpes
And the accomplished champion Er Kenen
Charged together.
