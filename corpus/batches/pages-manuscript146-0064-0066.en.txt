Kakansan grew wrathful,
We shall bring slaughter upon China.
Such was the testament that was made,
This is what the ear heard.
Over the eighteen thousand worlds,
There is the Heaven-khan in Beijing.
Kakansan grew wrathful,
Over the eighteen thousand worlds,
Go and declare your grievance before them.
You have many words already spoken,
In Chañu, which was laid waste,
At the corner where corrupt words were uttered,
You have a name that can bring ruin.
Over the eighteen thousand worlds,
If you grow wrathful, you will prevail,
If you refuse the words that are spoken,
If they will not walk the road appointed,
The great people of Beijing,
When the warrior speaks in anger,
If you lash the world onward,
Look upon it and act,
You have put it to the test; know this.
Take the word of Heaven from abroad,
He gained the land without making a raid,
He took the people who had been overrun,
The nation’s inherited wealth is a tethering stake,
Heroism requires a people,
To range abroad requires land.
The people are dear, the nation is a pledge,
What the ear has heard and the eye has seen,
We shall divide it as an inheritance for the people.
Kakansan grew wrathful,
All of the wide world,
You will see gathered together.
Saying this, Zhan Zhetpes continued,
Saying that he had become a sovereign in peace.
One from each and every people,
Saying, “Appoint one from among the envoys,”
Over the eighteen thousand worlds,
Saying, “Let Chyng Temir be the padishah,”
Send an envoy between us,
Without clashing or quarreling,
Take counsel and testimony from the people.
If you clash and quarrel,
His valor is not greater than the people’s,
Your thunder is not so great,
Do not turn this into a reckless affair,
What I have said to you is no great thing.
Bow to one who bows,
He is one of a heroic people,
They are a people who know cultivation,
My lord, his road and passage are open,
He is one of a heroic people.
From one who stands as a shield for the people,
Whose forefathers were heroes,
One who takes his stand beneath the banner.
What can excuse the multitude,
Who led a people as vast as a multitude,
Is hardship the fault of the sword,
From your eye that sees the distant target,
From one who foresees the people's fate,
Closing our eyes and saying that God will come,
If he should see it by chance,
If he acts rudely without warning,
The true banner named by Tengir,
Saying he will see it above the house.
Let us set out far, far away,
Let us remain and draw close,
Bulls, rams, and camels of every sort,
Let us name the fine ones we behold.
Let us measure him carefully and make him rue it,
Seeing the raven of power,
The finest knower of stratagems,
