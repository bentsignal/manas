And fight Kanchoro.
In Semetey's hands
He had been a darling to cradle.
If you ask about Karadöö,
“What will my future be?” he wondered—
This was the omen he chose:
“If my divination proves true,
I shall slaughter Küzhürmön.
If my divination is false,
I shall die at the wrestler's hand.”
So saying, Karadöö
Whipped the gray five-year-old beneath him
To right and left,
Spurred it on both sides,
And turned the champion steed's head.
Facing Küzhürmön,
He thundered forward and lowered his spear.
Before anyone could say a word,
In the blink of an eye:
“The pommel of the Akkangky saddle—
If my spear slips beyond it,
Let it strike the crown of his very heart!”
He charged and struck.
God forbid anyone should behold it:
Dust whirled everywhere,
And the whole arena ran with blood.
To see what would become of Karadöö,
The people cried out and filled the ground.
Such was the deed they saw.
As the two fought with spears,
Their spears shattered noisily;
White splinters from the spear points
Flew buzzing into the sky.
The brutally striking spears
Snapped across the middle.
The stirrup straps
Were sliced to little pieces.
Küzhürmön and Karadöö
Fought a mighty spear contest;
The heroes staked their lives
And crashed against each other.
The people roared in tumult,
The women raised a black lament.
Karadöö and Küzhürmön
Met with spears extended;
Neither hero retreated,
And they smashed together.
Each sought a warrior's stratagem;
When their spears were spent,
They hewed at each other with battle-axes.
The Alangir bow and painted arrow—
Told aloud, it is a dreadful tale.
All their axes shattered,
Leaving only shafts in their hands.
The people cried out in tumult;
The nation shouted in uproar.
The blessing of a holy man had touched him:
The hero Kutu of the Zhediger,
Karadöö's adoptive father,
Galloped the Surbuzul forward,
Turned toward Karadöö,
And handed him a fresh spear:
“The fur collar of my coat,
My foal, hero Karadöö!
Be among those who survive;
Fight the mighty wrestler Küzhürmön
Without rest for seven days!
If you are a hero, be a bullet;
If you are brave, be fierce!
Stand firm and give no ground;
Strive for honor and every stratagem.
Ah, Karadöö, my foal,
A bay colt fit for pursuit
Is not born from an ordinary mare;
When your father grows forlorn,
No son like you, Karadöö, will be born.
Stand face to face,
And strike your pole with all your might!
