Who transformed into a white swan,
And carried it away.'
Hearing the truth, he shouted,
The lion-born Kulchoro,
Calling his elder, Khan Bakai:
'Akun is his father-in-law,
Aichurok is his wife.
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Must have caused great strife.
Unable to endure it, my sister-in-law,
Roaming the whole world,
Wandering across all the earth,
Must have become distracted,
And carried off the white falcon.
She must have achieved her wish.
"Let him come if he is a man,"
She must have taken it to Oogan.
I heard certain news from my sister-in-law,
I heard, "Aichurok took it."'
Then leopard Bakai,
The saint protected from his origins by Shai:
'What you say is true.
Khan Akun, khan of Oogan,
He and the bloodthirsty hero,

They poured through the roof opening.
There was a promise they had made,
They had sworn brotherhood before God.
His elder, old Bakai,
Tell him to heed me without humiliating me.
The Kyrgyz and the Argyn,
Gathering a very great army,
A multitude beneath which the earth bends,
Tell him to go only then.
Many make demands that cause strife,
Many enemies are ready to clash.
If he disobeys, there will be much sorrow.
Let my only one heed me;
Many enemies stand confronting him.
If my only one has sense,
Wise and resourceful,

Jamgyrchy finds a way;

On the road to Oogan,
There is Kambyl-Bel itself,

When Kulchoro heard this,
He said this to Kanchoro,
He spoke of a scheme no one could know:
'Ah, elder Kanchoro!
Must not a mortal die?
What God lays upon his head,
Must not his frail, entrusted soul endure?
With Khan Bakai grown old,
With Jamgyrchy grown old,
With Sarykan past his time,
Bolokbai will suffer hardship.
As though we were giving them as bride-price,
Taking the old men along,
What desire shall we achieve?
