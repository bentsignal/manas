May calamity rise before you, Aydar!
May the masses dig your pit, Aydar;
May dogs tear your flesh, Aydar;
Who would wish to see your face, Aydar!
May Kalmyks eat your head, Aydar;
May infidels eat your feast, Aydar!
Who would listen to your words, Aydar?
Let a single dark night listen, Aydar!
When force came, your chance to come—
Did you not see from the beginning
Beijing where blood was spilled, Aydar?"
Bloodthirsty Manas swelled,
And his thirst for blood seized him.
Flame hissed from his mouth;
With white teeth like a gate,
Your tiger, bloodthirsty Manas,
Gnashed them section by section.
He drew Zulpukor from its sheath;
Young Aydar's face paled and changed,
His forelock spread wide,
And he fell beneath the boy.
As he brought the sword into line,
As he was about to slaughter
Young Aydar with the spreading forelock,
Dear lady Kanykey,
The beauty of the dark-gray people,
The glory of the Muslims—
If you had seen your sister-in-law,
Your mother of surpassing wisdom!
Her white throat trembled,
Her white breast gleamed;
Forty keys in a row
Rang in the pouch at her waist,
And your sister-in-law shone.
Her dawn paled like an argali's,
Her white breast showed;
Before the magnanimous lord,
She spoke softly in sorrow.
Sweet-worded and sharp-eyed,
Fragrant perfume rising, twisting her brass ornaments,
She interceded and begged for his blood.
Her dear quail-like eyes
Shed separate streams of tears;
Her dear currant-black eyes
Shed tears a whip-length long.
Moving gracefully in grief,
Holding him dear,
She pleaded with her lord:
"Can one who is alone strike another alone?
Can striking a lone man prosper?
He is no dark, rugged mountain
That you cannot cross,
No enemy come from the Kalmyks,
No costly crag of a mountain,
No enemy come from China.
What you do is not right;
Please stand without striking him.
If your sword struck the whole age, would it be sated?
If you rend him because he is alone,
Will every hollow in you be filled?
He too is one father's only son;
Can you make him a wretched captive?
Good heavens, can killing a messenger prosper?
If you rend the lone man,
Will your sack be filled by this?"
She poured out many words;
Kanykey grasped the sword,
Her tears spilling.
"Will they not call you witless,
Saying you killed an intercessor?
If the saying is true
That one of seven days is death,
At dawn on the day of Mahshar,
Will they not ride you as a black donkey?
From a gorge with a divided ravine—
My lord, you were the nest's only one.
Were there no racehorse Akkula,
There is no steed to bear and carry you;
