Proclaimed an age like the end of time.
I have seen men and giants,
I have seen nations of every kind,
I have seen champions and lions,
I have seen a man-born hero
Who was the pillar of the whole world.
I wrestled with the mighty,
And contended with devourers of multitudes;
I was wounded and struck by bullets,
Yet never saw fury like this,
And always returned alive and well.
Now my strength is exhausted, my power gone;
Have I withered like white tumbleweed?
At my departure, treacherous world,
Have you truly diminished me?
My heart and head became embers,
I burned, I blazed, I became fire;
My courage fled and drained away.
The enraged Sarybai
Seemed about to seize and hurl aside
The fierce lion born of the magnanimous hero.
Sarybai, son of the ogress,
Flamed like a demon haunting the summer pastures,
As though he would swallow any soul who approached.
If you grappled with him,
He seemed certain to crush your precious life;
When he strained in his madness,
He seemed able to destroy the whole world.
You created him long ago,
Made him a lion greater than Mount Kap,
And in the end, with Sarybai,
You made Sarybai a hero,
But laid the stately princely lion
Level with the black earth!
I burned, I blazed, I became an ember,
I was seared, consumed, and brought to a halt.
Once a cliff, I became level ground;
Unable to prevail against Sarybai,
I became a man bereft of spirit.
Among all mortal sons ever born,
If told to drink water, I drank poison;
If challenged to battle, I spilled blood.
Through many clashes with condemned foes,
I broke many ribs.
Called the warrior son of Manas,
I was mightiest of all creation;
Unable to prevail against Sarybai,
My strength failed and I was disgraced.
You made all twelve parts of my body whole,
And from the time of my earliest forefathers
Made me a spirit-aided lion;
Yet unable to overcome the wrathful foe,
You have cast me into this very disgrace.
You made me a hero before all others,
Yet in the end you laid me
Level with the black earth.
Scorching the face of the land,
Will Sarybai, son of the ogress,
Seize the land where I was born?
Will all our descendants be slaughtered,
And the Kyrgyz remain as plunder?
He is the accursed one himself,
The very eye of wrath,
The moment when true death arrives,
The very calamity itself.
He comes filling every pass
And every mountain saddle;
With every step Sarybai
Comes on ever stronger.
He rumbled and raged,
Growing stronger and more terrible;
Courage departed from the hero,
And this vain created world
Seemed a handful of lies.
My dear ones are bleeding,
My stalwarts are weakening,
My wild colts have grown timid,
My lion warrior stands bewildered.
How many times have I faced
An infidel in the fullness of his strength?
