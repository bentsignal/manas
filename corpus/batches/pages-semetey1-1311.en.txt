Thunderously thrust his spear.
From the other side Külchoro
Also readied his spear.
Your warrior born a lion
Charged and thrust his spear.
Spurring on in fury,
The lion-prince reached him.
Skillful Toltoy, your blue-maned wolf,
Also drove his spear.
They stood without fleeing and jousted,
Meeting one another with spears;
When they faced each other,
They were utterly battered.
Their spears could avail them no more,
Their jousting ceased,
And they fought with battle-axes.
The battle-axes went shrieking,
Leaving their hafts in their hands.
They fought with swords,
And all the swords shattered,
Leaving the scabbards in their hands.
They seized each other's collars,
Tearing at whatever they grasped.
When nothing else availed them,
They dismounted.
They seized each other's wrists,
Wrestling like champions,
Tearing whatever flesh showed,
Butting like blue bulls.
They heaved worse than camels,
And grappled worse than stallions.
Neither could overcome the other,
Neither could throw the other;
While they stood unable to prevail,
Making Buurul surge,
Looming like Opol-Too,
Venom pouring from his mouth,
Semetey suddenly appeared.
“The elder and younger brother
Will join against me,” he thought,
“They will kill me and reach my end,”
And his heart sank.
Drawing his sword grimly,
Semetey reached them.
Toltoy of the Jediger
Had fallen and lay there.
“When he reaches Judgment Day,
Let him not say two attacked him;
Because you would not spare his precious life,
Let him not say you joined against him.
Give Toltoy's blood to me,”
Külchoro asked.
Without Semetey charging with his sword,
He yielded the man's blood to the boy.
He lifted and struck Toltoy,
Twisted and flung away his mustache,
And tore and scattered his beard.
He began the slaughter;
From the weapon at his side,
Drawing it from its sheath,
He cut off the head
Of Toltoy, son of Bagysh.
Dragging it onto the road,
He displayed it to the Dangyt,
Taking at last his vengeance
On the swine Toltoy the warrior.
Brave Toltoy's Surkoyon—
This Koyon was a peerless courser—
Would let neither hand near
The reins tied in a knot,
And seized them in his mouth.
Wheeling around, he sped off,
Heading toward the Jediger.
He had crossed
The saddle-shaped ridge.
No wind matched his gallop,
No beast matched his stride;
Thus he fled away.
