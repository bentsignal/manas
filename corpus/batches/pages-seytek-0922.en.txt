Such was his splendor.
His army strained forward,
And behind Sarybai
His champions made ready.
They climbed the ridges and crossed the passes,
Surging over them toward this side.
The mountains seemed dug out and hollowed;
They drew and stretched their bows.
At each one's grim appearance,
The whole body of anyone they seized
Shuddered from head to foot.
With musket, bow, and spear,
The enraged warrior's
Renown surpassed all others.
Among the people on earth,
Across the world struck by his turmoil,
He yielded his weapon to no living soul.
Swaying and streaming,
The earth bent beneath the upheaval;
Many hosts had he fought,
Many foes had he grappled.
His eyes reddened like a lion's;
Whoever came before him,
He crushed to dust,
Drenched and steeped in blood;
When he struck, he spared no life.
Such was Sarybai's way.
Fearsome and unconcerned,
He counted the entire world
As less than the hem of his robe.
“I alone shall conquer it,” he said,
Crushing all who opposed him.
“Have I not driven men before me and devoured them?
Over the whole vast world
I alone shall be sovereign,” he said.
Sarybai raged,
And the vast, frantic people
Found their days constricted;
The land fell into turmoil.
The raging madman
Did not know his end was near.
When he reached the great battlefield,
He reined in his charger and halted.
Making the blue heavens resound,
His forces began arriving and stopping.
Flame burst from the great guns;
When their mouths opened, fire poured forth.
They climbed the ridge and braced themselves,
Swaying as though shooting this way,
Menacing and straining,
Shouting as if possessed,
Calling their people to come.
At the sight, the mind reeled;
The blue crags of the splendid mountain
Seemed like a whole vast world.
Like a man who never walked slowly,
It seemed to stride forward.
Like the ravines of a valley
Down which waters plunge, it appeared.
“Ah, the enemy!” they cried, straining forward,
Their eyes dimming.

From the enraged boar,
Ferocity burst forth,
And hair bristled on both cheeks.
He raged and would not stand still;
The people scattered in clamor.
When they came before his army,
They could not face his wrath,
Nor endure his fury.
Before his prestige and appearance,
Even a mare could not stand her ground.
The people despaired of their lives
At his aspect and nature,
At the calamity of his strife,
At his strength and venom,
At the wrath he visited upon men.
Once confronted, he spared no life;
No mortal had ever been like him.
