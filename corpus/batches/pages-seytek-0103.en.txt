Brave Kyyaz laughed mockingly,
And now he set aside his anger,
Then Aichürök said:
“Ignorant Kyyaz, hear my words!
No racehorse has flawless hooves,
No falcon has flawless wings.
If you carry on like this, Kyyaz,
If I do not flood your home,
Just wait, accursed Kyyaz—
If I live, one day I shall bring you down!
When costly crimson cloth
Is torn at the hem, it does not unravel;
Once you fall into Kyrgyz hands,
You will not even dare look back.
If my father Akun has no means,
If I do not pursue you and prove my words,
If I do not soar thunderously, seeking warriors,
If I do not range across the earth,
If I do not gather men from passes
High as towering cliffs,
If I do not gather lions
Who have subdued the whole wide world—
Was your heart not content, taking captives,
Humiliating them and gathering slaves?
Had you no other maidservant or slave but us?
Since time beyond remembering,
You have barred your way with iron,
Slaughtered nations in your wrath—
Why have you subjected us to such disgrace?
You smirk and fawn, brave Kyyaz;
Can good come to one who strikes a slave?
You have done the vilest of all deeds for a warrior;
Can a hero truly be like you?”
With a handspan waist and moonlike face,
Your renowned sister-in-law Chürök
Showed her heroic mettle;
She separated him from Kyyaz
And brought the helpless Choro
Inside the white pavilion.
“When I enter the white pavilion,
If I do not shoot arrows like hail
And utterly destroy your host!
If I do not cast your troops into hell,
If I do not take my vengeance without pause,
If I do not bring slaughter upon your borders,
If I do not cut off your head with my sword!
If I do not reap your green grain,
If I do not ravage your entire village!
If I do not rally my father Khan Akun’s army,
Make bows rain like a storm,
And arrows fall like hail—
Should you die, brave Kyyaz,
If I do not unleash open war
And slaughter your settled people!
If I do not bring calamity upon your head,
Whatever your last wish, brave Kyyaz,
If I do not exact my vengeance upon you!
If I do not summon Akun’s people
And have your giant bound,
I shall draw out, brave Kyyaz,
All your long-stored bitterness!”
Chürök, daughter of Akun,
The moon-faced woman of tumult,
A champion among women,
A heroic tigress,
Your fierce and precious sister-in-law—
Can any woman surpass her?
Such was your fierce sister-in-law;
Can any female surpass her?
When brave Kyyaz heard this,
His very ribs quaked.
“A woman will do what she threatens,” he thought,
“She may do all manner of things.”
Then Kyyaz said:
“Incorrigible Chürök, you will never mend;
Since time beyond remembering,
You have never curbed your daring.
I would put an end to this,
Were you not moonlike Chürök,
And I would kill your slave!”
