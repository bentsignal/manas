And among those peoples
Are the accursed Kalcha lions.
Their language cannot be understood;
They have babbling speech.
One Chinese tribe
Has a single eye as large as a bucket.
While my bloodthirsty lord lived,
In the city of Kakan-Chyn,
With my lion prince at our head,
When we besieged and drove them,
Plundering their wives and daughters,
When the dear Kyrgyz took them,
Kara-Shaar and Kambyl—
Here am I, Bakai, who besieged them;
Making their Kaldai crack beneath the blows,
Here am I, Bakai, who beat him.
Laying tribute upon them, their gold—
Here am I, Bakai, who took it!
In Beijing beyond Solomon's reach,
Exacting judgment there,
Here am I, Bakai, who stood!
To Beijing beyond Alexander's reach,
While Manas of old lived,
My deeds reached that people.
I plundered their daughters
And destroyed many of their craftsmen.
I did not behold the face of Judgment Day;
While my mighty one lived,
I did not die in those days.
I passed on, a dear young man;
While my hero Manas lived,
What deeds did this dear head not do!
When we besieged the khagan's innermost city,
I did not die;
When we smashed and beat
Its stony warriors, I did not die.
When my appointed time came in Beijing,
While I stood exacting judgment,
I saw the sacred power
Of radiance-pouring Beijing;
Even there I did not die!
Of Beijing's forty gates,
When I looked upon one gate,
It opened toward our homeland.
Closed by day, by day it opened;
Closed again, again it opened.
Upon the face of that gate
The white prophet Mustafa—
The name Muhammad was written.
Lest one devil strike
The followers of Muhammad,
God's light was shed
Upon teeming Beijing.
Beijing is no easy place;
Do not put your uncle to shame—
They are no people for sport!
When I was drunk with spirits,
When I was young and broad-chested,
Parted from my elder Ala,
I returned maimed;
Parted from Khan Chubak,
I returned diminished!
My wing, my Khan Manas,
I let that swine Khan Kongurbai
Catch us unawares.
Such is the nature of battle;
In the course of fighting,
I was parted from my Syrgak!
Your aged uncle Bakai,
Parted from his gray wolf,
His chest remains rattling.
How shall I scout the road
Of the teeming Chinese,
My eyes left festering?
With my hair-thin beard grown white,
Do not torment me, my orphan,
At the time I am bound for Judgment Day!
Do not bring turmoil upon Beijing;
