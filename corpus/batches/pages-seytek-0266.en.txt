Unable to take his tiny life,
His grief and humiliation
The bloodthirsty warrior’s son, brave Seytek,
Could not master with courage;
“Restrain your grief.
Before the spoken words could be gathered,
Before anyone could say, ‘What is it?’
In the blink of an eye,
Sixty mighty caravan men,
Those fiends who stole horses,
Their shouts loud enough to split stone,
Their cries loud enough to shatter heads,
Roared, “Where is the enemy?”
And challenged, “Come out in single combat!”
Gripping battle-axes, they made ready;
Whichever one you looked at
Swayed as if eager to grapple.
They brandished unsheathed swords
And merely gnashed apart
Their shovel-like teeth.
They bounded like tigers,
As though none who fought them could survive.
The sixty mighty caravan men
Brandished their lances,
And swung their crescent-bladed axes.
They thrust forward their cast-iron mace heads,
Swaggering and working themselves into a fury.
Toward Seytek, who stood grieving,
They came calling him a bandit;
The strongmen were approaching
As though to lash and beat him,
Their words vile, their faces grim,
As though they would destroy him utterly.
Those hulking brutes,
Those boundlessly insolent thugs,
If left to their own devices,
Looked ready to mow down and seize everything.
The lumbering caravan men
Had hell showing in their faces,
Intent on stirring every kind of quarrel.
My tunic, blue-mottled as a lake,
She had torn open and cast aside;
When she saw noble Buurul,
Your precious mother Aichürök
Had fallen down as though dead.
Aichürök stood shrieking,
Seytek sobbed and wept,
The speechless horse Buurul wept,
Everyone who beheld them wept,
The people watching wept,
The broad-breasted earth itself wept.
His face went pale and wan,
And the tears flowing from both eyes
Poured down over his chest.
They gathered all along the cliffside camp,
As if the colossal, mountain-like lion
Had only just died.
Their bellies churned and their souls burned,
Their bodies went limp and their blood burned;
The world closed in around them,
And anguish smoldered within.
As though the bloodthirsty gray-maned hero had died,
As though they had seen calamity that very day,
They sobbed and wept, yet could not die,
Unable to take their own lives.
Snot streamed from their noses like spring water,
And they rode on, weeping convulsively,
Standing there bereft of strength,
Their thoughts utterly distracted.
The gray-maned hero’s Buurul
Appeared before his eyes exactly like
His lion-hearted father Semetey.
Every joint trembled and gave way,
And the orphan wailed in anguish.
To the lion-hearted son of his father,
His father’s heavenly steed,
Noble Buurul, appeared before him,
And when the orphan saw him,
Tears poured from his eyes.
He wished to die, yet could not die.
