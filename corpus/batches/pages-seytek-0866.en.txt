The reins fitted Kulansur perfectly;
He gripped them hard in both hands,
And wrestled with the mouth
Of the black steed flying like a cloud.
The reins stretched thin as thread;
When the black steed saw the mountain
And bucked, the black earth
Was gouged out on every side.
When he struck out with unerring steps,
The ground he trod burst apart;
A cloud of dust as large as a house
Whirled above the head
Of the fortunate hero.
His broad front teeth, like a gate,
Shone like steel within his mouth;
The black steed’s splayed stride
Appeared as broad as a ravine.
The polished steel spear in his hand
Danced and darted in his grasp;
The descendant of the noble lion
Wore the aspect of one thirsting for blood.
Those he charged were scorched to ashes;
Those he struck ceased to exist.
He reached the army and cut through it,
Spearing everyone he met.
He swung the spear in his hand,
Aimed it at the hollow
Above each breastbone, and drove it home.
The axe flashed in his hand,
The iron mace crashed upon heads;
Blood poured from those he struck,
Life fled from those he smote.
His spear blazed like a fiery ember,
And no one struck by it remained.
Sarybai, who had advanced in force,
Halted as though he had drunk poison.
Every rein was cut,
And the vast host he had summoned
Was now gathered
Before Sarybai.
The people lost their senses,
The vast army rose in uproar,
Pressing around Sarybai;
Many commanders of the host
Gathered around Sarybai,
And the people assembled with a clamor.
The great army that had gone to war
Despaired of its precious life.
His matchless lionhearted courage,
His valor famed through the ages,
Revealed the hero Seytek;
Wherever he carved into them, they split apart.
He did not merely raise a furious clash—
Every soul despaired.
“My wrestlers, my lions,” he cried,
Toppling them backward from their horses,
He defeated and drove them onward.
The spear he thrust seemed to lengthen,
His eyes reddened like a lion’s;
Breaking the ranks of the countless host,
He swallowed them up and drove them on.
“May his forefather Manas stand beside him,” they said,
“May God set every deed aright,” they said.
He roared in fury,
Slaughtering, driving, and scattering them;
The world filled with dust,
And their heads reeled in bewilderment.
He speared them one after another,
Crushing the ground wherever he passed;
The side he advanced upon became deserted,
Those he faced were consumed by fire,
And all he encountered were slain.
He made Kulansur dance,
And made everyone he met cry out;
In groups of a hundred and forty
He herded them away.
He bent the brave double,
Slaughtering as he went.
Those who resisted and stood their ground,
Those who swaggered like men
