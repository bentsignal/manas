Despairing of their fly-like lives,
He saddled Tookyzyld,
His spirit estranged and divided,
When he glowered in fury,
He looked ready to suck them dry,
Such was Sarybay's state:
Sixty wrestlers and forty warriors
Fell one by one in their places,
Upon Tookyzyld,
Into the steel-soled stirrups,
He leapt without even setting foot,
Alone from the massed army,
He rode visibly forth to single combat,
He appeared ready to drain
Every mortal human being,
A savage frenzy seized him,
Against that dread man's wrath,
No one could stand,
His appearance utterly transformed,
Chasing the whole wide world,
Drawing breath like a dragon,
He looked ready to suck all life away,
His eyes distorted and blazed,
Tookyzyld's reins
He gripped now right, now left,
No soul could endure his fury,
The storm never left his brow,
The army gathered with a clamor,
Unable to withstand his wrath,
All whom he looked upon were slain,
Every living creature
Bowed and bent its head,
Whoever stared wide-eyed at him
Despaired at once of life,
Those skilled at thrusting spears,
The stalwarts who struck with battle-axes,
Those who faced fearsome foes
And were drenched in black blood,
Those who guarded the people,
Eloquent men who never faltered,
Marksmen trained in stealth,
Who never shrank when facing the foe,
Men born but once
Into the hands of fortunate mankind,
Who never withdrew what they extended,
Whose grim aim never failed,
True, sure-shot marksmen—
When Sarybay poured out his wrath,
All the heroes gathered,
And wise men were slaughtered,
Their hands struck against one another,
When Sarybay poured out his wrath,
Many among the people lost their wits,
Rushing about in panic,
Many mortals fled,
Their faces yellow as straw,
Finding no earth to enter,
Finding nowhere to take shelter,
Finding no flattering words to speak,
Their hearts corrupted, their faces bitter,
Behind the infidel slave,
They found no refuge from death,
The people lost their senses,
And this was why they faltered:
Rebuking the whole army,
Sarybay stood thundering,
“All our stratagems are exhausted,” they said,
“In two months he has reached us,” they said,
That swine Sarybay's
Fierce venom became apparent,
All who heard with ears and saw with eyes,
The people despaired of life,
Beginning their laments, crying “alas,”
Every person brooded, crying “woe,”
“If Sarybay flies into a rage,
He will pursue us to the bitter end,” they said,
“He will boastfully drive his army,
Not sparing even one,
And pass through in slaughter,” they said,
