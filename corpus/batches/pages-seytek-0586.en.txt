As he mounted the ridge and girded himself,
Swaying as though striking this way,
Holding his spear unusually high,
The noble, peerless, precious man
Having bound his life to a hair and cast it away.
At the sight of him, hearts were torn;
He looked like a pillar supporting the sky,
Like a fortress built for the people,
Like a mountain citadel amid an advancing host,
Like a backward-slanting, jutting crag,
Like a solitary projecting peak;
Your wrathful blood-drinker
Was like a promontory propping up the sky—
Such was his appearance.
Like a lamenting guardian of the nation,
Like a champion leading the people,
He roared in fury,
Calling Külchoro and Seytek:
“Come here, both of you!”
When the two arrived, he said:
“My champion brother Külchoro,
My younger brother, you stand here.
When I see you, my heart is content;
When I look upon you, my hunger is filled.
My one cherished colt,
My daylight, you stand here!
Sarybai, son of the ogre,
Has come like a black, snowy mountain;
A mighty and courageous foe has come.
He has begun conflict and war
And slaughtered so many
Of the countless living Kyrgyz!
Against that rabid swine,
Wage the fiercest battle.
The beloved Kyrgyz, our precious people,
Face misery shrouded in wrath:
A cage of iron and steel,
A skilled foe with a sharp spear,
A merciless champion
Who would swallow the whole wide world.
With every protector gathered behind him,
When the gray wolf strains his might,
No mortal can truly stand against him;
When the gray wolf exerts his power,
No son of man can endure him.
When the leopard grows wrathful,
He never walks away without drinking blood;
When the gray wolf becomes enraged,
The people of the age cannot remain unshaken.
He is a lion blessed by Khizr,
He is the blood-drinker’s great eye.
Having suffered many calamities,
The magnanimous lion was led astray,
Vanished utterly without trace,
And was found after sixteen years.
His grief became immense,
And his suffering was complete.
He is the magnanimous lion himself;
Not only his person—even
The gray wolf’s dark watching eye he was.
Driving them behind him,
Because of that Sarybai’s affliction,
He poured out his wrath and roared,
Calling the gray wolf’s brothers, sons,
And every kinsman without exception.
The one he will grapple with is Sarybai,
Who has caused turmoil many times.
He grieved the gray wolf
And plunged many souls into sorrow.
When the carefree hero took aim
And advanced to begin the great conflict,
Their spears could not prevail in thrusting,
Their strength could not prevail in wrestling,
Their power could not prevail in slashing,
And they could not bring him down in surging combat.
When their strength was failing,
He turned toward them and roared:
“My champion bound to my waist,
My brave one sprung from my loins!”
