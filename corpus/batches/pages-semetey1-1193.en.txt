Foal of the bay mare,
Fleetest of the black herd,
Akkula, whom I ride into battle—
Why did I insist on racing him?
The black Chinese and the Manchus—
Their prowess, before my own eyes,
I had held for nothing;
That blood-drinker, the infidel Kongurbay,
That hero whose wrath is full,
Would enter this joust—
I had failed to understand it."
While Manas stood speaking thus,
His thoughts, it seemed, were divided.
Kanykei, the gray wolf's wife,
Appeared like the whole world,
Kanykei, mistress of every craft,
Showing the whiteness beneath her chin,
Her white breast gleaming,
Spoke softly and said this.
Having caused much bustle,
She finished it that very day.
The blood-drinker's Akolpok armor
She lifted up then
And had it brought to Akayar.
Beauty of the dark-bay folk,
Radiance of the Muslims,
Most graceful of the long-haired,
Most excellent of women:
"It would be wrong not to prepare," she said.
"My hero, my wolf, my sultan,
If he meets the Kalmyk face to face,
Might he come to harm?" she thought.
From scraps and bits of material,
Kanykei had spent six years
Making the Akolpok armor.
Its lining was velvet, its outside calico,
And it was half a cubit thick.
Your formidable sister-in-law Kanykei
Had the lands from Orol-Tobo to Margalang,
And all the country between,
Supply her with the cotton fiber,
Which she had brought by Kembai.
Its collar was gold and its sleeves were brass,
With paired mail rings like lark's eyes;
A wide coat with a broad collar,
When worn in battle no bullet pierced it.
The point of a spear could not puncture it,
Nor could an armor-cutting arrow pass through.
The matchless Akolpok armor
Was supple as a snake's belly;
Those who looked at it were amazed,
And people's eyes slid from its sheen.
Its lining was packed with tempered steel;
When she bent that dark metal,
Many awls made of steel
Were snapped in two.
She had its weak places hammered,
Had damask steel filed down,
And poured into its middle.
Around the outside she wrapped silk.
While the Akolpok was being made,
Much of your gray wolf's treasury
Was spent away.
Between its layers of dark metal
She placed every kind of tempered steel.
When the hero donned it and girded himself,
Any mortal who faced him withered.
Burning buckthorn could not scorch it,
And a shot from Akkelte could not pierce it.
"My hero, his father's only son,
Must stand wearing the Akolpok,
So when there is a shouting enemy,
He may throw himself forward without fear.
I cannot leave this undone;
If I do not perform this service,
My labor will never prosper.
The Akolpok you now see—
I could not rest until I made it;
