Ümötöy, son of Köksö,
Raised an uproar and launched a raid,
Charging in with his spear.
As though his wife had borne a son,
Without a care in the world,
Your valiant warrior rode laughing.
“Ümötöy of heroic Köksö,
Mark well what I tell you:
Father Ümötöy, stop—or sink into the earth.
I have spoken weighty words; heed me.
Your shield will be torn apart,
And if you will not listen, Kazakh slave,
You yourself will now be shattered.
Great fortune lies in my counsel;
If I charge alone in fury,
Your flesh, Kazakh slave,
Will now be torn to pieces.
I shall unleash my wrath upon you;
If my anger rises and I rear up,
Though you two are honored cousins,
I shall surely strike one of you.”
When Ümötöy kept thrusting,
Then mighty Külchoro
Swelled with wrath and strength;
Upon your princely warrior’s face
Lay ice four fingers thick.
His noble blood boiled fiercely;
Gripping his weapon’s hilt,
His spirit could no longer be restrained.
His countenance was transformed,
His face paled and his heart burned;
Unable to contain himself,
His eyes blazed like fire.
His broad white teeth, like a gateway—
Teeth fit for a stout tethering post—
He ground apart in rage.
When your lion warrior grew impassioned,
He would take his sword in hand;
Though forty ranks of foes should come,
Though strength narrowed his eyes,
Though forty thousand spears struck at once,
That warrior would never flinch,
But leave slaughter in his wake.
When he took the sword in hand,
Such was his terrible prowess—
He unleashed doomsday calamity.
When the warrior grew wrathful,
Blood flowed every way;
Once blood was shed, lives departed,
And he left no living form behind.
Those who approached, that tiger of a man
Swept utterly away.
When Külchoro was enraged,
He brought slaughter upon the age.
He was the leopard, he was the lion,
He was the dark-maned lone wolf.
Külchoro cried out in anger:
“When a warrior comes as an envoy,
Did I say he should be met with wrath,
With shouting and a leveled spear?
Did I say any such thing?
No blood-debt of your seven forefathers is owed;
Blustering Ümötöy,
Have I consumed what you hold dear?
Into your old homeland, Sary-Arka,
Did I send hostile scouts?
Did I slaughter your people in mischief
Or raid and take your horses?
Have I fallen prey to a sly demon?
Ümötöy, son of Khan Köksö,
My wrath has risen and blinded me;
If I attack, you will learn your lesson!
If I rear up in anger,
You will flee all the way to Sary-Arka!
If I charge with a single shout,
You will be crushed to dust!
If I grow wrathful, you will die;
These hands that reached the Kalmyks—
