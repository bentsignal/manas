Was a thoroughbred that seemed to fly.
Ruinous Kojo would come to no good,
Though he was Semetey's kinsman;
He gathered a great Black army
And engulfed them in battle,
Having turned black with rage,
To seize the wife
Of hero Khan Semetey.
Toltoi inflicted violence;
By making war on Semetey,
Chynkojo inflicted humiliation.
The peoples of the world gathered;
Because of Chynkojo and Toltoi,
Innocent, meek people were slaughtered.
Khan Akun was no feeble khan;
Azhibai and Tümönbai
Were not souls who would simply perish.
Where he cast his gaze was red fire;
Semetey was no ordinary child.
When he saw the engulfing army,
Azhibai, son of Azhy,
Flew like an arrow,
Hurrying and stumbling onward.
Aichürök's white palace
Was ringed by a fortress wall,
Surpassing in its splendor;
Emil and Ezhibai
Were guards at its door.
Passing among the guards
And seeking Semetey's protection,
When dawn shone white
And light touched the earth,
He went to the pavilion in the palace
And brought news
To hero Semetey.
His knee bent to the ground
As he ran in panic.
The young hero, son of Manas,
Dipped his hands in water
And performed the hand ablution;
He hurriedly recited the prayer,
Saddled Taibuurul,
And set his arms and armor in order.
He gathered himself like a black tiger;
Like a cloud about to rain again,
Wrath spread across his face.
He girded on Zulfiqar,
Inherited from Manas.
Grieving over Chynkojo,
He put on Akolpok,
Slung on Zhazyiyl,
And prayed to the Creator.
Smoldering anew whenever depleted,
Growing stronger from the root,
Such was Manas's deep-rooted line.
Increasing whenever one of them died,
Such was Manas's ruling line.
Semetey took after Manas;
Semetey wore
A blue lynx-fur cap.
He grew wrathful at Kojo,
As though he had no wife.
With a face like oiled wheat,
With eyes like twilight mist,
He was offspring of the brave.
When the inherited fervor
Of his fathers and grandfathers seized him,
He burned fortified cities.
When enemies pressed together,
He never opened his flank in fear.
Whoever grappled with him,
He crushed into the ground like a hare.
He subdued the enemy he faced;
No enemy dared come out
To face the valiant hero.
When an enemy did face him,
He drenched his sword in blood.
No mortal defeated him face-to-face,
