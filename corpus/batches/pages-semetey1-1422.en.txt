How have I now become so feeble?
When I possessed perfect strength,
When my courage was abundant,
With my leather warrior's belt girded at my waist,
If seventy thousand troops came,
I would enter lightly clad and attack,
Would I not stand firm upon the pass and road!
When I possessed my dear strength,
Clad and girded in bulletproof armor,
If one hundred twenty thousand enemies appeared,
I would enter alone among the host and attack;
Would I not stand firm—was I ever absent?!
Joy and suffering, side by side,
It was the time they accompanied me;
The accursed, barren world
Made sport of Bakai
And deceived him.
With my beard grown white,
I have come on campaign, my foal,
In this very year when I am ready to die.
My accursed joints wobble,
My accursed eyes merely glimmer.
I have no teeth to chew food;
It is Bakai's time to die,
My precious son, I have no strength.
I have become your traveling companion,
But have no power to clash in battle;
If the black Chinese below
Suddenly surge over us,
I have no means to support you!
Would that a horse never tired after six months' riding;
Would that a horse never grew old.
Until this dear head entered the grave,
Would that a hero never grew old.
Old age is humiliation;
Rather than live on, aged and unable to die,
It would be better to be dead.
From the road I reconnoitered at night,
Traveling by day, I lost my way.
It seems I have beheld
The oppression of judgment;
This accursed, barren world—
While sitting fully alive,
It seems I have died!
My son, unwillingly abandon hope
Of your support, uncle Bakai!
If my Allah sets your work right,
If the spirit of Manas,
Who departed for the next world, is close at hand,
You shall bring turmoil in due order
And raid the horses
Of the Kalcha whose tumult is unlike any other's!
There in the hollow of the flowing river,
Along the Sary-Suu,
There is a tent with golden poles;
Inside that tent
Is the infidel Karagul.
That swine Karagul
Has a cunning beyond that of mortals.
That accursed Karagul—
Here is the dark hour!
Broad-jawed and flat-tongued,
A great orator who never stumbles,
If you let him deceive you, my foal,
The time of a brave man will be lost.
The stratagems of Karagul
Are inferior to none.
That is the accursed man himself;
Do not let him deceive you, my foal,
For he is the eye of calamity!
Watch carefully, my only one;
He is the true enemy himself!
An eloquent and skillful slave,
May his wish never be fulfilled,
A mighty slave, the best among the Chinese.
His speech is like honey and sugar;
He has twice deceived
Not only you, but your uncle Bakai.
