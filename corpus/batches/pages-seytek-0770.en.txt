I shall submit to His command.
This precious, priceless golden life—
Sarybay is a tyrant,
So I shall surrender it to Sarybay.
At the funeral feast for my khan father,
Before the vast assembly,
I shall grapple and test myself
Against this savage enemy dog.
Dueling before the people,
I shall see how great his courage is.
How could I surrender my khan father’s treasure
And ever show my face again?
The sly boar seems to have gone mad;
I shall meet him squarely in battle
And now, once and for all,
Test myself against him!
Standing face-to-face,
Swinging axes and thrusting spears,
We shall clash and snarl in battle,
Making the whole world ring with our fame
And covering the earth in dust;
Thus shall I measure our strength.
The sly boar has gone mad;
Now, once and for all,
I shall test myself against him.
When he charged from that place,
He leaned back without losing strength
And came on in a frenzy.
If one falls into the infidel’s hands,
At the final end of time,
One’s mail will not remain whole,
Nor does he look inclined to spare a life.”
Thinking thus, she said:
“I shall press Zhelzhetpes with my thighs,
Take up my well-fashioned spear,
Turn my charger’s head,
And thrust my spear without delay.
The edge beside the lungs is the seam,
The place where throat, liver, and heart
Join together is the border.
That is the destined place;
The sovereign Allah is the one God,
And this is the moment
When the dreadful, monstrous foe must die.
If I strike downward by chance,
The Kalmyk saddle-bow is there;
If I thrust sideways by chance,
The head of the shoulder blade is there.”
Beneath her was the charger Zhelzhetpes;
Seizing the black-jawed whip
And wheeling it around,
She drew the woman-like swine
Off toward the flank,
Broke through the dead end, and opened a path.
Before one could say a word,
In the blink of an eye,
Before Sarybay, son of a monster,
Could turn his charger’s head,
She thundered out a battle cry;
The people fled before her menace.
If the great gate-like buckteeth bit down,
They would crush with a crack;
Unable to find their bearings,
The multitude fled in clamorous panic.
At the earth-bending boar
My impetuous fury flared;
I turned Zhelzhetpes’s head,
Caught him, and struck with my spear.
Before all the people, I charged
And speared the villain.
For seven days without rest
I battled the giant in fury.
Facing one another across the slope,
Exhausting ourselves with gunfire,
We fought in a bloody clash;
Every axe shattered with a crash,
And spears snapped with a shriek.
Hillocks and hollows were leveled,
And for mortal humankind
It became a deed never to be forgotten.
