To Esenkhan, pillar of heroes,
Say, ‘We have avenged the ancient wrong;
The enemy who stood against us
We have left without descendants.
Not merely three generations, but six,
We have utterly avenged.’
Go and tell this to the great lord.
Ask as a reward for the gold-bearing mines,
The lands where tea is grown,
And the slopes of Khan-Jailak,
And claim them as your due.
You are one Burut against our multitude—
Fight on without turning back!”
So saying, Chongbilgich
Stiffened the resolve of the fainthearted,
And drove them on with sharp exhortations;
Those of his best men who never retreated
Chongbilgich chose and sent forward.
He praised his champions
And drove them into battle.
Once authority gave its command,
Would they turn and merely stare?
Once the great lord’s order had come,
Would they fail to charge where they were told?
Would the champions stand and watch?
The champions now surged forward.
When they had surrounded him,
The champions numbered eighty,
All who remained alive;
When they sprang into battle,
Chongbilgich came behind them,
Driving them forward and clearing their path.
The champions who obeyed the command
Advanced straight upon Seytek
To capture him alive.
Those casting lassos ran forward,
The spearmen plunged into turmoil,
And those who cast iron nets
Circled and drew near,
All advancing together and loosing their bows.
The bowshots cracked,
And as death drew near,
Far from fearing, Seytek rejoiced;
The peerless Seytek stood firm.
From every side they drove their spears
Against him with pounding blows.
The splendid gray wolf, heir of his grandsire,
Neither flinched nor twitched,
Nor faltered or stirred.
He rang like steel, unconcerned,
Counting the assault of all eighty
As less than the hem of his garment;
He struck down everyone he met,
And laid flat those whose death had come,
Waging battle without mercy,
Locked with them in combat.
Chongbilgich, who had given the order,
Now entered the battle in earnest.
Charging with a battle cry,
He did not cease from wreaking havoc.
From the side
The madman came spurring in;
The earth churned and blinded the eye,
As the masterful Bilgich raised his war cry
And bore upon Seytek riding Kulansur,
Just as he was about to reach the beleaguered lion…
Akjoltoi, who had suffered grievously,
Was standing atop a hill.
For the legacy left by his grandsire,
For the son left by his father,
In the poor hero’s pouch
There was medicine for every need,
Every sort of remedy was there.
There was also the meer herb
To feed a thoroughbred steed.
If he fed it the meer herb,
Kulansur, ridden day and night,
Would lose his breathlessness,
His body would ease and recover,
And his argali-like eyes would open wide.
