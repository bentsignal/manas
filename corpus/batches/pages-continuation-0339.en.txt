I, blood-drinking Odu, am ready!
All of you, come here!” he cried.
He came onto the battlefield shouting,
Making heaven and earth resound:
“Leave your weaklings; send your lions!
Send the bravest of your heroes!
Beneath me is my horse Kongkul;
Today I have the strength
To destroy the whole wide world!”
Thus he thundered,
Standing there in fury.
Wondering what horror this might be,
The people trembled.
As the people stood so,
Er Kogoy, father of the nation,
Whose mount was Toburchak,
Leapt down from his steed
And turned his face toward the qibla.
Before Kenen, pillar of the people,
He now asked for a blessing:
“Black-maned leopard,
Descendant of the lion hero,
Your first ancestor was Tugol Khan,
Blessed by Khydyr from the beginning.
All your forefathers were khans;
The forty saints aided them of old.
Each time one died, his line flourished;
Khidyr himself followed him.
Your ancestors planted banners on grassy lands
And brought fire upon
Those who came in arrogance!
Heed what your elder,
The hero Kogoy, says;
Grant me your highest blessing!
If death comes and my days end,
If I perish from a stray shot,
And am destroyed by a chance blow,
Say that renowned Kogoy died,
That this honored, precious soul
Gave the life that was needed
For the soaring Ala-Too,
For the white-capped Kyrgyz people,
For honor and for dignity;
Bury me with your own hands!
To go against Odukan,
And answer the infidel who humiliates us,
If death does not take me—
Noble white-capped Kyrgyz people,
With Er Kenen in your midst…”
Every wise diviner,
Every hero from whom wisdom springs,
Spread their palms and gave their hands.
They gave him armor no bullet could pierce,
A helmet of blue steel,
And richly fashioned plate armor.
They dressed him in blue steel
Beautifully suited to his body.
Toburchak, with his handsome neck,
Who grazed on the Chambyl ridge,
Born a true racing steed,
They gave Er Kogoy to mount.
They clasped and pressed his hand,
Entrusted him to the spirits of the ancestors
And to the Creator,
And sent Kogoy forth.
Toward fork-necked Akyska,
Toward remote Tankyska,
Into mist beyond the reach of sight,
To the very edge of the haze,
To the yellow plain where battle was joined,
Across the face of that yellow plain,
Kogoy rode.
When Odukan saw him,
His eyes flashed like a lion's.
A fit of rage seized him;
He ground and gnashed
Those gate-like front teeth.
He turned his rhinoceros mouth,
Cut a road across the plain,
And attacked Er Kogoy.
The hero on one side and giant on the other
Shouted and leveled their spears;
They fought by turns.
They measured and matched each other,
Fighting with spears.
They came at one another head-on
And fought with iron maces.
The rim of the blue-steel helmet
Was battered and crushed.
The broad-rumped bay stallion with mattock-like hooves,
Who never tired on patrol,
Toburchak of the open steppe,
Had his back broken.
His wings were clipped—
What could our elder Kogoy do?
He was parted from Toburchak.
A bullet brought Toburchak down,
And the foe tore Kogoy's coat.
