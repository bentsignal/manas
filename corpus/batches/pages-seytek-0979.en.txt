Not reckoning that he might die.
A fierce clash began.
The life that Almighty God bestowed,
He staked upon a single hair.
The wretch had worked himself into a frenzy,
Making the face of the earth bristle,
Tooqyzyl, the outstanding swift horse,
Urging it into a racing gallop,
Tearing up the face of the earth,
If Kuyaly were given her turn,
Such is her agility.
Toward Kuyaly
Sarybay, swaggering defiantly,
Drew his bow, sending arrows like rain,
Firing shots like hail,
With a stratagem, firing six at a time,
A matchlock gun was on his back,
Many quick-firing guns were in his hands.
With this he fired a hundred shots,
Before its bullet could reach,
Blood flowed from the lionhearted warrior,
Before it reached the very crown of the head,
A polished arrow toward the shoulder,
If one were to tell the tale,
With it he fired a hundred shots,
The swaggering wretch,
Drew his bow, sending arrows like rain,
Firing other shots like hail,
While his shots fell all around,
Kayypchal's valiant daughter,
Sensing the turmoil,
As she turned this way and that,
There was wonder in the work she made,
The valiant Kanykey,
'Wear it when you face the foe,' she said,
When facing that foe,
If Father Khizr wills it,
Its collar was gold, its sleeves copper,
Double mail with rings like lark's eyes,
Even burning spirea embers could not pierce it,
Even if an onogor gun fires, its bullet cannot pierce it,
And in a wrestling struggle, no hand can get through.
Fitted to the body,
It was stitched with a master’s firm stitches.
Even if six thousand spears thrust at once,
They could not reach through to the other side,
But glanced away in every direction.
As for piercing it with a spear,
Many of the craftsmen
Were rebuked for not knowing how.
They filed down the steel
And had it cast at the appointed place.
That Sarybai would start a fight,
That the hour of it was beginning,
That they would clash fiercely
And bring on a great calamity,
Surely she had known it beforehand.
Deliberately drawing aside,
She asked coaxingly for this coat
And came wearing it.
The bullets he fired came thudding,
Pouring in from every side.
That infidel Sarybai,
In the blink of an eye,
To take his proper turn
And strike with his spear,
Without heeding the words that were spoken,
Without looking around in turn,
Shouted loud enough to split stone.
Sparing his life not a hair’s breadth,
Without looking about him,
Without reckoning the grave danger,
Without giving a thought to his death,
He was bold indeed, a lion indeed,
A man born ready to die.
He struck down whoever crossed his path;
He was one who would shed blood.
His breast held bitter grievances,
A seasoned man, steeped in wickedness:
What he sought was a great quarrel.
