Killing an only son is a great shame;
You have no brother to stand shoulder to shoulder with you.
If you do not take the lone man's life,
If you do not extinguish his hearth,
We shall stand watching in sorrow;
Though I am a woman, I ask this."
Kanykey spoke these words,
Standing as an intercessor.
Then, wearing a blue lynx-fur cap,
Wielding a blue-mottled shield,
Foreseeing today
The torment that would come in six months,
His words plain when he spoke,
Saintly Bakay, whose eyes were open,
Who found words in sudden danger
And found sight in darkness,
Good-fortuned when sent ahead,
Like a countless host when sent behind—
The elder stepped between them,
His uncle, saintly Bakay.
Then Bakay said:
"Understand my precious words;
My falcon Manas, heed my tongue.
He is an only son, and so are you;
Do not take the lone man's life,
Do not extinguish his hearth!
Bloodthirsty Manas, my soul's friend,
If you truly are a khan,
Heed what I say
And grant his spoonful of blood!
If you maul the boy,
What you do will be shameful.
Until you go to the great feast,
Until you receive the people's news,
Remain patient.
If you kill one of your own people,
What you do will be tyranny.
In reedy broad Keng-Kol and broad Talas,
You watered the horse Akkula;
Bokmurun acted foolishly,
And you vented your anger here.
You have seen the toll of many lands;
Do not inflict upon the boy
Bokmurun's torment."
Speaking words of many kinds,
His uncle, elder Bakay,
Stepped between them.
Dear lady Kanykey
Pulled the boy out
From beneath the tiger.
The lions Bakay and Kanykey
Saved him from death
And parted them safely.
Tiger Manas roared,
Thundering in wrath:
"The six-edged blade fallen from heaven,
The finest-tempered sword,
Zulpukor, which I myself obtained,
Came to me through the unseen
In the desert of Mediyan.
When facing the enemy
And standing in combat,
It came precisely to me.
When drawn screaming from its sheath,
It never went without drinking blood;
If it did not drink its fill of blood,
It would not return to the sheath.
When Zulpukor came from its sheath,
It had the custom of drinking blood;
When swung, it passed through Karkybar.
The honored sword Zulpukor
Had the worth of a thousand yearling mares.
When drawn roaring from the sheath,
Does your holy man know?
Will Zulpukor, drawn from the sheath,
Go without drinking blood?
