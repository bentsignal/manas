You gathered maidens and played with them;
You left not a single man untouched.
Ready as a swift horse,
You sought a husband every day.
Old slave woman who took no husband,
Wretched woman who has seen so many;
Yellow, broad-soled slave,
Old slave who remained with her father until childbearing age;
Old slave fallen from her perch,
Wretched slave cast out by the people—
I shall pour out my contempt upon you.
Give me the falcon, and let me go!
Her tongue filled with venomous words,
While in her father's keeping
She sat giving birth to six children;
Black-faced source of calamity,
She grieved while wandering as a traveler.
Whenever she saw a man upon the road,
She ran with her skirts lifted.
I shall take that old woman.
An errand has come to my mind;
Give me my falcon, and I shall go!"
When Aichurok heard this,
The thought would not fit within her heart;
After hearing those deeply buried words,
Her tears scattered in ten directions:
"Enough, my lion-like lord!
Can a bridegroom be like you?
Can grieving Churok prosper
While burning like a cursed crow?
Ruined lord, may you come to no good!
I learned your coarseness long ago,
Yet I have honored the spirit
Of your father who departed this world!
Among the impetuous black Kyrgyz,
They said none was as coarse as you;
They said when frenzy seized you,
You were a rogue who slaughtered his Choro.
Among the gathered Argyn and Kyrgyz,
They said none was as harsh as you;
They said when anger truly seized you,
Beginning with his father Jakyp,
He was a rogue who slaughtered Abyke.
Among the six tribes of Kyrgyz and Argyn,
They said there was no fool like you.
I went about knowing you were a fool,
Yet I honored the looming spirit
Of your father who departed this world!
As though you had taken a wife,
As though you had placed a bridal scarf on my head,
As though you had entered into my delight,
As though you had lain in my embrace,
You went on roaring viciously
And pierced me to every bone.
I myself am broad-soled;
It is true that I am yellow.
It is also true that I grew old
At my father's home until childbearing age.
It is true I stripped the weariness
From my horse throughout the world;
It is true I went wandering,
Approaching all who appeared.
What you said is also true.
Yesterday, to the city of Temir Khan,
What sort of man were you, wandering away?
Saying, 'When will my companion return?'
What sort of woman was I, gazing down the road?
To the city of Kara Khan,
What sort of man were you, fleeing away?
Saying, 'When will my cherished one return?'
What sort of woman was I, grieving?
Was it you who lay in Bukhara,
Never turning aside or returning,
Or I who kept mounting wheat-toothed horses,
Saying, 'When will my champion return?'
And remained faithful without straying?
What sort of man were you,
Scattering toward your maternal uncle's city,
