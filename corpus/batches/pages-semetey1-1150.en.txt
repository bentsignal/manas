How can you take his bride?
If you continue like this, my Kozho,
You will fall into calamity!
When bloodthirsty Manas the leopard
Found the Black Kalmyks and Manchus
Lying there in arrogance,
Letting them plunder meat from the hearths
And beat many Kalmyks with pokers,
Baymyrza came like a khan,
In turmoil and dust.
“Do as you please, even if you slaughter them,
Alas, Manas, as your heart desires!”
Baymyrza stood and said this:
“We held Khan Kökötöy's feast, Manas;
We held a feast mixed with turmoil, Manas!
We alarmed many and gathered the people;
We have caused excessive suffering, Manas!
In the eyes of the Black Kalmyks and Manchus
There was a hostile glare.
If these dogs have the strength,
They are ready to strike me.
They brought turmoil upon my feast
And hell upon my mind.
Fed full on meat, they went mad
And grew eager to attack.
How could my wretched head escape
The seething infidels?
Perhaps they will bind our heroes;
These infidels, like a flood,
May bring hell upon our heads.
Perhaps they will do as they please
And slaughter every soul.
They seized the hearths
And set out causing turmoil.”
While Baymyrza spoke,
Baymyrza, the rich man's son,
Lost heart.
His eyes darted wildly;
His joints went limp.
Baymyrza fled in grief;
Suffering pressure from the infidels,
He despaired of his entrusted life
And ran toward Manas,
Hoping there might be a way.
Seeing this, mighty Manas—
Mighty Manas, who had never suffered humiliation,
Saw a dreadful sight
When his people came weeping;
Mighty Manas suffered humiliation
From the Black Chinese and Manchus.
“Rather than see this humiliation,
May I sink away unborn;
Rather than this, may I die and not live.
Let me witness this humiliation
From the seething Chinese and Manchus.
Bring Aybanboz; I will mount him.
I will drive the seething Chinese
As far as Kakan.”
Thus brave Manas spoke.
His eyes flashed like fire;
His thirst for blood truly seized him.
When he advanced in earnest,
Venom poured from his face
And his heroic form appeared.
“Golden-cheeked battle-ax—
You have come seeking a fight.
Great Kalcha, huge as a yurt,
The people you beat are of another kind.
