He drank and gulped down blood.
Crushing the mighty,
My child, I fear he will slaughter us to the last.
Leaving not a hair of the Kyrgyz,
I fear he will pass through in massacre.
My child, will he knead us like dough,
Will he tear out our roots?
He will diminish our hopes,
Will he destroy the mighty?
He will wipe out the noble,
And unleash calamity and ruin.
Will this poor people gathered here,
Stripped of mane and tail,
Be left hornless?
With the horn broken from our brow,
Will we be left barren?
If this infidel continues so,
He will unleash terrible wrath;
Bride and widowed wife alike
Will abandon the village’s shelter…
Unable to match him in combat,
Fighting on without strength enough,
He will make girls and women widows,
And turn the mighty into slaves;
I fear he will destroy us all.
Sweeping away every last trace,
I fear he will leave us slaughtered.
The people wailed in anguish,
Despairing of their fly-like lives.
All the warriors groaned,
And the people despaired of life.
What will become of us in the end?
Not one of us will remain.
Our blood will flow like water,
And all of us will be massacred.
I fear he will utterly destroy us,
And now bind and carry off
The people who once lived in peace.
I fear he will crush and kill the leaders.
The one God, the Lord Most High,
Had grown wrathful, and Sarybai
Strode about roaring.
Every human creature’s
Whole body shuddered;
In furious anguish,
Every living creature
Despaired of its wretched life.
Your infidel son of a monster
Showed himself a demon;
His cavernous mouth opened,
And fiery, venomous breath
Sprayed from his mouth.
Everyone who saw him
Was broken in body and crushed.
The people wailed in anguish,
And the watching multitude said,
“Sarybai is a demon,”
Despairing of their fly-like lives.
Külchoro was a lion upheld by the saints,
A lion and tiger
Who had subdued the whole world.
For these Kyrgyz, Külchoro
Was an iron gate and fortress.
“God, preserve Choro!” they cried.
Hero Semetey wept blood,
The white-bearded elders wept,
All the common people wept;
All the nation grieved,
And the people despaired of life.
Would he slaughter the heroes and khans,
And destroy so many souls?
Would he exterminate them all together,
Leaving not a hair of living souls,
And depart after massacring them?
Sarybai was no man, but venom;
He was the great God’s wrath.
He bore a vengeance passed down from old,
The vengeance of Mader.
He mangled all who crossed him,
And devoured all whom he encountered.
