To Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
She carried the child.
At that time brave Kyyaz
Kept a royal court and ruled as khan,
Living like a prince.
Dust rose wherever he rode,
And his voice made souls flee.
His mount was like a golden throne,
And this was the height of his power:
He cast all who fought him
Into utter darkness;
Like a wolf, he tore apart
Whomever he encountered.
This was the time when Kyyaz
Had drunk his fill of the world,
Subjugating every land
He came upon.
At the sight of Kyyaz’s insignia,
Even a prince despaired of life.
At the sight of his silhouette,
The people cried out and bowed.
Such was Kyyaz to behold:
His two cheeks hung loose,
Like two sated Dungans;
When rage seized him,
He looked ready to slaughter every living soul.
Not merely a child, but even a hair—
When rage truly seized him,
He seemed capable of massacring all.
His sword was flame, his face a live coal;
Brave Kyyaz was a crimson scourge—
No such infidel existed in all the age.
His brows were haughty, his heart narrow;
As you see, his words were venom.
Before Kyyaz upon his throne,
Laying the child in her lap,
Your poor sister-in-law Aichürök
Let her argali-like eyes shine,
Her pure silks rustling.
Though deeply downcast and broken,
She thought, “Why should I reveal the truth to Kyyaz
And behave defiantly?
I shall say, ‘This is your own son,’
And instead approach Kyyaz
Without arousing suspicion.
With sixty stratagems and a thousand thoughts,
Let me consider carefully;
Saying, ‘I have borne you a son,’
I shall place a heavy obligation
Upon Kyyaz, son of Bagysh.
If he secretly harbors evil,
If he suddenly acts like a beast,
I shall don my swan’s shape,
Roam across the whole world,
Hide my child within my bosom,
And seek a land where we may live.
Why should I show no courage,
And, like some wretched woman,
Remain here grieving?
If brave Kyyaz grows violent,
There is one who girded himself for battle,
A renowned warrior indeed;
I shall seek and find my support,
And muster the army of my father Akun.
Before anyone else, I shall hear
What Kyyaz has to say.
If Kyyaz shows hostility,
I shall have Khan Akun rally his people.
Seeking support in a known land,
Seeking a place to gird myself for battle,
I shall stand firm against distress;
I shall steel my soul
And raise this only child.”
Making a warrior of her woman’s head,
Making a banner of her grieving head,
Moonlike Aichürök
Washed the afterbirth herself,
And cut the cord herself.
Then she laid him in a white shirt
And carefully swaddled him.
