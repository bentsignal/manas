I have a spectacle to show the people;
My dear father left me
Abundant, glittering wealth.
I shall open the doors
Of forty treasure-filled rooms.
My dear father's countless riches
I shall squander and scatter
Among infidel and Muslim alike.
I have robes of honor and possessions;
I have exactly seven myriad sheep,
And means to render service.
To carry my riches,
And reach whatever land I desire,
I have camels that have crossed the world.
Kökötöy's vast riches—
Disregarding all restraint,
I shall try spending them utterly.
I shall camp in the midst
Of infidel and Muslim alike
And hold my father's feast;
Thus I shall test the strength
Of the countless herds my father gathered.
On the ridges I have six ilek of horses;
I shall drive and stake them as prizes.
I have innumerable dark camels;
Without counting, I shall stake them as prizes.
At my noble gray-wolf father's death,
Proclaiming the grave prizes
Before all the people,
I shall now stake great prizes.
At Khan Kökötöy's feast,
What mortal will rival me?
When infidel and Muslim
Come against one another,
The bald men will butt heads with a crash;
Then the spectacle will begin.
At this feast of mine,
They will seize one another by the trouser cuffs,
And the wrestlers will wrestle.
I shall open a city bazaar
And scatter my dear father's
Countless riches in heaps.
I shall gather all the giants
Of infidel and Muslim alike.
When I stake abundant prizes,
Their lance shafts will bend,
And heroic men will joust.
My father Kökötöy's
Surging, countless riches—
I shall scatter them all for my father.
As arbiter I shall appoint
Old Koshoy of the Katagan.
I have abundant, ample herds;
I have the means to feast
Infidel and Muslim alike
For seven years.
If Kökötöy's great wealth
Proves little rather than much,
If much babbling arises among us
And open kinsmen become foes,
I shall divide the four kinds of livestock.
I shall impose hardship and grandeur
Without holding back,
And overwhelm all the people
With my full force.
Until only one bull remains,
I shall scatter my great riches.
There are many peoples who do not eat meat,
Many mortals wandering about agape,
Who know neither feasts nor horses.
I shall devise something worse than a raid:
Let the gathered people be ruined;
I shall hold a feast for Kökötöy.
