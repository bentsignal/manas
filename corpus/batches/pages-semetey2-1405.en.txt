From Chayan and Boyon,
From Ilek and Tumon,
From Nogoi Khan himself,
From the line of hero Kara Khan,
From evil-minded Zhakyp,
His true-born descendant,
Born after going to Altai,
Who brought commotion to the world,
Whose eyes saw into the distance,
Who alone won every contest,
Whom no man defeated in combat,
Whom no hostile foe approached,
Zhakyp Khan's only son,
The plane tree of the world's peoples,
The refuge of the Kyrgyz—
To Manas, Zhakyp's only son,
In Keng-Kol and Talas,
Give her as a bride, he said.
"Give her to Manas and you will prosper;
Give her to the hero and you will flourish.
Gold coins and ruby stones
You will place in your treasury,
And from the strife upon the earth,
Hero, you will preserve your head."
Dear Akun heeded
The discerning judge's words,
And gave the fierce woman
As a bride to Khan Manas.
She was the khan's wife;
Now she was an ill-fated bondwoman,
Her head seized as spoil—
The very accursed woman.
For this graceful woman,
This grievous infidel woman,
This woman grown old,
There could be no place among the people
Of these people-wearing infidels.
Look at the way she moves!
She was the wife of a hero blessed by Khydyr;
Her calamity will touch many.
Her son is famed as Khan Semetey.
Will this bloody clan bow
And submit to another?
If she does not die, this woman's conflict
Will fall upon the Zhediger.
Will princely, gray-maned Semetey
Allow another to tread his bed?
Will he not strike from above?
Will anyone who treads the pillow
Of the famed hero, the Magnanimous One, prosper?
This evil deed, this disastrous affair—
Will it not strike us to the soul?
Hearing Kanchoro had spoken thus,
Dear Kiyaz grew agitated.
He had come to take a wife
And met with great turmoil.
Kanchoro turned blood into blood
And laid the bloodthirsty hero low.
Finding no place to flee,
Finding no deep pit to enter,
Will we die a wretched death?
With the people ruined and the nation fleeing,
Will we die a wretched death?
The wise men said these things;
Some souls whispered,
Listening to one another
And probing what lay behind the words.
They nudged one another in the ribs
And trembled together.
Some wise men among the Zhediger
Had begun to judge the matter.
Thinking of what would follow, some
Had entered into dispute.
Some witless men,
Excited after eating at the feast,
Said, "We have taken a woman as spoil,"
And reveled in their own thoughts.
"What will become of our precious days?" they wondered.
