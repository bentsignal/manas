Accursed Zhediger,
Is that precious child Aychürök
To become wife to all of you?
Accursed Chynkozho,
God bears witness to your violence;
Does the man who gives you a daughter prosper?
You strain and surge without cause;
Can a Muslim be like you?
Is it right to call
A fool like you a Muslim?
I shall not give the maiden—do what you can!
Accursed Chynkozho,
If you cannot do it, take your mother!
Take the lint from my pocket;
You seem to have grown excited—
Take the root of a great prick!
If you are offended, take my sheath;
Here, accursed one, I shall give you
The handle of my battle-axe!
Hey, bastard Chynkozho!
You have no Argyn people in Talas;
I shall not give the maiden—take this of mine!
Do not rattle without cause.
Is Tümönbay a man who lets a pair pass,
Or is Tümönbay the black earth?
Is Tümönbay now less
Than a fugitive who wanders fleeing?
I have given Chürök—take her husk;
Here, I shall make it tribute:
Take the root of my cunning!
Your people may be many,
And my people may be grass;
You may possess spears—
Do I possess only a poker?
For the waist you have girded,
Come out with resolve, Kozhokul,
To the place where we shall exchange shots!
Rejecting good words for wickedness,
Take your mother instead of Chürök!"
When they call him "Kozho, Kozho,"
Does he not swell with pride,
And like a male black crow
Begin hopping about!
Like a black-faced young ram,
Do you not grow swollen with pride?
Like a tethered yearling colt,
Do you not begin prancing?"
Roaring and shouting,
Bending the tops of the apricot trees,
Flame shooting from his eyes,
And smoke billowing from his mouth,
He seized a black-edged battle-axe
And made himself ready.
Straining like a white tiger,
As he was about to send
The pretentious Kozho over the hill,
About to make dust whirl in confusion,
And spill mottled red blood,
As ruin was about to break out,
Azhybay, son of Azhy,
Such was his wisdom:
"He will send the pretentious Kozho
Over the hill," he thought;
"This pig who struck from above
Will be left disgraced."
As he was about to make the tale compelling,
Toltoy and Chynkozho,
And the swarming Zhediger—
As he was now about to deceive them,
"God has taken the fool;
He has begun the ruin now," he thought.
Azhybay struggled to intervene,
Quarreling with Tümönbay.
Raging and straining,
From the other side Chynkozho
Now stood surging forward,
Beating the earth as he strained,
And surging in anger.
