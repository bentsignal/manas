To the tumult of the assembled Chinese,
Along the Karkyra,
To Khan Kökötöy's feast,
Heroes and lions will come;
Unbeliever and Muslim,
All alike will come, none remaining.
The spear-points will glitter;
The ground will heave, filled with people.
The multitude will come seething;
Their numbers will resemble a black swarm.
If you do not come to this feast,
If you do not see this spectacle,
Say he will raise an uproar
And take not only the Kazakhs' horses
But their cattle as well!
When you pass beyond there,
Traveling still farther,
When you reach Kakan-Chyn,
There are the Manchu and Kalmyk peoples;
There is the hero you must tell.
Khan Zholoy of the Kalmyks—
Tell him to put on his karapchy,
Mount Achbuudan,
And drive his people
To Üch-Karkyra,
To Khan Kökötöy's feast;
Let him arrange his own order.
For this feast of Kökötöy,
Let no one among his people remain;
Let the Kalmyks, numerous as cow hairs,
With great Zholoy, their great champion,
Drive forth all together;
Among those Kalmyks,
Let not one remain apart.
For seven years he traveled on foot
And greased his accomplished spear;
He slew seventy men—
Hero Zholoy, reeking of demons.
For sixty years he traveled on foot,
Fighting and greasing his spear;
He slew sixty men—
Hero Zholoy, reeking of blood.
Tell him to put on his great boots
And mount his great racehorse.
Tell him to see this spectacle
And come to my feast!
If he does not see this spectacle,
If he does not come to my feast,
Taking seven standard-banners,
Taking a road across the land,
Taking a host that makes the earth sag,
I will beat out his raging madness,
Raid Khan Zholoy,
And cause three great tumults.
I will raid away not merely the Kalmyks' horses,
But their entire herd.
If Zholoy heeds the command, tell him to come;
If that Zholoy will not heed it,
Tell him to behold Kökötöy's red banner with its golden crescent
From above his own blood!
When you go beyond there,
Along the river that flows backward,
There, taking shape in the haze,
Rising indistinctly to a point,
The Chinese gave it a curious name:
They call it Tash-Köpürö.
Dwelling in Tash-Köpürö
Are the renowned Sibe people.
The formidable champion of the Sibe
Is called Great Ylama.
They have peoples of many kinds;
If we told of them, there are many.
The Sibe people's custom is this:
When a young wife gives birth,
They call the child Sibe
After the reed that cut its navel cord;
If it happens to remain until dusk,
Wolves tear it apart and eat it.
