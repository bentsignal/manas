Anguish struck the Muslims.
The dear people, who had never seen such a thing,
Their temples drawn tight,
Could find no composure
And quarreled with their leader.
They stamped the ground where they stood,
Each blaming another.
“We were a people living at ease;
Have we now met disaster?
Coming thoughtlessly to this feast,
Have we suffered this humiliation
From Kökötöy's bastard Bokmurun?
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Had an extraordinary design;
He is the son Khan Kökötöy
Found through grief.
Gathering people like ants,
He has cast us into turmoil.
The tale would never end
Of the strange peoples who have come.
May God strike these Shibee!
Perhaps they will charge with spears
And slaughter every one of us.
We came to see
Kökötöy's great memorial feast;
After suffering great disaster from the Chinese,
It seems we have come here to die!
When nine wolves fall upon sheep,
Will they leave even the carcasses?
When the surging Chinese beat these people,
Will they leave even their carcasses?
We came to witness
The service of Köpkölöng;
It seems we came to die
At the hands of Kalmyks covering the earth.
If they charge without restraint,
We will all meet our end together;
Will they not slaughter us to the last?
We ate no feast but ate stones;
We ate no celebration but ate bullets.
If these Kalmyks attack together,
They will all draw their bows;
After slaughtering us all,
They will sing their victory song.
Leaving none of us unharmed,
They will take us as plunder.
They will bring hardship upon us together;
They will slaughter every one of us!
This is a glassy, open plain;
There is no ridge to flee toward.
When we suffer calamity,
The renowned lion Manas is not here
To separate and rescue us.
There is no Talas to flee back upon;
If they clash and fight,
Princely Manas the leopard is not here
To separate and rescue us.”
The Muslims sighed and grieved,
Overwhelmed
And despairing of their entrusted lives.
Before much time had passed,
Before one could say this or that,
In the blink of an eye,
The Muslim people parted,
Splitting straight down the center.
If you had seen Orokkyr,
He came prepared into their midst.
A thousand wrestlers serving the feast
Surrounded him and took his horse.
The wrestlers took his horse;
Swelling with pride among the people,
Orokkyr dismounted.
From Kökötöy's great store of gold,
For a hero such as Orokkyr,
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Presented him a gift.
Orokkyr accepted his gift
And was pleased.
