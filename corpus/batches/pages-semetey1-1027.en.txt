Ready Taitoru for me!
That Taitoru I tell you to prepare
Is a renowned swift horse.
When my heroic father died,
When my distressed mother suffered calamity,
Khan Koshoi of the Katagan,
My uncle, father of the people,
Gave that horse at Asa.
Drive them here, my Choro, drive them here!
Quickly drive the fourteen horses;
Saddle Taitoru and drive him swiftly!"
After hearing this command,
Kanchoro flew like an arrow.
Some twenty-five souls,
All sons of khans,
The great cauldron used for cooking food,
And six grooms who tended the horses—
All rushed about in confusion.
In their haste, those young men
Nearly bowed their faces to the black earth.
They pulled the stakes of the gold-tethered tent,
Folded it together,
And loaded it without a moment's delay
Upon the bay horse Archa-Toru.
They drove all fourteen horses
Loose before them,
And reached their lord
In the blink of an eye,
Before anyone could say this or that,
All in a single breath.
Kanchoro flew like an arrow,
Taking the direct road toward his lord.
For the swaggering unfortunate man,
Kanchoro saddled without delay
And led Taitoru forward.
He dressed his lord
In the blue armor-coat;
If you would see such a deed,
Then, making him loom magnificently,
He mounted him upon Taitoru.
All his equipment was fastened on;
Upon a frame fitted exactly to the camel his lord rode,
It was loaded behind the saddle.
Skilled beyond all others,
While the heroic lord still lived,
Poor master Bolokbai
Had crafted it to fit perfectly.
The gold-edged war drum
Was covered with rhinoceros hide.
The warriors marching into Afghanistan
Numbered twenty-five.
When they traveled on a distant campaign,
From their fathers and forefathers
It was their custom to beat the war drum.
Receiving word from his lord,
Kalyk of Sarykan
Took the iron-headed mace in hand.
He set the war drum deliberately
In the very center.
The earth split and the mountains shifted;
Ears were deafened by its roar.
For as long as meat takes to cook,
All fourteen horses
Shied and stamped without ceasing.
They set out toward Opol.
Of that swaggering unfortunate man,
Which deed can one recount?
They traveled day and night,
Never stopping to rest on an elbow;
They had the horns and trumpets sounded,
And loaded the army's gear upon the coursers.
They traveled without sparing the horses,
Covering great distances without restraint.
Your lord began the spectacle;
He whipped the fourteen horses onward.
The lion, rough by nature,
Slaughtered and left behind
Every living creature and head of livestock.
