Yet if we truly were foster brothers,
Spare me and leave me alive.
Were we not slopes upon the same mountain pass,
Foster brothers sharing a single soul?
Whatever we found, we gathered together;
We grew up together, companions of one age.
We kicked and tussled like colt and yearling,
And quarreled as we suckled the same breast.
We slept in the same bed
And shared the same delights.
We gathered our allotted herds together,
And grew like twins, companions of one age.”
Thus he spoke these words,
And Külchoro stood in anguish.
Kanchoro, that once-noble hero,
Stood enraged and ready;
No pleading could save a life.
He would destroy everything there was—
Kanchoro the khan felt no regret.
Its steel haft was thick as a fist:
Such was the battle-axe.
His waist sagging, his back bent,
His eyes rolling and bulging,
He gripped his black-steel axe
In both hands and swung it round.
Kanchoro gathered all his fury:
“The cleft of his shoulder blades is the spot—
Here is where this raging false slave
Shall meet his death!”
He seized his blood-drinking axe
And swung it round;
He charged the hero
And struck at the nape of his neck.
Then, shouting in rage,
“Surround and bind him, wrestlers!”
He summoned his champions,
And Kanchoro stood facing him.
As Kanchoro confronted him,
The peerlessly agile,
Clashing lion-hero,
The merciless warrior who had subdued the world,
Stood ready.
Yesterday, while leopard Manas yet lived,
A wondrous blade had come to him by fate
From the heights of the Mediyan Desert,
From a sheer ridge facing the traveler,
From a crag where mountains vanished,
From the jutting brow of a stooping peak:
The Six-Edged Blade that fell from heaven,
The finest and most deadly of swords,
The strongest of living steel.
When drawn from its sheath with a battle cry,
It never went idle without drinking blood;
Until it had drunk its fill of blood,
It would not return to its sheath.
Laid upon grass, it kindled fire;
Whoever it marked was swiftly destroyed.
Drawn from its sheath, it glowed red;
When swung at the foe with a cry,
It lengthened to forty fathoms.
The renowned Six-Edged Blade—
Külchoro grasped its scabbard,
Drew it by the hilt,
And before Kanchoro could pull back,
The ever-ready Külchoro
Had the sword in hand.
Before Kanchoro could twist away,
Turn and draw back,
Külchoro flared in rage;
He caught the slave upright
By the bottom hem.
The falcon warrior drew the blade crosswise
And stood brandishing it;
The slave’s right coat-tail
He sliced clean away.
One thigh of Akborchuk
He hewed through to the bone,
