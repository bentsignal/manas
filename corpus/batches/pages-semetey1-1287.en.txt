Having fought in many battles,
Was Kojo not one who knew
How to unleash upheaval?
Yesterday, when your bloodthirsty lion Manas
Brought turmoil upon Beijing of Chin-Machin
And the people of the true Kakan,
When he seized the khanate
Of the swarming Chinese,
When he imposed tribute upon China
And raided its city,
Was Kojo not the one who besieged
Its cities looming in might?
Was Kojo not the one
Who made their khans stand rigid
And beat them down?
Was Kojo not the one
Who unleashed relentless turmoil?
At the gate of walled Beijing,
From the hero called Kangur,
From sixty sorcerer-warriors
Learned in wondrous arts,
Was Kojo not the one who saddled
The accomplished swift Kara At?
Was Kojo not the one who remained seven months
And studied sorcery?
Was Kojo not the one who wore
A coat woven with spells?
Was Kojo not the one who knew
The Chinese people's mighty war-prayers?
At the name of your uncle Kojo today,
People started in fear;
He had read forty-two forms
Of the Chinese script.
Was Kojo not the one who went
To Kakanchin's war?
Was Kojo not there
In the seething, terrible slaughter?
Was Kojo not the one who learned
The ways of war?
When the great Chinese and Manchu people,
Swift on foot before their khan,
Engulfed them and reached them together,
When the heroes' shots went astray,
When, at Beijing's fortress,
Chubak, son of Akbaltai,
Was struck by a stray bullet
And vanished from sight,
When young Kojo grew wrathful
And spilled Muslim blood,
When all sense left
The Muslims' heads
And black fog covered them,
When the greatest of the sixty,
The finest of the Forty Companions,
Syrgak, lay dying,
When young Kojo grew wrathful,
When Akkula fell to a bullet,
When Almambet was martyred,
Was Kojo not the one who went
To his uncle Almambet's side?
Was Kojo not the one who took
The weather-stone that conjures weather,
Thinking it might one day prove useful?
Was Kojo not a champion
Of fighting by stratagem?
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Was taut as red-hot iron;
When you looked upon his frame,
He resembled a spur of Kara-Too.
Do not ask what Kojo was like:
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
A giant as large as a mountain,
With a sable cap upon his head,
In this false world
A singular black-gray wolf.
He had the flying Kara At;
All who challenged him,
