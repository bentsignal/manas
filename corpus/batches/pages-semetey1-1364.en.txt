To the Forty Saints and Khizr-Ilyas,
Entrusting him to their protection,
To Manas, who had passed into eternity,
Dedicating him to his spirit,
They spread their hands, saying, “Amen!
The piebald-headed one is the tulpar among horses;
My orphan is setting out on a journey—
Reach him, heroes, and give him aid,
Almambet and Chubak among you!
May God set the undertaking right;
My heroes who passed away long ago,
Give aid to my only one!
If you turn toward Kakan and Beijing,
It will be a disgrace if you let him be taken;
To Almighty God
I entrust for safekeeping
The only one I gained through suffering!
Go safely and return unharmed,
My only one, whom I bore near death;
Come back quickly, without making us pine!
For six months we shall burn with longing;
Children, if you do not return in six months,
Give up hope of your mother—
We shall behold the face of the next world!
Parted from you, our only one,
What desire can we attain?
For the true place from which none return,
If you are delayed, we shall depart!
You will fight the Kakanchin;
May gracious Allah grant you a road,
And may your bloodthirsty falcon of a father
Come out upon your road and give you his hand!
You will fight Kongurbai;
May that infidel be brought low.
My foal, may what you accomplish
Remain, for the people of the final age,
A deed never to be forgotten!”
His two mothers gave their blessing,
Their hearts painfully torn,
And both mothers wept,
Tears pouring from their eyes.
When they wept in grief,
The people wept, wept blood;
Unable to cease their sorrow,
All the multitude wept,
As though they were about to part forever,
Their hearts painfully torn,
Their ribs seeming to split,
Tears pouring from their eyes.
Among the Argyn and Kyrgyz,
The khoja in a white turban wept,
The mullah with the white Quran wept;
All those people wept.
They sacrificed a mare to the moon-shaped hoof,
And, invoking Allah, sacrificed a camel;
For the young son of the prince,
All the Kyrgyz sobbed
And loudly gave their prayer.
In high spirits he drove fourteen horses,
Loading provisions upon the tulpar.
While the people grieved,
He received their blessing;
The Kakanchin was the foe he would fight;
The lion prince rode out.
From Kanykei's palace
His countless host set out on the journey;
The young son of the hero
Was bound for Beijing.
In high spirits he took fourteen horses,
Received the blessing of the saint,
And with Khan Bakai as their leader,
Your sovereign rode out.
Eagerly girding himself,
Straining and swaying,
Saying, “My journey is far;
Kongurbai is the one I shall fight,
And I shall avenge my father,”
