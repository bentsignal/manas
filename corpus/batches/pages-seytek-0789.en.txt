Your mercilessness became your strength;
Whoever challenged you was laid low.
The valor became known
Of the doomed man who boldly rode out.
Mortals despaired of life,
And whoever faced her died.
The people who sought her died,
And warriors of many tribes died.
Those who went were swept by a black wind;
No human being could match such might.
Kayypchal’s valiant daughter,
A maiden who brings strife to the whole world,
A maiden seasoned in bloody battle,
Gave true punishment
To every person who fought her.
Her arms and armor numbered eighty-four;
Where she struck, red fire flared.
She brought the whole world under her sway;
In all creation there cannot be
Another calamity like her.
Her father was Kayypchal,
And his people were said to be wicked.
The warrior’s homeland
Was a place called Jelpиниsh.
Her father was a khan who died;
A woman in name but a lion in truth,
She caused extraordinary turmoil.
She was a fiercely glowing ember;
Among all the sons born to mankind
There was no champion like her.
Her father was rich in people.
Her fortune was perfectly full,
Her pegs were made of steel,
And all her worldly goods stood ready.
Fortunate Kayypchal
Possessed every treasure he desired.
Fifteen tigers guarded her path;
No person dared approach,
And no enemy who met them prevailed.
Her gate was narrow,
And against the world that stormed it
She held such dreadful wonders.
Looking beyond it,
One saw a path meant for the young.
She had trained herself thoroughly:
With one blow she destroyed,
When enemies appeared she overturned them,
And those who provoked her wrath
She uprooted utterly.
She has an elephant with a sword-like trunk,
And until Judgment Day
It seems it cannot be captured—
Know this, my poor lady.
This is what Ajybay has seen;
If you are wise, heed my words.
No person has approached
That fortunate, ill-starred woman,
And none who met her survived.
When I looked upon that farther side,
My heart leapt from its place;
When I counted all her deeds,
My arms came out of their sockets,
And my heart burst from its casing.
Every bone in me shook,
And my fly-sized soul shrieked.
Who could love her so greatly
As simply to despair of life?
I struggled for my very soul,
And nearly abandoned life.
The reason for my despair was this:
Many giants and ogres had come,
Yet he did not heed what they said
And would not give them his valiant daughter.
Farther on stands a golden throne,
Surrounded by a grove of plane trees.
There is a path wide enough for one soul,
And beside it lies a deeply dug pit;
That God-stricken woman
Seems ready to trample all who pass.
