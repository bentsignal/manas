Dark within and blue-gray without,
My peerless heroic companion.
Your eyes have seen a thousand roads;
Believing they would one day serve a great need,
I cherished and raised you myself.
I slaughtered ninety beasts for your feast;
I gave you a fitting name
So that you might subdue every foe you faced
And crush him into the earth.
Thinking to feed the children,
I had young mares slaughtered.
The honeyed fat of those mares
I cut for you with my teeth
And made you suck from my tongue.
Fearing the wind and breeze would touch you,
I had you covered with a fine veil;
Fearing you might defer to another,
I raised you wild and headstrong
And let you heed no one’s word.
I chose the finest brisket and rump fat
And fed them to you.
Among the countless Argyn and Kyrgyz,
Who could ever call you a mere child?
For a champion son like you,
Your precious elder would sacrifice himself.
When I dwell in the Ala-Too
And settle beside a river,
If my belly grows hungry,
I cut off rump fat and eat.
If my Külchoro is beside me,
I think, ‘I shall destroy the Jiangjun.’
When my belly grows hungry,
I cut off rich belly fat and eat;
While I have my treasured companion,
I think, ‘Let alone China,
O my colt, I shall spread slaughter
Across the whole world.’
If I stroke Taibuurul’s tail
And race the steed afar,
I think, ‘My horse will return victorious.’
If my mountain-sized Külchoro
Stands shouting beside me,
I think, ‘Let alone China,
I can make my claim against the whole world.’
With the black sword Zulpukor,
I think, ‘I shall cut them down,’
So long as my beloved companion lives;
Against the whole wide world
I think, ‘I shall drive my way through.’
With the red sword Zulpukor,
I think, ‘I shall cut them down.’
I have a musket before me
And many enemies who oppose me.
I shall storm in and crush them;
So long as my beloved companion lives,
I think, ‘I shall become a khan.’
If my Külchoro remains true,
I shall become a mountain pass,
Lofty and towering
Like the Ala-Too.
My fame spreading throughout the world,
I think, ‘I shall become a lion.’
If I contend with the khan,
I used to say, ‘I know how to hew him down.’
The Chinese and Manchus who grapple with me,
By sheer force
I think, ‘I know how to drive them back.’
The khan himself is my opponent;
I shall strike again and make them swarm,
Spill their blood and subdue them.
Those who heed words
I shall persuade and subdue;
Those brutes who understand no words
I shall bring to the faith and subdue.
Through the soaring Kara-Too
And along the lower reaches of the Kars River,
I intend to make a road.
The vast Chinese city of Beijing—
