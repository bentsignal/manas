She sobbed beyond measure.
Her attendants beside her,
Chachykey’s forty daughters-in-law,
Girls and married women mingled together—
Among those women
There was every kind and none lacking:
Some had handspan waists and moonlike faces,
Some were radiant beauties,
Some spoke words sweet as honey,
Some had wide, fawn-like eyes,
Some had black hair like meadow grass,
And some had finely drawn black brows.
Your imperious sister-in-law Chachykey
Moved with an extraordinary bearing.
Her attendants beside her
Would not let her feet touch the ground;
They spread Semetey’s great carpet
Upon the black earth,
And would not let her tread God’s bare ground.
The imperious woman Chachykey
Approached the gated fortress
And filed inside;
The boys had not yet arrived from the field.
Kulchoro and Kanchoro
Could not retreat after hearing her words,
Nor tell the broad-backed wretch
What had become of the falcon.
They could not endure
The heat of that truly formidable woman,
Could not go before her and speak,
Yet could not turn back without entering.
The twin choros
The two twin companions
Stood taking counsel,
And shaped their words carefully;
Neither would heed the other.
Of the woman whose wrath was fierce,
Unable to enter her encampment,
Unable to greet her and withdraw,
“We lost Akshunkar,”
Or explain what had happened.
They stood braced together,
Having brought this misery upon themselves.
Kanchoro, the son of Chubak, said:
“Ah, my dear Kulchoro,
Heed these words of mine, choro.
Go to your sister-in-law, choro,
And tell her plainly,
‘We have lost Akshunkar!’
Ask whether she knows the falcon,
And learn where she thinks it may be.
You have a gentle face and fluent tongue,
And were always a man who did not misspeak.
You are a nimble orator and quick thinker,
A boy accomplished in speech,
One who could always find a way with words.
Go to your sister-in-law.
Ask whether she knows of the falcon,
And approach her with tactful words.
Whether she knows or does not,
You are a man, my choro—go.
To that devilish woman,
That impetuous wretch,
My Kulchoro, go.
Tell her what followed Akshunkar;
Tell of the suffering that befell it
Along the shore of Ala-Köl.
She who has licked the edge of a sword—
If she tells where the falcon is,
Tell her of your magnanimous lord’s pain!”
While Kanchoro spoke,
“Very well, elder, very well,” he said,
Bending his knee to the ground.
From atop Akborchuk
He ran down at speed,
And Kulchoro flew like an arrow,
Looking to either side
And licking his lips.
