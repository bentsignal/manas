Behold old Bakai:
His resolute heart was torn,
Tears poured from his eyes;
Facing what had befallen him,
He raised his hands and cried, “God!
Oh, my son Seytek, my tiger,
Oh, for your black eyes, bright as glass,
Oh, my cuckoo left behind,
Let me cherish you!
On the mountain ridge you are a bloodthirsty eagle,
A champion left as our living sign;
You are a spring flowing since ancient times,
A champion left to inherit our legacy.
While the people wail and the nation cries out,
Thus do we hold your celebration;
For your life, my foal,
We keep praying to God!
For every flutter of your eyelashes,
My son, we keep praying to God.
Seeing you brings peace to the heart;
When we look upon you, no grief remains.
My plane tree risen on barren ground,
Whose shade shelters and sustains the people!
My black-browed falcon,
Who catches wild geese without a hood,
At whose soaring my soul rejoices,
My falcon unerring when he takes aim,
My racehorse at the tether,
Who wins the prize without cooling down,
My foals Kuyaly and Seytek,
My gyrfalcon who drove
The croaking ravens
Back into their proper place!
My poor dear ones, for whom I would give my life.
Maiden lion Kuyaly and brave Seytek,
My matched pair of plane trees,
Now that my beard is white as a swan
And the hour of my journey draws near,
I pray that He preserve you both—
This I ask of the Almighty!
My matched pair of lions,
You who tirelessly wage battle,
You have delivered a sorrowing nation
From its torment.
You are the figure before my eyes,
The presence on whom I rely;
My finger’s breadth when I measure,
My cherished descendants
Who sit beside me.
Ancestors and children, amen!
May your embers turn to gold;
May your life be long!
May the throne you mount be golden;
Amen, may God favor you!
May fortune settle upon your brow.
My son Seytek, hear me:
If you observe me, I hold mystery within;
I am a soul of Lokman the Wise,
I am great old Bakai,
I am the giver of blessings.
I made the withered grow green,
I made the aged young again,
I made the pale bloom with life;
Whoever prospered when I spread my palms,
Whoever heard me spread my hands and say, ‘Amen,’
Found happiness.
My blessings never went astray;
A curse I uttered in wrath
Never took more than six days.
There is gold within my words,
And every wonder dwells within me.
His spoken word became proclamation:
No less than a prophet
Was King Bakai, the elder;
He was himself a saint,
He was the discerning eye of wisdom.
Spreading his hands and saying, “Amen,”
Our mighty forefather Bakai
Bestowed his righteous blessing—
The wellspring of the world beyond,
