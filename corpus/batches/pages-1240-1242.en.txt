He devised a plan, thinking hard,
Considering where he might
Capture Syrgak by a trick.
Coughing in great heavy bursts,
“I shall shoot the warrior Syrgak,” he said.
He set fire to his match,
With Chinese firesteel and striking stone
Scraping out a glowing spark.
“I shall shoot,” he said, preparing,
Raising and leveling his gun.
Algara beneath him
Coiled and sprang away.
Taking Syrgak squarely in his sights,
He fired once with his matchlock.
As soon as he pressed it, the matchlock
Puffed smoke and cracked.
Syrgak stood firm, untroubled;
The fierce slave’s shot
He counted as nothing.
He did not think, “I shall die.”
The mountainlike fierce one’s
Appearance and bearing
Your Syrgak stood observing.
“On fescue ground where dust cannot rise,
If only I could meet him there
And face him in combat!”
Your Syrgak was dying with eagerness.
“There, in distant Talas,
If only I could arrive ahead of an arrow,
And upon arriving, with that slave,
Fight a shattering battle!
If my appointed death comes suddenly,
Spear in hand, struck by a bullet,
Let me die a martyr.
If I remain alive and do not die,
Let me grapple with Kongurbay
And see which of us prevails!”
His insides boiled and blazed;
Ulak Khan’s Syrgak
Longed to join a spear fight.
His eyes strained with eager waiting.
His stoutly made lacquered spear
He gripped firmly in his hand.
Grasping the spear,
He aimed it at a chosen spot.
Without drawing back what he extended,
Without relaxing his stern expression,
Without recoiling in fear,
His pouch belt girded at his waist,
He raised the cry, “Islam!”
And shouted “Manas!” as his battle cry.
When the fierce one heard that cry,
He too charged forward.
Without drawing back what he extended,
He too came on without fleeing.
Syrgak and Kongurbay
Were charging at each other.
Sparing not his flylike life,
Alooko’s Kongurbay
Also raised his spear
And came on, drawing it round.
He aimed at a deadly spot,
Readying himself as he came.
“At the lungs and liver,” he said.
He had known the chosen places
From his earliest youth, that slave;
With Syrgak, in former times,
He had entered many battles, that slave.
At that moment the warrior Syrgak,
With Kokchebich beneath him,
Shattering the stones it trod,
In the opening and closing of an eye,
The warrior Syrgak reached
The great fierce one with a rush.
When Syrgak drew close
To the thundering fierce one,
“Above the saddle’s pommel,” he said,
“At the top of the bladder,” he said,
And Syrgak drove his thrust home.
At that moment Kongurbay,
Keeping to the spot he aimed at,
Your wolf-man also drove his thrust home.
The two warriors, sparing neither,
Struck with their spears.
From the space between the two
Fibers flew in clouds.
The two spears with which they thrust
Shattered with a shriek.
Fierce Kongurbay was a warrior;
Your Syrgak was a greater lion.
Clashing without flight,
They were both mighty men.
When Syrgak charged and thrust,
Their knees bent;
The two battle steeds fell,
Spent from the force of the joust.
“If Kokchebich could stand lower,
Before the fierce one could prepare,
Let me seize his lower hem.
If his head comes into my hands,
Let me slice it straight off
And follow after Almambet,
Bringing him joyful news!”
Your Syrgak was dying with eagerness.
He thrust, making them reel and break,
Like small birds struck by a merlin,
Shattering all who came his way.
Like creatures attacked by a wolf,
Your Syrgak split them apart.
“Where is their wolf, the fierce one?” he cried.
His eyes strained with eager searching;
Your Syrgak burned with eagerness.
“Manas!” Syrgak roared,
Making the many Chinese thunder away,
To the edge of Kurpuldok,
Driving and scattering them.
His pride stung, Kongurbay,
Without stopping, the Chinese
Drove back, beating them;
Kongurbay slaughtered them.
“Before the lone champion Syrgak,
Bury yourselves—call yourselves Chinese no more!
You flee all together.”
That vast thicket lying spread out—
What seemed a forest was people;
