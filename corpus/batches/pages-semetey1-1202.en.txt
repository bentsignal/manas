They scattered and cleared the road.
The consummate, peerless Manas
Reached him before an arrow.
Kongurbay's Algara—
He caught it then
And took it away as booty.
Sarala flew like an arrow,
Taking the narrow shortcut;
Kongurbay's Algara
He turned toward the lowlands,
Leading it away as booty.
As Manas was taking it away,
His knees bending to the ground,
Alöökö's Kongurbay
Said, “Leave Algara with me,”
And, turning aside toward Manas,
Bowed like a young bride.
As Kongurbay stood this way,
Crying, “Generous Manas, grant it as a gift!”
Almambet came from beyond,
Flying like an arrow, his limbs outstretched,
Crying, “Generous Manas, grant it as a gift!”
The two skirts of his coat flapping.
Before Almambet could reach him,
Kongurbay's Algara—
Before he could take it away as booty,
Khan Manas thought wisely:
“Another man's coat is no coat;
Another man's horse is no horse.
Kongurbay was a hero;
What shall I do with his horse?
If I seize his horse,
What inheritance will it bring me?
Better than that, here and now,
I will leave his horse with him.”
To the Kalcha who stood bowing,
Manas showed his contempt.
“Here, infidel, is your horse,” he said.
Snarling in anger,
He hurled the reins knotted with an okoro
Toward Kongurbay.
The many Manchus, barely standing,
Took Algara
And set the infidels' master horseman and footrunner,
The lion Kongur Kalcha,
Upon the horse.
The helpless Almambet,
With many cares too great for his breast,
Said, “Alas, bloodthirsty man, may you never prosper!”
His tears streamed in ten directions.
“Alas, Manas, may you come to ruin;
May you never have a second chance!
Would anyone release
A hare that had fallen into his hands?
Could anyone be like you?!
Lion prince, leopard,
Could any person behave so?
Was Kongurbay not
The strength of steadfast Beijing?
Had you given me Algara,
Would I not have drawn the line?
Would I not have made sport
Of the dark mass of Chinese?
If he were parted from Algara,
Would I not have shown him the city?
Would I not have brought upon the Chinese
A calamity no person had seen?
Would I not have requited my old grievance
And exacted from the Kalcha
My affliction from long ago?
From Beijing itself,
This Kongurbay will not disappear.
Unless this Kongurbay dies
And beholds the face of Judgment Day,
The Kyrgyz cause will not prosper.
He is that renowned, formidable stronghold;
He is that lion unlike any mortal,
The most skillful among spearmen—
