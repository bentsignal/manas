When they set out on campaign,
In the desert of Mediyan,
It appeared from the unseen,
Found upon the open steppe.
When Akkula was honored with a feast,
Ninety mares were slaughtered;
The name Akkula was given him.
This year was the time
For him to lower his head and run,
The time to win
Kökötöi’s grand prize.
After Akkula fell behind,
What was I to do when this infidel’s Achbuudan
Surged to the front?
Before he could get away,
If strength allowed, I would reach his end.
Lion Manas flared up,
Raging in fury,
Buckled on the gray kise,
And struck Aibanboz once.
When Khan Manas came from below,
He charged from the flank,
Aiming below the ribs,
At the edge of the kidney.
Charging in fury,
Khan Manas kicked Achbuudan
Across the flank.
The creature that came surging,
When Manas struck in force,
Stumbled away.
Three rows in succession
He knocked aside as he fell.
Manas strained in fury;
The place where Achbuudan fell
From that day to this
Became known as Ak-Zhar.
As Achbuudan lay fallen,
Kongurbai took advantage—
Now God struck him.
His accursed Algara—
No living soul could match its blink;
Unless it were a flying crow,
No four-footed creature could match it.
Achbuudan, lying fallen,
Now saw the accursed horse.
As though black doom had struck his wife,
Kongurbai grew furious.
“The many prizes gathered by the people—
Why should I not take them?
If they do not give me that prize,
Why should I not bring upon
The accursed Burut
A slaughter no man wishes to see?”
Speaking thus, Kongurbai
Roared in fury;
At the Kalcha’s might,
The earth broke and thundered.
At the Kalcha’s might,
The many Chinese and Manchus
Split open with a crash.
Algara beneath him
Was a true tulpar, a wondrous beast;
The mighty Kalcha, a powerful hero,
Now raised a shout.
Before one could say a word,
In the blink of an eye,
The loop-knotted reins
He pressed against its belly.
Trained upon level ground,
He had mastered the stallion,
Dragging others like a cap.
Making it plunge downward,
Sending it flying like tumbleweed,
Kongurbai, son of Alööke,
Dragged along
Hero Zholoi’s stallion with immense force.
He had trained it on level ground;
