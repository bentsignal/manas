Good heavens, a gray-piebald tent is visible;
When I behold that tent,
My wits scatter in amazement.
That tent they have pitched—
When I look toward its qibla side,
It could hold a camel being slaughtered;
Within that tent,
Four thousand people could lie down,
And ten thousand people
Could fit inside to hold their games.
If you would see a wonder, behold this one;
No such dream comes even in sleep.
Is such a thing known among any tribe?
If we do not arm and go against him,
If, at a moment such as this, we do not prepare
And demand to know who he is,
He seems a dreadful creature,
A sheer red calamity.
If we leave this one unwatched,
He seems the kind who will slaughter us!
Zhöözheldet spoke these words,
Saying that the son of Manas had come.
Dwelling himself in Kyzyl-Kyya,
Muradyl, the son of Kyrym,
Was called a hero greater than the Chinese.
His wrestling strength was beyond measure,
His valor was of another order.
He was a lord, he was a khan,
And such a calamitous man.
When he heard this news,
He drew himself up like a blue tiger,
And those who saw him despaired of life.
Wrapping himself in his blue-gray coat,
If you had seen Muradyl,
He raged like a blue lion;
At the word “enemy” he made ready,
Settled into his wrath,
And fastened on all his weapons.
He tensed himself and thundered,
Roaring in true fury.
At the sight, one’s senses scattered;
Blue flame poured from his mouth.
His anger rose and his soul burned,
And his rage grew fierce.
The Manchu standing beside him—
The valiant Muradyl
Struck him down and killed him.
Do not ask what Muradyl was like:
He was a mighty weather-making slave.
He saddled his own Ker Küröng
And had studied the wondrous arts.
His courage was rare, his venom strong;
He was khan of the Soloon people.
Abandon all hope before this infidel!
He was a formidable hero,
One who stood barring the enemy’s advance.
He wore his sword fitted close,
And occupied the borders of the land.
On the slopes of Kyzyl-Kyya,
At the edge of a perilous enemy land,
He oppressed travelers
And abused every merchant he saw;
By the roadside he continually
Dealt out humiliation.
Every person within his reach
He abused and crushed,
Taking whatever they possessed.
He had a grievance to speak of,
And vengeance for his father,
Who had been killed in the Great Campaign.
When he heard this news,
Distress fell upon him and he started in alarm;
He saddled Ker Küröng
And turned all his thoughts to battle.
Beneath him was Ker Küröng—
That surpassing thoroughbred beast,
The offspring of a bay stallion.
