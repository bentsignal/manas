When I pinned it atop my head,
My prince, despairing of you,
And wandered among the peoples,
I traveled through many lands.
Buyanbek of Muzburchak—
He too is no less than you,
No hero to be belittled!
Dwelling in Sary-Arka,
Called a formidable, weighty hero,
Kökchö's son Ümötöy—
He too is no less than you,
His valor is no lighter than yours!
Ürbü's son Zhosho—
In the land of Eki-Kemin
I saw him as I passed.
He too is no less than you,
His valor is no lighter than yours!
Dwelling in Kyzyl-Kyya,
Kyrym's son Muradyl,
Called a strong-backed hero—
He too is no less than you,
His valor is no lighter than yours!
Alas, because of your murderous fame,
How many did I test in passing?
Why did I test him yet not marry
Kalcha, the khan of Beijing!
With cotton sash and broad boots,
I saw with my own eyes at the ford
Kalcha standing there in splendor.
His beard was grizzled, it seems;
He was already growing old.
For the well-fed Kalcha,
His great boots jutting out,
His neck arched like a rooster's,
His stature proved fitting;
The soles of his boots
Were a full span broad.
Your father Manas
He was fit to strike down!
If only he were not so old,
The Chinese people's wide,
Red-mottled gown
Was what that hero would wear.
If only he were not Chinese,
That accursed Kalcha
Was a lion I would have married!
If you will not take me, do not, my murderer;
I shall go to Kongurbay.
If I do not die, I myself shall go,
Reach him, and marry him.
Karagul, chief of the herdsmen,
He too was one of the lions;
His valor is no less than yours,
No hero to be belittled!
I learned it from the transmitters,
And asked those who know.
I knew your temper long ago;
For the sake of your father gone to the other world,
I have honored your temper!
When driven to true fury,
They say, the madman slaughters his father;
However excellent you may be,
Among the six Kyrgyz tribes
They say there is no fool like you!
To a husband born unruly,
Let me stand yearning and speak:
If you will not take me, do not, my murderer;
I too shall find my equal and leave!"
Saying this and tormenting him, Aychürök
Was deeply hurt by the hero,
And sobbed and wept bitterly.
Unable to draw back the mucus from her nose,
Unable to wipe the tears from her eyes—
Whose first ancestor was Tügöl Khan,
Whose root was blessed by Khydyr,
Descendant of the deathless one,
Growing stronger whenever one died,
Descendant of a sovereign hero:
