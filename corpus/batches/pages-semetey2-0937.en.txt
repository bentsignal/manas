They led him inside.
The heroes and lions
They roused into a frenzy;
They made the longing old men
Dance at the celebration.
They raised an unceasing tumult,
A royal commotion of frantic haste;
Holding such a feast for the warrior,
The gathered people stood there.
The strongmen surged and pressed together,
The great drum thundered loudly;
The multitude of excited nobles
Honored him amid a roaring commotion.
As the people stood thus,
Broad-jawed and eloquent,
The unerring lion-warrior,
Whose smile made the sun rise,
Whose face glowed red as the sun,
Whose sight gladdened every beholder—
The gray wolf Külchoro—
A nimble and keen-edged hero,
If you ask about that fierce man’s nature,
Was just such a sage.
Speaking softly but with great weight,
Külchoro began to speak:
“Great and small are gathered here;
Your elders stand here.
Khan Ezkara and Kongurbay,
All of you stand here.
Your elders of forty tribes,
Countless as ants, stand here;
All your warriors who boast of valor
Stand here.
Those most skilled in the stratagems of war,
Your champions stand here.
Among the spear-wielding warriors,
Your most capable men stand here,
Ready now to look upon
The ordeal of a six-month journey—
Your mighty one stands here.
Your wise one is your khan.
All of you, great and small,
Are wondrously capable,
Mighty cultivators of wisdom.
My child, the valiant blood-spiller Semetey,
Son of Manas, had said:
“Bring me my uncle’s idol;
Before the fair-faced Chinese
I shall truly bow in reverence.”
Your graybeard, old Kalcha,
Take heed, all you people!
What kind of idol is he going for,
Or what kind of idol has he left behind?
When a bronze-shafted spear strikes,
And a grave, weighty deed is at hand,
What use is caution?
What kind of idol is he going for?
Mighty Bakai and Semetey,
The two khans, had said:
“If he counts us as kin,
And does not call us Buruts,
He will heed the words we speak.
If his spoken word is true,
If what he says is bright and pure,
If the revered Kongurbai
Is truly a khan,
If there truly is
Some sacred power within him,
If falcon-like Kalcha
Is truly keen and bold,
Then he will heed these words.
Old Kalcha will surely give
The great idol he worships.”
If you are willing, give the idol
To Semetey.
To the silver-eyed, sun-eyed,
Thunderous champion,
Say, “Here, Kalcha has given it.”
