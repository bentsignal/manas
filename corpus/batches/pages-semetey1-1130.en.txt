I knew it long ago, my son!
Why did you provoke the obstinacy
Of leopard Manas the bloodletter, my son?
As for the humiliation wrought by the Chinese, my son,
If you heed me, then submit.
Take counsel with Manas;
If you give Maaniker, give him only then, my son!
Leopard Manas, bloodletter and lion,
Is the one whose fortunes are complete;
When you look within him,
He is broader than the face of the earth.
For the palmful of Muslims,
He is the hero who will protect them.
If that Manas does not come,
If he does not witness this oppression,
If he himself does not answer
The seething host of infidels,
The youngest of the six lions,
The servant beloved by God—
If lion Manas does not come,
The Chinese, numerous as cattle hairs,
Will trample us underfoot
And scatter all four kinds of livestock.
The countless Kalmyk people
Will bring about our end;
With accursed Joloy at their head,
They will slaughter every one
Of the Muslim sons.
You became rich and acted grandly;
Gathering all the people,
You brought humiliation on your own head, my son!
Send word to Talas;
Send it swiftly without delay
To the noble leopard Manas.
Tell Manas to come if he is a hero,
Tell him to unleash a great tumult,
And rescue the small band of Muslims
From the seething Chinese host.
Tell him to deliver them.
Say all the Black Kalmyks
Have reached us together at Tyup.
Say they raised their whole force
And utterly raided the Kyrgyz.
Say the Kalmyks bound
Your old hero Koshoy,
And utterly slaughtered
All the remaining giants!
Tell the valiant leopard Manas!
Wearing a blue lynx cap,
Wielding a mottled blue shield,
Khan Bakay, the rich man's son,
Who rests his head upon pure gold,
The resourceful sage—
Tell valiant old Bakay;
Tell all the valiant men!
Golden-crested, with a tasseled belt,
Only son of Khan Azezi in Beijing,
Who came and settled in Talas
And became Manas's companion—
Tell my leopard Almambet!
The peerless hero, born of Amyz,
Who rides a blue-gray horse,
Who wears a blue rapys coat—
Tell the gray-maned hero Chubak!
The finest of sixty,
The best of the forty companions,
Light on a horse and fit for a colt,
Sleepless and vigilant on the road,
The champion's support,
Khan Manas's hope—
Tell Syrgak, son of Ulak Khan!
Tell Shuutu with his tent-shaped kalpak;
Tell the old man like Kyrgylchal;
Tell all forty companions!
Say that it is Khan Kokotoy's memorial feast,
And that Kokotoy's Bokmurun
Has encamped at Karkyra.
Without sending word to you,
