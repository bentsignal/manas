His strength was beyond compare.
When you looked upon the fierce warrior,
His courage was just as extraordinary:
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
A fierce man resembling a lion,
He leveled his spear wherever battle raged.
The champions who clashed with him
Craved a patch of ground as small as a hare’s.
He did not flinch when a gun was fired,
And when they closed in combat,
He did not fearfully leave his flank exposed.
His tigerish eyes blazed,
And he bit his lips until they split.
When he turned and looked you in the face,
He seemed the very mold of seasoned warriors.
The wind issuing from his mouth
Was like a tempest from Bukhara;
The fury of his two eyes
Was like embers in a riverbed,
And when he muttered, his voice
Was like the shot of a cannon.
When one looked before him,
A black-maned tiger
Seemed poised to tear open a wound;
A black-striped tiger
Crouched directly in front
Of this ill-fated man,
While a devouring dragon,
Its jaws gaping cavernously,
Crouched behind him.
The giant Zümrük bird
Seemed ready to swoop down at once,
Its talons stretched wide.
He wheeled like a tiger,
And none who grappled with him survived.
His wrathful aspect was unlike any other,
Overawing the whole wide world.
The renowned master of battle,
His quiver curved like the waist of a maiden,
Was a raging black calamity
That seemed ready to devour any foe.
His appearance was strange, his venom strong;
If you met him, you abandoned hope of life.
Such was his form:
Broad-chested, with expansive shoulders,
Wide-mouthed and beetle-browed,
Broad-browed and deep-eyed—
Thus had the Creator made the warrior.
Taking a mace as large as a house,
He scowled and raised havoc.
He struck a black rock once,
Sparks bursting from his mace
And fire leaping from the stone.
In the close-fought struggle
He never fearfully exposed his flank,
And of the champions who faced him
He crushed many into the earth.
When Kyyaz saw Külchoro,
He could not endure his heat
Or withstand his cold;
He could not advance to confront him
Or plant his spear firmly.
Turning his back, hero Kyyaz
Could not bear it and withdrew.
He wheeled his horse and fled;
When he saw the warrior’s might,
He could not turn back and wreak havoc,
But pleaded with Kanchoro:
“My dear comrade,
Your lifelong companion Külchoro
Is blessed with perfect fortune.
Look into the heart of this matter—
Good heavens, comrade, consider it!
He is a son destined to be a fortress
For the eagle of the yellow bluff,
A son destined to be a shield
Not merely for the Kyrgyz, but for all the ages.
He is a peerless, valiant hero.
