Kanchoro was born afterward
To his namesake father Chubak.
Abyke’s Aktelki
Was the horse Kulchoro rode;
The horse Kanchoro rode
Was Khan Köbösh’s Köktelki.
The two truly raced one another,
And rode out in anger,
Taking from Semetey’s own mouth
His firm promise and oath,
And carrying the letter their elder had written.
When the sun was sinking red,
Before the people had gone to rest,
They came below Tört-Kül,
To Prince Manas’s orchard,
To the dark eye of the spring,
To the solitary plane tree,
To Kanykey’s encampment,
To Chachykey’s fortress with its gate.
The boys now entered.
The two choros matched their pace;
Unable to find a way forward,
The boys whispered together,
Chatted and laughed softly.
They had long known
Their sister-in-law’s fierce temper;
They made a firm agreement,
And stood without exchanging blessings.
The delicate woman Chachykey
Had a delicacy all her own,
Her scarf like a golden amulet,
A troubled, beautiful white brow.
No man went boldly before her;
Whoever saw the cherished woman marveled.
No stranger entered her chamber,
And no soul apart from Semetey
Walked openly beside her.
Her brow was high and her venom strong—
Do not ask about your sister-in-law’s nature.
This was one of those dark days,
The day when her true fury was touched
And she poured out her wrath.
No traveler came to her,
And she received no news from me;
This was one of those dark days.
Beside Chachykey were
Thirty daughters-in-law and thirty girls,
A retinue of no small splendor,
Her attendants gathered for amusement.
Around their throats hung
Golden earrings and amulets;
All were stately and beautiful young women.
Semetey had a tower,
And from the top of that tower
Your poor sister-in-law Chachykey
Stood keeping watch.
Realizing that Semetey was absent,
She cried out wildly—
God had struck the woman.
Chachykey’s forty daughters-in-law,
All of them skilled women,
Stood surrounding her.
Now God had struck the woman;
The delicate Chachykey
Spoke to the daughters-in-law:
“The end time has surely come!
Some accursed man seeking a master
Must have taken away
Badisha’s falcon.”
Grieving when she saw the boys,
The delicate woman Chachykey
Rushed down from the tower
And came hurtling below.
“The accursed thing has led him astray
And taken it in the end,” she said.
Her dear eyes, like a quail’s,
Shed tears in separate streams;
She sobbed and wept uncontrollably,
