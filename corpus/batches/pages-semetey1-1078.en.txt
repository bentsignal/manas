"Chynkozho and Toltoy are no heroes;
This boy could slaughter the Chinese.
In this form, Külchoro
Could take not only the Zhediger
But all of Zhet-Kayt,
And stir the whole age into turmoil."
Rejoicing at what he saw:
"I may devote myself to Allah;
I may sacrifice myself for the boy.
God will grant my prayer;
Somehow the boy will return."
Turning back toward the maidens,
Semetey entered the clamor;
Semetey entered festivities
That could not be subdued.
Here Semetey's guest
Remained in delight;
Now let us tell
The tale of Külchoro.
Bold young Külchoro,
A distinguished youth who knew all things,
Had a stature apart from other boys.
If he wished, there could be much lament;
Watch the spectacle:
He would bring great conflict upon Toltoy.
The bridle fastened to his horse,
He was Almambet's six-month
Unborn legacy.
Not merely Toltoy—the boy's turmoil
Would reach across the world.
Have you heard the report?
He was the descendant of one
Who made enemies of the Black Kalmyks and Manchus,
Who crushed the foe he grappled with
And brought them low;
The descendant of one who began
The great tumult against the Kakan;
The descendant of one who killed
And abandoned his own father;
The descendant of the hero
Who scattered Beijing's wealth;
The descendant of the hero who, angered
By his position as khan,
Fled the Kakan's Beijing.
As though he had seen many trials,
Almambet had given Choro
Courage and lion-heartedness
Without measure.
Looking around him,
Licking his lips and nose,
Scanning both sides,
Crouching like a lion,
Straining like a blue tiger,
Couching his spear for battle,
Külchoro raged
And twisted this way and that.
He crossed rough, uneven ground,
And traversed hummocked sand;
Unable to feel the earth's hardship,
Day passed and night came,
Darkness settled as brows turned black.
His eyes bound by night travel,
He crossed winding ravines,
And passed twisting mountains.
Seeing a broad black river,
He traveled without turning aside,
Covering much ground without sparing his horse.
Now he drew near
The junction of nine roads,
At the boundary of Börü-Koy:
One road came into Afghanistan,
A black road went to Kashgar;
One road came into Afghanistan,
A straight road went to Turpan;
One road came into Afghanistan,
A marked road went to Beijing;
One road came into Afghanistan—
