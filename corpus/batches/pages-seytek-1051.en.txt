Dedicate a moon-hoofed horse,
So the people may rise in a thunderous celebration
And the great multitude may behold the spectacle.
Saddle your steed, valiant gray wolf;
God has granted aid with both hands”—
Thus Akjoltoy spoke,
Relating all the tidings.
As he was telling half
Of God’s true, life-giving message,
There stood his eleven companions,
And Baimyrza, son of Baitaylak—
Twelve thousand tigers,
Men of immeasurable swiftness.
He knew how to outwit the foe,
The best and cleverest strategist,
The one who tended the leopard hero,
And truly knew his innermost secrets;
When a grim enemy appeared,
He sent him tirelessly against the foe—
Ask no more of Baimyrza’s greatness.
He was the fierce hero’s companion,
His comrade even in death,
The one who rode out on reconnaissance,
And seized the hardest objectives;
If an enemy confronted him,
He brought devastation upon the foe.
Ask no more of the twelve heroes,
Külchoro’s chosen warriors,
Handsome men radiant as the sun;
Ask no more of their greatness.
When black muskets cracked,
They tirelessly attacked the foe
And entered the engulfing flames;
When fierce guns thundered,
They drove the enemy back without fear,
Wearing armor no bullet could pierce.
Every one of them
Struck the enemy with devastation.
They pitched the tents along the road.
Even if slaughter lasted six months,
These heroes never lost their wits;
Ask no more of those warriors.
At “Charge!” they spurred their horses;
At the slightest word they laid down terms.
Told to take a cap, they took a head;
Told to spill blood, they spilled an evil spirit;
Told to drink water, they drank poison.
When their righteous fury seized them,
They chased wild asses and turned them into cattle;
When their mounting rage seized them,
They laid the whole world waste.
They set grassy lands ablaze
And burned the meadows;
Whenever they seemed spent, they smoked anew,
Growing stronger from their very roots.
They poured blood into the dark waters,
Raising vast clouds of dust in battle,
Kicking at knucklebones and storming the khan’s court,
Throwing themselves wholly into such sport.
Where he stepped, Mount Küdür seemed to rise;
Whatever he stalked became his enemy.
The fierce leopard Seytek
Appeared magnificent as a lofty mountain.
In those days no mortal
Could approach Talas to face him.
He had barred every perilous road;
The Black Chinese and the countless Manchus
Stayed huddled within Kakhan, never venturing out,
For none possessed such courage.
All the heroes gathered,
And the enemy’s path was blocked;
No foe raided their pastures,
And no confronting enemy survived.
Once, amid the vast world,
While his fortune still stood secure,
Within the fortress with gates of steel,
In Kanykei’s stronghold,
Five stout palaces had been built—
Such was their heroic magnificence.
Many monuments of them remained in the world,
