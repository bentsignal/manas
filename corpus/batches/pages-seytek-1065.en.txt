‘My leopard father is Semetey;
I am Seytek, son of Semetey.
My coats of mail are eighty-four,
And when I strike, I am a crimson fire.
My homeland is broad Talas,
And my grandfather is gray-maned hero Manas.’
‘If you raise your royal camp, that is the land;
If you seek a husband, that is the hero.
Leave your former homeland,
Seek Seytek, your equal,
And make that land your own.
If you endure the hardships of the journey,
The Creator will grant your wish.
You will attain your heart’s desire;
But if you fail to seek him,
Your tiny soul will shriek,
And every bone in your body will ache.
You will suffer many hardships,
Your soul burning as you cry, “Oh, Seytek!”
And you will waste away in longing.
The realm from which none return
You will see all too soon.’
Having said this, the four great men
Vanished from my sight,
And my bosom filled with light.
‘We plunged into pleasure,
And lay upon the narrow bed,
Promising each other a blissful night,
Rejoicing as we made love.
When we reached the height of delight,
Seytek and I together,
Lying upon a single bed—
Alas for that lovemaking!
It was only a dream, with no truth in it.
Sorrow came to me from the unseen;
It was the Creator’s doing.
When the dawn shone white,
And light entered the earth,
When the eastern sky grew bright,
I awoke from sleep;
The hero who had lain in my embrace was gone—
Such filth and falsehood is this world.
It was as though he had changed and vanished;
God Most High indeed creates
Souls of every kind.
I searched everywhere but could not find him;
Though I wished to end my wretched life,
What could I do? My hand would not obey.
I burned and blazed into an ember,
Raging until I became fire.
Unable to take my own life,
I stood close to death.
Whenever I remembered, my heart became an ember;
In the time it took to open and close my eyes,
The lion in my embrace was gone.
The sun above me turned gray;
Parted from my radiant hero,
My grief stirred within me.
The moon in the sky turned gray;
Parted from my lionhearted hero,
My sorrow stirred within me,
And I could no longer endure this world.”
So she cried out,
And to the attendant maiden beside her
She spoke, sobbing bitterly.
Her soul-companion Külayım,
Her confidante who untied every knot,
The companion of that radiant unfortunate one,
Who would share even death with her,
Who walked and stood beside her,
Her companion whose wish was one with hers.
“My sun-bright friend Külayım,
I shall sing my song to you,
And tell my secret as sacredly as my faith.
Because of lionhearted Seytek,
My insides become a blazing ember.
Pining with thoughts of Seytek,
I shall fall wan and sick and perish.
