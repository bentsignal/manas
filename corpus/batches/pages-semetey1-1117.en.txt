They had scarcely any sense left.
When Bokmurun, son of Kökötöy,
Learned that the people were afraid,
From Kökötöy's ingots of red gold
Wrapped in great sheets of hide,
Saying, “This is your reward,”
He brought them before him
And weighed out six.
Khan Zholoy of the Kalmyks
Looked at the boy
And was pleased.
For Khan Zholoy's slaughter beasts,
He released and drove out a herd
From Kökötöy's countless livestock.
Into brave Zholoy's hands,
He gave six thousand wethers and a thousand mares
For slaughter, they say.
Sugar piled like camels and cattle
He gave them to break up, they say.
Firewood enough for six thousand camels
He gave them to burn, they say.
Black liquor brought in loads,
And liquor in Kalmyk leather flasks,
From the days when trouble first arose,
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Had prepared beforehand.
Six zhaga measures and one leather flask
He gave them for drink, they say.
All the wealth in the world
He cast into the very center, they say.
He put Zholoy's heart at ease
And, with his great army, settled Zholoy
Against the foot of the hill, they say.
Let us leave this matter here
And begin from the other side.
There was yet another spectacle.
Then dust curled into the sky;
The great army that came covering the land
Collided with one another,
Drawing back and standing fast.
The great army that came to the feast
Knew no human language at all,
And quarreled in Shibee speech.
Their spears held ready,
The Shibee people were a strange sight,
Their swords belted on without scabbards.
They walked in swaying rows,
Tying dirty black kerchiefs
At the napes of their necks.
They dressed in the Shibee manner,
Fashioned spears from reeds,
And each man tied at his side
A club as large as a fist.
When they roared, their speech could not be understood;
They shouted at one another in Shibee.
They caught and ate badgers,
And ate frogs and snakes alike.
Calling it Khan Kökötöy's memorial feast,
The great mass of Shibee grew excited
And covered the land.
Orokkyr, khan of the Shibee,
Raged upon Kongkulboz;
If you had seen him then,
He was prepared in all his gear.
The sight divided one's attention;
If you saw the Shibee people,
They poured forth like ants.
He made Kongkulboz buck,
Made it rear and wrench itself free.
Twelve targets ahead,
He separated himself from the Shibee;
Then his figure
Looked like a naked blade.
Seven standard-banners came;
A din that split the earth came.
