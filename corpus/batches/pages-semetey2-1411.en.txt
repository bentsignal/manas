And the hero's affliction will subside;
The wounds in his heart will heal,
And the sun's face will brighten.
If disaster is shattered and cut away,
If every fetter is undone,
The grieving breast will grow calm,
All sorrow will scatter,
And the pain of lament will subside.
If prosperity returns once more,
May poor, withered Aichürök,
My sister-in-law lovely as the moon,
Be said to have borne
A hero, the son of Semetey;
May it be said that from the hilly, narrow pass
A spring remains,
That from the precious, magnanimous hero
A young heir remains,
That strength remains
To oppose Kyyaz,
That from the lion Semetey
A male strength, a single support,
A child remains.
If our undertaking is just,
If our dream comes true,
Our life's strength will blaze up,
Crackling like fire and flame.
Calamity is the food we have eaten;
Our heads remain in captivity.
Though we have lamented for nine years,
The tears do not cease from our eyes.
A terrible fire rose in the valley;
Kanchoro became our enemy.
Our chiefs went into captivity,
And all our strength and means departed.
Our deep-rooted ravine collapsed;
Our grave was dug deep,
Our two hands were bound,
Our arms were shackled.
Our precious lives burned;
Our aged mother Kanykei
Was humiliated, tending calves.
Only a spoonful of our blood remained;
Our flesh became a bruised blue pulp.
Hard times and ill fortune came;
Into mouths that once disdained wheat,
An ox's head scarcely came,
After we lost our champion lion
And became captives in this land.
Our eyes stare glassily;
We stand bound as slaves and maids.
If a wondrous, precious night should come,
If joyous fortune should be born,
If Aichürök, lovely as the moon,
Should bear a son—
If the spring at our brow,
The little one left by the hero,
Makes his heroic name known,
The Kyrgyz and many Argyns in Talas
Will rejoice and take him as their support.
There would be countless black camels,
Countless black-blazed mares;
If your sister-in-law bears a son,
Our dear heads will win freedom
And set out toward Talas.
May my bitter lot be washed away;
May the sun shine upon me again,
And may my elder Kül fall into my hands!
Let me overthrow the hero and find peace;
To learn what is to come,
Let me cast the divining lots now.
If in his old age God shows Khan Bakai
The prince once more,
If in Kanykei's old age
God shows me my mother,
Whose days have passed in lamentation—
There is snow upon its mountains,
Game within its forests,
Deer among its broken hills,
