Give Akbala to me.
That proud woman
I shall make my junior wife.
If she will not give me her heart,
If she will not obey my word,
I shall dress her in coarse felt,
Make her know a low slave’s lot,
And send her behind the sheep;
I shall make her fetch wood and water,
Heap misery upon her and torment her,
And demand her years while she still lives.
If the shrew gives me her heart,
I shall erase all her sorrow
And give that radiant unfortunate
A name honored above all.
I shall make her shine like the rising moon
And captivate every watching heart.
That proud woman,
That surpassingly beautiful infidel,
Shall wear rows of yellow keys,
Keys fashioned wholly of gold,
Fastened to her sash.
I shall have her tended in the shade,
Rally the nation and gather the people,
And make her my cherished wife.
I shall stir and gather the people
And make the dark-eyed woman my wife.
If that woman gives me her heart,
I shall have ninety beasts slaughtered for her feast
And name her Ayym.
I shall have a separate palace built for her,
And have emeralds and rubies cut
For the crown-ring of every palace,
And set into its roof-ring;
This is what I shall do for the bride.
But if that woman disobeys,
I shall have the herdsmen beat her
And the shepherds abuse her;
I shall not let her wear rawhide shoes,
Nor let her hem touch the ground;
I shall make her drive camels in the rain
Until her eyes blaze with torment.
If I do not make her the slave of slaves,
If I do not torture the life from her,
May I die no longer Kanchoro,
May I sink as though never born!
As for this warrior Semetey,
I shall torment and round up his people,
Torment his mother and destroy her;
If I do not slaughter their leaders
And extinguish them like a fire,
When I rear up in wrath,
Will not black stone crumble to flour,
Unable to withstand my fury?
When they provoke
The rage of mighty Kanchoro,
Will not the whole world be shattered?
When he rises in fury,
Will he not suck the age dry as a strand of hair?
Is this not what happens when he grows wrathful?
When angered, if I raise my battle cry,
Can the whole world endure it?”
So spoke Kanchoro.
The finest among those in ample robes,
The foremost among those in ample robes,
Your heroic, fierce mother
Stood before Khan Kanchoro,
Along with all the wrestlers who served him.
All were armed with daggers,
Their arms were fearsomely strong;
All were eminent champions.
If even one champion broke loose,
No man could escape his reach.
If you ask of former days,
They were Semetey’s champions;
If you ask of the present,
They were Khan Kanchoro’s champions.
They numbered forty-one.
