“Let the hero saddle up and ride;
If a mighty challenger comes,
If a foe comes who can uproot mountains,
Let him enter battle without retreating;
Let him drive out his enemy’s soul
And rout them all together;
May it never chafe his back,
Nor trouble the heart of its rider;
Though he journey for six months,
Though he ride the horse hard and long,
Though he travel day and night,
Let him not falter when battle begins;
This white saddle was specially made
So it would not press the horse’s flanks.
May those who come after Bölökbay
Know his meticulous skill;
When they remember him in desperate straits,
May they think of us.”
The White Saddle, fashioned with such care,
Was set upon Kulansur,
But the dear lion, proud as a cock,
The valiant hero, did not stir.
The lady took his hand and embraced him,
And softly spoke:
“What fate awaits him?”
He lay back without stirring,
Revealing himself a lion among lions;
The dark-blue warrior took no heed,
But rejoiced at the thought of death.
Akjoltoy, son of Kutubiy,
Whose valor equaled countless troops,
Whose figure led the vanguard,
Whose presence loomed in peril,
Whose path was blessed when he went ahead,
Who, when an implacable foe came
And threatened his entrusted life,
Alone was equal to countless troops,
Mounted a horse like Kulansur,
Put on his coat of tempered iron,
Prepared his arms and took his spear,
And girded on his naked sword.
Tall and broad-shouldered,
He took a spear without a tassel,
Turned its point and hastened
To brave Seytek’s palace,
To Perizada’s fortress;
Gray-maned Joltoy now arrived.
At Akbermet’s palace,
Before it stood a painted hitching post,
And ten champions guarded the court;
What a wondrous host to behold!
Dark-eyed and radiant,
With sable fringe and broad hair ornaments,
Graceful beauties,
With shining camel-calf eyes,
With words sweeter than sugar,
Waists like sheaths and faces like the moon,
Serene beauties,
Whose sight gladdened the heart,
Serene and radiant women,
Who entered the house with stately grace,
True companions to heroes,
Who understood their inmost secrets:
The forty brides appointed for ceremony
Saw Akjoltoy
And darted forward like does,
Showing all the skill
Of those precious, luckless women;
They streamed forth like arrows,
And now took Joltoy’s horse,
Helping the fortunate champion
Down from his tall mount.
The white reins braided by forty companions
Were drawn from Joltoy’s hand,
And tied to the five-sided painted post,
Inlaid with precious stones,
Its every side
Encrusted with pure gold,
While its lower part
Was sheathed entirely in silver.
