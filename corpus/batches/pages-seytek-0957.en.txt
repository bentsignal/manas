From the venom of the struggle,
Eyes dim, the earth shifts,
The mirage flies, the people move.
Girding his renowned waist,
When yesterday they stood face-to-face,
A fierce clash began.
My fly-like soul shrieked,
Every bone in my body throbbed,
My whole frame shuddered,
My body turned to fire,
And began to burn away.
My child was slaughtered,
Doom overtook this wretched soul;
All my mighty strength
Nearly deserted me.
Once encountered, none leaves his place;
I nearly departed.
Like the rising moon, bulging forth,
I nearly sank away.
At the hands of a mere elbow-high boy,
True death came, misery seethed,
My eyes closed and blood surged—
I nearly saw my end.
It seems that by the boy Seytek
I was nearly killed.
Both arms that gave me strength ache,
My heroic courage, my manly limbs—
All ache without moving.
One edge of my marrow aches,
My very allotted span aches,
Everything I know as mine aches.
My head throbs without relief,
As though struck by an axe’s fury;
My back aches without relief—
Has affliction entered me?
This battle is a grievous affair;
Our strength has utterly failed.
Sarybai, distressed,
Unable to stand for grief,
His heart aflame, his liver an ember—
Brave Seytek had become his match.
He had no band to bear him up,
No Chañdayak warrior’s hand
To sit beside him as his support.
This world that crushes men—
Such a burning world it is.
My fly-like soul shrieking,
I would stand there sobbing,
My spirits unable to rise.
If I fight face-to-face,
If true death comes by my hand,
My heroism has been humbled;
I was a wave, now stilled.
The moon has darkened into night;
I go toward death and doom.
Was I destined to miss death in the field,
Was this the day death would come?
If Death summons, it casts its spell, it seems;
Upon the mighty bowman
It lays its cold hand, it seems.
We walk among the living,
Created as mortal servants;
When the poor soul closes its eyes,
It is shut in the grave it sees but once—
Such is harsh, true falsehood.
From the stony ravine where battle rages,
From the barren gorge where no bird flies,
A shudder came and consciousness fled.
From the thick-ranked rocky gorge,
Fighting in this way,
Across the broad open field,
Sweetly arrayed for display,
Among a singular people who race their horses,
He was a lion born to fight.
In the deep valley of Keng-Kol,
Along the great Talas,
Sarybai was a hero—
Is it fitting to fear a child?
Could a ravenous lion be fainthearted,
Could he be a fool like me?
