Whose multitude, we hear, is like black worms.
We continued from tradition
And asked those who knew.
To Beijing, which Alexander could not conquer,
Leveling his spear and drawing his bow,
Was it not Bakay who went?
While my Sultan Manas lived,
Beijing, which Solomon could not conquer—
Seizing its khanate there,
Was it not Bakay who took it?
When battle begins, a bringer of good fortune,
Against a fighting foe, like a vast army—
Is he not skillful Bakay?
Making known what was unknown,
My learned old Bakay;
Making one perceive what was unnoticed,
My falcon, saintly Bakay.
My elder, old Bakay,
A saint who has seen much;
Though my elder is old,
I will take him as my companion.
The reason I make him my companion:
My poor uncle Bakay
Is my falcon left as an inheritance
By my departed father Manas.
If I put my falcon uncle Bakay before me,
He brings good fortune;
If he walks behind me, he is my blessing;
If I take shelter in him, he is like a vast army.
I will journey to the khan,
For I know the greatness
Of my blue-maned uncle Bakay.
One road to Beijing
Has a dug-out pit.
One road, I hear,
Has boiling salt water.
One road to Beijing
Has a blocked pass.
One road to Beijing, I hear,
Has a flood that sweeps all away.
When pursuing my royal father's blood price,
If I extinguish the fire kindled
By the clamoring Chinese,
Seize the khanate
From their lord, Manchu Kongurbay,
And drive him toward the khan.
My mind has turned to hardship,
To girding my waist firmly,
To rolling up my sleeves against
Kongurbay in Beijing,
And pursuing my father's blood price.
Upon the Gün-zhang-zhung people
I will bring terrible slaughter;
This year I will avenge
Falcon Chubak and Almam.
Great Beijing, unreachable by flood—
If venturesome Allah supports me,
I will test myself against it;
If fate arrives and my days end,
I will die in Great Beijing.
How can I live
Without charging and waging war
Against the teeming khan,
Without avenging my royal father?
For the sake of the falcons,
I will bring slaughter upon China;
I will go and avenge the falcons
Who met Judgment Day
At the hands of the red-naveled Kalmyks.
I will bring slaughter upon Beijing.
Without avenging my hero father, the lion,
And the falcons,
How can I remain alive?
I will campaign against Kakan-Chyn,
And contend in battle
With warrior Khan Kongurbay;
I will fight him at close quarters
And spill his blood without ceasing.
