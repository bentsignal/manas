So that he might know its worth,
So that in Beijing of Chin-Machin
He might enter the true battle...
I have a wonder to tell of:
This one axe of mine
Is worth six hundred broodmares.
Its socket is steel and its point is sharp;
I had gold drawn over its handle.
I sharpened its edge and sheathed it;
I had its edge honed hair-fine.
In the venom of a dragon
I tempered it sixty times.
I made the battle-axe ready;
Not for one year,
But for six years without pause I prepared it.
I knew that in Kakanchin's Beijing
You would pursue the blood-price.
If you grew into a mighty hero,
The equal of any hero's son,
I believed you would bring turmoil
Upon Beijing.
From the mouth of the treasury,
I truly fashioned this battle-axe
From ore of Kaiypchy-Too.
Upon the Argyn and Kyrgyz
I laid much hardship;
From the Argyn and Kyrgyz
I exacted tribute by force.
Unable to withstand the charcoal it consumed,
Many forests were destroyed;
Unable to withstand its bellows,
Many young bulls were slaughtered.
I had its point forged sharp,
And heated its joined edge red.
So that wherever it struck would not remain whole,
But would flare and burn away,
In a dragon's venom
I tempered it sixty times.
Once swung, it did not draw back;
It was not stopped by steel.
When swung, it seemed to lengthen;
In the struggle the battle-axe
Glowed red as an ember.
I wound its cheek with gold
And destined it for the campaign
Against the seething infidel,
Hero Khan Kongurbai.
If you strike with it, no regret will remain;
If you go and bring great strife
Upon the seething infidel,
Hero Khan Kongurbai.
I had its wrist-loop nailed fast,
Calling the axe a surpassing companion,
So that he would take it especially,
Charge upon the swarming Chinese,
And unleash strife.
So that my only one, whose heart holds vengeance,
Might avenge himself upon the Kalcha,
Made especially for close combat,
I have this axe I fashioned;
And, my only one, hear this too!
When the reins draw side by side,
He may draw it from its sheath;
When the struggle grows fierce,
He may plunge it into the short ribs—
For this purpose I have made it.
Its pommel is gold and its handle brass;
Its length is half a fathom.
Its sheath bears an engraved inscription;
Made fit for war,
It bears the name Aktinte.
So that he may enter the encircling fight
Against infidels numerous as ants,
I fitted it perfectly for my only one;
So that on a desperate day
He might thrust it into the belly,
And, finding the enemy's opening,
Split him apart in terror.
