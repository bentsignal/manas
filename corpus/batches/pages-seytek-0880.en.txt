He fixed his gaze on Sarybai,
On the wrathful and venomous one,
On that thick-armed champion,
That black fiendish warrior
Who scattered peoples and ruined nations.
He went to taste his true torment,
And, if true death had come, to die.
He summoned stern resolve;
The young lion, that noble champion,
The warrior Seytek, staked
His precious life upon a hair.
Esteeming himself able and lionhearted,
He went against the furious foe,
To shed blood and take life
And clash against him,
To die if true death had arrived.
Boasting of courage and lionhood,
Boasting of strength and valor,
He went to settle the account
Of the infidel Sarybai.
He went to begin the strife and break the host,
To be struck by a musket ball and fall,
To perish and die,
To grapple and contend,
And spill without mercy the blood
Of the oppressor who brought lamentation;
To strike and raise the conflict,
And deliver the poor people, once living free,
From this fevered, calamitous war,
And bring them safely away.
The young lion and tiger
Revealed himself a true champion;
He donned his coat no bullet could pierce,
Flicked his eyelids and arched his brows,
And the noble tiger showed no concern.
He did not esteem the monster’s son,
That swine, worth even his boot-heel;
He reckoned him less than a hair and never flinched,
Never stirred in apprehension.
Turning toward the qibla,
He lifted the enchanted spear inherited from his fathers
And aimed it toward the enemy.
With Zulfiqar fastened at his waist,
He invoked the spirits
Of his forefathers who had passed before.
When the gray-maned wolf rode forth,
Ancestral spirits settled upon him in majesty;
He towered like a lofty mountain,
Appearing like a blue-ridged peak,
And all who saw him felt their hearts stirred.
He girded on his fearsome steel
And prepared for the combat,
Calling upon the spirit
Of the magnanimous hero gone to the next world.
The descendant of the sovereign lion
Looked neither up nor down,
And counted the calamity of death
As no more than a hair.
When one gazed at him, the heart was stirred;
If six great rivers turned,
They seemed no more than a cleft in the earth;
The confluence of six streams
Seemed no more than a mountain pass.
When the noble lion grew wrathful,
He seemed able to engulf the whole world;
Even the vast, broad-breasted earth
Seemed unable to withstand
The prince’s valiant son.
Broad-chested and wide-breasted,
The doomed hero stood imposing,
Both a champion and a lion;
Such was he when setting out on that long road,
Like one who had suffered bitter torment,
Like a champion entering the field.
God Almighty in His power
Creates living souls in every kind.
As the youth sat mounted,
The memory stirred his heart:
“Urge your Akkula onward;
Now charge, my son!”
