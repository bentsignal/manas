And rode proudly on a gray pacer.
If you make trouble, my colt,
How will you depart,
Flapping in a kementei,
Swaggering on a bay mare?
Know that this would be a great shame.
How will you wander away,
Swaggering on a gray mare,
Flapping in a hide coat?
My child, heed these words of mine:
All the people blessed by Kydyr are here;
Dear old Bakay is here;
Sarykan of the bowl-shaped eyes,
The khan of sacred power, is here;
And Jamgyrchy, hero of the Esheks,
Is also here.”
When the senior wife said this,
The witless woman Chachykey
Now stood and seethed,
And spoke in an altogether different way
To the khans of the Esheks:
“Women who came as mediators,
Leave now, you wretches!
Dig your deep graves—
I shall boil your suffering!
You came here seeking support
From your sprawling elder Bakay.
I know all of you perfectly:
Your white-bearded old Bakay,
You three women, his playthings,
And your hairless bald man
Whom you sit worshiping as a saint.
I know what you are doing,
And the one you chose to bring,
Your witless, lowborn fellow
Called Jamgyrchy.
You old ones with swan-white beards—
May God strike all of you down!”
The poor woman did not know
That Bakay was listening nearby;
Beginning with Khan Bakay,
She cursed every one of them.
The three senior wives led the way
And fled outside in a rush,
Their necks stretching like geese,
Both cheeks turning red.
There was no cure to be found:
“She has insulted the khan,” they said,
And all pinched their own faces.
Old Bakay heard
Her vicious torrent of abuse,
And all that had been said.
Misery fell upon Bakay.
The three khans sat drooping,
Hunched like frozen camels,
Unable to die from shame,
Or enter the grave in death,
Unable to understand
What manner of woman this loud daughter-in-law was.
Since Bakay first became Bakay,
Since Bakay first mounted a horse,
Since he stretched himself and became a man,
Standing equal with the sons of heroes,
Since Bakay first mounted a horse
And became a pillar of the people,
He had never died when fate reached him;
Bakay was no woman,
Yet he had never seen such treatment from a man.
To witness this judgment-day affair,
To have his beard and head reviled—
It was the death of Khan Bakay.
Prince Bakay the tiger,
Who had mustered armies and struck the Kalmyks,
Your elder Prince Bakay,
Broke down and spoke in a low voice:
“In the time when our fortune remained safe,
When the tiger Manas lived,
