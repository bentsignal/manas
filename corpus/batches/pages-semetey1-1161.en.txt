Her heels have never bled.
Such a grievous day has come.
Is it good for a man
To keep taking clever women as wives?
Elder Koshoi, old man,
Hear my answer clearly.
When I slaughtered the Kyzylbash people,
Because the Kyzylbash people ran wild
And committed their outrages, I slaughtered them.
When I overran
The khan's citadel and treasury,
Plundered their abundant livestock,
And scattered it among the people,
Akylay, daughter of Khan Shooruk,
That rustling infidel woman,
Was the wife I seized.
I have had no child by her.
Yesterday, in the city of Bukhara,
Loading gold and jewels by the batman,
Saying, “If I pay a bride-price, she will bear a child,”
I became a bridegroom and scattered livestock.
The youngest of twelve daughters,
A servant beloved by God,
The beauty among dark-complexioned women,
The radiant one among Muslims,
Kanykei, daughter of Kara Khan,
Was the wife I chose and took,
Paying a bride-price and giving livestock.
I have had no child by her.
God creates every living soul.
Blessed elder, hear my words:
They used to say a man would not pass away without a son,
Yet I am passing away childless.
Elder Koshoi, I have a plea;
I have a sorrow I must tell.
If you accept the kandagai trousers
And give me a sure blessing,
For a hero weighed down with grief,
Is there any night like the Night of Power?
I go about in sorrow,
My saintly elder khan;
Will a day come when I see a child?”
Hero Koshoi knew
That Manas's place was in heaven.
Saying, “The Creator will give him a child,”
Your blessed elder Koshoi
Smiled beneath his mustache.
The kandagai bestowed with honor—
Koshoi the leopard put it on.
The finest among needlewomen,
The most capable among women,
The noble woman Kanykei
Had made the kandagai to fit;
Mighty Koshoi put it on.
When it reached his calves,
The kandagai would go no farther.
Your elder's buttocks, huge as a millstone,
Would not fit into the kandagai.
When he pulled it upward, it would not
Come over his backside.
However he pulled, it did not reach.
Your commanding elder Koshoi
Then grew angry:
“Let them stretch out the temple hair
Of the woman who made this,
Of an infidel woman such as this,
And place in her hands
A letter of divorce.
She did not make the seat wide enough;
She did not make the cuffs broad enough.
That woman who made it so tight,
That God-stricken woman,
An infidel woman such as this—
Let them slit her soles and rub in salt,
Slit the crown of her head and put worms in it,
Dress her in a coarse felt coat,
Mount her on a bay mare,
And drive her away without another word!
