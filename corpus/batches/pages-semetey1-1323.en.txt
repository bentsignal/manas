Oh, my darling foal!
Beijing is no easy place,
Kakan-Chin is no easy people;
It has fortified passes,
It has the hero Kalcha,
And everyone who attacks it
It can cast into ruin.
Swarming like black beetles,
Kakan-Chin has its people,
And everyone who attacks it,
By custom, it drinks their blood.
Great Beijing, with its Kakan,
Whoever went there never returned.
To that great and imposing people,
No man ever made the journey;
Whoever attacked did not survive.
It has the people of forty tribes;
No one who faced them prevailed,
No one who advanced survived.
Beijing is no trifling place;
The seething unbelievers
Are no people a man can conquer.
It has a firmly fortified road,
And the mighty Kalcha hero,
Who never retreats once he advances.
The road to Beijing has five divisions;
If you seek it, its road is a great hell.
Oh, my beloved only son,
I have followed the tradition,
I have asked the learned men!
When the Flood surged and overflowed,
And all the world
It covered and overwhelmed,
Light poured upon Beijing,
And those people survived.
Beijing, untouched by a holy man,
Do not say, “I shall take it myself.”
Do not rave so!
In the Prophet's time,
Against Beijing and its Kakan
A holy war was waged there.
Men were gathered into an army,
And the whole force was made abundant.
They waged holy war against the Kakan,
But a saint saw then
How strongly Beijing was fortified.
If told, many sorrows emerge:
At that time, to Beijing
A multitude covering the earth went.
An army covering the world went,
No small host, but a great one.
From among those people there,
From Beijing, whose women were beautiful,
They took wives, and in Beijing
The Turks remained, it seems.
In the city of Chinmachin,
The Turks truly remained, it seems.
When we consider it, the grief is deep:
So many people remained in Beijing.
Descendants of the old Kyrgyz,
If you ask, you can find them;
Even to this day there remains
A people called the “Salaalyk.”
They made those who fought them fall,
