Thirteen years have passed.
When you ponder this treacherous world,
So great have its horrors become.
Since the magnanimous lord departed,
The head has gone to ruin,
The feet have gone astray,
Fire has engulfed the royal camp,
The people have been carried off as spoil,
Aichürök has remained in mourning,
Külchoro has remained wounded.
Kanykei and Bakai met with disaster,
His right shoulder was gouged,
And Külchoro was taken into slavery.
Aichürök donned black,
Went to Kyyaz, and lived as a widow.
Brave Seytek, left within her womb,
Became a son to the Jediger.
Grieving and humiliated,
Aichürök endured affliction;
Both shoulders gouged,
Külchoro carried firewood.
He fed the bustard to the birds,
Blotted out his mountain-high name,
Made Seytek his dependent,
And had him called Kyyaz’s son.
Talas became a desolate road,
His mother Kanykei
Was left utterly helpless,
The daughters and brides became widows,
And Muzoochu became a black slave-woman.
The nobles fled and scattered,
His elder Bakai, the old man,
Was plundered by wrestlers.
May his equal be steeped in sorrow—
Such varied fates were created.
For whom was this humiliation?
Though one looks, he cannot be seen,
And no one knows where he is—
It was for the vanished lord,
For the lord had disappeared from sight.
And from that poor Black Giant’s
Two eyes the tears flowed,
Streaming down his sacred mane.
The sword had slipped from its sheath,
Many of his words rang true;
What he had seen of the bloodthirsty lion
Proved true, not false.
At the soothsayer’s black cave,
Where the path into the dark cavern was stained red,
The elder and younger kinsmen,
Külchoro and Semetey,
Met upon the open plain.
Külchoro wailed and wept,
Steeling himself like a bird of prey,
Pleading, “Elder brother!”
He seized the bridle
And turned the noble Buurul.
When he saw the gray-maned lion,
His heart was wrung;
The tears flowing down both cheeks
Fell over his breast.
The blood drained from his ruddy face,
And Külchoro lost his strength;
His heart was wrung,
And the tears flowing from both eyes
Fell over his breast.
His strength failed and he grew faint,
His joints went slack and his life ebbed;
His tiny soul became flame,
When his true elder brother appeared.
When he saw his true elder brother,
The poor man could not endure it;
His heart was pierced and torn apart.
The precious lion had come into his hands,
And this accursed, treacherous world
Seemed no broader than a handful.
Külchoro and the Black Giant
Clung to the hero and wept,
And the Argyn-Kyrgyz people wept
Because the gray-maned lion had vanished.
