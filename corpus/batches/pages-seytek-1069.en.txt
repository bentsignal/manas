No wicked word ever passed their lips.
Whoever saw them
Lost his senses and grew bewildered.
Every living soul
Felt as though its deepest thirst were quenched.
Their faces were smooth and flawless as candles;
In Bermet, daughter of the fairies,
Every imaginable beauty could be found.
Those coquettish, hapless beauties,
Their breasts like tightly knotted silk,
If a living soul gazed upon them,
Even the dead would burn with longing.
Their hair was like sable fur,
Such as a fortunate man would love.
If their hands happened to touch him,
A man’s heart would take wing like a bird,
And he would laugh and play without a care.
The unfortunate queen Bermet,
Whose wisdom surpassed most,
Turned toward the skilled women
And gave orders to the maidens.
The maidens did not stand idly by,
Nor did they disobey her command,
But delighted the hearts of all who saw them.
Upon plane trees towering beyond sight,
They formed equal groups of six
And had swings hung,
Until games and laughter thundered,
And the clamor split the sky.
In groups of six, each party
Walked side by side in rows;
Such was their splendid display,
A wondrous spectacle to behold.
To satisfy the hearts of all who saw,
Across a low blue-mottled hill
They spread the carpet of fortune.
Such was the carpet’s nature:
Rubies and precious jewels
Were strewn along its edges.
Yellow gold, like a reclining camel,
Was shaped into the figure of a tiger
And set in its center.
Countless costly treasures
Had been lavishly worked into it.
Anyone who gazed upon it
Was utterly robbed of his senses.
Carpets of that kind
Were fashioned in many designs.
That very carpet
Had been soaked in water for forty years;
Not even its patterned edge had faded.
If left damp for eighty days,
Its border did not rot.
Bearskins and tiger hides
Were spread as splendid mats.
Whoever stood gazing upon them
Feasted his eyes to his heart’s content.
The wealth of that serene beauty
Dazzled the eyes.
How many forms her riches took!
The treasures spread across the field
Looked like radiant paradise.
The wealth of Bermet, daughter of the fairies,
Was poured forth in abundance.
Like a prince beloved by the people,
She had spread out her riches
As though expecting honored guests.
From costly treasures
She had heaped up mounds.
To the unfortunate woman,
Wealth had been given without measure.
Eighty wives and eighty maidens
Played the game of Orompoy;
The clamor of their swinging
Rang out in a ceaseless roar.
Just then the hero Seytek,
That dear lion bold as a rooster,
The steadfast gray-maned hero,
Clasped his hands behind his back
And sighed as though weary,
