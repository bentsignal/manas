One hair of his mustache resembled
The handle of a greasy axe;
His great nose resembled a saber’s sheath.
The ill-fated Choro
Readied himself like a tiger,
And none who challenged him would survive.
He grasped his bow and prepared,
Appealing to the spirits
Of the gray wolves Almambet and Manas,
And drew the bow without tiring.
The vigor of the crackling warrior,
His swiftness, was beyond measure;
Choro shot at the mattocks…
Through all ninety mattocks in a row
The arrow passed without stopping;
After piercing the mattocks, the arrow
Plunged into the ground beyond them.
Seeing this, Külchoro
Quickly cried, “Baabedin!”
And swiftly vowed a moon-hoofed horse
In honor of his princely father,
Rejoicing so greatly that Choro cried:
“Long ago, when we were young boys,
When we were drunk and sated with liquor,
In the marshlands of Jylgynduu,
While the Kyrgyz lived in joy,
If bored, we would shoot antelope;
If not, in Talas
We would play at ease.
When we camped high in the passes
And ranged along the mountains,
With games by day and girls by night,
Such were those days.
We routed foes and took plunder,
Defeated hosts of countless myriads,
And won spoils from the lions we sought;
Such were those days,
When our fortune was whole
And my gray-wolf uncle lived.
He was a vigorous, fleet-footed hero,
Tall and broad-shouldered,
Both wrestler and lion.
When roused, that poor hero
Would subdue the world.
His figure was like a plane tree,
His charge swift as a falcon,
His courage like the wind of the world;
When he charged, he seemed to devour lives.
When Choro surged in wrath,
No mountain could withstand him;
He charged without loosening his ranks,
And none across the world could defeat him.
A wise servant descended from heroes,
A grappler surpassing the world,
He prepared to draw the bow,
Donned his coat of mail, gleaming,
His moonlike face shining,
And girded on his gray belt.
He gave a deep rumbling cry,
Took the steel bow in his hand,
And fixed his gaze upon the mattocks.
His back bent and hunched,
His eyes bulged and clouded;
Fat oozed from his thumb,
And from Choro’s forearm
Dark blood dripped.
He labored with fierce resolve,
Clenched his teeth and gathered his strength,
Until the bones of his spine crackled.
Driven mad by wounded pride,
“Will he die a wretched death?” they wondered;
The people cried out in tumult
And stood crowded round, watching.
“How will he shoot the mattocks?” they asked;
The people stood crowded round,
Marveling at Külchoro.
The poor man favored by the spirits
Revealed himself to be a lion;
His waist bent like a racing hound’s,
One hair of his mustache—
