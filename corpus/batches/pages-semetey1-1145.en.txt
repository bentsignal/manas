In the land of our enemy.
Had you not heard?
What he gathered remains in Kakan;
What he desires is the faith.
What he gathered remains in Beijing;
What he watches is the faith.
He has words of resentment;
Have you heard
Of his revenge for his mother's death?
If Manas is driven into a rage,
He has reason to clash with us.
Khan Zholoy of the Kalmyks
And Kongurbai, khan of the monks,
Grew wrathful and excited,
And spoke to Ezkara:
“If he does not agree to my demand,
If he does not give Maaniker,
I will gather an army from Beijing.
While my valiant soul lives,
I will not stand idly by.”
Brave Kongurbai said this,
Thudding in anger.
Dear Zholoy blustered,
Saying, “I will bring disaster upon his feast.”
Saying Manas the leopard had come,
The Muslim people rejoiced.
Had this Manas not come,
They were going to intensify the wounds,
Bring calamity upon
The Muslim people,
Begin their devastation,
And make a spectacle no man should see.
A spectacle of horror.
A saint supported by the spirits,
Your elder uncle Koshoy,
Turned to the matter
And spoke these words without cease:
“Bloodthirsty lion, Manas the leopard,
Do you see how grass grows on the slope?
The infidel people are many.
The grass on the upland is sparse;
The Muslim people are few.
The raging Zholoy and Kongurbai
May seize the plunder,
And bring devastation upon us.
Kökötöy's livestock was driven in crowds
From Tashkent.
Coming to hold the feast,
The renowned boy Bokmurun
Has become badly entangled in trouble.
Making merry to hold the feast,
Perhaps Bokmurun cast havoc
Upon his great wealth.
Ah, my colt Khan Manas,
There are the people of Kakanchyn,
And the wrathful Kalcha, the formidable one,
Whose custom is to drink the blood
Of anyone who attacks him.
Here are the people of Chynmachyn,
The bridle-haired Kalmyks,
Ready to contend with us.
I know the truth of Beijing:
Long ago, in the prophet Noah's time,
The flood passed here.
When the floodwaters surged and overflowed,
Their waves did not reach Beijing,
And six hundred households remained.
Receiving a blessing from a holy elder,
Because they survived,
They were called Esenkhan.
Of Kakanchyn's forty gates,
One gate was never closed
And opened toward Mecca.
Upon that gate
Were written the names
Of the white prophet Mustafa and Muhammad,
Lest a devil should strike
The mere handful of Muslims.
