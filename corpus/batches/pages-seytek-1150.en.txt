Though Akjoltoy was his chosen target,
He could not kill him and ride away;
Seytek struck him with the spear,
And when Seytek’s spear struck,
He fell backward from the saddle,
His feet slipped from the stirrups,
And both his legs dangled loose.
As though something now seized his skirts,
His two eyes rolled wildly.
His surging courage vanished,
His frenzy crumbled and was quelled;
He had collided with brave Seytek
And made ready to flee.
That roaring boar—
Where now had all his fury gone?
When champion meets champion,
Both men’s legs begin to twitch;
When one tongue answers another,
The hinges of their jaws begin to quiver.
Bilgich’s spirit nearly left him;
The falsehood of this accursed world
Was laid bare before him.
Bilgich despaired of life,
All strength deserted the warrior,
Blood poured from his side,
And grievous was his wound.
Unable to gain his desire,
His reins cut through together,
He had collided with a leopard of a hero,
Encountered a man of majesty,
And fallen upon a wrathful foe.
His horse was swift and huge,
The winged gray Kerik;
No wind could match him at a trot,
No beast could match him at a walk.
A message had been tied to his tail:
“He will catch whatever man he pursues”—
And so they named him Gray Kerik.
Gray Kerik, bred for battle,
Could halt atop a mountain;
For nine full years they had watched him,
Letting him graze untouched by tether.
They had raised him in darkness,
And Niyanker, whose eye was keen,
Had approved him as a master judge of horses.
Carrying Bilgich off,
He sped toward the Khagan.
Over ridge after ridge,
Through gaping valley lands,
Skirting Imerchik,
Across Kürküldök’s gray plain,
And farther down the road he roamed,
Fleeing all alone.
Seytek was a reckless, simple-hearted hero;
He knew nothing of this,
Nor did brave Seytek know
How many perils lay in Beijing.
There were countless excavated pits
And misery for any who encountered them;
That scorched boar
Had troops stationed in the trenches,
His venom lay in many places,
His infernal wrath was stored in ambush,
And the overthrown Bilgich
Still possessed all his cunning.
There were sentries and boundaries,
And warriors stationed at every frontier;
Words would fail if all were told—
That accursed one had many stratagems,
Every kind of calamity was there.
Knowing nothing of those dangers,
With skirts loose and breast exposed,
The gray-maned hero Seytek
Still bore the fault of innocence.
He pursued the cunning fugitive:
Black without and cold within,
He chased the sorcerer, saying,
“If I do not turn Kulunsur’s head,
If I do not pursue this maddened Bilgich
All the way to Beijing itself!”
