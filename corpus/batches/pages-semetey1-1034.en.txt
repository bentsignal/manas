He is a champion upheld by holy spirits!
His right shoulder is broad;
He is a hero who will shatter the Oirot!
At the end of his spine,
He lacks only the breadth of two fingers.
Were it not for that,
His courage would exceed your own
By the length of a spear!
Among all the sons born of humankind,
In what way is he lacking?
He is a hero who dons
Bright-red crimson garments;
If your eyes should turn askance,
He is a hero capable of taking
What stands here before you!
Surging and puffing in fury
At someone you have never met—
This scolding of your younger brother
Is a shameful deed.
For a boy born like this,
Let any bloodthirsty lord be a sacrifice!
Many of his deeds remain unfinished.
Among us too are many boys of twelve
Who cannot manage to walk outside
Or put on their clothes properly.
Even now those boys
Feel no shame before their mothers.
Do not grieve and rage,
Saying, 'I was left in the steppe.'
By joining the boy in your reproach,
Your words have struck us as well!"
After she had spoken in this way,
Semetey stood and said:
"Joined when we were no larger than lice,
United when we were the size of a thumb,
Betrothed when we were tiny as ticks,
When the black cliffs were daubed red,
When infidel and Muslim
Came spreading across the land,
They named us and joined us.
Ninety animals were slaughtered for the feast,
And the people gathered for the wedding.
Ah, my wife Aichurok,
Our marriage was ordained long ago!
Your beauty has long been tested before the people;
My sultan-father, Khan Manas,
Was the matchmaker who found you for me.
When I set out from broad Talas,
Driving fourteen well-fed horses,
I heard your fame from far away
And came expressly in search of you.
You are the dear woman blessed by Khizr!"
When he saw the maiden Churok,
He began speaking delightful words.
Your magnanimous lord's fierce desire
Nearly killed him outright.
When he saw Aichurok,
Lion-born Semetey
Lost all sense of time;
His two eyes became four.
He seized her roughly by the neck,
Wishing to take one kiss from her lips;
Semetey's two eyes became four.
Your noble and magnanimous lord
Immediately dismounted from his horse.
In his handsome mottled coat,
Striding briskly along the road,
He took Churok, the daughter of Akun,
By her slender forearm,
And placed his hand upon her breast.
His eyes fixed upon her like an argali's;
He drifted away in rapture.
Your gray-maned lord panted,
And, about to press himself upon Churok,
Nearly died where he stood.
Seeing her lord's condition,
The beautiful Aichurok below him
Darkened Allah's sun in the sky,
