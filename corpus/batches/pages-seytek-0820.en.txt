Your scattered Kyrgyz people,
Your nation’s defenders and possessions—
From the fury of painful calamity,
Deliver them and save them.
This is the secret we have told;
It is no lie, but our truth.
Sarybai has brought us suffering;
It is no lie, but our truth.
I have whetted my black tongue,
Told every secret to the end,
And poured out my song, your dear Choro.”
When Kuyaly heard this,
Her thirst for blood seized her.
“Father, my brother-in-law Külchoro,
My dwelling place is Jelpiniş,
The white tulpar is the steed I ride,
And Sarybai is the one I shall fight;
This is how the quarrel stands.
In those dark and distant days,
When your dear sister-in-law was still a maiden,
I understood what you would need.
To your heroic uncle
I became a bride pledged until Judgment Day,
And for a long time shared his life.
I knew of this deed
Of our vengeful enemy Sarybai.
That madman, the ogre’s son,
Has passed the tale down from teller to teller;
He asked for the giant beauty, daughter of Kaiypchal,
At the urging of a mischief-maker.
Winter vanished from its season;
Because of wretched me,
Those two madmen schemed
To cast the nation into turmoil
And carry me away,
Bringing conflict upon my people,
Pouring suffering upon my people,
Starting rebellion among my people,
And spreading turmoil through my land.
At the great funeral feast for my khan father,
He began the fighting at its outset,
Driving my people frantic
And many times disturbing my heart.
He ruined my funeral feast,
And left my head ringing.
He sent a matchmaker between us,
But God took that vile man.
He sent an envoy from his people,
But God took him from the earth.
They drew their bows like pouring rain
And fired arrows like hail;
When he unleashed his violent fury,
He stood and waged battle.
Destroying the people and slaughtering the nation,
They fought in bitter conflict;
There was a clamor of battle cries,
A mighty tumult of warfare.
My dear hair, long as meadow grass,
I wound and knotted atop my head,
And donned armor no bullet could pierce.
This infidel Sarybai—
I drove him headlong from Jelpiniş.
Never letting his tail touch the ground,
Never letting him walk upon the earth,
I raised a clamor that split the sky.
Unable to lodge for even two nights,
Unable to find room upon the earth,
I thrashed him like crushed grain.
I drove him out of Jelpiniş
And bound his hands without rope.
He fought in earnest, burning with rage,
Despairing of his fly-like life;
His world closed in around him,
And his valor became known.
Against this infidel Sarybai,
This is the arm that shall wrestle,
This is the foe that shall trade shots with him.”
Maiden Kuyaly said these words,
And spoke many wondrous things;
