We shall find a way to settle their account.
If they swagger as heroes,
Claiming to be lions greater than us all,
If by chance they glare and bluster,
We shall knock the petty fury from their brows.
If the enemy attacks first,
We shall find a way to settle their account.
If our journey opens a fortunate road,
If my tiger forefather supports me,
If Khidr stands as my guardian,
If the spirits of the champions
Who departed for the other world are with us now,
We shall mount our eager coursers;
If this is a grave and grievous challenge,
If it is an enemy come from China,
We shall drive them all the way to Kakan.
Whether they are foes or peaceful folk,
We shall learn when we meet face-to-face.”
He donned his battle garb,
His gray-wolf valor plain to see;
Unable to remain still in his fervor,
He rode out from the palace.
As he mounted and departed, Akbermet
Spread her palms and sobbed,
Crying helplessly,
Her thoughts scattered a thousand ways,
Her mind racing through the world
As many sorrows rose before her eyes.
The prudent, luckless woman
Brought forth a green-blue coat,
No mere camel-hair coat, but a mighty one:
“Many champions have lifted this;
If you wear it against the enemy, it brings great havoc.
I knew this in my heart,
And saying, ‘Let the champion wear it in battle,’
I had this broad coat made.
Its fastenings are of steel;
For seven full years I had it ceaselessly cut,
And had its rings twisted;
For ninety mighty smiths
I had it hammered ceaselessly
For nine months with mallets.
Cobblers stitched it
With awls made of steel;
At the mere sight of it,
A person’s eyes recoiled.
Lest any hole remain,
An old craftswoman with a brass thimble
Stitched it with such care.
When thrust at with a spear,
The point glanced noisily away without piercing a hair.
Its whole surface was covered in mail,
With lark-eye rings of steel
Set into every part.
It is armor no bullet can pierce,
No spearhead can penetrate,
A coat made for entering battle;
My lion Seytek, put it on,
And rout whichever side you charge.
If a dreadful, perilous road confronts you,
Drive your foe to the ends of the earth;
Remember your poor Perizada
As you go.
The one you will grapple with is a mortal enemy,
And your campaign is a grievous trial;
My leopard, return alive.
My father too was once a khan,
And my poor, valiant father
Possessed treasures of every kind.
The Achalbars sword left by my father—
Its edge never dulled when it struck stone,
And when it hewed through tethered camels,
Its blade never caught or turned.
Its hilt was perfected and fitted
By five smiths descended from fairies,
Who forged it for five or six years.
Unable to endure the labor of its bellows,
Many bulls were slaughtered;
Unable to supply enough charcoal,
A whole forest of green saplings was felled.
