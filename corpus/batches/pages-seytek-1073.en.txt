“What can I do for him?”—
So saying, he grew impassioned,
And spoke aloud to the empty world;
The noble warrior hastened toward his beloved,
The poor warrior rushed on anxiously.
Over the lofty Akjoltoy Pass,
And through valleys broad and deep,
Lion-hearted Seytek, the leopard,
The noble hero who took after his father,
Never turned Kulansur’s head aside,
Nor paused to yearn for his beloved.
He followed the length of Keng-Özön,
Watching the trail of Kulunsur.
Hero Seytek rode in front,
Driving fiercely across the hills,
Having covered the courser beneath him
With a fine silk horsecloth.
They took the well-trodden road,
Following the pale trail of Joltoy,
Who had departed on his journey,
And joyfully took the road,
Following the dusty tracks
Of worthy Joltoy, heir to the legacy.
They traced their way along the valley,
Having shod every one
Of the many steeds with steel.
Whenever the chance arose, they raced;
The many steeds flew like clouds,
Straining neck and neck.
In some places the coursers
Stretched their strides beyond all measure,
As though despairing of their borrowed lives,
As though about to plunge into an abyss.
Whenever they reached open ground,
They darted like flying birds,
And drawing straight toward the distant phantom,
They pursued and drew near.
Whenever they came to another stretch,
Their dust whirled like a whirlwind;
The coursers, now in their prime,
Whistled onward like arrows from a bow.
When their hooves struck stone,
Sparks scattered like millet.
Their qualities as thoroughbreds showed plainly,
And wherever their hooves struck,
The ground split like a riverbank.
The restless heroes raced,
Their stirrups clashing;
The coursers, now in full form,
Pulled so hard their riders’ arms grew numb.
Kulansur, leading before them,
Thrummed like a drawn bowstring,
Roaring onward like a flying cloud.
Now he appeared in sight,
And when the wretch vanished,
He dissolved into blue smoke.
His cavernous mouth gaped wide,
And dust sprayed from the earth;
Every living creature
Was overwhelmed by his thunder.
His stride opened wide as an argali’s,
And the grey wolf riding upon him
Opened his eyes as wide as bowls,
Saying, “What a matchless courser!”
His aching desire was soothed;
When his steed flew like a cloud,
The grey wolf’s spirit brightened.
His heart surged and he lost himself,
And through the hero’s mind
A thousand different thoughts passed:
“To ride forth and take vengeance on the foe—
That is the custom left by my forefathers.
Well done, Kulansur!
With sovereign God as my ally,
You are my wing descended from the fairies.
Though I travel a distant road for six months,
I shall fly like a shimmering mirage.
Whatever deed I pray to accomplish,
I shall attain my desire.
My iron-hoofed courser never tires
