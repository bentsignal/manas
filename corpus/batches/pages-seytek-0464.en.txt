You sought to chill my breath.
You sought to knock the wind from me,
You sought to break my back.
You would not listen to my words,
You made me, the gray-maned hero, waste away.
Now, sultan, hear my words!
In earnest I shall spill my blood,
In truth I shall sacrifice my life.
You knocked the swelling wind from me,
You truly chilled my breath.
If you remain silent without a word,
And persist in such stubbornness,
If I do not cleave your head with my sword,
If I do not die right before you,
May I die without another soul being born,
You turned my burning soul into an ember,
I shall quarrel with you once
And see what comes of it.
Let us bind on keen red spears,
And wage fiercer war than against the Chinese,
Let us bind on keen black spears,
And fight like mortal enemies,
Thrusting spears at one another,
Spilling blood like water,
And smashing our stout ribs
And vertebrae with cracking blows.
If I do not fight you and see,
If I let you go without dying,
May I die unworthy of the name Külchoro,
Let me meet the death I have awaited
Head-on before you,
You valiant, bloodthirsty tiger.
My wrath has risen and I stand rigid,
Let me grapple with you and see,
If my mortal doom comes today,
I shall meet it face-to-face.
The ultimate, utter catastrophe
I shall bring upon you, noble lord.
If I do not find a way to overcome you now,
If I do not find it by swinging my sharp blade,
Then I have been sold as a black slave.
I wandered gathering firewood,
Until the flesh of my soles was blistered,
My strength departed and I became blind,
My head grew crazed with grief.
Long did I live in servitude,
Wondering whence the tiger of the walled city
Could ever come.
Now I have seen you alive with my own eyes,
Though I needlessly let myself waste away.
You made me roam as a helpless wretch,
Without speaking a word or uttering a sound.
Why have you tormented me so much?
You drained away my strength,
Why did you set my soul aflame,
Why did you make my blood boil so often?
When I clung to you in hope,
Why did you crush my soul?
You ruined my blooming countenance,
Why did you provoke my rage?
For many days you made me endure
The bitter day I see today.
When I turned to you, you ignored my words,
Though we were kin who never turned away—
Could you not at least look me in the eye?
Alas, my uncle, sultan and lion!
May our father be your sacrifice,
O luckless hero dearer than a father,
Without a word you made me cry out,
Why did you make me lament so?
Our sheep shared the same fold,
Our food the same fellowship,
Whatever we found, we gathered together,
We shared death and shared the faith.
Our grazing herds and encampment were one,
Our lives and our common death were one.
When we entered battle, we shared its dust,
Our lives and destined death were one,
Though I pleaded and stared imploringly,
You stared in confusion like a mute.
