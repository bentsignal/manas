Your bloodthirsty lion Manas
Set right whoever attacked him.
The forty companions were beside him;
Beneath him was his tulpar.
There had been times
When he slaughtered the whole world.
His eyes blazing like fire,
The great black Muslim host—
When your lion elder grew angry,
He slaughtered them and left them.
“While the Chinese swarm and seethe,
The time has come to fight.
If doom comes, let us die;
Taking Akkula's prize
Away from the infidels,
Let us divide it completely!
Why stand looming in Talas
Before the Black Kalmaks?
Why fold our hands before infidels—
What inheritance would that win us?!
Let us drive the oppressive Black Kalmaks
Toward Beijing.
Let us drive the snarling Chinese
Into the khagan's realm.
Let us slaughter their leaders
And drink our fill of their blood.
The infidels have shown their greed;
Let us slaughter them.
Let us face and test them,
And if doom comes, die.
If we do not fight them to destruction,
These Chinese will grow insolent
And strain greedily toward the Muslims.
They will turn back upon us
And bring fierce upheaval.”
When Manas said this,
The great black people of Islam
Were souls of another temper toward wrathful
Hero Khan Kongurbai.
“If China takes the livestock, let it take it;
Let us not attack the infidels.
Let us submit to what they say.
Kökötöy's prize—
To the great Black Chinese,
Let us give it without quarrel.
Let us withdraw safely to our own lands,
And tell those who know
What venom
The Black Chinese and Manchus possess.
Without quarreling or raising conflict,
Lion Manas,
We gave the feast—let us return alive!
We have long known
How numerous the Black Kalmaks and Manchus are;
We thought, when these infidels grew angry,
They would multiply against us.
Why should we measure ourselves
Against wicked infidels?
Without matching them, Khan Manas,
Let us depart safely for our lands.
Your infidels are a grim people;
If by chance they come by tens of thousands,
They will plunder everything.
These infidels will not take only the prize;
They seem able to take
Every one of us completely.
Let us submit to the words
Of Chinese as numerous as ants.
Akkula's prize—
Noble Manas, if you heed us,
Let us give it as an offering.
Since we invited them with honor to the feast,
The shameless, foolish kinsmen
Have loaded troops upon camels
To fight your Chinese;
They loaded bullets upon Bil,
And from the Kangly people
Have taken countless tens of thousands of troops.
