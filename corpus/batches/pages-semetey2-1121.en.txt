It seems we are to die a shameful death.
Whimpering like young pups,
Are we to die miserably?
Raising an uproar all together,
At the hands of that gray-maned prince,
Are we to die dishonorably?
Did we come surging here merely
To scatter and leave this world,
Turning our faces toward Jeti-Suu,
Slaughtered by that peerless lion,
Only to flee away?
We Jediger came surging forth—
Are we not now firm as mountains and stone?
Assailing the panther,
Needlessly entangled in calamity,
Are we to perish in ruin?
With broad reins knotted in a loop,
“Ah, hero, spare our lives!” we shall say—
Are we to cast honor aside, place them
Around our necks, and die?
“Generous lord, spare our lives!”—
Are we to die begging?
Bearded men and elders
Grew anxious and raised an uproar;
All the Jediger and Kipchak wondered,
“How can we ever reach
The bounds of this bloodthirsty man blessed by Kydyr?
Who would dare attack
The lion Manas’s champion
And the people left by that lion?
Who could invade them?” they said.
“He is the son of the great lion;
He will strike me in battle,” they said.
The people clamored, the nation roared;
To recall it, there was great turmoil.
Some men drew close
And spoke among themselves,
While others leaned together
And whispered among themselves.
They returned in grief;
Wretched women and young children asked,
“What will happen now?”
And spoke their last wishes.
When the matter was discussed, great turmoil arose;
The Jediger entered the fray,
Planning with care and reckoning the task;
The Jediger plunged into the uproar.
Some stood stubbornly firm,
Crossing their steel-tipped spears;
Some, in their anguish,
Abandoned all care for life.
The people swarmed, the lake heaved;
Their host was vast beyond counting.
They drew their bows and rained down arrows;
Brave Kyyaz charged with them all.
Swinging his sword and thrusting his spear,
Brave Kyyaz plunged into the battle,
Roaring as he poured out his wrath,
Raising the battle cry, “Kyyaz!”
The two sides faced one another,
Firing muskets and drawing bows,
Fighting with utter disregard for life;
A terrible conflict broke out.
They stood locked in battle.
Then Külchoro appeared,
Choro the lion, upheld by the spirits,
Agile beyond imagining,
The fiercely clashing warrior Choro,
Choro, perfectly formed in every limb,
Who had slaughtered not only Kyrgyz
But Chinese armies too.
Then Külchoro
Made all his weapons ready
And buckled his sword at his side.
The Jediger were ten thousand; he was alone.
All the agony of cherished life
Külchoro endured;
He fought having cast life aside.
