When the two equal warrior choros
Had caused this calamity,
“You let the swan take it;
My poor elder, lay the blame on me!”
Thus spoke Kulchoro.
Then Kanchoro stood and said:
“It was a swan come from Mecca,
And it had five young;
It was a being of wondrous power.
Brave elder, lay the blame on me!”
“Grant us as a gift, elder, grant us!” they said.
The two bastards, fierce as flames,
Thought the prince would kill them.
Speaking pitiful, bitter words,
They cried, “Mercy, grant us our lives!”
And the two wretches kept pleading.
Brave Semetey then said:
“My cliff no goose can circle,
Kanchoro and Kulchoro,
My mountains standing watch,
My steel battle-axe in hand,
Whose sight fills my belly,
My two sheltering supports!
I have nine thousand horses in the mountains,
Yet no courser fit to ride the hunt.
Do not torment your elder;
I have no kin apart from you two.
You kicked together like young colts,
Can such a calamity befall us,
You who struggled for the same breast?
My fortunate choros, both of you, listen!
Many people have flown falcons,
And many mortals have tasted joy.
Our dream has broken apart.
Trace this grievous event
Through the lines of tradition;
Ask the learned men
About this affliction we have seen!
Tell the long-lived elders,
Tell those whose ears hear far.
They used to say that when a bird was flown at a swan,
If the falcon was a true killer,
It would take its swan;
If that falcon failed to take it,
The swan would escape into the sky
And the bird itself would remain.
The swan is gone, and so is the bird—
Is this a day of God’s punishment?
Was the falcon’s flight away
Some enchantment that blinded our eyes?
My two companion equals,
Because of what has happened,
Because of Akshunkar,
I shall scour the whole earth on horseback.
Of the eighteen thousand worlds,
I shall leave none unvisited.
From Kakan and Beijing,
From the Black Chinese and the Solon,
From the bearded Russians,
And from the muskets before our brows,
I shall seek news everywhere
Concerning Akshunkar.
I shall gird my waist firmly;
Because of Akshunkar,
I shall raise an uproar wherever I confront a foe.
I shall put on armor no bullet can pierce;
Because of this falcon,
I shall grapple with every mighty man.
Because of what has happened,
Because of Akshunkar,
I shall scatter as tribute
The countless gold coins left by Manas.
I shall gird my renowned waist;
Because of this falcon,
I shall lay hands upon whoever stands against me.”
Kulchoro and Kanchoro
Went to tell my tiger elder Bakay
That Akshunkar had flown away,
