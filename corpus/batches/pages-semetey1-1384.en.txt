It is no easy road to reach.
The capable youths did not heed my words;
What they have done is not right!
Bakburchun lies before you;
When we rode out to reconnoiter,
When the heroes took vengeance
By bringing slaughter upon the Chinese,
We turned back from our wings,
And lost the fourteen horses
That stood there like a dark mass.
How shall we go straight in and strike
Deep-rooted Beijing,
Now that we have lost our swift horses?
How shall we reach Beijing?!
When I spoke, you did not heed me;
You did not return to guard the horses,
And would not obey the words I spoke!”
Thus Khan Bakai
Reproached the warriors harshly.
Hero-sultan Bakai said this,
Uttering all manner of bitter words.
Turning back in sorrow,
Külchoro and Kanchoro
Came together from one direction,
Having lost the fourteen horses.
The companions came then,
Turning toward their prince.
The boys stood before
Bakai and the hero;
God had struck the boys,
For they had lost the fourteen horses.
Their hearts were torn,
And tears poured from their eyes:
“Our dear heroes,
We let them be raided from the Kyrmaly side,
And let the mighty swift tulpars
Be taken from that narrow place!
Here is the sovereign city;
If you would cut off our heads, here we are!
Here is the lovely upland;
Lion uncle Bakai,
If you would take my life, here I am!
Nothing at all appeared
Before these accursed eyes—
Did we drink too much water in the night
Until it rose into our eyes?!
Nothing whatsoever appeared
Before these benighted eyes—
Did we drink water in the darkness
Until God struck our sight?!
We turned back from the wings
And lost the fourteen horses!
Traveling on campaign, we ate no proper food,
But took our meal from black liquor;
We became drunk.
By failing to guard the horses on campaign,
We acted like foolish youths!
Traveling on campaign without tending the horses,
We became drunk!
Riding reconnaissance without tending the horses,
We have come to ruin!
Riding reconnaissance and sleeping too long,
We have proved worthless!
Gouge out our blinded eyes;
If your anger rises in fury,
Khans, slaughter us!”
The boys wept, their hearts torn;
In their distress,
Tears poured from their eyes.
They drew back their heads, sobbing;
The boys wailed as they wept.
Their hearts were torn,
Tears pouring from their eyes;
Grieving in sorrow,
The gray wolves could not endure it,
And their whole insides burned with fire.
