When I travelled far upon a journey,
I would shoot wild sheep for food,
And of the one I held in place of a father,
The generous leopard Manas,
I would say, ‘May he live in safety.’
My champion Manas, do not leave me!
Do not bring me to a hapless day,
Do not leave me plunged in misery!
The countless Chinese will swarm upon me,
Seize me and slaughter me,
Alas for this only child, this wretched head!
If my bones lie unburied,
Vultures will shit upon them!
They will surround me,
Alas for my precious life!
They will plunge a dagger in my heart;
The vengeful Chinese and Manchus
Will take their vengeance upon me!
If only he would turn Akkula’s head,
If, when the Chinese pressed upon me,
Manas would stand and watch!
Then let this only child die,
And let Manas see it then!
If only they would dig my dark grave wide,
And the great host of Muslims
Would bury my dead body!
If only he would turn Sarala’s head,
Golden-crested, with a belt like embers,
And, while this lone soul stood thus,
Almambet would stand and watch!”
Before he had finished speaking,
Azizkhan’s Almambet
Came galloping from the foothills,
The Chinese who came before him
He slew and slaughtered and destroyed;
And on the nearer side of him,
Ulak Khan’s Syrgak
Had come close now,
Right beside Bokmurun.
Ulak Khan’s Syrgak
Speared them wherever he reached,
Cutting seven thousand to pieces,
“Where is Kokotoy’s only son,
The warrior Bokmurun?” he cried.
While Syrgak was doing this,
Your leopard Alma arrived;
He swept him up from the dark earth,
Like a sparrowhawk taking a little bird,
He snatched him up and carried him away.
Showing himself a lion,
He bore the champion swiftly off
And mounted him on Maaniker.
Before they could set themselves to rights,
See the reeds, see the flood,
See the people surging over them!
See the embers glowing through the haze!
When they looked, they could make nothing out;
Daytime had become black night,
They could not tell which was which.
They had set the one-eyed men apart
And placed them before the whole host.
The dog-eating, louse-eating Chinese people
Had surged upon them all together.
When the Chinese came pouring in,
Almambet saw this
And despaired of his lone life.
When he caught sight of the army,
“Now I shall trade blows!” he said,
And valiant Syrgak rejoiced.
His quilted coat that bullets could not pierce,
Up from the level of his navel,
He hastily donned as he ran.
“The countless Chinese, that deep-rooted people,
Will overwhelm us!” he said,
And Chubak stood in anguish.
The renowned warrior Syrgak
Rejoiced still more than before.
“I shall raise the battle cry against these dog-eaters,
Drive them, slaughter them and heap them up!
Grant patience to this life entrusted to me;
I shall drive the Chinese before me
And kill them, leaving not a single one!
The Prophet is true, my faith is true!
I shall wage battle in earnest.
Alooke’s Kongurbay—
If that fierce man falls into my hands,
I shall split him open on the road,
Thus shall I take my vengeance!
If you will give me no comradeship,
If you will not come near, you Muslims,
Then go upon your way!
I shall fight the enemy alone,
And survive wherever death spares me!”
Saying this, the warrior Syrgak
Girded his waist firmly,
And against the Chinese surging in
He attacked without a pause.
Those he struck and speared
He flung aside like fur caps;
Those who came close
He cut to pieces with his sword.
Azizkhan’s Almambet
Also plunged into the battle,
Slaying, slaughtering and spearing.
Tall in stature, broad of shoulder,
Like a myriad troops against a fighting foe,
You must have heard of him—
Khan Baltay’s Chubak,
And Yraman’s son Yrchy,
With the forty companions at the front,
Now joined the crushing battle.
Once the Muslims lent their support,
From the Chinese and Manchus
They made red blood flow;
The three now wrought a slaughter.
With no chance to exchange shots,
The heroes fought at close quarters;
Untiring in their blows,
A merciless slaughter
The heroes were inflicting.
Not letting their coat-skirts touch the earth,
Not letting them break through among them,
Driving them back and hemming them in,
Shattering the many Chinese,
Keeping them from approaching the Muslims,
Warrior Syrgak stood barring their way,
