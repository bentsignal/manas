Rather than turn our necks in submission alive,
Of the Black Kalmyks and Manchus,
Rather than endure their oppression,
Let us die in slaughter!
The Kalmyks seem eager for it.
Let me enjoy the clash of battle!
If I have the strength, let me fight,
Let me throw myself back and wage war.
If Akbaltay has let himself be caught,
And especially if Kutubiy has died,
Let me bring a mighty slaughter.
If those two have been captured,
Fighting as far as Beijing,
Let me free and rescue them!
If the Black Manchus surge upon us,
Standing face to face,
Firing guns, drawing bows,
Facing them in a long battle,
Let me bring grievous suffering.
Against the Chinese who pour out fierce wrath,
Let me go and face them.
My adversary is Kakan-Chin;
How can I fail to fight!”
So saying, the leopard,
At whose sight attention was drawn,
Spoke many words to Jakyp:
“Alas, father, what are you saying?
You have no wisdom, no sound words;
You seem to have come to ruin.
Your honor has fled, your dignity gone;
You seem to have become worthless.
When the Chinese took the Kyrgyz,
Breaking and smashing your court,
When they took it as booty,
Destroying your girls and women,
When they brought devastation,
Then you grew afraid, wealthy Jakyp,
And seem to have become worthless.
Driven away from Ala-Too,
You moved away from Ala-Too.
Not remaining alive, Akbaltay,
You were extinguished that very moment!
I am pained by what you say.
Wealthy father, I have lost hope in you.
Does a warrior not die by the enemy?
Do those who remain not carry on living?
Against the infidels like black spiders,
Against the warrior at full strength,
I will charge and wage battle.
Without fleeing, fighting for six months,
My wretched people of forty households
I will free and rescue!
I will slaughter those who provoke us.
If mighty Allah supports me,
For the people who came wandering,
I will sacrifice myself utterly.
I will seize their finest men.
Fighting a splendid spear-duel on the plain,
I will devour their strongest.
Upon these Chinese eager for it,
To bring a costly slaughter,
The small, scattered, wandering people
To free and rescue,
Struck by a jezail shot,
To die if fate arrives,
Against the assembled Black Manchus
To keep waging war.
His eyes flashed like a hungry lion's.
Without looking up or down,
The hero Manas was bold.
Without sparing his life from death,
Without considering this way or that,
He was a wrestler, he was a lion,
Created in this world,
A hero born beyond others.
Hearing the word from Khan Jakyp,
That upon Akbaltay and Kutubiy
The Kalmyks over there had gathered,
Hearing that they had taken them,
Toruchaar beneath him,
He whipped from side to side,
Spurring this way and that.
Riding a horse no shot could reach,
Wearing a coat no spear could pierce,
If mighty Allah decreed,
To bring a fierce clash,
Without sparing his bare life,
Khan Baltay and Kutubiy
To free and rescue—
Such was your blue-maned one's intent.
To reach the Alman river,
For those two wretched souls,
Their comrade Manas would not stop,
Ready to sacrifice himself.
The more he was spent, the more he blazed—
Such was the deep-rooted lion Manas.
The more he died, the more he flourished—
Such was the bold hero Manas.
He had a blue mane upon his back.
No mortal could defeat him in a clash;
He was born an utter blood-drinker.
To his forty-household Kyrgyz people,
Roaring in fury,
Blood-drinking Manas called:
“You, Burut, were the ones humiliated.
From the swarming infidels,
‘Let him free and rescue them,’
‘Let him wreak havoc,’ it was said,
And I, Manas, was created.
My adversary is Kakan-Chin.
Move without great panic or haste.
Move without swimming the ridge's waters.
Saying, ‘God, protect the champion,’
Move without giving up hope in me!
Move away from Altay and go.
From the Black Kalmyks and Manchus,
Whether he is dead or alive,
Of the sultan you entrusted,
Learn certain news before you go!
If I should happen to die,
For you, my dear people,
Stop and camp in a secure place.
‘The champion Manas has died,’ say,
And set a strong guard.
Grieve, weep, and mourn before you go.
If I should happen to die,
Scatter your countless livestock and go.
Against the infidels like black spiders,
You have no means to fight.
My dear people, flee!
My dear people, for you
I will camp along the hills.
Fighting six months without wavering,
Then I will sacrifice myself!
Against the Chinese who use force,
I will go crashing into battle.
If the pig grows overbearing,
Without my wretched life dying or perishing,
