Stumbled along on their staffs;
The women walked in haste,
And the young men hurried,
All racing
To reach that feast.
Among the Jediger of Jeti-Özön,
No one wished to remain behind;
They all wanted to attend
The wedding of Kyyaz, who had taken a wife.
From Alty-Shaar and Margalang,
As far as Bukhara and Samarkand,
Above to Kokand and Andijan,
And before him to Margalang,
Kyyaz, warrior of the Jediger,
Sent greetings throughout the lands;
To peoples as far as his name was known,
He sent his greetings.
When they heard this news,
The people clamored in disbelief,
And the crowd buzzed at the strange affair.
All the judges grew stern,
And every khan bristled,
Speaking against warrior Kyyaz.
Many upright men,
Khans seated upon their thrones,
Those fit to govern peoples,
And mighty men who faced disaster,
Their hair standing on end,
Glared at warrior Kyyaz:
“Manas’s son, the great blood-shedding hero,
Possessed complete good fortune;
If you looked within that hero,
His heart was broader than the earth.
He never tormented a living soul,
Yet of all men ever born,
He never turned back in fear.
To say that from such a gray-maned hero
We have surrounded Talas
And seized his wife—
Look at the boastfulness
Of Kyyaz, son of Bagysh!
Look how he puffs himself up
And calls himself a giant!
Look how Kyyaz claims
To be master of the earth!
When he moves, his quarrel
Seems destined to reach all the peoples of the age,
As though when he stirs he could conquer
The whole world beyond the Kyrgyz.”
The people spoke in anger:
“Can one prosper by attacking
The people of Manas, father of the nation?
Can one slaughter the descendants
Of the true gray-maned hero?
He was renowned, a lion;
He was a noble, gray-maned champion,
A mountain pass that protected us
From the raging khakan.
He was the lion set over the people;
While the well-rooted descendants
Of the great-hearted hero stood watch,
What enemy could ever devour us?
He was a wrestler and a giant;
In every direction that blood-shedding hero
Was the champion who sheltered us.
Having lost the people’s mainstay,
Do we boast that we seized his livestock,
And congratulate ourselves
For taking his wife?
Look at swaggering Kyyaz—
Look how mad he has become!
Having killed the accomplished lion,
He has apparently become
Master of the lands and waters.
His white-blazed Jelerde was swift as wind,
And served as his mare.
