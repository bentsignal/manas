They butted like gray bulls,
And fought like wolves in kok-boru.
They gripped whatever opening appeared;
The wrestlers, finding an opening,
Mauled each other like hungry stallion-camels.
Along Jeti-Ozon,
The people bustled and raised a din.
The one thrown was left empty-handed;
The victor claimed the prize.
The spectacle grew immense.
Dust flew into the sky;
The great crowd streamed and bustled.
Now, to begin the celebration,
To perform Churok's marriage rite,
Those with turbans wound around their heads,
Those who prayed to the Almighty,
The khojas in white turbans,
The mullahs in mottled robes—
He gathered every khoja without exception.
As twilight came and the sun set,
As darkness fell and people grew still,
When the sky filled with glittering stars,
When every living creature
Had reached its time for sleep,
He had the marriage rite performed at once,
And Kiyaz took Churok.
Caught in a net she could not break by struggling,
Aichurok had fallen into the snare.
She had fallen into hands
In a way no sleeping person could dream.
Conceiving a plan no person could discover,
Kiyaz, hero of the Zhediger,
Held a feast for all the accomplished people.
They called it hero Kiyaz's design,
The wedding at which he would take Aichurok.
He slaughtered ninety mares for the feast;
Abundant wealth and countless livestock
He set out without measure.
He seated all the champions facing the feast,
And made a mountain of meat like Ala-Too,
A lake of broth like Ala-Kol.
He filled the feast with slaughtered stock
And plunged into the clamor.
A great spectacle beyond human knowing
Had plunged into uproar;
A vast commotion that would not subside
Had engulfed them.
A spectacle that could not be stilled
Had plunged into clamor.
The people who came to the feast—
Some called for a stallion and mounted a camel;
The shameless went on foot.
All the women streamed along,
The old and the young;
Even the drunkards came, none staying behind.
The long-eared gossips,
All the modest people,
The eager connoisseur and the handsome,
The meek man who knew no speeches,
The khoja wrapped in a turban,
The lettered mullah,
The wrestlers and spearmen—
Hearing Kiyaz would take Churok,
They all came, none remaining behind.
They came to this feast,
People of every kind.
Drinking kumis, they grew heated,
Their bones tingling;
Drinking spirits, they panted,
Straining like hungry birds.
Kiyaz, son of Bagysh,
Showed himself to be a hero.
Many people rejoiced at his feast;
Great were the games and spectacle,
When one looked upon such events.
What could poor Kiyaz do?
Without looking up or down,
Never thinking he might die,
