Is this not the true delight?
When we were in Talas,
Among all the Argyn and Kyrgyz,
This Choro who followed me
Resolved, "May I become a sacrifice
Before my elder brother;
May I pass on before him."
Such is exactly my resolve.
The ground of grappling is great turmoil;
The ground of clashing is great delight.
Do not grieve because I am young,
Blessed elder brother, leopard;
Do people not die while young?
Do not old and young alike meet
The decree laid down by the Creator?
My dear khan and elder brother,
Against Toltoy who has come to our border,
To give him his measured answer,
To die locked in combat,
I am the amulet hung upon the neck
Of the piebald herd's tulpar.
While still young, I long
To die once at Toltoy's hands.
If we shout and level spears
Against Toltoy of the Zhediger,
And fight him man to man,
If the spear strikes with a shriek,
If blood spills with a rush,
If Syrnayza, as we joust,
Splinters through the middle with a crack—
Would that not be splendid?
If you give me Akolpok, I shall wear it;
If you give me Arbuudan, I shall ride it.
I have my Buurul steed beneath me;
I have the strength to shatter
Not merely Toltoy, but Kaldang.
Listen to my words, mighty elder brother:
If I spear no man at twelve,
Will I, at thirty-five,
Catch even a tethered cow?"
Then your gray-maned lion, son of Manas,
As the boy stood pleading,
Saw God's sun cloud over in the sky,
And sixty worries came upon him:
"You mean to bring him face to face,
My dear Almighty,
With an inescapable sorrow;
What can I do, when you have made
A twelve-year-old boy gird his waist?
Külchoro, for a boy like you,
I would sacrifice my own dark life.
You are only a boy of twelve,
A soul whose time is still young.
If I grieve in sorrow,
What will our days become
If I lose you,
My companion, my Külchoro?
My ribs will be torn apart,
And tears will pour from my eyes;
Because you are your father's only son,
My heart is cruelly divided.
I have no mountain pass lying secure,
No place where I may gird my waist;
If hardship presses this toil-worn soul,
My renowned lion Bakay,
Who finds firm wisdom, is not here.
I came here among the Afghans;
None of the renowned Kyrgyz are here.
My elder Sarykhan, who would be
A binding support around my waist, is absent too.
There is no forest into which I can flee,
No stepmother on whom I can rely,
No cliff where even a goose cannot circle,
No rugged black mountain of mine.
I have no thoroughbred to ride out;
Sovereign God, it is only you,
And I have no kin except the boy.
