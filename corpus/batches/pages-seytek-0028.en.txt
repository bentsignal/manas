Her many thoughts unraveled,
Her bewildered mind broke apart;
The dark earth, sealed below,
Seemed ready to take her
If her head survived this ordeal.
From the distraught poor woman’s
Quail-like eyes,
Tears fell one after another;
From her currant-dark eyes,
As the wretched woman trembled,
Tears fell a whip’s length.
Unable to gather and steady her thoughts,
She wept in anguish from her sorrow
And could not even draw back the mucus from her nose.
As the distraught woman lamented,
Her joints loosened and came apart;
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Appeared before her eyes
Like a ravenous monster.
The golden doorway and gate
Had panels of solid tin.
As for the approach to Khan Kyyaz,
The looming figure’s
Appearance was like this:
Within the white palace,
Beside the dark spring,
Right by the plane tree,
Surrounded by a fortress of lions—
Who among humankind had ever possessed such things?
Your radiant Chürök,
Your formidable wonder,
Your moon-beautiful wife,
Your infidel rich in wisdom,
Had grown old among the people, keeping the nation,
And become renowned.
Without shame she had Mother Umai Zuura
Set her womb aright,
And, letting no one suspect,
The distraught woman labored for seven days,
Spending her frail, fly-like life.
This woman earnestly begged
The true God for a child.
After seven days had passed,
When Chürök’s true labor
Had lasted seven days,
Her blood was spent and her strength was gone,
Her power failed and her breath grew faint.
Wailing and wasting away for the child,
She was left utterly helpless.
Then Aichürök
Raised her voice in a scream;
The lifeless trees wept,
All the Creator’s works wept,
Every living creature wept—
Chürök’s ordeal had begun.
Sixty stars and seven wild sheep,
Arrayed high overhead,
The moon and stars sharing her travail,
Labored together above her.
Gathering all around,
The shamans of every tribe
Joined her travail in a great throng.
Then Aichürök
Labored without pause for eight days;
Only a spoonful of blood remained.
She lost consciousness, unable even to groan,
And her flesh became a bruised, blue pulp.
She had no voice left even to moan,
And no strength remained
To hide her state from Kyyaz of the Jediger.
Agonized by true labor,
The poor woman could endure no more
And despaired of her frail, fly-like life.
When nine days had passed,
There was no way to restrain herself;
None of her own gathered people were there.
When the bright dawn advanced
And its first light glimmered,
When daylight touched the earth,
Stars poured down from the sky.
