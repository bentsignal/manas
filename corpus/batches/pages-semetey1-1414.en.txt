Beating the drum and raising a cry,
Your capable uncle Bakai
Came riding here and there;
Kökcholok came snorting,
As Bakai fired his gun and drew his bow.
Your uncle Bakai, bringer of good fortune,
Urged it on without cease.
At that moment Kuuördök
Flew up from the lake;
When it had risen,
Your young son of the gray wolf
Cast Akshumkar at it.
Striking a rock and falling limp,
Akshumkar lay unconscious
For the time it takes tea to boil.
Bakai shouted out:
"Wretched child, be ruined!
You never heed my words;
You are always ill-fated,
Bent on gouging out my eyes!
No matter what I say, you will not listen;
Arrogant son, you will come to no good!"
Tearing out and flinging away his beard,
Tugging and flinging his mustache,
Staggering up and down,
Your uncle Bakai wailed
And beat himself upon the head:
"Alas, Manas—if only you had not died!
Then this wretched soul would not see such disgrace!
Curse you, Manas—if only you had not died,
Then Bakai would not see such suffering!
I have withered like grass in a dry streambed;
When Manas passed away
No one heeds my words,
And I am like a fire gone out!
Curse you, Manas, for dying
And leaving your dependent
To a wretch like this!" he cried.
As Bakai stood thus,
Akshumkar recovered its senses,
Shook itself and voided its bowels;
The gray wolf's falcon
Rose and flew anew,
It circled in the air,
Flying backward and banking;
It climbed and righted itself,
As though it had lost sight of the quarry;
With twelve tail-feathers and forty pinions,
It now beat its wings mightily,
Drawing itself tight with a hiss,
Its roar breaking forth.
At times,
It looked like a gray sparrow;
Fluttering its wings,
Quivering faster than an arrow,
Its wingbeats could not be seen;
It began the tumult,
Risking its very life.
Toward the fortress with iron gates,
Toward old Khan's palace,
Ringed by plane trees,
With a golden throne at its center,
Beneath the golden throne,
A bottomless black well;
The gray-winged duck had nearly arrived
And was about to descend.
Just as it was about to alight
Before mighty elder Kary Khan,
Spread the news in every direction,
And reach the bottom,
The gray wolf's Akshumkar
Folded over with a hiss;
Reaching before the arrow,
It seized the duck where it caught it,
Without having his khan informed,
The leopard's Akshumkar
Struck and carried it away.
