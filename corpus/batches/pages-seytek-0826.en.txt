Level your spear and thrust it home,
Drive your spear in till it drips with blood,
Pierce both his eyes!
That swaggering brute,
That thick-armed would-be wrestler,
Has raised this great commotion from the start.
Rip open his belly like a donkey’s!
Choose your opening and thrust without wavering,
Drive it home before he can flinch,
Twist the spear in openly,
Watch him closely and strike!
Tear the flesh from the shins
Of that raging swine.
Keep clear of the crush and thrust,
Face him without fear and thrust!
Let dust rise where your spear strikes,
Let blood gush where your fist falls,
Let life flow mingled with the blood!
Let the true spear reach its mark,
And let not a scrap of the swine remain—
Let them all be ground to dust!
Thrust with unblinking eyes,
Gather all your wisdom and strength,
And at that gaudy swine
Strike swiftly till he topples.
Aim carefully and thrust,
Aim for the place that brings death.
Choose his flank and thrust;
Strike surely, not as he does.
Let me see the grievous wrath
Of that swaggering swine.
You have fought many times
Against that strong-armed swine;
My heroic son Külchoro,
Let me stand as your support.
Do not look around you;
This world is vain and deceitful—
Spare not your life at all!
Do not consider what is precious;
Spare not even your threadlike life!
Charge, my son, charge!” he cried,
“Give that fearsome swine
No chance to flee—finish him!
No man escapes his destined death,
No one turns back from dying.
Unleash a fierce battle,
Until not a hair’s breadth remains whole,
And slaughter one another.
Such is the harshness of battle.
Charge, my son, charge!” he cried,
“Seize that swine
Whom the whole world could not overcome!
Beat out his fury in earnest
And settle the final reckoning!
Grapple and wrench each other,
And test yourselves in combat.
Single out the forces
In which the swine places his trust;
Learn the measure of their courage,
And slice their bellies like fat.
Turn the river from its course,
And until you die in battle,
Slaughter the swine who came himself
As though fire had swept over him.
Smash him into dust,
Bathe the sword in blood,
And strike down all around him.
Beat out completely the rage
Of the wrathful boar—
Such is the nature of battle.
Let me see his prowess;
I came seeking you,
To grapple with him and spill his blood.”
His voice was heard, though he was unseen.
When he heard this news,
Choro, son of Almambet, said,
“That is my father’s voice,”
His heart filled with burning grief.
In the blink of an eye,
This wretched world passes away.
