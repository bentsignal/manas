Morning and evening, twice a day,
Stretch him out and beat him like a hide.
Deliver punishment with your own hands
Upon this slave who calls himself a hero!
Do not kill him quickly;
Kill him through humiliation
And give him his punishment!
Your valiant Khan Kyyaz,
Surge in and enter;
Take the monstrous warrior
As your spoil!”
Against the warrior in the cave,
The lion favored by fate,
The hero lying helpless
With ninety wounds,
The Jediger closed in from every side,
Loosing their bows like rain
And shooting arrows like hail.
From every direction
Came shouting men with leveled spears.
All those shouting
Raised the battle cry “Kyyaz!”
Their bows rained shafts,
Their arrows fell like hail,
And the Jediger swept inside.
Then Külchoro,
His strength spent and power gone,
His whole body drained of blood,
Was burning inside and out,
His entrails aflame with fire.
He had the soul of a champion in battle,
But the wretched warrior was helpless.
His eyes alone stared pleadingly,
His joints had failed and hung limp;
His vigor was spent, his strength gone,
His face pale from loss of blood.
All power had left Külchoro.
As the champions surged upon him
And encircled him,
About to plunge him into bitter misery,
He grasped Joykuma, his khan-father’s sword,
By the hilt,
Drew it from its sheath,
Swung it in his grip,
And hewed fifty champions
Clean apart.
Those farther away
He made crawl beneath his spear,
And those who came nearer
He toyed with upon his sword.
The famed lionhearted hero
Whirled about in fury,
Hurling every champion he seized
Like a blue-gray kid.
He crushed all who faced him—
Where now were their brave men?
He smashed all who approached—
Where now were their tigers?
Warrior Külchoro kept them at bay,
Refusing to submit
To the Jediger’s violence.
Külchoro would not surrender
His fly-like soul to that host.
Hero Kyyaz of the Jediger
Came thrusting with his spear,
Intent on binding Külchoro;
Firing his gun and loosing his bow,
He drove resolutely in.
Wearing a coat like a mountain’s ash,
Riding his swift bay stallion,
He tied up short the streaming tail
Of his restless sorrel horse.
Burning with eagerness, hero Kyyaz
Charged against the warrior.
When you looked upon Külchoro,
The breath from his mouth
Was like the wind through a mountain valley,
As though it would devour whoever faced him.
