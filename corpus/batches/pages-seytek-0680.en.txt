“Upon the beauty who cannot be won by asking,
I shall cast my cruel lasso.
After battling you, I shall drag your beauty
From your very embrace.
I am a cool pavilion on the heights;
I am the warrior Sarybai.
Seven giants beneath the earth
I devoured single-handed.
The mighty giants upon the earth
I drove before me and swallowed.
Defiant khans
I crushed beneath my heel,
And scattered their blood like water.
Still unsated with blood,
I am the warrior Sarybai.
Finding no mountain to strike,
Finding no enemy to fight,
Finding no stone for my teeth,
Finding no head to tear away,
I am a bloodthirsty butcher never sated with blood;
Every worthy rival who faced me died,
And I am one of the measureless heroes.
Though I gulp and drink blood,
My thirst remains unquenched.
I am fury itself,
The black eye of the dread destroyer.”
So saying, Sarybai
Struck Jooruker once upon the flank,
Took the blue spear in his hand,
And roared in fury;
The wretch stood against him.
When Sarybai leveled his spear,
His menace split heaven and earth;
When he stood there enraged,
Could the hero-born Külchoro
Draw back even from stone,
Or turn away from death?
He rejoiced at the word “death,”
Revealing himself a lion.
The lionlike, vigilant man
Swooped straight upon him.
Sarybai, son of a monster,
Charged like a boar.
He sprang like a tiger;
Those who clashed with him did not survive.
He arranged all his weapons,
And the cast-iron head, broad as a room,
Of this infidel was made ready.
He had brows like a crouching hound,
And a head as large as a cauldron;
His two cheeks hung loose,
As though two Dungans had eaten their fill.
Upon the infidel’s two shoulders,
It seemed two great birds had perched.
He roared in fury,
Calling all the khans
To come before him:
“Do not come alone—come in pairs!
Do not come one by one—come all together!
I shall strip away your strength
And spill your blood like a spring!
Now I shall take your life,
And make your blood flow like water.
Come with your khan Semetey at your head!
Let no regret remain within you;
Come, all of you, and harbor no regrets.
Let the warrior Seytek come as well,
Let Argyn and Kyrgyz come together.
I shall erase every trace of you,
And smash to splinters
Your villages set in a circle.
I shall seize as my true prize
Your wife, the lovely Aichürök.
Your heroes and your khans—
I shall slaughter you all.
Without quarrel or strife,
Submit to what I say.
Enchanting beauty Aichürök,
If you do not obey my words,
If you do not surrender Aichürök,
