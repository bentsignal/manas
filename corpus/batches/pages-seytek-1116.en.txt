In the valley of broad Talas,
Are a hundred men looming like mountains,
Strange in appearance and mighty in wrath.
They are not of our Kyrgyz people;
Those black-hearted curs,
Their conduct is far from ordinary.
They are not of our people’s stock;
Their horses have mattock-like hooves and broad rumps,
Chargers bred upon spacious lands,
Not horses from our country.
Those black-hearted curs,
Their conduct is far from ordinary;
They are not of our people’s stock.
Their horses have mattock-like hooves and broad rumps,
Chargers bred upon spacious lands;
They are clearly of another breed,
Not horses from our country.
Those are no ordinary tails and manes,
No livestock of the white-capped people.
From the ridge I saw five champions;
Watching them, I despaired of life.
A curse upon my frailty—
Unable to approach them face-to-face,
I ran toward the champion.
Grief descended upon my soul,
And I departed despairing of life.
Looking closely from every side,
They seem a force that brings devastation;
If a Kyrgyz falls into their hands,
They seem a people who never yield.
The smoke of my home guides my road,
And when I set out, it does not leave me;
If a Kyrgyz falls into their hands,
Those snarling dogs
Seem fools who will never look back.
The horses on the mountain stand in herds,
Never scattering when challenged.
When I drew near to them,
They showed no sign of sparing life.
Their appearance is fearsome and powerful;
Their cunning is formidable.
Watching them from the ridge,
I saw their spears like pine trees.
Almighty God of power,
Brave Seytek, our protector,
If you can devise no means,
The Kyrgyz seem to have no hope left.
If they are merchants or peaceful folk,
Send word to their people;
If they are sorrow-bringing enemies,
Do not let them grieve your Kyrgyz people;
If they are a mortal enemy newly come,
Hero, drive them away.”
Hearing the herdsman’s words,
Hearing that a baleful foe had come,
Behold the lion-hearted courage
Inherited from his fathers and forefathers!
Unconcerned, his armor ringing,
Even if the Flood itself came surging,
Brave Seytek, heir of his fathers,
Would not count it equal to his boot-top;
His strength was beyond compare,
And your hero stood utterly unconcerned.
Grinning broadly, he began to speak
And summoned brave Akjoltoy:
“My stalwart uncle Akjoltoy,
Your strong back is as high as countless troops.
If death arrives and one’s days are done,
Would not even one who attacks me die?
The true land of no return—
Does not everyone journey there and see it?
When a hero desires to ride,
What night is like the sacred Night of Power?
When will the archers
Of the black Chinese and Manchus come?
We were a people who stood shoulder to shoulder;
If the Chinese can be brought to reason,
Without abuse or quarreling,
We shall send them peacefully on their way.
