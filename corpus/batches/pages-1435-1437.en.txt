Before Almambet
He rode up and stood.
“The dear child is safe, the people safe,
All the people you remember are safe.
With Kanykei at their head, the people are safe.
When I went to Talas with news,
All the people you know were safe.
Your many people, taking thought,
Were prospering there.
Kumys by day, girls by night—
That was how things were.
Caps faced with marten fur
Were swaying to and fro.
When bored, they shot roe deer;
When not bored, they lay at ease.
They were enjoying life.
Why should I tell of all the rest?
Keng-Kol, Talas, the plains are safe.
When we set out this way,
The newly born child was safe.
Aruuke, the wife you married,
She too is safe at this time
And sends you many special greetings!
The bridle you kept for your horse—
Inside Aruuke,
It is about to be born,
Your unborn child left in her womb!
‘Let him come to Beijing without delay,’ they say,
‘Let him see this sorrowing soul,’ they say.”
When he heard such words,
With golden forelock and tasselled belt,
The only son of Azizkhan
Had his heart pulled many ways.
Rejoicing beyond measure,
Stretching himself like a lion,
Restless in his sorrow,
The precious warrior stood looking on.
“That precious bird, the wild duck,
Flies over the plain without returning,” they say.
Your precious Almam rejoices
For the child left in the womb.
“That wretched bird, the kite,
Swoops over the plain,” they say.
Your Almam rejoices in delight
For the child within the womb!
Sarala beneath him,
Ceaselessly turning sideways,
Came bounding forth.
Joy entered his heart;
In the wake of joy,
Your Alake, for the sake of one child,
That exceptional wolf-man lion,
Came forth weeping!
The reason for his tears was this:
Nine thousand horses in the mountains,
Among them warhorses and noble steeds.
A herd of swift horses, with no minds;
Almambet could not contain himself:
To tend and take them over,
He had no male child behind him.
His noble steed, a blaze-faced charger—
When death comes and his days end,
Where might this wretched head not lie?
The chargers he rode were all well fed;
What made your Alake grieve
Was having no male child behind him.
His wife, Aruuke, Agynai’s daughter,
Since she had sent a certain gift,
Since he had received and seen her gift,
Sparing not his troubled life,
He vowed sheep to Khoroson,
He vowed a horse to Babedin:
“Even if I die, I have no regret;
Not a speck of regret remains in me!
I may give myself to Heaven.
Now that such news has reached me,
How could I remain downcast?
Sarala, the charger among horses,
I shall slaughter as a sacrifice.
The sorrow within my heart—
Thus shall I clear it away!”
Almambet came rejoicing,
That outstanding charger Sarala
To slaughter as a sacrifice.
Then Khan Manas
Among many different thoughts
Was dividing his attention.
From atop his golden throne,
He took up his spyglass,
Turned its ring,
Set it firmly to his eye,
And Almambet approaching
He saw there before him!
When he saw Almambet,
The precious lion who had sat unmoving,
Lest he suddenly slaughter Sarala,
That lion warrior took fright.
Lest Almambet in his joy
Truly bring disaster upon them,
Lest that noble steed Sarala,
In his joy, Almambet
Slaughter as a sacrifice,
He thought and found a plan.
Argynkhan’s Ajybai,
With Kalkaman’s Black Horse alongside,
He sent galloping to meet him.
Kalkaman’s Black Horse—
Whenever a companion took offense,
It was the custom to offer him that horse.
As Sarala was about to be slaughtered,
It went as a gift, did you see,
That Black Horse I spoke of.
A gleaming scimitar in his right hand,
Preventing Sarala’s slaughter,
Ajybai stood holding him back.
“Do not slaughter Sarala!”
Ajybai kept imploring:
“Warrior Almam, heed my words.
The days that God has brought upon us
We have no choice but to endure.
Consider it, Almambet:
You lived childless for many years.
If you slaughter Sarala,
We shall camp along the river;
Amid an enemy blazing like fire,
We shall be like a fire put out!
