They remained beside lion-hearted Seytek,
Serving as his comrades.
Much of this is false and much is true;
No one has returned from there after seeing it.
Half is invention and half is truth;
Unless the Creator himself knows,
No one has come back from beyond.
How could we leave untold
The tale of your forefathers?
The tale has turned into a sorrowful song;
Let us speak again of the ordeal
Of that vanished warrior,
Brave infidel Sarybai.
His thrusting spear must have pierced,
His reach extended across the world;
He crushed every man who grappled with him,
His strength swelled and overflowed,
And in his fury he overran
The whole wide world.
Almambet’s son Külchoro,
In the battle of the day before,
Had fought for seven days
And taken wounds in seven places;
With scarcely a spark of life,
His lungs pounding painfully,
He bore anguish too great for his breast
And wounds across much of his body.
A spear had lodged in his side;
When he seized and wrenched its head,
A blue flame flared.
He had raced upon Kulansur,
And before fair Saikal
Had vied steed against steed.
The great army was caught in the flames,
And spirit-favored Külchoro
Barely escaped death.
Because the mighty Kyrgyz host was there,
Drawing near in a darkened mass, he was saved.
Before this, Sarybai’s
Dread power had become clear,
And the people had despaired of life.
But then Manas appeared,
Dazzling every heart,
Looking vast as blue Song-Köl,
His majesty striking terror into souls,
Making the Kula horse beneath him
Step proudly and high.
He dazzled every heart:
The lion whose patron spirit lived,
The youngest of the six heroes,
A servant of almighty God.
He dazzled every heart,
Appearing before their eyes as though alive.
The wandering saint Khydyr
Came straight forward to lend aid,
Leading the proud-stepping Kula horse,
With twelve noble warriors in his train.
He dazzled every heart,
Appearing like a lofty blue mountain pass.
Such was the man’s form.
He seemed to fill the sky,
Appearing as though alive,
Like an alloy
Of gold and silver,
Like the moon and sun
United in a single being.
Look closely upon this marvel:
He seemed able to pass through the spindle-narrow cleft
Of a gaping gorge.
His vast majesty rose like a glacier;
Not merely Sarybai, but the whole world
He seemed able to conquer if it opposed him.
The horse he rode was Akkula,
Akkula, the racehorse swift as an arrow.
Manas loomed enormous as a hill,
Broad and towering as a blue mountain spur.
The living spirit of your forefather
Stood grandly as his companion,
Taking his place at his right hand.
His destiny was to wreak destruction—
