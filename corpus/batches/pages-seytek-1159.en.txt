Scion of an ancient land,
Let us begin the tidings from this side.
From the hero Semetey
Was born the hero Seytek.
He reached Talas safely,
Recovered the scattered herds,
Wrestled with his foes and took the khanate,
Then returned once more to Talas.
He became a man of weight,
A majestic khan,
Making the broad-backed Ala-Too
A settled and ordered homeland.
The ancestral realm left by his father
He tended wholly and made into a nation.
He turned every weakling into a man of substance,
And gave the homeless a homeland;
He made all his people skilled.
When weary, he had gazelles hunted,
And let the people live and play in ease.
While Seytek watched over them,
The people’s hearts were at peace,
And they became immensely wealthy.
He achieved all that he intended,
And filled every chest
With yellow gold and treasure.
They dressed in fox and sable,
And lived as though in the blessed Night of Power.
His people, countless in number, grew rich,
And the nation’s heart was at peace.
As they sat governing the people,
The leopards Seytek and Akjoltoy
Together served as ruling khans.
Such was the state of their people:
No outsider dared travel among them,
And none who attacked remained alive.
They stood like the barrier of Mount Kap,
Lions guarding their nation;
Thus did those noble men live.
Then, when the people began moving uphill,
At the season when horses’ coats turn dark,
When the grass had reached full growth,
The people headed for the summer pastures
And settled on the lower slopes;
The nation spread out and made camp.
The noble lady Bermet,
The renowned wife of Seytek,
Craved tiger meat in her pregnancy.
Her craving drove her to agitation,
For a wondrous child was within her,
And she could scarcely bear its force.
Learning of this, Akjoltoy
Struck down a bear far away.
For six days and then seven,
He was gone upon the hunt.
Yearning as he ranged the wilds,
He tirelessly rode for many days.
The khan’s courage waned as he searched the land,
Stirring turmoil in many places.
No tiger crossed his path,
And the quarry he sought could not be found.
Bewildered, he continued hunting,
Riding his horse until its flanks grew lean,
And roaming lands six fathoms wide.
He saw cliffs beyond the reach of sight,
And mountains like blue crags,
Driving argali and chasing wild asses;
Though he ranged far, he found nothing,
And Akjoltoy was sorely troubled.
Because of what had happened,
For the sake of unfortunate Bermet,
For the sake of that renowned lady,
He grieved at failing to find his quarry.
Wearied from his wandering,
Toward a jagged mountain spur,
To a pine-covered rocky headland
Whose mystery the mind could not fathom,
He turned his mount Kulunsur,
And Joltoy rode onward.
Over Kara-Kyya and Kapka-Tash,
The sun now set in darkness.
