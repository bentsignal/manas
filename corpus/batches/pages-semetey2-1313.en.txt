That serving woman has seen it all while roaming;
She is the very soul of a man.
This great woman Aychürök
Is the khan of womankind.
Gather the settled peoples
Of Jeti-Özön, filled with Jediger;
There is no sieve over the people’s mouths—
Restrain your gossiping nation.
Let no rude-tongued troublemaker
Call her a captive woman.
Let Aychürök not hear, become enraged,
And fly in fury to the city
Of her father Akun Khan;
Let her not gather an army from Afghanistan,
An immense force no easy thing to face,
With pale hazy banners like mountains,
With piercing cries and rallying shouts,
With a roaring battle tumult,
Mounted and armed upon gray stallions,
Carrying mottled gray war standards,
And wage war without restraint.
Let them not fight summer and winter,
Stand packed together in battle,
Choice horses dying among the herds,
The people shaken and heroes killed,
The nation subjected to suffering,
All its champions ruined.
Let there be no further conflict;
Take care, friend Kyyaz,
Let there be no such quarrel.
Do not reproach her by accident;
Hero, do not treat her as your equal in anger.
Approve whatever she says
And keep inclining your neck to her.
Leave her in peace for six years,
Then slowly make her your wife.
Submit to what she says,
Give her whatever she desires,
And cherish the dear woman Chürök
Above all your people.
Cast a cold lasso
On countless horses no petitioner could obtain;
Take the beautiful woman Chürök yourself,
And do not leave her dejected.
Win Chürök through hardship,
And take her as your gift.
Champion, raise the hero Kanchoro
As khan over the world!”
All the Jediger together
Now seated the hero Kanchoro
Upon a white felt rug.
With a plane-tree garden around him,
A golden throne in the tower,
They placed a golden crown upon him,
Raised him onto the golden throne,
And proclaimed him khan.
To the walled fortress,
To Chachykey’s palace,
News came that brave Semetey had died,
Had seen the true place
From which none returns,
That the dear man had passed away.
Yesterday, when he scattered great wealth,
When brave Semetey, the mighty gray wolf,
Set out toward Talas,
He had gathered eight khojas.
The aged khan, ruler of Bukhara,
Had given her to him while she was a maiden;
She was his maternal aunt.
This spoiled woman Chachykey
Was Semetey’s wife.
She summoned the messenger
Who had brought the news and said:
“If what you say is true,
If it is sound and not a lie,
I will consent to what you ask;
I will give you sixty camels
As your reward for glad tidings.”
