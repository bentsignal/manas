Who waded through embers when told to wade through bullets,
Who never drew back from a dispute,
Who never turned away from death,
Their horses grown fat and fit,
Each alone the equal of forty,
Who had made Talas their homeland,
Who had raised as khan and made their lion
Manas, the true blue-maned hero,
Who had hunted white deer,
Who had raised an uproar wherever they met—
All forty valiant men were there,
The precious Chubak and Almambet,
All the champions were there.
Koshoi and Toshtuk, their names in accord,
The two matching khans had come.
“If death arrives, I shall die,” he said.
“For this handful of Muslims,
While this precious life remains,
I shall serve as imam,” he said.
Aykojo, who had come from Mecca,
Had come then to lead their prayers.
Warrior Urbu, son of Eybit Khan,
Jamgyrchy of the Eshteks,
When their mighty khan Kongurbay
Made the whole earth wretched,
And the Chinese surged upon them,
All the great khans
Gathered together in anguish.
A mighty torment fell upon them,
And together they despaired of life.
The reason for their despair:
The sky was clear, the earth overcast;
Not a single blade upon the ground was visible,
Earth swirled and dust flew,
Not one soul among the people could be seen.
There were gray-mottled pennants and standards,
An uproar of cries of “Alas! Alas!”
White-mottled pennants and black standards,
Shouts and a tumultuous roar.
This was the Great Campaign itself,
The hour when unbelievers and Muslims
Were to meet face to face.
The bloodied men were drenched in blood,
And the heroes did not draw back;
They stood and fought without fleeing.
All the heroes grew weary,
Those very heroes,
With painted spears of black iron,
The heroes stood without fleeing
And drove them straight into each other’s hearts.
The earth’s surface rumbled,
All the people were scattered apart.
Then, golden-crested, with a belt like embers,
Azizkhan’s only son,
Ninety governors against him alone,
My Alake held them off.
When he leaned right and thrust,
He brought down more than ten;
When he leaned left and thrust,
He laid their sultans low.
Within that very campaign,
Lean-haunched Sarala
Your Alake kept saddled.
The incantation called Uyba,
Throwing them into turmoil,
Your Alake went on reciting.
Into their center Almambet
Carved his way;
Around their edges the forty companions
Slaughtered their way forward.
Into their very midst the seven khans
Plundered their way;
Against one flank Chubak
Battered his way forward.
On the side by the steep bank, Ajybai
Came hemming them in;
Against one flank old Kyrgyl
Slaughtered his way forward.
The whole plain became dust,
All the level ground became blood.
With their fangs jutting out,
Dead horses made a mountain;
With their mustaches bristling,
Dead warriors lay in bundles.
All the heroes,
Shouting, “Islam!”
Held the Chinese back again,
Scattering them toward Beijing.
The Beijing people with their emperor,
Making the Chinese howl,
They drove and pushed them back.
Like tumbleweed flying,
Tossing them this way and that,
They hemmed them in and drove them on.
Wherever the spear could reach,
He struck them down with the spear;
Those who came before him
He brought down with steel.
Girding his waist securely,
Against seven myriad black Chinese
He plunged alone and attacked.
When the vast Chinese people,
That formidable host, surged in waves,
A Chinese man called Manzhuu
Led them surging forward.
When the black Kalmyks and Mongol people
Surged in and gave battle,
A tribe called Shibee
Charged from the flank,
And they too overwhelmed them.
There was a people called Soloon,
The Beijing people with their emperor,
An inexhaustible mine of men.
The Torghuts from the woman khan
Were also surging upon them then.
The countless Chinese swarmed together;
After the formidable, fierce, blue-maned warrior
Drove them back and held them to their front,
Ninety Beijings rose in motion.
See the grass upon the earth,
See the Chinese, countless as the grass!
See the roaring flood,
See the Chinese people, vast as the flood!
Spearheads clashed together,
Men’s heads struck against each other,
Mist hid the moon from sight,
