When the struggle reached its fiercest,
Seytek brought forward a special force
And compelled them to submit.
A mighty battle and tremendous uproar—
Such was the clash they waged.
But Sarybay
Was an extraordinarily powerful weather-worker;
Rain poured over every ridge,
Hail beat upon every hollow,
Fog covered the open plain,
And fierce winds and creeping mist
Closed in and blinded every eye.
Clouds gathered across the sky;
Sarybay led Kuyaly astray
And escaped from her,
Barely saving himself from death.
Your bellowing champion,
To wage battle against the full-armed infidel,
And go forth to strike him,
Gathered her strength for a desperate assault;
To stand as a warrior on the field,
The lion-maiden Kuyaly
Chose and donned a pale-gray robe,
And mounted the swift steed Jeljetpes.
Composed yet fierce for combat,
The mighty maiden cast her life aside
And girded on a naked sword.
Excited by her own fury,
She panted with rage
And turned Jeljetpes by the mouth.
Saying this to the hero Seytek,
The lion-maiden came and stood before him:
“I am the one who will fight Sarybay;
I shall cast him into the melee.
Sarybay’s burden of calamity
I shall take upon myself.
At the call to battle I shall charge ahead;
His torment and his death
I shall seize with both hands and prevail!
I shall slaughter his heroes.”
So spoke Kuyaly,
And such was her bravery.
Her precious hair, long as feather grass,
She wound and knotted upon her crown,
And donned her arrow-proof coat.
A golden-cheeked battle-axe
She slung upon her bare forearm;
A double-edged Zulfiqar
Was gripped in her hand.
Breaking through and opening a path,
She charged the host without pause,
Shouting, “Do not flee!”
And raising a true battle cry,
She turned her face toward death.
With a steel shaft and sharpened point,
A decorated spear fit for war,
She thrust forth with a thunderous roar.
Before the maiden’s courage,
Even khans could not bear to look;
No mortal could approach
The maiden in her heroism.
When she merely surged,
Even mountains could not withstand her;
When she moved against the wicked,
Even swarming Chinese hosts could not defeat her.
Then Kuyaly
Turned straight toward Sarybay,
Taking her steel spear in hand.
When she lashed her horse,
Its mouth gaped like a cavern.
Her bravery was extraordinary,
Her appearance majestic;
When mounted, her bearing
Was unlike that of any other human.
Throughout the entire world,
To every long-eared elder,
To all people everywhere,
To all who knew or saw her,
Her name was famed like that of a saint.
The poor soul’s fortune stood in perfect balance.
