And when the seven days are complete,
When the count reaches the eighth day,
Abide by the words we have spoken.
Then let us fight a mighty battle
And satisfy our hearts’ desire.
Let strength overwhelm the weak;
Let us kill them and thrust them into graves.
Until the seven days have passed,
Until my allotted time is fulfilled,
Let the terms I have spoken take effect.
A good word given in promise
Must hold until its appointed end.
Make your promise firm;
Pledge the mortal oath of men.
Think wisely and do not be rash;
Though the bones of your shins be bent,
Do not flee in childishness,
And do not lose your courage.
If by chance you run away,
It will be a shameful deed.
Mighty son of Manas,
You must keep your promise!”
When brave Seytek heard this,
He agreed to his demand
And granted the ogre’s warrior son
A respite of seven days.
He had all the spears gathered
And brought the whole conflict to a halt.
“Sarybai has taken a seven-day respite,” they said.
To discover whether his word was false or true,
Sarybai withdrew to a secure place
Five stone-throws away.
Before him stood the racehorse Tookyzyl;
Left alone in that desolate place,
He removed its saddle and let it stand bare,
Fettering and hobbling it,
Then tethered it securely
In the roaring icy wind.
Using his white helmet as a pillow,
Keeping his white steel close at hand,
And wrapping himself in his arrow-proof coat,
With his outstretched legs unable to draw back
And his closed eyes unable to open,
He rested upon one elbow
And snatched a little sleep.
The long-limbed madman
Lay utterly unconcerned,
Esteeming the entire world
No more than the hem of his robe.
Until the seven days were complete,
Until the appointed word came due,
Until the time arrived,
The long-limbed madman
Sank into a giant’s sleep.
His true snores split the rocks;
For six days he slept,
Lying on his side at ease,
His soul steeped in restful comfort.
When he had slept his fill,
He led the rested Tookyzyl
To graze on fresh grass
And quenched its thirst
At a cold, clear spring.
Leading it about for a long while,
He filled the handsome charger’s
Broad chest with grass;
Then, leading it a little farther,
He filled the swinging-jawed stallion’s
Bare belly with grass.
Wondering whether they would come to wrestle,
And fearing brave Seytek,
He saddled Tookyzyl.
Fearing for his precious life,
He adjusted and donned his arrow-proof armor;
His destructive nature was plain.
Sarybai, son of the ogre—
Such was the extent of his courage.
