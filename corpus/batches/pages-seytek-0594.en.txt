It was as though he had made slaves
Of every living soul.
Into the nostrils of his nose
A mighty giant might have fit;
When the madman snorted,
It seemed he could suck in
The whole universe without letting it stir.
His body was tall as a plane tree;
The people, despairing of their precious lives,
Seemed unable to remain standing.
His hands and arms were like pillars;
It seemed he subdued the universe
And crushed every living creature.
His drooping eyelashes,
On the glowering infidel,
Were like the handle of a rawhide whip;
The accursed one’s appearance
Had always been just so.
When he moved, he was a glowing ember;
Among many marvels, none was so dreadful.
When the madman roared,
It seemed he seized the universe,
Plucked up every mortal hero,
And casually
Stuffed him into his sack.
By Almighty God’s power,
One of you is a warrior, another a hero,
And another is made of black earth.
Like beasts of countless kinds,
Living creatures are born in every form;
Such was Sarybai, the monster’s son.
When he saw Sarybai,
The gray wolf’s heart grew troubled;
His face was like a burning coal,
His nose like a vast ridge,
His eyes like tethering irons,
Able to overtake anyone he pursued.
His bristling mustache
Seemed sharp enough to pierce an army;
Such an upturned mustache—
No other human creature was like him.
The infidel’s thought,
The thought of this blazing warrior, was:
“If only I could crush the universe
And stuff it into a single sack;
Until the Day of Judgment,
If only I could take this entire world
As provisions for my journey;
If only I could destroy every living soul
And remain alone;
If only I could annihilate all existence
And bring slaughter upon the ages.”
Such was Sarybek’s nature.
Standing upright at his full height,
When the gray-wolf-born Semetey
Saw Sarybai,
His face changed.
When he saw the madman,
The false world seemed to constrict;
As he looked closely upon death,
The broad and tranquil world
Appeared narrow to his eyes.
You infidel fit only for the grave,
You end of the created age—
Who has ever seen such a warrior?
He alone was born into the world
As though intending to end the age.
The plate armor upon his head,
The felt skirt of that armor,
Was broad as roofing felt for four yurts;
It seemed able to crush all the ages
And cram them into one hole.
Every mortal human being,
His fly-like soul shrieking,
Would burn and crackle beneath his wrath
And be ground into dust.
Upon the brows above his eyes,
Nine vultures and black eagles
Seemed to have gathered and built their nests;
