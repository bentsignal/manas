Akun Khan, khan of the Afghans,
Was a khan of sacred power;
To Manas, his children's betrothal partner,
He had given a promise.
You talk so much, sister-in-law,
And make me babble so much;
When you mock a slave,
What wish will you fulfill?
Why should I listen to your taunts?
Give me the falcon and let me go!
The mount beneath me
Is Semetey's winged steed;
The one lost, whom I am seeking,
Is my fierce lord's falcon!
Do not ridicule my errand;
Do not mock without cause
A man bound to another's service!
What I wear upon my body
Is my poor lord's Akolpok;
Though he is a slave, to wear it
The coat fits Kulchoro.
The coat I wore belonged to someone else;
The horse I rode belonged to someone else;
He would come roaring and strike—
[HOLD]
Now agree to what I say;
Without ridiculing me, dear sister-in-law,
Give me the falcon now.
Arguing with girls,
What wish should I fulfill?
Dear sister-in-law, give it now,
Let me take the falcon and go!”
When the companion spoke thus,
Churok stood and said:
“Oh, goodness gracious!
[HOLD]
You are young, my dear;
Do not go on so without cause!
'You wandered all the world
Seeking a husband,' you declared;
'By the shores of Ala-Kol,
You took my Akshumkar,' you said,
Speaking empty slander,
You have attacked me on that score!
In my wandering, my dear,
Did I travel the world's roads?
To your rash lord,
Saying, 'Take me,'
Did I go with threats?
Saying, 'Give six months' labor,'
Did I take the falcon from your hand?
You stand saying, 'Give me my falcon';
Have I become subject to your claim thus?
You are young, my dear;
You lay claim to your falcon,
As though the marriage rite had been performed,
As though you were filled with joy,
You keep calling me 'sister-in-law';
Whom are you mocking?
You come right up to me
And address me as 'sister-in-law';
[HOLD]
You start demanding the falcon.
What an extraordinary boy you are!
As though the marriage rite had been performed,
As though a scarf had been put upon my head,
You call me 'sister-in-law.'
Do not lose your sense of propriety;
Speak your words properly!
My husband Chynkojo
Is like tempered red iron;
My husband-to-be, the warrior Toltoi,
Is as mighty as half a mountain.
My would-be husband Kadyrbai
Is no enemy who would stand and watch;
He would rain arrows from the flank.
The husband I rely on is like a crag,
His courage is like blue iron.
