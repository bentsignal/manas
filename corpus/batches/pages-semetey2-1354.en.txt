Kanchoro drove them on without respite.
At the gate with its steel posts,
The keeper of the gate
Met the flood and joined battle.
The khans who guarded the gate,
The champions expressly placed
By the bloodthirsty hero as sentries,
The ablest of the guards,
The strongest of the gatekeepers—
Have you heard of them?
With the lion Manas the leopard,
That hero long since departed,
With the gray-maned accomplished lion
Who once shook the earth with myriad troops,
He had been a friend unto the other world.
When he ate, he chewed iron;
He spoke with the prophet David
And forged from precious steel.
With hero Manas the lion,
Their sheep shared one pen,
Their meals one fellowship.
When his craftsmanship was heard of,
Whatever Arkesken struck fitted true.
The people of Bakburchun
Rejoiced in Bolokbai.
Bolokbai forged steel,
And armed your great Khan Manas.
Hearing he was a champion smith,
The people of Beijing were
Left astonished.
When valiant Bolokbai died,
The steadfast, true-tempered hero
Received as a gift was Akzholtoi.
His courage was worth countless troops.
Agile on horseback and swift on foot,
When he fought he wrought great havoc.
His valor was extraordinary,
His wrestling strength immense.
When he thrust with a spear,
He was surpassingly deft and tested.
For hero Semetey,
He was the inheritance guarding the gate—
Both wrestler and lion.
His marksmanship was also true;
He knew Zhydymak and wielded a practiced blade.
That he had come was also true.
He was a prime example of a capable hero,
The son of the smith Bolokbai.
At the gate stood Akzholtoi,
When enraged, worth countless troops.
Kanchoro and Kiyaz's vast army
Could not make him submit.
He would not cut down and surrender
Widows, orphans, and old women to the foe.
They stood facing one another,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
Fighting with a crash.
Smoke trailed into the sky;
They stood face to face.
Akzholtoi alone against that host
Fought them with a crash.
The Zhediger flooded in and attacked,
Striking without sparing life.
Those who met each other laid one another low;
The wretched souls who entered battle
Hung by half a hair.
Those whose death had come died,
Meeting calamity and hardship,
Ripping lungs and taking gallbladders,
Shedding extraordinary blood.
Bolokbai's Akzholtoi,
Whose renown was worth mighty hosts,
The wisest master of counsel—
Such was his excellence.
Long ago, his father, the smith Bolokbai,
When Akzholtoi was a playful child,
When he was a playful child,
While his father was still alive,
