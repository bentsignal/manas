Until Sarybai had no people left.
He disgraced and slaughtered the nation;
In fierce strife and great massacres,
How many times had he slain them?
All the multitude
Had learned the infidel’s wrath.
Unable to understand it openly,
They endured that infidel Sarybai,
The warrior who devastated nations,
Until their tears pooled like a lake.
There was no blood left to spill by the spoonful,
No hardy soul left to fight.
He would not let them sow their grain
Or reap a harvest from the land;
Driving them about and leading them astray,
He brought the people’s misery to the boil.
The people raised a mighty uproar,
Clamoring beyond all measure.
We fell into the grip of that dog with whelps;
From the hands of this infidel
We barely escaped by finding a way.
Amid slaughter we raised an outcry;
Our souls were scorched and withered.
The people murmured among themselves,
“Enough of what Sarybai has done!”
Pouring out his wrath, he turned back
And sat abandoned and bereft,
Deprived of his countless host.
His ribs felt torn apart,
His mind was utterly distracted;
Death, doom, and true disaster
Appeared above his head.
He twisted all his mustache
And tore at all his beard.
The conflict had become overwhelming;
A dreadful burden pressed upon his soul,
As, wrathful and grieving,
He lost the army he had commanded.
“I have neither tail nor mane;
I have been left a blunt beast,” he said.
“My horns have broken from my head;
I have been left hornless,” he said.
“Bereft of my beloved people,
I have become a brute,” he said.
His blood boiled and his rage swelled;
He stirred rebellion, his eyes blazing.
“What fate is coming upon me?
I am finished,” he despaired of life.
Kuyaly confronted him,
Brave Seytek grappled with him,
And Külchoro fought him.
They seized the initiative there,
But he would not submit to the three heroes.
Taking turns,
They fought him face-to-face
And waged a mighty battle.
Firing guns and loosing arrows,
They cut down all who faced them,
Waging strife beyond mortal bounds.
When a khan grows obstinate, he dies;
All who grapple in battle die.
In such a place heroes die,
And many men die together;
Great rulers die and khans die,
And all who fight die.
When the whole world gathers,
When two sides clash,
When two heroes fight,
The guiltless multitude suffers;
There is a great tumult of battle cries—
Such is the fury of holy war.
Yielding to no one who approached,
His prowess was extraordinary,
His heroism beyond compare;
He cut down all he faced,
Slaughtering as he advanced.
Yielding to no multitude,
Sarybai, son of a monster,
Grew far fiercer
Than he had been before.
