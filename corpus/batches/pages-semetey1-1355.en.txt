Beijing is no easy place,
Nor are they a people easily faced!
No one has brought them low;
None who encountered them remained alive.
My khan and uncle Bakai, you know this!
Heroes died upon the road they scouted;
Valiant men died and khans died;
Every soul that met them died.
Many giants who attacked them died;
Grappling and fighting,
All the lions died together.
When I speak, I have this grief;
My auspicious uncle, listen well!
Radiance-pouring Great Beijing
Is no place to trample underfoot;
The Chinese are numerous as ants,
No people who yield their companions.
In the khagan's realm are the Black Chinese;
Their arrogance is great and they have no faith.
Saying you will go and take Beijing,
You have begun this calamity—
Such is this filthy world!
The boys you rely upon
Have not yet begun to face
And enter mighty, turbulent Beijing;
They do not yet know the stratagems of war,
Nor have they campaigned against an enemy.
That they would bring turmoil to Beijing,
I knew from the beginning with great fear;
Against the khan of Kakan-Chyn,
I knew they would grapple!
I knew long ago you would go;
I had the whole earth searched
And costly crimson materials
Fetched from many lands!
It will not admit a bullet in battle,
Nor an ember when saxaul burns;
I lined its inside with silk,
And had it worked without pause for eight years.
I sent it from master to master,
So no stray bullet could pass;
I had experts test it.
I had them reject what was unsuitable;
Plate after plate of steel,
Filed with an Albar's blade and mixed,
I had poured into its middle.
For a time I fashioned the coat,
More splendid than all the possessions
Of my noble prince Manas.
When the six vile men, Abyke and Köbösh,
Tore away the ak-olpok armor,
Its fiber remained frayed;
Those lowborn villains
Had worn it hanging loose.
At last, when Semetey
Took it back again,
Resting neither by day
Nor sleeping at night,
I took its weft and spun it,
So no stray bullet could pass,
And no needless death overtake him.
From precious materials of every kind,
I worked them into its hem.
I stretched it on a frame and shaped it.
Far finer than all
My noble hero had possessed,
I fitted the ak-olpok armor.
I wearied the eyes that stared at it.
Having steel filed and the Albar's blade
Ground entirely fine,
I had it placed between the layers.
I adorned it with gold,
Lest a spear pierce through
Any place between its layers.
I slid slender rods through it.
I patched whatever had worn out;
Resting neither by day
Nor sleeping at night,
