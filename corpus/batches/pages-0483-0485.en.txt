My wrath rose; I stood defiant.
By killing Khan Orgo,
You have roused my fury now!
I shall gird my waist tight;
Just watch: I shall drive you
All the way to Mecca!
You reckless blood-drinker,
My name is Zholoy the champion;
Come here and let me see you!”
So spoke the hero Zholoy,
Standing there, whirling his spear.
When Manas saw this,
He unleashed his wrath.
The youngest of the six lions,
The leopard mighty in spirit,
Cast all care for life aside.
Such was the blood-drinker's way:
He prepared himself in fury.
To the blood-drinker himself
An unseen inspiration came:
“You fled in a flurry when I faced you—
So that is the infidel you are!
You let all the monks be slaughtered,
And now you come to quarrel:
Zholoy, what a hero you have become!
The black poplars of wide Altai—
Of the poplar-covered promontories,
When I reached twelve years of age,
I brought hell upon you all,
Slitting the monks' throats,
And seized your tea on a thousand camels.
You babbling, crafty Kalmyk,
Where had you gone then?
When I chased your great army,
Drove it from Altai and scattered it,
You scurried away before me.
Damn you, greedy dog,
When you fled toward Esenkan,
I unleashed my wrath
And slaughtered your people!”
The blood-drinker, the fierce-maned leopard,
Great Manas, born resourceful,
Who never shrank from a clash,
Great Manas, born open-handed,
Took his immense steel spear
And settled it in his grip.
“Right at the heart,” he thought,
“Here, at the lung's edge,
At the windpipe itself—
For that bastard Orgo,
It is time to gasp and rattle.”
The fierce-maned one's ways were different:
That born champion, poor fellow,
Hunched behind the ridge,
His blood-drinking eyes then
Seemed about to leap out,
Staring from the farther side.
His ribs creaked;
His prominent front teeth
Crackled like millet.
Without looking around him,
Without sparing his life at all:
“Upon Orgo, huge as Opol Mountain,
I unleashed my wrath.
Your cattle, not your horses,
I utterly wiped out.
Where were you then?
If you truly mean to fight,
Great Zholoy of the Kalmyks,
Stand your ground ahead; do not flee!”
So spoke the hero Manas.
He headed for Khan Zholoy,
His eyes a tiger's eyes,
His wrath a dragon's wrath.
Without closing his open eyes,
Without drawing back his leveled spear,
With wrath entering his arm,
With courage entering his heart,
He struck Toruchaar on the flank
And took his painted spear in hand.
His wide eyes blazed;
The peerless, fierce-maned champion's
Bloodlust truly seized him.
A company of nine protectors
Had come to give him their aid;
A company of nine supporters
Had come to uphold him.
He thundered forward, spear extended,
And in the blink of an eye,
As he was about to reach Zholoy,
Khan Zholoy of the Kalmyks—
That Zholoy was indeed a mere boy—
Could not endure his heat,
Could not withstand his cold.
Unable to turn Achbuudan's head,
When he saw the lion's bearing,
Zholoy could not stand his ground.
Twisting his neck like a stag,
Toward a rocky outcrop a bowshot away
He went galloping up.
Toruchaar beneath him,
That true winged steed,
Went wild and strained,
Toruchaar, running loose,
Straining as though his rider had died.
From the winding ridge,
Before he could wheel back steadily,
Before turning his swift horse
And coming back toward Zholoy,
The hero Manas was in a frenzy.
There on the farther side, a great marksman—
Karazhoy—lay in his path.
He performed deeds unlike other men's,
Assuming many different forms.
A man who emerged when China was in turmoil,
A great marksman of the royal treasury,
He raised an uproar when challenged.
That infidel Karazhoy,
An exceptional weather-conjurer and trickster,
Had outstripped others in cunning.
A black boulder as large as a hut—
From that black boulder, for Manas
The swinish marksman lay in wait.
A master of marksmanship,
When Manas approached him,
His firing mechanism stood ready.
