Has reached the time to be given!
Give Khan Akun my greetings;
Tell him to come to my feast.
If he does not come to my feast,
The clamorous shooting,
The struggle that cannot be healed—
Tell him to behold them from atop his house.
Tell him to surrender his beautiful daughter
As booty, without begrudging her!
Go no farther; turn back this way,
And return directly from that place.
With red banners and blue spears,
Let not one Kyrgyz remain behind.
When you head straight on from there,
You come to a place called Chambyl-Bel.
Dwelling in Chambyl-Bel
Are Zhanaaly and the hero Karach,
Who summer in Belendi
And tether their tulpars
Upon the ridge of Belendi.
They never shrink from death
Nor turn and flee from doom.
Say it is Khan Kökötöy's memorial feast,
The foremost of great gatherings;
Say the elder like a prophet,
The blessing-giving Khan Koshoy, the holy man,
Is the head of that feast.
Tell the iron-cored hero,
The swaying brave,
To come to my feast
And see this spectacle.
When the people move uphill,
When the horses' tongues turn black,
I will trample their heads
And scatter all four kinds of livestock.
I will destroy their lives
And send their children into uproar.
If they do not love this spectacle,
Tell them to come to my feast!
In that distant land,
In the midst of the Kara-Kum,
Is the sultan of spearmen,
Urum Khan's Kökbörü,
Who has taken spoils from many peoples,
A champion born surpassing
All his father's other sons.
Say Kökötöy's camp has settled
Along Üch-Karkyra.
Say the time has come for Chinese, Kalmyks, and Muslims
To grapple with one another;
There are champions and mounted combats,
A mighty bald champion comes from the Kalmyks,
A mighty bald champion comes from the Kyrgyz,
And these too compete for prizes,
Striking one another in the chest.
A naked woman untethers a camel;
If he does not see this spectacle,
The people will depart with their eyes opened.
Tell Urum Khan's Kökbörü
To come to my feast.
If he does not come to my feast,
I shall show him Kökötöy's red banner with its gray crescent
From atop his house,
And tumult crying from side to side.
If that arrogant man does not come,
If he delays, I will challenge him;
If he is very late,
I will whip him and start a quarrel!
When you turn back this way,
To the great holy city of sheikhs,
To mighty Kokand and Margalan,
To Kozubek of the Khan of Kokand,
Malabek of Margalan,
Fork-bearded Synzhybek of Andijan,
Alabek of Alty-Shaar,
And Sanchybek in Samarkand—
Give all of them my greetings.
Tell them to come, not one left behind!
