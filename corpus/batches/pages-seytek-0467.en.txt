I have poured away much of my strength,
I have made a stake of my arm,
I have spilled my blood by the spoonful.
To this blackhearted bloodthirsty man
I shall now sacrifice
My precious life, no greater than a fly.
I shall ask his inmost secret,
And if he will not tell it,
My wrath shall rise till I stand rigid,
And I shall do something to this bloodthirsty man.
Why should I go on living as a slave;
What else remains for me to do?”
So saying, Külchoro
Drew a line as a sign;
The poor falcon among men
Was about to do something extraordinary.
He was about to begin disaster,
About to inflame the madness,
That incurable, festering wound.
He was about to bring upon the hero
A calamity no man had ever seen.
Refusing to release the young man,
He was about to tear his mind apart.
Standing rigid in his rage,
He held fast to his lord,
The dear one who had made him helpless.
How could he ever release
That man whose stature loomed like a mountain?
Allah Most High indeed creates
His servants in every form.
Some souls He made heroes,
Some He made lionhearted warriors,
Some souls He made deficient,
And some souls the Creator made
Equal to the dark grave.
Then Külchoro,
Clashing and refusing to let go,
Clung to the bloodthirsty lion.
He began circling through his speech,
Hurling out forceful words,
Each word's ending
Devouring anyone who understood.
Heedless of your vastness,
Showing mercy to no living soul,
Heedless of the blessed lion,
The poor hero who had endured many ordeals
Did not spare his threadlike life.
“Why does he stand there without speaking?
Why does God strike
This witless, bloodthirsty sultan?” he said.
“For this I have been diminished,
Yet he gives no thought to me;
For this I was taken into slavery,
Yet he does not grieve for me at all.
I spent my precious youth,
And made a club of my head.”
When Külchoro and Kara Döö together seized Semetey, saying, “Now that I have caught you,
I shall not leave you alive but kill you,” Külchoro grew angry and said, “Neither entering
The grave in death nor living among your people, you made me bear your torment. Now,
If I do not kill you and then die myself, unless you renounce the life of this world, speak
A word to me, or come to your senses”—and in his fury Külchoro seized his lord,
Shaking him and refusing to let go. Then Semetey, frightened by Külchoro's voice,
Began to speak:
He seized him and twisted his mouth,
Standing rigid in his rage,
And spoke to him harshly.
As he spoke so harshly,
At that moment Semetey
Quietly began speaking weighty words:
