If Akkelte strikes farther over,
That is the lower edge of the lung.”
Pure in marksmanship,
The ready gun-rest at hand,
Külchoro flicked it into place;
Akkelte went to work.
The Akkelte gun cracked;
Though your supporters be countless,
The valiant khoja dropped flat.
The bullet fired at his middle
Grazed through his wing,
Entered farther into him,
And shattered his lung.
From the dear valiant khoja,
At the root of the thigh muscle,
It tore away a span of flesh.
Chynkojo's black horse
Wheeled around then,
And he fell backward from it.
The khoja came screaming,
Unable to find his bearings,
Raising the battle cry of Shygai.
His voice reached God;
Upon Külchoro
He fell in a heap.
Külchoro, who had lain concealed,
Roared in wrath,
And stood looming there,
Raising the battle cry of Manas.
Your orphan born of the hero
Now reached Chynkojo.
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
The khoja whose deeds were bold—
He and the companion
Now seized each other by the collar.
They gripped each other's wrists
And wrestled like champions.
Seizing and twisting him by the collar,
Outmaneuvering the bow-bearing khoja,
He lifted and dashed him to the ground.
The reeking khoja's
Mustache he tore out and flung away;
His beard he ripped out and scattered.
He pummeled him again and again;
Black blood leaving his mouth,
Yellow bile followed behind it.
The prince burst from the cave,
Running with long strides.
Shouting, ‘Baabedin!’
And rushing in wrath,
The lion came,
Pleading with Külchoro:
“I tended a brave man and won a brave man's fortune;
I have now seen its benefit.
My dear Külchoro,
Blessed am I with a kinsman like you.
I tended an argamak and won a horse's fortune;
My dear one, blessed am I
With a charger like Taybuurul.
For the boy who served as my support,
My dear Külchoro,
May your bloodthirsty lord be the sacrifice!
You rekindled an extinguished fire;
You revived a lifeless soul;
You joined what had been severed;
You gathered what had been scattered.
He does not tire when ridden for six months—
My charger, noble Buurul.
We kicked each other like foals,
And struggled together at the breast—
