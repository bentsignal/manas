When told to take a cap, we cut off a head;
When told to drink water, we drank poison;
When told to spill blood, we spilled our souls.
We were a people equal to long clubs,
Crooked cudgels, and stout staves.
When a man loves whom he chooses,
What night equals the blessed Night of Power?
While the bloodstained warrior watched this land,
Was there ever a day of merely standing by?
If the many Kyrgyz and Argyn
Suddenly stretched in marching ranks,
And unexpectedly joined battle,
If our blood ran in slaughter
And all of us were locked in combat,
Where now is the tawny mountain pass
To which we might flee in a stream?
Where is the lion like the Greathearted One,
Who would spare these people torment
And divide the warring sides?
Now there is no ridge to brace against,
No place left to fortify;
You have no khan whose figure is a mountain.
Parted from your protecting khan,
You many Kyrgyz have no strength.
What becomes of a rolling, hilly land
Whose people have left it desolate?
What becomes of a soul burning openly,
Or livestock abandoned without an owner?
Parted from our noble lion,
What kinds of hell
Must we Kyrgyz now endure?
No cotton is planted for us
In Keng-Kol now;
There is no time to lounge at ease.
Is there still a garden planted in Talas?
We have lost our fortunate lion;
Is this a time to sprawl in idleness?
Have we now a garden planted
Upon the hill?
Is this a time for swaggering rest?
Many enemies weigh upon our minds;
An enemy stands ready to bury us.
There is the oppression
Of Alty-Shaar and Kokand.
With sashes wound like turbans,
Praying to the Almighty,
Their crooked threat still stands.
You have no supporting presence, no backbone,
You have lost your true gray-maned lion.
The one seen today is gone tomorrow;
Your heart is flame and your breast is ember—
Such is this worthless world!
The Greathearted gray-maned lion
Who endured every hell is not here!
The assembled people grieved and said this,
Speaking of mankind's dreadful calamity;
Their ribs seemed to split,
And tears poured from their eyes.
They heard the tale from the great host:
No one knew where he was,
And they heard that Semetey had vanished.
The people wailed in anguish,
Despairing of their tiny lives.
Semetey had gone from among them,
And the multitude stood in uproar.
As though each person's father had died,
The crescent-tipped golden banners filled the field.
As the multitude stood grieving,
A clamor split the sky.
Overwhelmed by dreadful disaster,
They abandoned utterly
Not only their livestock but their lives.
The people wailed in anguish,
Running up and down;
When they heard about Semetey,
They plunged into the tumult,
And fell into a vast,
Unhealed uproar.
