These were Esenkan's words:
‘Jakyp's son Manas—
Capture him and bring him here,’ he said.
After letting him wreak such wonders,
After letting all our champions be slaughtered,
Saying, ‘We suffered horrors from the Burut;
We died when we met him face to face’—
How can we withdraw without dying?
To that stern and mighty king,
How can we speak without shame?
By these fucking Buruts
We are being slaughtered as we retreat.
Until the soft kuray stalks ripen,
Until autumn gossamer takes flight,
Let us fight hard for six whole months;
Let us retreat only after a slaughter.
These fucking Buruts—
Let us keep driving them out,
Chase them in terror,
And pen them where they seek refuge.
Let us wreak boundless havoc.
These fucking Buruts—
Let us surround and seize them.
Let us follow hard upon their trail,
Pursue them doggedly to the end.”
The champion Zholoy roared,
Addressing them all.
Zholoy spared them no abuse;
He drove the whole army on.
Warrior Zholoy, from the army's rear,
Was driving them forward.
The warrior seemed unwilling to spare anyone,
Flaring up in all his might.
At the army's head was Great Döngö,
Whose speech no human understood.
Far beyond Beijing,
There was a people called Sazangshang.
His speech was unknown to men;
In his head was a single eye like a platter.
His father was a raging Chinese brute;
Unable to understand human speech,
He was a beast-like swine.
The hair at his nape stood bristling;
His anger rose, and he stood rigid,
Finding no one to grapple with.
That infidel of mighty wrath
Was a warrior full of strength.
In Beijing, seat of the Khakan,
They called him formidable Döngö.
His guardian spirit was lofty, his name renowned.
The surpassing champion, warrior Döngö,
Was a creature uniquely made.
Raging, he grasped his banner;
The sight of him seized the heart.
He looked like a towering mountain.
When he roared in fury,
The gorge shook and thundered.
Not merely Manas,
But the eighteen thousand worlds
He seemed ready to lay waste.
Formidable Döngö surged forward;
The steam from his nostrils
Billowed like a little hide-smoking pit.
As though struck by slaughter,
Behind him the Chinese army shrieked.
No small host swept forward;
As the countless army approached with a roar,
Manas the lion, the generous hero,
To battle and slaughter the Chinese
Came there once again.
Surging onward on Toruchaar,
Roaring in his fury,
The champions who came sweeping forward—
Blood-shedding Manas the leopard
Drove them back in disarray.
If you looked at warrior Döngö,
He was straining forward on Kylküröng.
From directly ahead, that accursed one,
To thrust a spear at Manas,
Had made himself ready.
Setting all his weapons in place,
As though he would pass through slaughtering,
Wrath spread across his face.
Pouring forth anger, he roared;
Before he had reached Manas,
He spoke as though he would devour him:
“Why did you slaughter the army?
When the order came from the great khan,
What did you do to the camel?
‘I will slaughter the Chinese’—
That is what you have been thinking.
Among the battle-shouting Kalmyks,
You have grappled only with the weak;
You have merely been playing.
You fucking, wretched Burut,
Today you have roused my fury.
Today I will ruin your looks.
My anger has risen; I stand unyielding.
Today I will leave none of you unchallenged.
Today I will do as I see fit;
I will slaughter you without sparing a single one!”
Thus Döngö thundered.
The cudgel in his hand
Looked like a traveler's small tent.
A helmet of blue iron
He had pulled down over his head.
The instant he saw Manas,
He leveled his blue-steel spear at him.
Lashing Kylküröng with his whip,
Without sparing his slender life,
His mouth opened like a ravine;
Fire poured from his mouth,
Spraying out in arm-thick streams.
As though about to work some terrible deed,
His fearsome wonders on display,
Warrior Döngö from among the Chinese
Had broken away.
He had come out for single combat.
From the infidel's fury,
The earth split apart.
The sight of him seized the heart.
That heaving Döngö
Looked like a mountain's gray ridge.
From behind Döngö,
The champions sweeping forward
Launched a mass assault.
Lashing Kylküröng with his whip,
From among the Chinese,
Champion Döngö had broken away.
His dread presence was unlike a mortal's;
Anyone who clashed with him
He seemed ready to leave dead.
When he saw warrior Döngö,
Valiant Manas the leopard,
Upon his swift steed Toruchaar,
Laid on the whip without pause
And attacked the Chinese troublemaker.
He turned Toruchaar's head;
At mountain-like Döngö,
Without hesitation, he leveled his spear.
Your renowned lion Manas—
His valor was of quite another kind.
Beijing, seat of the Khakan,
That fierce, marked warrior would wipe it out.
