Let us begin the tale of Joloy.
For six years he traveled on foot,
Greasing his great spear,
Laying low those who fought him—
Hero Joloy, reeking of blood.
For seven years he traveled on foot,
Greasing his well-made spear,
Laying low all he reached—
Hero Joloy, reeking of demons.
Fuming and roaring in anger,
Dear Joloy came thundering:
“I will tear off the head
Of the Burut who has come to wrestle.
Upon this palm-sized Burut
I will heap suffering all at once,” he said.
Your infidel Joloy roared.
“I will take
This fucking Burut's prize.
I will take his prize and bind him,” he said.
“This Burut who calls himself a hero,
I will chase as far as Mecca,” he said.
From among the infidel people,
To wrestle on foot,
Joloy came out alone.
Joloy's form
Looked as large as a yurt.
Joloy of the Kalmyks
Was like a hungry lion or tiger;
No one who wrestled him survived.
On the field where they would wrestle,
At the high center
Of the gleaming plain,
Ancient Koshoi stood.
Saying, “I will seize and throw the old man,”
God had struck Joloy.
Do not ask what Joloy was like:
He was drunk on liquor;
This infidel was
Far younger than your elder.
It was the time when he settled
The Black Kalmyks' disputes.
It was the time when that infidel,
Steeled with courage, was in full force.
It was the time when Joloy
Was mounting in strength like a stallion.
The Kalmyks' küpsör garment
He wrapped puffed around his waist.
Sixty billy-goat hides
He had sewn together as its outer layer
And made into trousers.
Bareheaded, his head huge as a cauldron,
Down the back of that damned infidel
Hung braided
Black hair thick as a club.
Koshoi went striding forward;
Hero Joloy, a mighty man,
Came out before him.
Joloy spoke to Koshoi:
“You are dead, Burut,” he said.
“Fortunate Manas's blessed one,
My fucking Burut,
I will break your leg.”
Roaring in fury,
Joloy came upon him.
Your dear elder Koshoi said,
“Kalmyk, take your turn,”
And held out his forearm.
Joloy, huge as Opol-Too,
Seized him by the forearm.
Was it not Koshoi who divided
The frontier of Esenkan?
Was it not Koshoi who had grown accustomed
To grappling with many men?
Was it not capable Koshoi,
Born to be a wrestler on foot?
Was it not renowned Koshoi,
Who had grappled with so many giants?
The day passed as they wrestled,
