The earth split in shimmering waves;
Khan Joloy's Achbuudan
Came in hard at Akkula's heels.
The forelock of Maaniker—
It was steeped in ill fortune;
For the tulpar driven in a race,
There was no prize ahead.
For not racing Maaniker,
Bokmurun of Kökötöy
Had no sense at all.
See the ants, see the worms;
The people like swarming Chinese worms
Had gone to urge on the horses.
When one looked, nothing met the eye;
Soil flew and dust burned,
The sky clear but the earth overcast,
And no one could tell which was which.
They were exceptional chargers—
Akkula and Achbuudan,
As alike as rabbits.
Among the Black Chinese and Manchus,
Just as Akkula was about to be caught,
When Khan Kongurbai was coming,
When he was about to see Akkula,
When turmoil was about to break,
When Achbuudan was about to kick and fling him,
Dear lion Almam, the falcon,
With pearl otogot and coral stones
Glittering on his head,
Gathering himself like a blue tiger,
Donned Chinese clothing.
Elder Ala turned toward the people;
His ancestor Chylaba
Had left a great emblem there:
The tail of a parrot,
Worked in nine colors.
Seething like the Chinese,
Roaring in wrath,
He raised the khagan's battle cry.
Khan Manas's Akkula—
He urged it on with the khagan's cry.
Pearl otogot and coral stones,
Together with the khan of the Zungars,
All the great Chinese host,
When they saw elder Ala,
Thinking him one of their famous khans,
Urged without restraint.
They raised a great shout.
The Chinese people shouted together
And entered the tumult.
Fiercely urging the kula horse,
They entered the clamor.
Deceived by Almambet,
They entered the uproar.
Into a haze no man could see through,
They plunged amid the dust.
Alman broke away from the Chinese
And appeared among the Kalmaks.
When he reached the Kalmaks,
He raised the Kalmak urging cry.
Champion lion Almam
Sent his horse down the slope
And took up the cry of “Möndü!”
The Sarala beneath him—
He whipped it right and left,
And spurred from both sides.
He was a khan of the Black Kalmaks,
One who donned the khan's attire;
Wearing a Kaldai coat,
“Möndü, möndü, möndü!” he cried,
Urging them with the Möndü cry.
“Jaghy, jaghy, jaghy!” he cried,
Urging them with the Jaghy cry.
The many glittering Kalmaks
Now urged on together.
The Kalmaks came thundering,
Their hair streaming behind them.
While lion Almam lived,
