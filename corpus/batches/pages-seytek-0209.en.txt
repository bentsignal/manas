His singular face shone brightly;
He girded on a silver belt,
And the thundering falcon’s
Face beamed with sunlight.
He girded on a golden belt,
And the poor, far-striding falcon’s
Face shone with moonlight.
When you beheld the falcon warrior,
He had filled out like a spring-fed lake;
His imposing bearing,
His face of many moods,
Had become as it was before.
Such is Külchoro’s nature:
His worth sets him apart from other men;
Let alone the Jediger, he possesses the might
To crush the whole age.
When Külchoro saw his sister-in-law,
He rejoiced and took heart.
He donned his arrow-proof coat,
Prepared all his weapons,
And belted on his unsheathed sword.
Like a cloud about to rain again,
His wrath spread over his face.
Thick-wristed, stone-hearted,
An imposing giant built like an elephant,
Your falcon, it seems, had come to full strength
Among the Jediger of Jeti-Özön,
And was about to unleash a great calamity.
Unable to depart from the black ridge,
How could he stand without avenging himself
By spilling blood and severing heads?
Never sated though he drank blood,
He bore the aspect of a wrestler;
Broad-chested and wide-breasted,
He bore the aspect of an avenger.
He looked ready to strike down
Whoever confronted him, like a wild boar.
He looked ready to subdue
And capture all he encountered.
To all his beleaguered people,
He was a mighty support, great as a mountain.
Your primal ancestor was Tügöl Khan,
And a holy man bestowed his blessing upon you;
This Külchoro standing here—
Consider him your father, my son.
He is that boundless, towering mountain pass;
If you enter among the enemy, he is your counsel,
If you enter the fire, he is your ember,
If you seek wisdom, he is your stratagem,
If you enter combat, he is your spear—
He is the lion who will stand firm for you.
He teaches what is unknown,
That wise, mace-wielding elephant.
When you enter battle, he is your helmet;
When you charge against the enemy,
He is your spear, stout-shafted and sharp-tipped;
When you stoop to strike the foe,
He is your mace from Emil.
If you train him well, none like him can be found;
He is your falcon, a keen goshawk.
If you receive him, he will be your stronghold;
Be sure to take him to yourself.”
His mother Chürök began before Seytek
To raise the clamor,
And began to stir
An incurable calamity.
While Aichürök was still speaking,
Before she had finished her words,
Divinely guarded Külchoro
Prepared as though to flee.
Inspiration came to him from the unseen;
He stiffened like cured leather,
His shovel-like teeth gleaming white.
The blazing fire within him was boundless,
Its like had never been born;
He was a tempest sprung from mankind,
And no such man existed among warriors.
He pranced sideways like a racehorse,
And began to speak with laughter,
Gleaming in his mail coat.
