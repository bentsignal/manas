There is wisdom enough to enter the heart,
She has conceived a craving for tiger.
There is a woman, a fairy-born princess;
Look upon her form and judge:
Say, “This is tiger’s flesh,”
And give Ber­met, dear soul,
This pale walnut as large as a fist.
If she but smells the walnut,
It is far finer than lion’s flesh,
Far finer still than your tiger.
It will satisfy the wings of her craving.
In the desert of Midian,
Where the dragon perished,
Where it scattered its venom,
A single walnut tree sprang up.
If you heed these words,
And, as a sacred trust,
Give this walnut to Bermet,
Her frenzy will subside,
Her eyes will open wide,
Her madness will disappear,
And all her affairs will prosper.
Her people will enjoy surpassing fortune.
When this woman’s craving is fulfilled,
A dark-maned black panther,
Blood-drinking hero Kenen, will be born.
That giant’s name will be Kenen;
He will be the age’s sheltering fold,
The refuge of the Kyrgyz people,
The companion of all living souls.
Slaughter ninety beasts for his feast,
And name him Kenen.
That prince Kenen
Will land upright upon his feet.
Whoever hears his name
Will say, “Calamity has been born,”
And the hearts of envious foes
Will split apart;
Those he confronts will fall,
Those who meet him will be shattered.
“The heir of that mighty champion
Has now arrived,”
Every foe who sees him will cry.
Wherever he charges and lays waste,
All will be smashed and scattered;
Wherever he passes will turn to embers,
He will be a bullet against the foe.
A hero above all heroes,
The black earth will stalk those he hunts.
The gray wolf of the whole wide world,
The renowned hero Kenen he will be.
When Kenen is born,
His radiance will be known to every soul;
Mighty Aigan in Beijing
Will despair of blood and life.
The black Chinese soothsayer,
The great marksman Chongbilgich,
Will recoil in fear and lose hope.
He will let no bustard escape across the plain,
For he is that pale-gray falcon;
He will let no wolf escape in the mountains,
For he is that gray-pouched eagle.
When a day of grief befalls the troubled,
When a night comes that knows no dawn,
He will be the hero who unlocks
The fortune of the fettered marketplace;
He will be the lion who unbinds
The hands of those entangled in sorrow.
He is the hero who will restore their vigor.
God alone grants children;
Put every sorrow away.
In an age of ease and plenty,
Say, “Here my son has come,”
And name him Kenen.
If that child remains alive,
Will he sink when cast into a river?
Will he burn when cast into fire?
Before the insolent, unruly foe,
Would he ever live without wreaking ruin?
His might will reach through many an age,
