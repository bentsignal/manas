We said, ‘Where could calamity be?’
While our warrior sultan lived,
When bored, we hunted deer;
When not, we poor souls
Played ordo for stakes amid loud rejoicing,
Played orompoy amid merry clamor,
Making them say, ‘Ah, the valiant Kyrgyz!’
We raised an uproar against every foe,
Serving as a stronghold that spared no life.
To those who knew and those who saw,
To those who heard and understood,
To any who merely narrowed their eyes at us,
And to the enemy who overran us,
We brought sheer affliction.
We were a broad, mottled mountain range
No living creature could cross.
We were the heroic Kyrgyz people,
Yielding to no one in all the world.
The sultan and stronghold have left the Kyrgyz,
The hero has left the people of the age,
The lion blessed by Khidr has departed.
Our glasslike eyes stare wildly;
Parted from our precious lion,
The nation wails in grief.
Bereft of our dear hero,
With no tail left to swish,
We stand like wild asses.
With no feet to stride forward,
We stand like snakes.
Will we become mounts
That do not even flick their ears as they walk?
When the enemy surrounds us,
Unable to charge and summon courage,
Will we become as the dead?
Will we cease to encamp
At Kan-Jailak, where the people spread?
Heeding no one’s word,
Unable to bear one another,
Will we cease to be a people?
Will this immense, incurable affliction
Leave us in turmoil?
Will painful misery and great conflict
Leave us in a single storm of chaos?
Bereft of our warrior lion,
We have fallen into disaster.
Our eyes dim and wander—
We have become blind.”—
So the nation clamored,
Yellowing like straw,
Amazed at the utter vanity
Of this battered world.
Every living creature
The Creator brought forth and set in motion;
All who knew, saw, or perceived,
The supreme decree of the Creator
Directed toward a single pit.
By His decree He fashioned
The eighteen thousand worlds.
Every living creature
Must cross this ominous black earth.
Oh Ala-Too, whose foothills are gold,
If only the Kyrgyz could dwell there forever!
If only our doomed prince Semetey,
His true kinsman Külchoro,
Poor Bakai and Kanykei,
And the noble lord’s wife Aichürök—
Alas, deceitful world!—
If only these, who should never die,
Had spines of iron!”
The whole nation cried out and wept,
Tormenting their fragile lives,
Unable to endure, lamenting,
Astonished that this exalted, painful world
Was empty at its core,
And marveling at it.
The great lion stood unmoving—
“Father, we have parted forever, dear lion!”—
The doomed hero stood unmoving.
The five valiant heroes united
And began their journey to the land of no return.
