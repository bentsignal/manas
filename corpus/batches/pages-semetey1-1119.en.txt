They say he gave seven thousand—a thousand yearling mares—
For slaughter.
They say he gave lumps of sugar large as lying camels and cows
For them to crack apart.
They say he gave the wood of seven thousand camels
For them to burn.
Saying it was Kökötöy's feast,
If you had seen the Shibee people,
Saying it was a feast for the great gathering,
They say they camped against the foothills.
There was yet another spectacle:
A famed champion who came from Beijing,
Whose form differed from mortal men,
The renowned Kalcha lion.
With a cotton sash and broad boots,
Kongurbay, khan of the monks,
Moved like a dragon;
Sixty nobles were his younger brothers.
Of all who came from Beijing of Kakan-Chyn,
Who among them surpassed the Kalcha?
Six thousand Chinese and countless kalday
Were among the great khans of Beijing.
Their renowned khan Kongurbay,
A servant more skillful than an intriguer,
Who openly knew the enemy's stratagems,
Was a mighty servant.
Do not ask of Kongurbay's station:
Upon his head was his attire,
Coral plumes and pearl stones,
And among his company mingled the khan of the Zungars.
His guard spread grandly;
A thousand souls came in his host.
Wearing their mail,
How many heroes came?
Among them,
The renowned one was Kongurbay,
Kongurbay, a khan known to the people.
Upon his coral headdress,
A parrot's tail
Was adorned in ninety ways.
He made the racehorse Algara buck,
Made its golden reins jerk.
Kongurbay, son of Alööke,
Was a famed man born full of cunning.
A war banner floated in his hand;
All manner of gear that could cut stone
Flashed upon his wrist.
Dust rose from his passage,
And souls fled at his sound.
Have you heard the tale
Of glory rising from the hearth?
From the entire Chinese people,
A mighty one named Kongurbay arose.
His human form was dreadful to behold;
Among Alööke's Kalcha people,
No mortal dared face him.
Beneath him was the racehorse Algara,
A creature surpassing other beasts.
If we aroused his wrath,
Kongurbay, son of Alööke,
Had the strength to slaughter alone
All the Muslims gathered here.
The nobles beside him
Separated into distinct groups;
Looking at a single noble,
He seemed like a thousand men.
The gathered people who saw them
Felt their spirits badly shaken.
Champions of myriad forms
Streamed behind him.
Forty princes who served
As Kongurbay's attendants
Had come together with him.
Koshoy, Töshtük, and the seasoned giants
Were left utterly astonished.
Kongurbay, son of Alööke,
Had immeasurable prowess;
He was the finest champion of them all.
