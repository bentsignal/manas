Just as they were about to surge over them,
Upon one flank of the Jediger,
About to reach their root together,
About to slaughter their champions,
And take their daughters as tribute,
When they had drawn near the city,
Tümönbay, son of Tülkü,
Leading many formations of troops,
Mounted a black-blazed argamak
And strained for battle,
Praying to the Almighty,
His wrapped turban wound around him.
The brass horn shrilled,
The drum boomed,
The surnai clattered,
And spear points flashed.
Tümönbay, son of Tülkü,
Raised the patterned spear
And faced Tölöbay,
Making his argamak prance,
His blue skullcap perched high.
Reckless and pounding in the chest,
Proud and thundering in the lungs,
Weak in command but himself a hero,
He faced Tölöbay;
That was where slaughter began.
Afghan and Jediger
Met face to face
And fought beyond mortal measure.
Spears struck and horses died;
Brave men quickly died.
They stood slaughtering one another
And fought with terrible fury.
Straining against one another,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
They fought with crashing blows.
Mounting stallions from the tethering line,
Taking poles up from the ground,
Shouting, “Japyrim!” as they fought—
Now it became a terrible battle.
They fought with spears;
Those who came especially close
Seized each other by the collar.
Keeping the battle in mind,
They fought in utter confusion;
When one looked, the eye could distinguish nothing.
The tumultuous battle grew immense,
And no one could tell which side was which.
Many more stood devising plans
And pondering words.
“Ah, wicked Aychürök,
Until she grew old she took no husband;
She understood none of the words spoken,
And roamed without marrying a man;
She never ceased stirring corruption!”
Many more went about reviling her.
Many more stood brandishing spears
And swaggering.
Saying, “She truly is shameless,”
The great host and Aychürök—
Many more fought over her.
“Since it was the khan's command,
How could we refuse to fight?”
So saying and exhorting one another,
Many more strove in battle.
Black and blue, born of the same mother,
Sparing not their mortal lives,
Many more exchanged fire.
“If we die, so be it—we trust in fate,”
So saying and fighting,
Many more exchanged blows.
Those without spears
Found other means in great numbers;
With whips, one another
Many more struck.
Bracing knee against knee and driving forward,
Many more clashed.
