Külchoro, son of Almambet,
We kicked together like colt and foal,
Quarreling as we nursed at the breast;
We joined when no bigger than a thumb,
United when no bigger than a louse.
Whatever days befell us,
We shared the greatest joys
And slept in the same bed.
When I placed him before me, he brought good fortune;
When I led him, he was like countless troops,
My proven friend, dear as life itself.
From my lionhearted gray-maned warrior,
The hero who departed for the other world,
Külchoro remained to me as an inheritance;
Now he too has set out upon the journey.
Whenever battle fever seized him,
He shot forth like a bullet-snake;
Before the bloodthirsty hero’s courage,
The Kara-Kytai and the Shibee people
All halted together, leaving none behind.
He was a blazing red ember;
Külchoro, upheld by fortune,
Was a steel bullet against the foe.
He was a cap of black steel,
The glory of the countless Kyrgyz.
When a grievous enemy pressed hard,
When calamity struck with force,
He was a black-burning fire.
He was a cap of red steel,
The morning-star glory of these Kyrgyz.
When an encircling enemy overwhelmed us,
He was a fire burning crimson.
Now cruel death has entered his thoughts too;
He too is departing
For the land from which none return.
My heroic mother Kanykei,
Until the five Ram stars had set,
Nursed me through five watches of the road;
Until the six Morning Stars had set,
She circled me and fed me.
She emptied her stone-hard breasts
And widened her narrow womb.
She suffered anguish for my sake,
Fleeing Talas and wandering,
Her soles blistering on the stones,
Following the banks of great Keng-Kol,
Saying, “He is the nephew left by my prince,”
She fled away for my sake.
Calling her father Karakhan but a corpse,
Calling it a plain where no lark flies,
Saying, “He is the child who bears my blood,”
She made her grieving head a banner,
Put her dear life to the test,
Fled and wandered, hiding her secret;
Calling it a plain where no soul can live,
Saying, “I will sacrifice my poor life
For my lone child, solitary as a crow.”
My dear mother Kanykei,
Purest of those in flowing gowns,
Foremost of women in white headscarves,
Most graceful of those with long hair,
Noblest of womankind,
Beauty among the dark-complexioned,
Greatest of all created souls,
Fruit of the fearsome desert,
Mother of me, Semetey.
My radiant lady, my sanctuary,
The wife of renowned Manas,
My mother who gave me shelter.
By the command of God the Creator,
My steadfast mother has gone to see
The true abode from which none return;
My caravan-leading mother has gone to see it.
My guardian above my head,
My mother Kanykei has set out upon the journey.
United when we were no bigger than lice,
At the age appointed for me,
With no battle cry and no public tidings,
With no mullah to say “Amen,”
When our fathers first pledged us in marriage,
