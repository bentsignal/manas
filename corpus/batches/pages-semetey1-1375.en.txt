They loaded the baggage and bustled about,
Bounding and bunching like roe deer,
Drawing the ropes and loading the packs;
Before one could say this or that,
In the blink of an eye,
They loaded the ammunition stored in saddlebags,
Loaded up a great wealth of goods,
And for aged Lord Bakai
Carved rump meat and served him mane-fat,
Dipped bread in fat and gave it to him.
Lion Bakai and Semetey,
With the two companions who followed them,
Set out and traveled on their journey,
Riding hard and covering much ground.
When the sun was sinking low,
At the dark pass of Kapka-Tash,
They halted at Kapka-Tash.
When they stopped, hero Bakai
Lamented his grief from long ago!
His white beard had grown sparse,
And the tears flowing from his two eyes
Poured down over his chest:
“When my noble Manas was alive,
We wore the flanks of our horses lean,
Roaming the four corners
Of the encircling world,
While my lord was alive.
In Beijing of the Kakanchin,
My lord Almam, the leopard, was there;
When I reconnoitered his road,
From its nobles and its khan,
From the great city of Beijing,
When we rode out to reconnoiter the road,
When we went to demand an answer,
Indeed, Almam and I
Stayed at Issyk-Köl.
When we faced and besieged
Beijing, which lay tightly shut,
We lived in this world
Free by our own will for a time.
Almam the formidable and I
Stayed in this very place.
At that time dusk came and the sun set;
The spur of that mountain over there,
The nose of the gray ridge,
Was a slope with a spring.
The shrine that the leopard Almam named
Was called Boz-Uchuk Shrine!
On that broad slope itself
The people pitched their camp;
The gray hill on this side
Was the place where your uncle,
Hero Bakai, kept watch.
My land remains, but my people are gone.
Now that my beard is white,
Now that my journey nears its end,
Parted from those valiant men,
I alone remain alive in this filthy world.
I have none of my own age, none my equal,
No hero to ride beside me!
My dear children,
This was a place where a holy father lay;
They called it Boz-Uchuk-Ata.
Boz-Uchuk is a noble shrine—
Let us camp at Boz-Uchuk!
Let us survey the lay of the land;
O calamity-stricken orphans,
Let us gauge the length of the road!
Clearing enemies and traveling the road,
Let us scout that Beijing!
Let us reach the khan of the Kakanchin,
The teeming city of Beijing,
Measuring the land as we go.
It has nobles, it has giants,
It has peoples of many kinds;
And among those people
There is the blue-mottled, gray-bearded great Manchu,
Hero Kongurbai, famed and mighty.
