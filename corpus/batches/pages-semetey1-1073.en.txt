The iron no bullet pierced,
The weapon Bölök had made,
He placed again
Over Külchoro's kidney.
There was a coat made as a short jacket;
Its lower edge reached
Only to his navel
When he put that coat on.
Letting its skirt fall upward,
He dressed in the bulletproof coat;
When told he was going to Toltoy,
Without a frown or knitted brow,
The boy laughed openly in joy,
Proudly dressing in
The splendid coat no bullet pierced.
When told he was going to Kozho,
Without a frown or knitted brow,
Without stirring or grieving,
He smiled openly in joy.
Semetey, the prince's son,
Revealing his princely nature,
Dressed the boy in at least
Twelve kinds of splendid things
No common mortal wore,
So that Choro, son of Almambet,
Would not be struck by a stray bullet,
And death would not reach him in vain.
Buttoning up his whole body,
Armoring every rounded surface
With blue iron,
The young prince Semetey
Showed his heroism
And dressed Külchoro
In every kind of battle gear,
Then stood grieving, downcast.
The Gray Purse left as a gift
By Manas, gone to Judgment Day,
He had Külchoro gird around himself;
Then the bold young lad—
To think of him made the heart burn.
He dressed Külchoro
In Akolpok left by Manas.
When blood-taking Manas lived,
The road on which he struck Kalday had closed;
Unable to keep looking at the boy,
The only son of Manas
Felt smoke sting his nostrils.
Your hero, called a warrior prince,
Looked upon the boy;
His black eyes, like a saker falcon's,
Filled with tears without blinking.
He raged and groaned,
Smoke stinging his nostrils;
The leopard, son of Manas,
Broke down and wept aloud:
"He was only a boy of twelve,
A soul still learning to think.
He has not yet reached fifteen;
The taste of Kanykey's breast milk
Has not yet left his palate.
His shoulder bones have not yet hardened;
His time has not yet come
To match himself against Toltoy of the Zhediger.
His ribs have not yet filled out;
His time has not yet come
To grapple with Toltoy of the Zhediger."
For the boy who had never seen an enemy,
When he rode toward the foe,
A heavy rule fell upon his thoughts.
Your tiger could not bear to watch.
When the blessed boy slung it on,
It was the mirror of his moonlike shoulder;
When sent against a clashing enemy,
It was the solace of his entrusted soul.
When wielded in play, it killed;
When wielded in earnest, it slaughtered.
No one struck by its bullet escaped capture;
No one who toyed with it escaped alive.
