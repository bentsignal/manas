The poor prince grew eager,
And appealed to the Almighty.
He gripped the falcon on his arm
And drew it close against his side;
He touched Buurul beneath him
With his heel beneath the foreleg.
The young prince made Buurul
Step forward with long, rolling strides,
And trot with a rocking gait;
He sat holding the bird at an angle,
Bringing it into the proper position,
And made the falcon quiver,
Ready to remove its hood.
The tiger who loomed like a mountain
Prepared to cast it headlong,
And made the falcon ready.
Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
These two legacies left by bygone lions,
Came racing up together,
With Kulchoro crying urgently;
The two of them arrived.
Tall and broad-shouldered,
Kulchoro, son of Almambet,
Both a strongman and a lion,
Began speaking these words
To his elder kinsman:
“Kulchoro and Kanchoro—
We two were twin boys,
And souls who became companions to you.
If we choro died,
We were sons worth no more than dog hide;
While we remained alive,
We were servants worth no more than sheepskin.
The brave Semetey, who girded his waist,
Was born together
With our own selves.
Your dear mother Kanykey,
Who shared the bloodline of Manas, now gone beyond,
Came forth wounded from her womb;
Look here, tiger—
When were we ever sons of Manas?
My poor elder kinsman, hear my words.
Though we are servants, if we grow unruly,
While you, my khan and elder, are here,
And rage and swallow our anger,
If we set the bird upon the swan
And drink our fill of joy,
While our tiger elder is present,
Let us cough loudly together
And raise our downcast spirits!
When you mounted a horse, you mounted indeed,
My elder, Prince Semetey—
What horns have you suddenly grown?
Your Akshunkar should alight
Upon the hands of these two;
If you fly the falcon alone,
You would need two heads!”
While the boys spoke in this way,
The world lay level around them,
And the sea spread out like this;
When one looked into its depths,
It seemed broader than the face of the earth.
He could not say of the falcon on his hand,
“I will not give it,”
Nor turn away the brave man’s wish;
The famous heir of Manas
Handed over the falcon.
Eager to fly it at the swan,
Kulchoro took it,
Steadied all his thoughts,
And settled the white falcon on his hand.
Taking the falcon, Kulchoro
Fastened and secured it again,
Attached Akshunkar’s long leash
To the belt around his waist,
Hurried Semetey onward,
Though he did not know the proper way to the contest,
