A man whose weight was like a mountain.
He now came to bear the name Kenen,
The hero now became his people’s fortress.
He now mounted Kertulpar,
And as a majestic khan,
He ruled the people.
While matters stood thus,
Janaaly and Karach were khans,
The two had been born as twins,
They stirred the people and ruined nations,
Those accursed madmen, blazing until death,
From ancient times had borne
Enmity toward the fierce leopard.
From those khans Janaaly and Karach,
The two giants Ebegey and Kögöy
Were born beyond all reckoning.
Beyond Bukhara and Sharip,
This side of ancient Chambyl,
Lay Belendi’s Sary-Bel,
And they were born in Sary-Bel.
The two giants Ebegey and Kögöy
Recently heard tidings from caravans,
They heard that Semetey was dead, and his wretched son
Seytek remained,
Black-headed and cloven-footed,
Led about by the hero Joltoy,
Like so much livestock, they heard,
So they advanced to wage war,
To avenge an ancient grudge,
Shrouding themselves as in mourning,
And gathering again like clouds
About to pour a violent rain,
Ebegey and Kögöy thirsted for blood,
The fools raged,
The murderous lions sought battle,
And the black earth grew hard beneath them.
They roared and thundered,
And loaded their great guns,
Oh, my child, the black earth, unable to overturn,
Was held firm by its massive weight.
Their raven eyes blazed,
They had their fine steeds tethered,
Terrifying all they threatened,
And had the racehorses saddled,
Then the two giants
Took eighty warriors from Kambyl,
Darkening with rage,
Rain falling from their fury,
Water streaming from their march,
An uproar rising at their tidings,
They prepared horses to mount with a bound,
And coats to don when refreshed,
They laid in abundant ammunition,
And had their weapons packed,
They loaded a red camel,
That could travel forty days without water,
And never once grow faint with weariness,
The equal of the swiftest riding camel,
No other beast like it existed,
Hook-nosed, with a back like a trap,
They mounted their horses in ordered ranks,
And fitted handsome harness,
They draped themselves in warriors’ coats,
The two lions Ebegey and Kögöy
Had long been making ready.
The two villains Ebegey and Kögöy
Set out to ravage the people,
Traveling for many days,
They reached great Khotan beyond Kashgar,
Made great Khotan their base,
Skirted broad Kashgar,
Encircled the Six Cities,
Watched the hill country closely,
And once again destroyed
All the fine horses of the valleys,
Brave Seytek was the one they sought,
Their hearts black as soot,
They came straight toward him.
Let us leave this matter here,
And of your heroic elder Akjoltoy,
Let us now begin the tale.
The well-built racehorse Kulunsur
Was his tethered mount,
While he lived as a hero on the people’s frontier,
In the middle of the night
He awoke with a start,
Tossing and turning anxiously,
And saddled Kulunsur,
“It has now been seven days,
And seven nights,
But forlorn Kulunsur,
My child, will not stand when I tether him,
And will not graze when I release him,
He sighs and gazes inward.
He neighs and seems to sob,
This frisking mad creature
Pounds the black earth.
