Say he has camped at Üch-Karkyra;
If Kongurbai is a hero,
Let him not miss this feast of mine,
Nor, by failing to come,
Bring harsh calamity upon his own head.
Say he must come to this feast of mine,
And see these festivities.
If he does not come to this feast of mine,
If he does not see these festivities,
Say he will see the bloodthirsty Burut at the head
Of a scene of devastation!
When you travel farther on,
There is the giant called Döödür Alp,
Tiken Alp and Mungush Alp,
And the great wrestler Tükübay,
Grim of face and strong of venom,
Who lays his opponents flat.
Tell all these giants.”
Having spoken and arranged it thus, Bokmurun
Had him mount Maaniker,
Had him don a mail coat,
And strike the Koroson fire-steel,
Inviting every one,
Infidel and Muslim alike.
Kökötöy's many royal yurts
Were raised side by side in rows.
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Made festivities for the ages.
When the great encampment
Of wealthy Khan Kökötöy settled,
When the people gathered and filled it,
I saw Manas's bearing.
I saw the fierce, formidable might
By which Manas surpassed all others.
I saw Manas stand as a barrier
Protecting the Muslim people.
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Made festivities for the people;
Exulting that he would hold a feast,
He cast the world into turmoil.
Traveling along the road
My father, brave Bagysh, had ridden,
When he went to Karkyra
And the people were counted,
He had an army of twelve thousand.
Zhортуулчу, riding Zhooruker,
Came down along the road
With many armies spread across the land.
Zhügörü, khan of Koton,
Also came in a dense mass,
Dust flying to the sky.
Akun Khan, khan of the Afghans,
Came with tasseled spears fluttering,
His great fur cap swaying.
The Afghans came along the road
With an army of twelve thousand.
Dust came from the lands between;
As if that were not enough,
They also came from India.
With resident sheikhs and blue-clad khojas,
From prominent Kokand and Margalan,
His thoroughbred snorting,
His mottled breast heaving,
Came Synzhybek, fork-bearded lord of Andizhan.
They divided into varied companies,
And looked grim in appearance.
Those who came had no sense;
The white-nosed men of Andizhan,
Their horses all stallions,
Had no cruppers at all.
