Hardened together with its bark,
Deliberately hammered and twisted,
Its crooked parts shaped by filing,
Its bent places straightened,
The master smith Bolokbay
Had repaired the poor thing.
He had it bound with sinew;
Leaving no hollow place,
He wrapped it with gold.
So it would not slip from the hand,
He lacquered its grip
And covered it with elephant hide.
When leveled and thrust at the enemy,
It glowed red as an ember;
When leveled and brandished at the foe,
A man's soul rejoiced.
When the patterned spear struck,
It bent without breaking.
So precious was it:
Its exterior was covered in hide;
So a spear stroke would not cut it,
He wrapped it with gold.
Its tip was wolf-tongue steel;
It felled whomever it was leveled at.
"Let the place it strikes not remain whole;
Let him burn and die;
Let an untimely death reach him,"
They tempered it with albar steel.
It was of hollow-forged steel,
Raised high in massive form,
And crushed a charging enemy
To the earth like a hare.
Taking the patterned spear and arming himself,
Your foreign-born lord made ready.
Upon his head he wore
A black cap of blue lynx.
Do not ask the valiant man's nature:
The direction he faced became red fire.
Semetey was an ocean;
His fortunes were complete.
He was the hero born greater
Than his generous father Manas.
This was when he would battle
The Jediger who had engulfed them.
The Akkolpok upon him—
When slaves seized its silk,
When the horde was destroyed then,
And it fell into the hands
Of a man clad in gray thread,
These dogs had grown in silk
And put the silk on themselves.
Semetey destroyed their horde
And took back Akkolpok
From the scoundrels' hands.
She twisted its filled places;
Your dear mother Kanykei
And skillfully repaired it all.
She had obtained the silk before,
Silk mixed with fine fiber,
And had its weave made of gold;
She patched what was torn
And your dear mother repaired
Everything that was damaged.
She had the unsuitable parts removed
And plates of steel
Poured into its center.
The jaws of tongs could not grip it;
The edge of Arkesken could not cut it.
No ember passed it when red willow burned;
No spear pierced it in a joust,
And no short blade cut its weave.
Its inside was silk and atlas,
Its collar gold and sleeves brass.
Wearing Akkolpok, he appeared—
The heroic lord, son of Manas;
The time for slaughter had come!

Of many splendid ornaments,
