This bloodthirsty man has gone mad.”
They muttered without speaking openly;
They grumbled to one another.
When Manas stood there snorting,
When the bloodthirsty man went forth in fury,
At Kökötöy's feast,
Before the great assembly,
On the banks of Karkyra,
Just as he entered
Khan Manas's camp,
Kanykei with her forty young wives
Led the forty young wives
Out of the camp.
Her rump showed white as a wild ewe's;
Her white breasts swayed.
With forty young wives following her,
She spoke softly and sadly
To the prince who was raging,
And would not let him go on Akkula.
His nose bulged like a leather flask;
As he raged and thrashed,
Twelve companions struggled
To hold Manas back.
They exchanged muffled words
And whispered among themselves,
Thinking with dread,
“This bloodthirsty man who kills his wife—
How could he spare us?”
They would not release the bloodthirsty man,
But stepped between them.
Shimmering in garments of yellow gold,
With forty young wives following her,
She came gleaming before him.
She spoke to the blue-maned lion,
Saying openly:
“Here are the teeming multitudes.
You have grown furious;
Though I am only a woman, hear me.
Here I am—spill my blood.
While the people stand here,
I have something fitting to say.
If you spill my blood,
My bloodthirsty lord, I have words for you.
Knowing your elder would wear it,
I lined its inside with brocade.
For six years without rest, I worked it with my teeth.
While the maidens and young wives embroidered it,
I had the places I disliked unstitched.
Plate after plate of steel
I had poured into its midst.
I made it with all my heart.
Weeping in supplication to the one God,
From the very beginning, wretched as I was,
I dedicated it to my elder.
I have endless words to tell.
If I made the kandagai wider,
I feared he would unleash a massacre;
I feared my bloodthirsty lord would take my head.
That was the measure of my fear.
I kept measuring its dimensions:
Six spans and four finger-widths
From the end of the cuff,
And from the rim of the fat rump,
I have a clasp joining it shut.
I have many words to tell.
Many cares cannot fit within my heart;
The tears from my eyes spread in ten directions.
Almambet and Chubak, whose names rhyme,
Let them take opposing sides and pull.”
Your furious bloodthirsty man
Grabbed the black-bladed whip,
Seized it and turned it around.
The towering leopard
Pulled it toward the middle;
With a ripping sound, the trousers tore,
Their folds came loose and opened.
