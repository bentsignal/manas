Kökötöy's Maaniker,
A swift horse surpassing a tulpar,
For a dead man's memorial feast
That stirred all the multitude—
The cause of Bokmurun's commotion.
Ah, the creature Maaniker,
A peerless one born among horses,
His form unlike other livestock.
He raced with flying birds;
The belly flesh from which fat is taken
Drew back, sleek,
And clung to his bones.
He did not panic at a battle cry;
In the darkness of the moon,
He never misplaced a single step.
He sprang apart at the sound of tumult;
Though there were countless hosts,
His qualities as a tulpar showed.
When he passed, the black earth
Split apart with a groan.
Were he not called a creature with feet,
He seemed to possess wings.
Unless his reins were held within a handspan,
He would not be taken by a determined foe;
Unless some wretched mishap struck,
He would not let fire touch his tail.
Unless a shot pierced his chest,
Unless a bullet went astray,
He would not let his rider fall to the foe.
The consummate swift horse Maaniker—
The wind could not catch his trot;
No beast could match his stride.
Unless his reins were gathered tight,
He shimmered like a flying bird,
And across deserts forty days wide
Shimmered like a cloud.
Though the desert were forty days wide,
He never once drank water.
The farther he ran, the longer his stride;
His eyes reddened like a spirit's.
Kökötöy's Maaniker—
A primeval dark-bay racehorse,
With sieve-like lungs and copper wings.
He never slipped from a height,
And was in no way inferior
To an ibex stepping upon rock.
Going to summon the peoples,
Ah, my colt, young Aydar,
Should you meet a desolate desert,
Do not let yourself race across it.
Do not wholly loosen his mouth,
Lest you fly over his back!
Take six swift horses;
Take a letter broad as a palm.
Travel the horse-road,
And roam the whole wide world,
Giving the news.
I shall tell you the peoples' order;
Hey, young Aydar, come here.
Behind that hunched mountain,
On the sun-facing brow,
At the lower foot of the pass,
This side of Old Turpan,
Beyond Wide Barbar,
Along the Lop River,
Where camels calve in the sand,
Who yield their companions to no one,
Are never sated by begging and consuming,
And never relinquish their greed,
Who, driven in darkness,
Prowl like lions—
With red whips and blue spears,
