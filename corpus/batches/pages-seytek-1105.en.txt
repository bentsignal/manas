The Burut are not a people to trifle with,
That looming mottled ridge—
Is no toy hill to cross for sport,
Nor are they ones who noisily stir up quarrels,
They are not the people of Manchu,
This is no land for casual scouting,
They are not a people to face unprepared!
Great nation of forty-tribed China,
Gather your armored khans,
Do not let the common people be disgraced,
Gather all your strongest men,
Speaking softly, he gave firm counsel,
Your renowned champion,
Chongbilgich spoke and offered advice,
The reason he began to speak was this:
He was both a wrestler and a lion,
A hero exceptionally skilled
In all the stratagems of war.
Now Chongbilgich spoke as follows:
“Let your armored strongmen come forth,
From among the bald ones girded with stones,
Let your heroes who defend the nation come forth.
When the black musket cracks,
Let the bold man who does not turn in flight,
Who does not dwell on death,
The capable risk-taker, come forth.
Against the vast, teeming host,
Let your finest man, who charges unchecked, come forth!
When the people are in turmoil,
Advancing like a raging fire,
When shouting and leveling the spear,
Let your champion of honor come forth,
Let your lion, a master of wisdom, come forth.
If you wrestle him, that Kyrgyz is the hero,
Seytek, a hero born of heroes,
If you grapple, that lion is the one,
Who satisfies his desire in combat,
That boundless hero.
When he thrusts his spear, hearts faint,
Such are the white-capped Kyrgyz people.
They whirl their blue-mottled shields,
At the word of strife, at the word of foe,
They drink deep yet never have their fill of blood,
Their hems are broad and their road lies open,
Such are the people called the heroic Kyrgyz.
The cuckoo calls in swelling song,
From mountain to mountain the roe deer cries,
On this side the waters roar,
Across the river the nightingale sings,
No foe can withstand them face-to-face,
These accursed Burut people
Never camp twice in one place,
But halt in the cool mountains.
Their steeds, huge as mountains and camels,
Graze hillocks as large as mountains.
If you attack, there stand the lofty mountains,
If you fight, there stand the Kyrgyz people.”
Thus spoke Chongbilgich,
Swift on foot and masterful on horseback,
Unequaled and deft with the spear,
An outstanding and supreme wrestler,
The man who comes forth when China faces ruin,
Whose road lies open when he scouts,
Whose hand is free when he enters battle.
He cannot be restrained where fury reigns,
When he draws his bow in darkness,
He will not miss even a hair’s breadth.
A marksman whenever he takes aim,
From father back through all his forebears
He inherited the gift of marksmanship.
When his wrath is roused,
He reaps every enemy he encounters.
A servant favored by the fortune
Of Beijing, beyond the reach of any deluge.
When Chongbilgich had spoken thus,
How could the Chinese warriors fail to assemble,
When the great lord himself stood before them,
How could the glory of the people be afraid?
The prowess of great Kite and Bilgich
Was plainly revealed,
