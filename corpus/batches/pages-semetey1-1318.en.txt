I long to wage a true holy war,
Strike their horses in a swift raid,
And drive them back this way!
Upon that people, numerous as ants,
I long to descend upon their heads.
Kongurbai is their prince;
I long to drink his blood!
Against the teeming Chinese khagan,
I long to unleash utter turmoil.
Against the black-hearted infidel,
I long to exchange blows once.
The enemy I shall grapple is Kakan-Chyn;
I long to return having taken revenge.
If my death comes from God,
I long to die while fighting!
Some people tell tales;
Who knows whether they are false or true?
That Beijing, whence those who go do not return,
They call a place of calamity and hardship.
With my uncle as commander,
I long to enter that Beijing.
The countless dark livestock gathered as tribute—
I long to pour down upon them headlong.
If the one God grants good fortune,
I long to ride forth and enter against the foe!
Dearer than a child was
This Külchoro standing here;
Against the teeming Black Chinese,
I long to unleash him!
Sated with flowing blood,
Standing and fighting without fleeing,
Of the swaggering Black Chinese,
I long to break apart many cities.
If I do not take revenge, this wretched soul
Would rather die than go on living!
They have Kalmaks and Shivei,
And peoples of many kinds;
Among those peoples
Are giants of many kinds.
Fighting them satisfies one's longing;
Clashing with them sets the soul aflame.
They themselves follow the Chinese faith;
Kongurbai is khan of the Kechil.
However mighty that infidel may be,
He is our enemy to the core.
From the blustering infidel,
Let us shake the bullets from his quiver,
And test ourselves in combat.
Let us shout and thrust our spears,
And satisfy every battle hunger.
Do not mortals destined to die, die?
The decree God has written—
Will this entrusted, barren soul not see it?
When the Almighty gives the command,
Saying, ‘Your entrusted life is required,’
When the angel Azrael
Truly extends his five fingers,
The speaking tongue will be bound;
From this seven-day world,
What soul can flee and escape?
Will the dug grave not receive us?
Will the black earth not cover us?
The foe we shall grapple is Kakan-Chyn;
We shall somehow find his weakness,
Our lion father who passed into Judgment Day—
We shall avenge him by destroying them.
Even if almighty God has decreed
Our fate in Beijing,
We shall bring fierce turmoil there.
The road to Beijing divides five ways;
The Kalcha who has cut down every hero
Is himself a peerless warrior.
If I do not ceaselessly scout the road,
If I do not go to demand the blood-price
Of my mountain-like godfather,
May I sink away as though never born;
Rather than go on living feebly,
Let me die instead.
The infidel son of Kaldai,
Hero Khan Kongurbai—
