Pouring out his wrath, he bristled,
I have long awaited our clash.
Now that my mortal enemy,
Who would tear out lungs and seize gall, has come,
How could I spare my life!
Ever since I became Külchoro,
I have subdued the whole world,
Driving, slaughtering, and despoiling it.
I donned armor no bullet could pierce,
I slaughtered a thousand men like you;
Where, then, is my death?!
At last, Sarybai,
I have met you in your fury.
Let us make our combat supreme,
Fighting without mercy,
Let us cry out and thrust our spears!
Let us stake our knuckle-sized heads,
And swallow lives as great as mountains,
Roaming freely over the pasture,
And, when we remember, drain each other's lives.
Let us test the champions,
Let us intensify the strife.
Let us make our fame manifest,
And spread the conflict far.
You began the campaign and war,
In which the people were massacred and died;
You have grown fond of such deeds!
Now behold valor and heroism,
Behold such a feat.
Let our sovereign souls blaze,
Let us rouse all our strength,
And wage war once more!
Our champions and our khans,
As though struck by slaughter,
Have all been laid waste.
Our heroes who boasted of valor—
May our mighty backs be broken.
What are turmoil and strife?
If you wish, behold them here!”
So saying, Külchoro continued:
“I shall spill your blood like water.
I shall bring your life to a clean end,
Cutting off your head like this,
And offer up your young life.
Sarybai, son of the ogre,
Why are you so wildly enraged?
Why rage and stir up strife,
When we have not carried off your land,
Nor scattered your treasure,
Nor raised an uproar in war,
Nor overrun your teeming people?
As though we had drained your beloved,
As though we had reaped your grain unripe,
As though we had plundered the domains
Of seven generations of ogre chieftains!
Why do you make trouble,
Descendant of warriors and heroes,
Why do you stand against us?
Is Talas such an easy land
That you might slaughter it,
Take it as spoil,
And dig out its roots before departing?
No migration of yours crossed the Seven Valleys,
You have no vengeance from seventy forefathers.
Do you possess the might, Sarybai,
To bring utter calamity?
Do you have the fury, Sarybai,
To fight until one of us is ended?
Did we tether mares in your valley,
Or settle upon your people’s land?
Did we massacre and despoil your village,
That you should glower so?
Did we slaughter your people,
That you should swagger and provoke strife?
If you are the hero Sarybai,
And I am the hero Külchoro,
A hero was the foe I sought;
What I asked of God,
What I prayed for, was you:
