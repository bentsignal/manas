He circled back, hovering,
And drew near the Pole Star.
From the falcon once carried
By his father Manas, the gray wolf,
A bird had been born as a legacy held in trust;
Allah ordained that it should come
To the young champion, the noble lord’s son.
When loosed, that champion was his treasure,
The treasure left as a gift
By valiant Manas, the gray wolf!
It had utterly torn apart
The breast and chest,
And feasted to its heart’s content
Upon the dark flesh of the breast.
Your noble Akshumkar
Flashed beneath the moon in the sky,
Lifting the wild duck,
And came down from the sky in triumph,
Crying out loudly!
His eyes beheld the falcon,
The accursed wild duck
That Akshumkar had snatched and brought;
His uncle, old Bakai,
Saw it coming with his own eyes!
“My beloved Akshumkar,
May aged Bakai circle in devotion
Around a champion like you!
Merely seeing you leaves my heart content;
Apart from you, my champion,
I possess no treasure—
From your eyes like black currants,
From you who caught the wild duck!
Now that I, Bakai, am old,
My beloved Akshumkar,
May I be your sacrifice!”
Rejoicing, Khan Bakai
Swiftly invoked Babedin,
Promised a sheep to Koroson,
And before Allah Most High, the One,
Also spoke the vow of Aituyak.
All his longing subsided;
Your uncle and Semetey
Had their eyes opened wide as umach,
And all their sorrow subsided.
Near the forked pass,
To the high, level upland,
To the level ground covered in fescue,
To lovely Kara-Kyya,
They turned aside to camp.
For your aged uncle Bakai,
They sliced rump meat and served mane-fat;
Without delay they had him begin his tale.
For valiant old Bakai,
They had a fattened lamb and sweet head
Boiled into bor.
They heaped up sugar like snow
And scooped out yellow butter.
They served a thousand and one kinds of food,
Plunging their elder uncle Bakai
Into every kind of pleasure;
The luxuriant turf and clean ground
Pleased the gray-wolf hero.
They had him begin tales of every kind,
And gave their poor uncle Bakai
A Bayandos pillow upon which to rest.
Their uncle Bakai, your sage,
Now fell into a hero’s sleep.
For the gray wolf born after
His father, Lord Manas,
There was the Akkangky saddle made by Almambet,
The Kangky saddle fitted for Semetey.
Its front bow was pure gold,
Its rear bow solid silver,
And its two sides broad iron;
It would not shift when ridden downhill.
The splendid hero, the lord’s son,
Rested his head upon Akkangky,
With his head toward the qibla,
