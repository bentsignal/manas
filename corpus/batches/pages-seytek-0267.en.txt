Have I taken your piebald horse?
Have I chased away your wild ass?
You swaggering caravan men,
Have I scattered the money from your saddlebags?
You began this wrongdoing yourselves,
You blustering caravan men;
Now see what sport you will get from me!
You say, “Give us your bundled money,”
And, “Obey what we say at once.”
Which of you have I wronged?
I have committed no offense today;
You sixty mighty caravan men,
Why have you provoked my anger?
When did I ever enrage you
By demanding half your money?
My wrath is rising; I stand unyielding,
I will not spare a single one of you.
Why did you rouse my fury?
You have sorely offended my spirit;
I let it pass unnoticed, caravan men.
You are turning this into an ugly quarrel;
If I simply leave you to yourselves,
You strike people with cast-iron maces.
All sixty of you band together,
Halting wherever you please,
And cursing mothers and daughters.
You have gone rabid, you unruly caravan men,
Swaggering in your insolence.
Refusing to leave peaceful people alone,
Why have you provoked my anger?
My wrath is rising; I stand unyielding;
Now I will not leave
A single one of you untouched!
I will plunder your livestock,
Cut short every one of your lives,
And, as though a terrible slaughter had struck,
Destroy you all!
You blustering caravan men,
Your guilt is upon your own heads,
And your punishment upon your black crowns!”
His courage was as hard as his loins.
To hear his battle cry,
It was like an icy gorge wind.
If left alone, he would finish them utterly,
And reclaim
The aged Buurul.
Scraggly-bearded Karasart
Stood ready at the very front;
The thickset, vulture-tailed caravan man,
That ill-fated wretch,
Had armed himself for the attack.
The sixty mighty caravan men
Spread out with grim faces,
Their hostile intent plain to see.
They rushed forward, striking in a frenzy,
Speaking the vilest words.
A cast-iron mace as thick as a house post
Whirled past in his grip
And reached brave Seytek.
A knobbed mace and battle-axe—
He gripped it in both hands and swung it,
Then hurled the blow at noble Seytek
The moment he came within reach.
One blow did not kill him;
His father, the peerless gray-maned hero,
Had counted such a blow no more than a fly.
His anger flared and his eyes widened.
Burning fiercely as he mourned his father,
Finding no dust vast enough to scatter,
No blood enough to swallow,
No mountain great enough to set right,
No enemy enough to exhaust his rage upon,
Grim-faced brave Seytek
Struck his dark-bay horse upon the flank,
Took tempered steel in hand,
Broke through the barrier and opened a path.
The falcon thundered without pause
And attacked the caravan men.
“To your fathers’ graves, caravan men!
Have I reaped your grain before it ripened?
