In the time it takes to open and close the eyes,
He saddled faster than the swiftest.
The white saddle made by Almambet
Had a front bow of white gold,
A rear bow of solid silver,
And two sides of broad iron;
He set the matchless saddle
Upon Buurul and stood ready,
He rode straight toward the encampment,
Readying the falcon for Semetey.
At twelve years old Külchoro
Was a blazing red ember;
The worth of all the others together
Could not produce a youth like him.
Girding on a silver belt,
The smiling orphan's
Sunlike face was touched by light.
Looking to either side,
Licking his lips,
He saw fourteen horses descended from Ogoy:
The mighty swift horse Buurul,
Kanykey's Karakaska courser,
Chyyrdy's Bozjorgo,
Chachykey's Tiktoru,
The inheritance left by his father,
The true swift horse Taytoru,
Sarykhan's Tuuchunak,
Khan Bakay's Kökcholok,
Abyke's Aktelki,
Khan Köbösh's Köktelki,
And the bay stallion Archatoru
Prepared by that hero,
Shayymbet's Akkökül,
And the girl's Kyzyljorgo.
Upon the heads of the fourteen horses
Were white halters knotted in rounded fashion;
With mighty Bakay at their head,
They had made fitted halters of that kind.
Growing eager, he had them matched together;
With coverings impervious to arrows,
He had all fourteen horses covered.
For a journey of extended lodging,
That orphan of Manas
Had tents and provisions
Loaded upon Nartelki.
He had abundant provisions packed,
As though departing for months and years—
That mad son of Manas
Had abundant provisions packed,
Prayed to his God,
And put on his weapons of war.
From there toward Ala-Köl,
He intended to roam throughout it:
“I shall fly my bird at the swans;
I shall have it take a swan.
Even if it is only a swan,
I myself shall drink my fill of joy.”
Semetey rode out in excitement;
The pampered wife Chachykey
Would not release him and cried aloud.
Wrapping herself around his stirrup leather,
Entangling herself in his reins,
Behaving more and more affectionately,
She tormented the hero
And led him back toward the encampment:
“Month after month, saying you will fly the falcon,
You go wandering away
And cast me into misery.
Saying, ‘I shall fly the falcon every day,’
You remain there boasting;
Going out upon the steppe each day,
You leave me longing for you.
Seven days are the measure of the sun;
For those seven days, my lord,
Do not fly your bird at swans,
And do not drink your fill of delight.
Do not rise and go toward the horses
And leave me in such great pain!”
