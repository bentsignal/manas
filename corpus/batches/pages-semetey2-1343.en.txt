They drew bows like pouring rain,
Shot arrows like falling hail;
The musket balls whistled,
The bowstrings’ arrows hissed,
The short guns’ balls glowed red.
They came and struck the palace,
Bullets scattering like hail,
The musket balls plainly visible.
When the Jediger closed in,
Old women and young wives
Had no strength to show courage;
There was no male child of the khan,
No champion girded for action,
No people on whom to rely,
No forest to give them shelter,
No homeland into which to flee.
When God destroys one’s equal,
This world is born as filth indeed.
Its gate was pierced with firing holes;
The keepers at the gate,
Fighting as men who had escaped death,
Fired guns and drew bows,
Clashing together in combat;
Dark blood flowed like water
As they fought to bar the gate.
What could the poor gatekeepers do?
They stood giving ground,
They stood without yielding passage,
Weary beyond all measure.
With steel shafts and sharpened points,
Fletched with black hair,
The bloodthirsty lord had armed the gate
With keen, unsheathed steel.
Men lay sprawled before the gate;
The Jediger’s hearts were crushed,
For those who ventured near the gate
Lay stretched out dead.
The seasoned guards,
Those appointed to keep the gate,
Took up red-steel spears,
Faced outward at every loophole,
Raised battle-axes and spears,
And fired at chosen targets.
Whenever the Jediger closed in,
They struck whoever came near
Right beneath the armpit.
The massed black Jediger
Tried courageously to storm the gate,
Yet stood afraid to advance and fire
Against the wrestlers and heroes.
The gate was strong, guarded by lions;
Unable to overrun the gate,
Those Jediger were dumbfounded.
The watchmen and guards grew fierce;
Unable to flood into the stronghold,
The massed black Jediger
Took on the look of men about to flee.
Their warriors were speared from the gate,
Taken suddenly by surprise;
The Jediger went about grieving,
Saying, “Our dear man has died;
The dear hero has seen
The true place from which none returns.”
The people broke into separate bands;
The massed black Jediger
Were gathering themselves together,
Lamenting, “Our dear kinsman
Died in such-and-such a place.”
This was when they raised their lament.
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger,
To send his people surging forward,
Struck the great war drum.
The host, poised to flee, halted;
The people stood divided.
Those who had turned to retreat
Now came streaming out again;
Every last one thundered forth—
The accursed-looking Jediger.
