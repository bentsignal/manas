The letter said Chynteñir had left Beijing
And was coming across
The rugged ridges of the Ala-Too,
Marching in to trample the world
As though it were ash.
His forty tribes had risen,
An army equal to an age;
They came inflicting boundless torment,
It said, advancing in full force.
They came well prepared
With disorder, strife, and calamity;
They came overwhelming all before them,
Taking many lands they encountered;
They came trampling everything flat,
Making hills and hollows level ground.
The letter was accurate and clear.
It was time to tell
The small white-capped people
Of the mad Teñir’s campaign.
It was time to set the people on one path
And, rather than remain alive,
Depart from this world.
When he heard this news,
Could brave Kenen remain still?
Would he let his people be overrun,
Or would God strike Kenen down?
Startling the heroes,
He saddled Kertulpar,
Who never faltered when mounted for battle.
He put on his armored coat,
With collar of gold and sleeves of bronze,
Its sharp mail proof against arrows,
And pulled a deep-blue iron helmet
Down over his head.
Without casting lots he now knew
How fierce the enemy was.
He made all his gear ready,
A gleaming sword and iron-headed mace,
A matchlock and a battle-axe—
He girded on all his weapons.
He covered peerless Kertulpar,
His prancing dark bay,
With an arrow-proof caparison.
Wearing an arrow-proof coat,
Mounted on a steed no arrow could catch,
He received blessings from the renowned,
And rode out against the furious foe,
Hardened like a black lion;
His wrath rose at the looming hardship.
For the white-capped Kyrgyz people,
For the soaring Ala-Too,
For our sage and for honor,
The dear lion staked
His precious life upon a hair.
Tempered like a keen sword,
Hardened like finest steel,
His lion’s aspect showing,
He departed from the Ala-Too.
Receiving leave and directions from the heroes,
He took a great host from the Kyrgyz.
Do not ask how large that host was:
He clad them in arrow-proof armor,
Mounted them on fiery steeds,
And had those who knew the route lead.
To scout the broad, open mountains,
To lend their strength
To the wolves Ebegei and Kogoi,
Kenen rode out without delay.
He traveled along the back
Of the ancestral Ala-Too,
For many days and many nights,
The eagle traveling without tiring;
He shot deer on the rough ground,
Argali upon the hills,
Ibex from the terraces,
And wild asses in the broken country.
Beyond the Bel-Moinok pass,
On the far side toward Urumchi,
At broad Shyikoo by Maral-Bashy,
On the far side of Tal-Mazar,
In the plain of the city of Urum,
Upon the ridge by the famed city
Of your renowned father Manas,
The massed army had its leader,
With giant Kenen at its head.
All the heroes were eager;
Your two uncles, Ebegei and Kogoi,
Met the hero Zhelzhetpes.
They spread the whole army across the ground,
Boiled a fat, mare-fed colt
To provide them food;
Fires burned for every group of ten,
The troops divided by tens,
A great communal cauldron in their midst,
Its rich broth spilling over.
The horses were tethered in a hollow,
On bear-skin pads and white felt
The champions’ horses could be seen,
