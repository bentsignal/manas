Kongurbay, khan of the Kalmyks—
I showed my childishness.
Not keeping watch upon that pig,
I let his axe strike my head!
The battle-axe struck my forehead.
May you live safely, my lions.
Without returning to the people on the way,
Without even turning toward broad Talas,
I shall go alone to Mecca.
Dark blood streamed from my forehead;
Strength left my wretched life.
My whole outside became fire,
My inside filled with burning coals.
I have no strength to move,
No power to turn my head.
My spear fell from my hand;
The stirrup slipped from my foot.
At times I can speak,
At times I cannot gather my senses.
Breaking your wing,
Putting my wretched life to rest,
I flee away—do you see?”
After he had said this,
Great Manas upon the summit
Utterly despaired of life.
“Struck from the vulnerable flank,
“Pressed hard and trapped,
My champion, the young Syrgak,
Lost by mishap to the Chinese—
Is this the time for such a thing?
Turn your horse’s head awhile.
My darling, bound to my soul,
Slow down a little!
If you reach Talas, tell them;
Tell all my worthy people!
Say that Manas remains in bonds.
The people of forty khans have risen;
Say they encircled Manas twelvefold.
Say Aibanboz can no longer move.
Let us arrive at once!
Unless you rescue him,
They will seize Manas,
Forcing him into their religion;
The forty wanton women of Beijing,
Say, took him away and kept him as a stud.
Tell my khan like Muzburchak;
Tell all twelve khans along the road!
Tell the champions Koshoy and Toshtuk,
The warrior Urbu of Eibit Khan,
Zhamgyrchy of the Eshteks.
I shall say it briefly, my Syrgak:
Tell Ajybai and my khan Bakai,
Tell all the peoples of the world,
Tell the khans who can devise a plan,
Who thrust their spears without wavering,
Tell all the resolute men,
Tell those skilled in swinging swords!
The forty were warriors from forty places;
Old Kyrgyl and the slave Shuutu—
Tell such fine men as these!
I have not slaughtered the unbelievers;
Aibanboz beneath me will not move.
Go tell my sorrow, Syrgak!
What is seen today is gone tomorrow:
Such is this shitty world!”
Saying this, he wept aloud,
That precious lion, born exceptional,
Parted from his Syrgak,
Deprived of his wing.
Black night fell over him,
Who had never gone on foot,
Never lost his presence of mind,
And when dense enemy hosts poured in,
Had never drawn back.
Before Syrgak, closing his mouth,
Had passed across the ridge,
Dust flew without ceasing.
Not mourning how many had died,
With earth trembling and mountains falling,
That accomplished khan Kongurbay,
The Chinese emperor came up.
The Chinese surged, racing each other;
The dense people of forty khans
Came sweeping over them.
Struggling for their lives,
Almambet and Chubak, the two lions,
Now held the mountain slope.
The Chinese swarmed and shouted,
Pressing hard upon Manas.
Tal-Choku, where he stood,
They encircled forty-twofold.
News reached Beijing;
Karykan himself
Sent swift foot soldiers charging,
And supplied his vast host.
At that time, the many Chinese
Could find no plan at all.
Alooko’s Kongurbay—
That boar who knew the enemy’s stratagems!—
Trotted to and fro.
Thinking out a plan and framing his words,
He arrived at this device:
Into every warrior’s hand
He gave a single brick.
“Let the wall be forty cubits high,
Let the ditch be forty cubits deep.
Build a fortress all around!
If God grants us Manas,
Take him alive.
Upon the Kyrgyz who attacked us first,
If Manas falls into your hands,
Inflict a mighty slaughter!
Mecca is what they rely upon;
Drive them all the way there.
Bear down upon their heads:
Prince Almambet and his Chubak.
I have killed his Syrgak.
Leave his Manas alive;
If Almambet falls into your hands,
And fearless Chubak,
Crush them, leaving neither alive!
If we take the four, people of forty khans,
There is no ship upon the dark water;
After that, among the Kyrgyz,
No one can stand against us.
Overwhelm them, Chinese, overwhelm them!” he cried.
