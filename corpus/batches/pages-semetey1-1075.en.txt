"Amen!" they cried over the boy,
Sobbing as they gave their blessing.
Your poor sister-in-law Chürök
Had more worries than her heart could hold:
"May the boy remain safe."
Her tears ran in ten directions.
"The moon is dark, the night overcast—
What will this night become?
If Toltoy defeats him,
What will become of the days
Of this Chürök standing here?
His grip is hard as iron.
Do not let people say,
'Because of Aychürök's affliction,
She had her boy, a flame of fire,
Slaughtered.'
God, fulfill my prayer;
If you created him as a living soul,
God, keep her boy safe!
You have hung a battle-axe at your waist;
At twelve years of age,
You have set out upon a journey.
May a host support you on your journey;
O keen falcon, return blessed with good fortune.
May autumn cool your thoughts;
May it lay flat the enemy you seek.
Oh, my dear young falcon,
Spear a man on the downward slope;
Spear two in a row together.
Return safely, my tiger;
May your father, the leopard
Gone to the next world, be your companion."
Thirty young women and thirty maidens
Sobbing together,
With Aychürök among them,
Spread their hands and cried out.
Semetey, son of Manas—
Let me tell of the hero's condition.
Wrapped in the blue Kübө coat,
Straining like a blue tiger,
With a rope slung around his neck,
He led a white-gray mare
That had borne no foal for seven years.
The hero, son of Manas,
Slung the rope around his neck
And prayed to the Creator:
He spread his hands, saying, "Amen!"
His heart cruelly divided,
Tears pouring from his eyes,
He slung around his neck
The great purse left by Manas
And prayed to the one God:
"Amen, God, amen!
The one horse equal in worth to a life
Carries the young boy away.
If you let it be flattened, that is disgrace.
Buurul, whom none would give when asked—
When the boy mounted without inspiring envy,
My age was cruelly scorched.
He is riding away to witness
The suffering laid down by the Creator;
A young boy of twelve
Is riding away to die
At Toltoy of the Zhediger's hands.
Will practiced Chynkozho
Spear through and overturn
The twelve-year-old boy,
I lament before God,
And take Buurul beneath him as plunder?!
You have multiplied these worries so greatly;
Will the Almighty reveal
The boy, small as a crow?
My thoroughbred Narbuudan
Would not tire if ridden for six months.
I lament before God
For Külchoro, my kinsman.
The prayer of my kinsman—
Unable to remain standing, this poor soul—
