At Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
While the Argyn ranged over the highlands,
In the delightful plain of Keng-Kol,
Along broad Talas,
When the Argyn and Kyrgyz chose the valley
And were encamped there,
With kumis by day and girls by night,
Such was their life.
When paired alaba coats* and sable caps
Were worn with swagger,
When the Kyrgyz had gathered under one leader
And lived in delight,
Shooting roe deer when they grew bored,
And when not bored, freely
Holding games in Talas.
The khan's game was of another order,
The dear sultan, splendid hero;
Among the yellow settlements of broad Talas
They laid out an ordo forty paces wide,
Played until their desire for victory was sated,
And held splendid games.
The winners slaughtered and took
Forty barren mares at a time.
In the garden planted at Talas,
They sprawled at ease;
In the garden planted at Keng-Kol,
They stretched themselves out.
They divided a whole beaver pelt
Into halves,
Fastened them to their leggings,
Mounted three-year-olds and flew birds,
And grew wild with delight.
They brought no trouble upon the age;
At that time no one remained
Who could gainsay the gray wolf.
The horses had grown fat and restless,
The heroes fat and pent-up;
For vigorous youths and swift horses,
The time had come to prove their worth.
At that time Semetey,
Finding no mountain to strike,
No enemy to fight,
No land to burn,
No hero with whom to die,
Was overcome by different thoughts;
In that poor lion-born hero,
His bloodthirsty nature showed.
Finding no ranks to break,
No unbroken rocky rampart,
The noble lion, clear in wisdom,
That dear hero like a lion,
At the coming of true dawn,
Started awake from sleep
In Aichürök's embrace.
He turned in anger,
Gathered himself like a lion;
That champion, almost a saint,
Had his former fury stirred.
When he sighed,
Blue flame burst above his head;
When wrath seized him,
His true strength split stone.
He halted in anger,
Appearing as a lion-prince,
And lamented his bloodthirsty father.
His gray-wolf ritual father,
Almambet, came to mind;
Your hero rolled over,
Coiling like a black snake.
Unable to endure his thoughts,
His old wound was stirred;
No mortal came within a handspan of him.
It was the time when he looked back
And counted his falcon uncle Syrgak.
