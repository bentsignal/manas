A gray tiger was his guardian;
No such soul existed in the world.
From Akyz, where no horse had trodden,
Suddenly, across the branching passes,
A sight appeared that divided the mind:
Sarybay, the monstrous giant’s son,
Loamed like a blue craggy mountain.
He carried a spear like a pine tree,
And his cast-iron mace, huge as Mount Opol,
Was strapped close at his side.
His shout split heads,
And his roar split stones.
Then Sarybay
Was seized by his blood-drinking fury,
And spared nothing before him.
He seemed able to leap across a ridge
And devour all humankind;
He loomed like a mountain where herds graze,
And the mad brute seemed ready
To seize and swallow anyone he met.
His form was strange and his venom strong—
Abandon all hope of your precious life.
They stood facing one another;
Sarybay and Semetey
Met and thrust their spears,
And began a mighty exchange of blows.
The watching people renounced
Their tiny lives entirely.
Spears bent when they charged;
“How can the two heroes survive?” they said,
Despairing of their mortal lives.
Sarybay, the monstrous giant’s son,
Was that accursed incendiary himself,
The very dark eye of the angel of death;
He devoured all he encountered,
And swallowed those who came near.
Of every creature that crossed his path,
Not one remained alive.
He froze those he charged upon,
And if his claws seized them,
He made them march at a ponderous pace.
Having heard the child’s report,
Having heard that the monstrous giant’s son
Was a brutal tyrant,
Having heard all the rumors,
And heard that no strength could prevail
Against the monstrous infidel’s son,
He showed himself a true lion.
Leaving all others behind,
His long life now drawing late,
He went to meet death at Sarybay’s hands,
To prove he would never retreat,
And test himself against Sarybay.
If his dear eyes closed in death,
He would leave his children and people
Thereafter to the savage enemy,
That vicious swine;
Still, he would test his strength and lion-heart
Against Sarybay.
He had grieved in life
And rejoiced when told he would die;
Born of an illustrious man,
Wherever he turned his face,
He carried tumult in his wake.
Descendant of a gray-maned lion,
He tore armored coats into four,
And his counterstroke was crimson fire.
He laid low all who opposed him;
None who grappled with him survived.
Whoever provoked his anger,
He drove before him,
And crushed between his fangs.
When the enemy met him, whole nations died;
Many giants perished grappling with him.
Your true gray-maned lion, your warrior prince,
Raised his steel spear,
And headed straight toward Sarybay,
A naked sword strapped at his side;
As though hail would pour down again,
Wrath spread across his face.
