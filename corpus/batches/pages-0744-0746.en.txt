I was a pillar risen from Beijing;
For the countless Chinese people
I was a fortified city, a stronghold.
The Chinese had need of me.
Bind me without hurting me;
Put me in an iron cage.
In Beijing with its forty gates,
He is a ruler, not a god,
Yet all the deeds he does
Are not far from those of your God—
Karykan, the khan of Beijing.
Provide six swift horses,
Give a letter the size of a palm,
And send him word.
Before Karykan
Go and explain it clearly.
He will agree to what you say;
For my sake,
The plane-tree garden in Beijing,
And the golden throne within,
Will surely submit to you.
The khanship of Outer Beijing
He will give you in full.
Restrain this rage of yours;
Let us remain alive.
If you do not let us live,
If you torment our fly-sized lives,
If you kill me, I have no recourse;
Lion Manas, mighty leopard,
You will gain not so much as a fly.
I was renowned; I was a khan.
To the seething Chinese people
I was like the Ala-Too mountains.
For Chyn-Machyn, for Beijing,
I was a warrior who bore the burden.
At the edge of the countless people,
On the ridge of swarming enemies,
I lived in a white dwelling.
Know this, my word to you;
Lion Manas, heed my words.
When you rode out on patrol—
‘When bloodthirsty Manas rides out,
He will slaughter every khan’—
We ourselves had heard the news.
We sent out men skilled in discernment
And had them search for you.
Those discerning men searched but failed;
We ourselves
Brought calamity upon ourselves.
I have much to tell; listen.
My Manas, blessed by Khizir,
In anguish I speak; heed my words.
When we held our white dwellings,
We were warriors who drained the world.
Now we are in your hands,
Wretched captives in your grasp.
The treasure where gold is dug—
I will give you my mine as well.
Kara-Kyr, where tea is gathered—
I will give you that land as well.
I have the city of Tash-Köpürö;
In the lands of Tash-Köpürö
I have villages in their tens of thousands.
I will give you those as well.
On its nearer edge,
Agala Chap and Kyzyl-Kum,
Where hunters go and find delight,
Where none could stay with me without enjoyment,
I have land of precious worth—
I will give you that as well.
Killing me will bring no remedy;
If you put me to death,
My ocean, you will gain nothing.
Though I die, words will remain;
There will be no end to my blood-price.
Born to me, Alööke,
I have exactly sixty sons.
Here is a wonder I can tell;
Hear it, generous Manas.
The youngest of the sixty nobles,
The darling of Khan Alööke, now in bonds,
Has studied wondrous arts;
Those who hear his fame take fright.
From the land of those one-eyed people,
From the lake called Abergen,
From the place called Suuk-Tör,
There is a sixty-headed dragon.
Under that dragon
He has studied six years as of this year;
Those who heard his fame took fright.
Six multitudes of boys went together;
All six multitudes were wiped out;
Six boys came back together.
Of Beijing's forty gates,
They say he opened all forty.
He gathered the discerning judges,
Karykan, the khan of Beijing,
And had them file past.
So many judges saw him, they say;
Many of those who saw
Died with their hearts bursting, they say.
Dust rose wherever he walked;
His voice drove the life out of men.
From the hearth an ember rose;
A mighty one named Kongurbay arose.
He began to show a terrible wrath;
The governors who fought him
He slaughtered, leaving none.
They saw the full force of his fury;
Many of the governors
Died with their hearts bursting.
Once they had seen his valor,
They made him a house upon the upland;
All the Chinese gathered,
And that warrior Kongurbay
They made a powerful khan of the Kalmyks.
His valor rose beyond all bounds.
These days the Chinese
Have the warrior Kongurbay
Guard the borders of their people.
I will agree to what you say;
I will send word to the city,
Summon Kongurbay,
And make him your companion.
I will send word to the governors;
I will spread the news far and wide,
