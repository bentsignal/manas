If you would slaughter us, here is the army!”
The people closed in with cries;
The great black Jediger
Dismounted and surrendered.
They cast down spears and swords;
The strongest men of renowned name,
Keeping out of Semetey's sight,
Began to flee toward their villages.
Once the common folk stood thus,
He did not attack the black multitude
Nor assault
The people who had surrendered;
He stood hearing the lament
Of the many weeping people.
“It was Khoja who caused the ruin.”
He flicked his coiled whip;
His wrath rose and he stiffened,
His eyes blazing like fire,
Bloodlust seizing him fiercely.
He moved apart in anger;
The lion prince Semetey
Appeared in the form of a lion.
His armed garments numbered eighty-four;
Semetey, son of the gray wolf—
Wherever he struck, red fire flared.
From his fathers and forefathers,
No mortal came against them;
None who faced them defeated them.
His wrath loomed visibly;
Those who came saying they would oppose him
Spilled their darkening blood.
He girded the jekene at his waist;
His accomplished father Manas,
Even against seven divisions of black troops,
Had entered alone among them and attacked.
He did not spare those who fought him,
Nor drink his fill of blood.
An enemy who came to battle
He slaughtered without turning back.
From his fathers and forefathers,
When enemies looked upon them,
Those born of other fathers
Could offer no resistance.
His root ancestor was wholly a khan,
Blessed by Khizir from the root;
Every forefather was a khan,
Blessed by Khizir from the beginning.
He burned grassy lands,
And set reed-filled lands aflame.
He poured blood into black water,
And greased valleys with streams;
When provoked in enmity,
He laid low his own father.
When they crossed him,
He “honored” the forty companions.
He overturned Abyke
And sent Köbösh into the grave,
Making his gray-wolf nature known.
He rejoiced at the word “enemy,”
And drank blood as though it were water.
He greased his white steel;
When they angered him,
He laid low his own elder brother.
The foe who faced him halted;
Before the hero's son,
There remained no man to face him.
Among spearmen he became a lion;
For the prince, son of a lion,
The black earth became his only opponent.
His fame spread through the world;
Prince Semetey became a hero.
He greased his keen steel
And laid low the foe who fought him.
He greased his tempered steel
And laid low those who pursued him.
The blessing of Khan Koshoy—
Do you know the tale
Of Semetey's father?
