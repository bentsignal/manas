Darkening with rage,
Kyyaz stood shouting,
“Great host of the Kara-Jediger,
Come, every one of you!” he cried.
“Great host of the Kara-Jediger,
My people, heed the words I speak!
Go forth to the borders of our land!
See what war is and what peace is—
My people, witness it for yourselves!
Once we were the forty tribes of the Kyrgyz,
Dwelling along the Ala-Too.
While lion Manas lived,
No enemy could scatter us with war cries.
At the frontier stood brave Manas,
Coiling like a dragon;
When they beheld the hero’s might,
Even the Chinese in Kakan were roused.
The sevenfold Ala-Too
He won as our homeland through valor;
The Kyrgyz of these towering mountains
He gathered and forged into a nation.
By fighting, he brought them prosperity;
By building, he gave them homes;
The scattered, wandering Kyrgyz
He labored to make one people.
The towering Ala-Too
He made a refuge for the Kyrgyz.
While mighty Manas lived,
He turned and struck the Afghans;
What people in the mountain folds escaped unharmed?
While formidable Manas stood,
We raided Kunjut and took it daily;
We opened a road into India,
Breaking through stone to make the way;
We attacked many lands.
We struck Jet-Kayt and conquered it seven times;
How could Jet-Kayt have remained
Unscathed by us Kyrgyz?
In those days the peerless hero Manas—
Whose dwelling was great Talas—
Drove white deer home and made them cattle,
And filled every battlefield with tumult.
My father Agysh often used to say:
“Since life was first created, one like Manas
Has never come among the Kyrgyz, nor anywhere
In all the world.”
When the Chinese host engulfed the land,
When all faced utter ruin together,
“Manas was our stronghold,” he would say,
“Manas took his keen steel
Into his hand,” he would say.
“Throughout the whole world he raised
A mighty commotion,” he would say;
“From the infidels he rescued
The descendants of the few Kyrgyz
And brought them home,” he would say.
“When he turned to face the foe,
He was like a written decree,” he would say;
“Not only to the Kyrgyz, but for all time,
Khan Manas, now gone to eternity,
Became a father,” he would say.
“To the Kyrgyz, whose saddles sagged,
He became a support,” he would say.
“From age to age, at some destined time,
Not only for the Kyrgyz but for all time,
He became a fruit of blessing,” he would say.
“From age to age, at some destined time,
Brave Manas, gone to eternity,
Would be needed again,” he would say.
“To those who endured affliction,
To those left beneath the scorching sun,
For the multitude brave Manas
Became a sheltering poplar,” he would say.
“Always and forever,
The wretched Kyrgyz cannot bring down the wolf,
Nor bear to see the son who does;
Khan Manas, who guided them,
Was their shared seed, like white wheat,
