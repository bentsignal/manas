Saying, 'Agree to what I say,'
They came and overran us again;
When death drew near,
When all our strength was gone,
They would not let us prepare Shamyyan,
Would not let us plow crops into the earth,
Would not let us break the earth's surface,
Would not let us scatter a handful of seed.
After they laid their foul siege,
After they made all pleasure costly,
After they slaughtered the people without looking back,
It is true that I resolved to agree
So the people might be saved!
It is true that I meant
To bind you and take you
To Toltoy of the Zhediger!
Corruption has entered your mind;
Do not bring disgrace
Before your renowned princely husband.
Who will believe your words?
Stop playing coy with your husband.
If suffering comes upon me,
I have my Argyn people and my own strength
To charge forth and make war.
You are my lawfully wedded wife in my embrace;
If hardship presses a son of a bitch,
I may resolve to give even her away!
For the waist you have girded,
Do not keep fiercely maligning
Your renowned gray-maned husband!
Semetey, born greater
Than his father, gray-maned Manas,
If you have heard reports about yourself,
And keep believing your wife,
Here is the city of Baashaluu,
And here am I—cut off my head!"
Unerring Azhybay
Had spoken his fitting words;
He revealed his eloquence.
Azhybay, eloquent beyond others,
Drove every weighty word
Into the heart
Of prince Semetey.
Then Semetey,
Hearing many kinds of words from Azhybay,
Found all he said persuasive;
Thinking, "She is acting like a woman,"
He came to hate Aychürök.
"God has struck her,
For maligning her own elder brother!"
His eyes reddened like a lion's;
The fearsome leopard
Showed his venomous face
And now stared at Chürök.
Unable to bear his anger,
What could dear Chürök do?
She hurried forward at an amble;
Unable to bear his venom,
While the gray-maned lion stared,
She cowered behind the curtain.
Azhybay, son of Azhy,
And Tümönbay, son of Tülkü,
Now put the day in writing
And received the reckoning of the date.
When seven days had passed,
They said, "Let brave Toltoy come
As bridegroom to Aychürök."
Azhybay, son of Azhy,
Now made the pledge.
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Was twelve years old then;
He was a remarkable one,
A glowing red ember,
With no one like him in the world,
A prodigy born among men,
With no one like him among mortals.
The horse he rode was gray;
