He made ready all his weapons,
And fastened the gold-inlaid battle-axe,
Your uncle’s Joykuma,
Unscabbarded at his side.
The one he would face was Sarybai;
Turning toward Sarybai,
The lion-warrior took the road.
Ready to die if death had come,
He went to meet fearsome, wrathful Sarybai
And test himself face-to-face.
His valor was renowned;
Külchoro climbed the rise.
When he paused and looked intently,
Ill-fated Sarybai
Stood prepared with his battle gear,
The mad brute standing there,
Straining like a blue tiger.
When one looked at his mustache,
It was like nettles filling a ravine.
His eyes were like lumps of coal;
When one looked at his size,
He was as great as half a towering mountain.
He had the majesty of a lion,
As though he could herd
And trample the entire world—
Such was his appearance.
His gray-wolf ferocity was plain;
The one he faced was venom itself.
Among men, none could overcome him;
He was God’s wrath lying in wait.
He fixed his hostile gaze sharply,
A creature who had come seeking conflict.
Such was the hero’s might;
He was truly God’s wrath.
Your enraged infidel
Seemed to await the whole world.
Forest and tree nearly collapsed,
Smoldering beneath his violence.
He had overrun the world—
Such was hero Sarybai.
To every one of those marksmen
He gave abundant provisions,
Armor the sun could not penetrate,
Pounds of gold ornaments,
A horse as payment,
And lavish rewards without stint.
He had the Dangdung Mountains combed,
For mountain goats from Dangdung’s heights,
Cliff goats with thick hides.
Long ago your valiant mother Kanykei
Had the marksmen shoot them,
And, keeping the scorching sun from them,
Had them covered in a white chest.
She had the hides thoroughly cured,
And, so they would be soft and supple,
Had them placed in the bark
Of apple trees from every mountain valley.
She had them tanned and worked,
And ninety skilled youths
Tended them without rest for nine months
And beat them for ninety days.
She had them folded like a serpent’s belly,
And the flawless leather
Stitched with smooth, close seams.
What proved unfit she had ripped apart;
She sent for sharpened steel
And had it poured over every side.
When the kandagai was pulled,
It stretched like flowing water.
When a facing enemy struck it,
The point of his steel was blunted.
It turned out just as intended:
Embers could not pierce it though dry honeysuckle burned,
Nor bullets, however expertly fired.
Its trouser cuffs were embroidered,
And its seams were bound with steel.
It reduced an enemy who came against it
To helpless frustration.
He donned the kandagai,
And slung the Syrbarang gun over his shoulder.
