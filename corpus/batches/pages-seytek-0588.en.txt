We shall be crushed to dust.
Test the wrathful one’s courage
By clashing with him;
Wage the fiercest struggle.
Though heads be severed and blood flow,
From the hands of the wrathful dog
Save the helpless women
And the young children.
Perform prodigies of valor;
Fight without peace,
And stand face-to-face
With the warrior son of the ogre,
The tyrant who has smeared the earth with blood,
With torment itself,
With the black eye of death,
With the infidel who ruins nations,
With the world-ending scourge who devours peoples;
Go and confront him.
For God’s sake, for my sake,
For the gathered nation and people,
Wage a mighty battle.
Make Sarybai, son of the ogre,
Bow down and overthrow him;
Destroy the wrathful one
Before the great conflict ends.
He is a fanged enemy, a wrathful khan;
Can any man afford to spare himself?
Will anyone who spares himself prosper?
Level your spears without mercy,
And sacrifice your lives held in trust.
If he is a champion and a hero,
Submit yourselves to the trial of his formidable strength;
If hostile death comes, then die!
Look to your forefathers of old—
Behold such courage.
Who were those who inhabited Talas,
Where many khans came to plunder
Yet could not crush and defeat its men?
They never yielded their place to another;
You are the descendants of those keen-edged lions—
If hostile death comes, fly into the bullets!
Plunge the accursed enemy’s head
Into the turmoil of battle;
If your strength begins to fail,
Split open his black liver
And lie face-down upon the earth.
If your power and strength prevail,
Give the devouring son of the ogre
The punishment he deserves!
My sons, do not remain alive—
Die by Sarybai’s hand!
How could we heed the words
Of the ogre’s swine of a son
Because he is said to be a wrathful champion?
How can we, the honorable ancestral Kyrgyz people,
While our dear heads still live,
Hand the nation over to him?
If hostile death comes, then die;
For the vast common people,
See once more, my sons,
The true path from which there is no retreat.
In this wretched world of meager fortune,
Does one who fears death ever prosper?
Has there ever been one born
With an iron core who neither dies nor perishes?
In this false and miserable world,
This speaking tongue will be stilled,
The span of worldly life will contract;
When the angel stretches out five fingers,
When the angel who takes life
Draws near,
What soul can flee and escape
The death that has been written?
The dug grave receives us;
Beneath it lies the vast black earth
That has reaped the whole wide world—
None of us is immortal.
If our wretched heads are destined for death,
If we are buried beneath the ground,
The earth is the millstone, and we are peas.
