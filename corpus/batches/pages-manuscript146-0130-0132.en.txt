Its mane and tail were finely formed,
It raced as swiftly as an arrow,
It seemed to gaze halfway up the sky,
Though well-fed, it never lost its discipline,
Each bound carried it a full stride,
It made the tamarisk-lined slope tremble,
For a brave and valiant warrior,
It was fit for the road he would ride.
Let us take up the tale handed down from long ago,
The tale of one who shook Ala-Too,
Whose swift horse stood ready at the tethering line,
A narrow-hearted, shallow soul,
Who routed a mass of four thousand,
Who cried “Let fate decide!” and flew into the gunfire,
Against the wrathful infidels,
Who leveled his spear toward Khan-Too,
Who gave his precious life for his people,
Willing to offer it in sacrifice,
Without flinching or weakening his arm,
He stood firm at the very foot of the assault,
The wrathful thousand stood grim before him,
The women sat there in alarm,
Though dark blood flowed through the ravine,
Though tormented, he never lost the power of speech,
His face was wrinkled, his venom spent,
Such were the deeds he did when he charged,
Where is the hero Kenen now?
He had been one among the heroes.
He broke through the enemy line and opened a road,
When arrows poured down like rain,
When bullets fell like a hailstorm,
When blood was spilled and people fell,
Whenever he met a furious foe,
He faced him head-on and stood his ground,
Firing his gun and drawing his bow,
He fought without flinching,
He was a peerless hero.
Oil flowed from his fingers,
Blood flowed from his nails,
He never strayed from his purpose,
Rich treasure and abundance emerged,
Blood flowed from what he drew,
A soul arose to return,
He alone drew the enemy against him.
If it was a moonless night,
If it was a day when sight was veiled,
Later he would not stumble in speech.
Once in moonless darkness,
On the side where Mana lay,
He was one among the marksmen.
He won fame with the spear,
Among masters who found a stratagem against the enemy,
Among heroes complete in cunning,
Among warriors skilled in wisdom,
He was one of the crafty men.
When he turned, he was worth a thousand souls,
The lion Kenen became khan,
He was a hero weighty as a mountain,
He won fame against a thousand in battle,
He was a hero of hard conflict,
The heroes traveled day after day,
Traveling among thousands without taking a horse,
He lay in Blue Kashgar,
Following Kayyp’s road to the left,
Joining Ala-Too to him,
Driving them onward behind him,
Safely through many nights,
He traveled the shadowed road by his own will,
Urging on his dappled horse,
Crossing the ridges with a roar,
His solitary horse granted by Tengir.
The grey-haired elder said this with force,
With a roar as though the earth had split,
A tumult rose to the heavens,
The many-colored host divided,
They met in a standing battle,
The warriors of the five passes,
Charged together and struck,
Manas at their head, Khan Shayköö,
They struck by the grave mound,
They drove straight ahead with a rush,
Of the radiant elder Manas,
It was told concerning the grave mound,
There were two wings of the enveloping host,
Before them stood Kenen’s mighty claim,
All of them were heroes,
Ebegey and Köböy, your two elders,
The hero Jeljetpes joined them as a companion,
They swept through the whole army,
Like a fat barren mare’s colt,
They laid them waste in torment,
Horses came by tens from the right,
They formed troops of ten,
In their midst stood the dear Kazakh,
His neck worn and wasted,
If the horse supports him, let it be tethered,
Your white axe tucked beneath your arm,
Your valiant hero appeared,
He had only just taken it in hand.
Ebegey and Köböy, the two lions,
At hero Kenen’s side,
Speaking softly, they began their counsel,
Ebegey and Köböy began to speak,
Hero Kenen, lend your ear,
You must understand one thing,
