The Almabash of surpassing steel,
Which would not fail for sixty days—
He now hung Akelte, left by Manas,
Upon Külchoro.
It struck whatever met its wrath;
Yesterday, in the desert of Mediyan,
It was found upon the open plain,
One of six blades fallen from heaven,
Well suited for striking the foe,
The strongest of sword blades:
When drawn in anger,
It never went without drinking blood;
Unless it drank its fill of blood,
It would not return to its sheath.
The fitting heirloom from his father
He proudly gave to the boy.
Semetey's battle-axe—
The master smith Bölökbay
Had joined and drawn out its point.
If an enemy came charging,
When swung in anger,
It lengthened to a fathom;
When about to strike the foe,
It glowed red like fire.
The battle-axe was a proud weapon,
Worthy of noble Semetey.
Hammered and polished underneath,
Master smith Bölökbay
Had made it through great toil.
If you ask about Bölökbay,
A star in the heavens had turned gray;
Dөөтү and Bölökbay
Had become friends twelve times.
While he was making the axe,
Unable to withstand the demands of his bellows,
Many young bulls were slaughtered;
Unable to meet its need for charcoal,
Many forests were destroyed.
The master of masters had forged it,
Wrapping its handle in gold,
Drawing its steel edge razor-sharp;
It felled anyone who attacked.
If he met Toltoy of the Zhediger
Out on the deserted plain,
If they stood rein to rein
And fought with battle-axes,
"Let its purpose strike home then;
Let him enter with the battle-axe."
He now beheld the boy's fierce spirit.
With deliberate instructions,
He now gave him the battle-axe.
If they stood rein to rein
And fought collar to collar,
He now gave the white Tinte
To hang around Külchoro's neck.
Looking nowhere around him,
Your ocean heaved in grief;
Sparing nothing for the boy,
He placed Syrnayza in his hand.
Showing his ocean-like greatness,
He had Külchoro mount
Mountain-like Buurul,
Standing saddled and ready.
When the boy mounted the horse,
Thirty young women and thirty maidens,
With Aychürök among them,
She voiced her sorrow, sobbing:
"Alas, Külchoro
Could have stayed here tonight!"
She cried out in regret.
When the boy mounted the horse,
Do not ask how the boy appeared.
Thirty young women and thirty maidens
Had nine black calves
Cut into pieces,
And offered them in sacrifice for the boy;
Sobbing as they performed the rite,
The thirty young women and thirty maidens—
