The Kyrgyz are those I shall fight,
The great prince Semetey.
I shall destroy the Argyn camp,
I shall destroy the Nogai stronghold,
I shall lay waste the Kyrgyz marches.
Leaving not a scrap of this people—
Could a witless coward who fears a child
Be a hero like me?
The fate ordained by the Creator
I have witnessed many times before.
Finding no mountain to strike,
Finding no enemy to fight,
Finding no stone to grind my teeth upon,
Finding no head to sever,
Driven wild by restless ardor,
Like a wolf straining at its tether,
Stirred up by the very wind—
It seems I came to suffer grievous pain
At the hands of this boy.
It seems I came to die
At the hands of lion-hearted Seytek.
Our dream has turned awry;
Our cause has been undone.
Ah, Seytek is a true hero,
His fortune is complete;
He has eighty-four weapons,
And their sweep is fire.
He is a fierce one born of the Kyrgyz;
Never has his like existed in an age.
Seytek became an ember in my heart.
My name is brave Sarybai,
The courser beneath me is my wings;
To make the whole world tremble
Has ever been my custom.
My host beyond counting is assembled;
I applied all my heroic skill,
But found no way at all.
He is a boy beyond reckoning;
I shall drive a spear into his breast.
I shall thrust the spear with force,
Smash him into fragments,
And hurl him aside in pieces.
I shall slaughter his village and take the spoils;
If the day does not end, I shall return.
An omen is evil, a dream is evil,
When fortune turns against you, the deed is evil,
And evil is the coat you lift to wear.”
Not a foal remained among the shod horses;
The uproar had wholly vanished.
I, Sarybai, bearing the name of khan—
Evil is the force that abandoned me.
Evil is the force of an outsider’s son,
Evil is strife that comes from within,
Evil is fire that rises from the core.
Chañdayak, who deserted me—
Evil is the dog who knew my secrets.
If true death comes, let me die;
Let me see what courage there is
In that sniveling boy.
Kulansur, who yields to no pursuit,
Is a creature born one of a kind,
Like a host of encircling wolves,
A beast come plainly into view.
Upon Kulansur, who yields to no pursuit,
I shall cast a cold lasso;
Kulansur, who will not yield when asked,
I shall escort away through spilled blood.
I shall raise an unflagging tumult,
Stir boundless calamity and uproar,
Find my chance and wreak slaughter,
And avenge my ancient grudge.
I shall defeat the princely people;
If the sly boy falls into my hands,
I shall drink his blood like kumis.
I shall open everyone’s eyes;
If by chance he falls into my hands,
I shall feast upon his young liver,
Spear his mighty warriors,
And conquer his hardy men.
If he falls into my hands, he will go no farther—
