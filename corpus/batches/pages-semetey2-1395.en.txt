He has apparently become their giant!
That vainglorious Kyyaz
Has apparently made himself
Master of every land he sees.
Yet the spirit of his forefather,
The lion Manas, has not vanished.
Kyyaz has set himself against Manas;
You shall see for yourself
That his undertaking will not prosper.
May that Kyyaz not belittle
Broad Keng-Köl and Talas,
Nor the majestic Manas who rests there.
This Jylgynduu, Keng-Köl,
And broad Talas
Are not lands where we dwell;
This leopard, the blood-shedding hero Manas,
Is not a hero we ourselves created.
His fame split the whole world;
We are but palm-sized beside Manas,
Whose warfare reached distant Beijing.
If they celebrate after taking his wife,
Will this great host of black-clad Jediger
Not meet with slaughter?
Were hilly Keng-Köl and broad Talas
A mountain pass one could cross without a word?
Was great-hearted, gray-maned Manas
A man whom anyone could attack?
This deed of Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
Is no ordinary act.
The Argyn and the Kyrgyz
Are not peoples for others to assail.
Warrior Kyyaz has gone mad;
This deed of his is no small thing.
In saying he will give a feast,
May warrior Kyyaz not be heading into a pit;
May the dear man who never turned back
Not be marching into captivity.
May his wedding not become a massacre;
May the teeming Chinese, hearing of it,
Not break his host and slaughter them,
Bring disaster upon his camp,
And openly destroy the people.
If he takes the wife of Manas’s son,
The blood-shedding hero, let him take her himself;
May the khan’s fury not unleash an uproar,
And may a people like the Jediger survive.
May the wedding not become a bloody conflict
That sets them marching to war.
May the Jediger not wake tomorrow
Bereft of warrior Kyyaz,
And find themselves taken captive.”
Buzumbek Khan of ruined Chambyl
Spoke these words;
To the muttering khans
He voiced the thoughts in his heart,
Opposing the wedding.
Amid the assembled people,
He explained what lay ahead.
“If what you say is true,
May the wedding become a massacre;
May that wretch Kyyaz
Meet his death this very day;
May livestock and people alike be struck down,
And may it end just so.”
Many khans who heard these words
Now returned to their own lands,
Spitting wherever they went
And scattering bitter curses.
Everyone who understood
Set off for his own country.
Rejecting in their hearts
The feast held by the khan,
The khans refused to come;
Cursing and rebuking him,
They returned to their own lands.
Among the Jediger of Jeti-Özön,
They said deadly poison,
Fatal to anyone who drank it, had been mixed in;
