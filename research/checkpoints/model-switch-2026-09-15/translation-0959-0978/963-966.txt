He chewed apart.
Eighty young wives and eighty girls
Were resting at their leisure;
Into the midst of the many girls,
As though about to slaughter them,
He came prowling.
Before they could raise a shout,
Before they could seize his collar,
Into the embers of saxaul wood
He swept his tail.
His tail touched the embers;
Three finger-widths of it burned.
'He will inflict terrible suffering,' they thought,
'On Kumayyk's account
He will start a quarrel.'
Before they could rub it with fat,
Remove the burned part,
And wipe his fur clean,
Because of what had happened,
On Kumayyk's account,
God struck them in an extraordinary way:
Looking as huge as a mountain,
Troubling the hearts of those who saw him,
On Kumayyk's account
He arrived on his bridegroom's visit.
Kumayyk, his tail burned,
Before the fiercely wrathful lord
Came prowling.
When Manas saw the puppy,
His blood-drinking fury seized him.
His white teeth, broad as a door,
Teeth unlike those of other people,
He ground again and again.
“God, who strikes, has struck her!
The puppy I told her to rear
She has made a singed puppy!
Without giving him her full attention,
God has struck the wretched girl!
My champion, unequaled among mortals,
She has made a cripple!
She herself is still a little girl,
A conceited young noblewoman;
I see that God has struck her!
The puppy I said to rear
She has made a singed puppy!”
He took her by the hand and swung her round,
And kicked her under the arm.
Before enjoying their happiness,
Before lying in her embrace,
Before gathering the learned men,
He seized her left hand and swung her round,
Kicked her under the arm,
And two of her ribs,
When the warrior's foot struck,
Were terribly shattered.
The twelve pairs of ribs
Nearly slipped out of place.
Then Kanykey,
Her heart broken,
Without speaking in protest,
Her tears pouring down,
'The blame is mine alone;
My own deed has overtaken me,' she thought.
She made no retort,
Nor complained of brow or eyebrow.
Because of that wrongful deed,
He laid punishment upon her
And left without lying in her embrace.
Kozubek, khan of Kokand,
Malabek of Margalan,
Fork-bearded Synchybek of Andijan,
The box-bearded Uzbek—
With these, the four khans' people were Sarts,
No strength to bind about one's waist,
But an affliction like an aching back.
As though that were not enough,
Dust rose from between,
And they came from lower India too.
Dwelling in Sary-Arka,
Called a weighty, formidable people,
With yurt lattices of wood,
Their forefather being Alach,
Hero Kökchö, Aydarkan's son,
Came to that feast.
Zhamgyrchy of the Eshtek,
And here Ormonbek of Khan Tekeche,
Summering in the Two Kemins,
Tethering a pair of black horses,
Sowing autumn rice,
Feeding the tethered horses its weeds,
Sharpening his dark tongue,
Speaking eloquently before the khan,
Gathering mixed folk into a people—
Hero Ürbü of Khan Eybit
Came to that feast as well.
From the people of lower Urum,
Muzburchak, Buudaykan's son,
He too came to the feast.
His wide eyes like the morning star,
His moonlike ears like shields,
Whose blessing enriched the people,
Whose care brought them light,
Khan Koshoy of the Katagan
Was there to speak impartially.
The twelve Muslim khans
At the marsh of Üyrülmö
Gathered round in council;
At the marsh of Chogulma
They assembled in council.
Laying their words before each other,
Gathering together,
Seeking and finding schemes,
The twelve Muslim khans—
What would they not gather?
To Manas, resting there,
What would they not do?
Upon Manas from above,
Why should they not descend?
That poor man's four kinds of livestock,
Why should they not scatter?
“Let us fall upon his head,
Let us scatter his four kinds of livestock.
Raiding and taking his mares,
Let us break his pride!
If Manas comes near,
Let us punch his brains out,
Let us break his pride!
Have you noticed Manas?
Have you seen his grand bearing,
Like a wineskin soaked in water,
Have you seen him swell with pride?
Chasing white deer,
Have you seen him hunt?
Openly among all the people,
Have you seen him stir up turmoil?
The dark forest and ravine,
Have you seen him make his enemy?
The poor man who fled to him,
Have you seen him make powerful?
Talas where he dwells,
Have you seen him make his homeland?
The companion who came from Khangay,
Have you seen him make a khan?
The troublemaker who fled to him,
Have you seen him make his equal?
Almambet and Chubak,
Have you seen him make them lions?
The twelve nations have not cast one;
Against Manas, with all this,
None remains to turn a lasso pole.
If we do not attack this Manas,
If we do not find a sure way to deal with him,
He will plunder the spoils;
Manas will stir up disorder!
Now Manas has
A dark fur cap as big as a cauldron.
If we let that poor man have his way,
Over all the people
He has the will to rule.”
Talking this nonsense,
The twelve Muslim khans
Concluded their consultation.
