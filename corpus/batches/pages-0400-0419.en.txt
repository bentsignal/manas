Not descend upon our very heads,
Crushing those who attack,
May he not drink blood like water!
He is a warrior, he is a lion;
Will he spare anyone who faces him?
Let us not die in regret;
Will he ever be sated with blood in combat?
Let us think wisely and act;
Let us restrain the army without fighting.
Let us send six swift horses,
With a letter the size of a palm,
And from Kakanchyn's Beejin
Gather a great army!
Then let us swarm upon him in battle,
Summon the sorcerers,
And then capture and bind him.
Just look at Manas:
He has a black cap like a cauldron;
The whole world,
He has the will to throw into turmoil!”
Then Zholoy spoke these words,
Words of horror beyond men's knowing:
“My ruler Esen sent us;
How can we leave without fighting?
If we should leave without fighting,
'You returned in fear of Manas,' he will say;
Our lord Khan Esenkan
May suddenly slaughter us.
Before this, when Esenkan
Gave his terrible order,
Giant Kaman and champion Döngö were there.
Against that Burut—may his house burn!—
Many of us rushed recklessly;
We swarmed in and fought.
To seize and bind the Burut,
Fierce wrath broke out.
Your giants Döngö and Kaman,
He slaughtered them utterly.
He brought great affliction;
Having the Kalmyks slaughtered,
He did whatever he pleased.
Against the Kalmyk who fell captive,
He unleashed terrible wrath;
Inflicting cruel humiliation,
He cut off his testicles.
He brought threefold turmoil at once.
Not merely my horses, that Burut
Raided and took my cattle.
Against this bloodstained one myself,
I attacked and learned suffering.
If I were my own master,
I would not attack the bloodstained one.
I myself would not grapple
With such a deceiver.”
Ezkara judged him and grieved;
The many soldiers who had swarmed there
Despaired of their precious lives.
Bells rang fiercely;
The army lost its senses.
To force gray-maned Manas to crouch,
The many lords prepared to overwhelm him.
Those swaggering unbelievers,
After Esenkan gave his order,
From Chynmachyn and Beejin,
Many heroes had arrived.
Loading ammunition on rhinoceroses,
Sounding trumpets and shawms,
Would those who came stand watching?
Without beginning battle and raising strife,
Would God strike the Kytay down?
They had taken orders from their khan;
Esenkan was their ruler,
They had sworn solemn oaths.
His blood-drinking champions,
Raising tumult and clamor,
Set off in a sweeping charge.
As his people migrated from Altay,
Such was the bloodstained hero's battle.
The Kytay intended to reach him,
That bloodstained one born fierce,
To crush him and pass on,
If they had the strength to fight,
To bind him and take him to the Kakan.
They had foot soldiers no horse could carry,
They had giants and mighty men.
Straining as though rabid,
Their champions held in collars,
Upon the gray-maned one touched by Khizir,
They swarmed and rushed.
Karazhoy, the great marksman,
Gathered his army and drove it hard;
He hurried his swift foot soldiers
And prepared along the bluff.
Black-maned Boroonchu,
The champion who struck with an iron-headed mace,
Took a thousand chosen troops.
Toward your bloodstained hero
He advanced in a sweeping charge.
Zholoy of the Kalmyks,
Deft and skilled with the spear,
Drove them onward without resting,
An excellent, expert spearman.
Great Döödür, riding a rhinoceros,
Rejoiced at the word of battle.
Such was that one accursed to be crushed:
Clad in deep-blue iron,
He rejoiced at the cry of 'Enemy!'
Told to drink water, he drank poison;
He never drew back in fear of a man,
He never retreated from death.
Their champions were renowned;
Facing one another,
They prepared to meet in battle.
Drawing bows to rain arrows,
They prepared to fall upon Manas,
Firing shot like hail.
They had outstanding men and giants;
Overwhelming the enemy in battle
Was their custom.
Riding rhinoceroses and elephants,
The mighty ones had arrived.
Firing guns and drawing bows,
They were esteemed for marksmanship.
When a black gun crashed,
They had warriors who would not flee;
When a fierce gun cracked,
They had warriors who would not startle.
From their great khan's presence,
To bring Manas bound,
To present him as a gift,
They had made thorough preparations.
Giants no horse could carry,
Esenkan had sent them,
Footmen swifter than horses.
Khans who would face him,
Esenkan had sent them;
Beyond any man's strength,
Champions like Ala-Too.
Like bulls fattened in summer,
He sent his great hulking men;
Half a mountain in size,
He sent his towering men.
Terrible slaughter in battle,
He sent those who would wreak it;
Simply binding whomever they looked upon,
He sent those who would seize them.
Facing toward Manas,
All raised their spears.
Now they unleashed fierce wrath;
Drawing bows to rain arrows,
They advanced in a sweeping charge!
