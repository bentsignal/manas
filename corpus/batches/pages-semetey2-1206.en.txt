Grinding against one another like millet.
His whole breast was fire and flame;
Unable to endure it, Sary Khan
Glowered and bristled,
His full courage rising like fur,
Rumbling as he broke away,
Looking like a gray lion—
Such was his fearsome aspect.
Taking up the red, mist-like banner,
Taking a tufted spear with a barbed blade,
And girding on his unsheathed sword—
Such was his prowess—
He prayed to the Creator:
“O soulless Jediger,
I shall mount my bannered charger;
Without regarding him as kin,
I shall drive this dog in every direction!
I shall unleash a storm of gunfire,
And rescue my noble wolf-born prince
From death, calamity,
And torment!”
He tugged and flung aside his mustache,
And tore wildly at his beard;
His whitening beard grew thin,
And, never sparing his life from the beginning,
Tears streamed from his two poor eyes,
Pouring down over his chest.
Making his bannered charger rear,
Sary Khan came into battle,
Destroying all who crossed his path.
Every ridge rang with cries;
The teeming countless host—
Mighty Sary Khan
Drove them back in confusion.
He routed them without leaving one behind
And drove them to the riverbank,
When, from a spur where the mountain vanished,
My broad-backed bay horse looming beneath him,
The slave Kyyaz appeared.
“What wrong have these countless people done?
Is not the khan of this black-hearted rabble
A fool himself?
I shall hew off with an axe
The head of Kyyaz, son of Bagysh;
If any of my former strength remains,
I shall bring such battle upon
This mad fool marked for death
That, turning back from Jeti-Özön,
He will never come again;
I shall fight the villain
And ensure he never returns!
I shall make him unable to cross
The forked pass and barren slope;
I shall make him unable to march
His agitated host through Talas!”
Sary Khan wheeled across his path
And turned his bannered charger’s head;
With his stout and mighty patterned spear,
Aiming straight at brave Kyyaz,
He thundered forward with leveled lance.
At that moment, brave Kyyaz,
When he saw Sary Khan,
Lost the strength in his wrists,
And the courage in his heart;
His ribs trembled,
And, unable to endure it, brave Kyyaz
Felt his fly-like soul quiver.
His strength failed and his power deserted him;
Over a saddle-shaped ridge
Brave Kyyaz fled,
Keeping beyond your uncle Sary’s reach;
Brave Kyyaz escaped,
Despairing of his precious life,
His face drained of color.
To the hero Kanchoro
Brave Kyyaz came alive.
When he reached Kanchoro,
A heavy exchange of gunfire began;
