His knees buckled to the ground,
And as he burned like a glowing ember,
Aichürök came running:
“You burned him before he could burn—
Burn to death yourself, brave Kyyaz!
You cooked him before he was ready—
Be cooked to death yourself, brave Kyyaz!
Would a hero such as you
Lay hands upon slaves and servants?
You lunged about like a madman;
Can anyone rightly call you a hero?
Accursed Kyyaz, may you come to ruin,
May you never find an equal!
Since time immemorial,
You have abused your bondwomen and slaves;
Accursed Kyyaz, you are beyond redemption.
Was this some host gathered by the thousand?
Among your countless people,
Were there no other captive bondwomen or slaves
Except us?
You cooked him before he was ready—
Be cooked to death yourself, Kyyaz!”
Gathering her courage, Aichürök
Shoved mountain-like Kyyaz
And sent him sprawling;
Then she pulled tiny Choro
Out from beneath him.
I thought you mountain-like, Kyyaz,
But you are no larger than a knucklebone!
It seems you are the Kyyaz destined
To see the true abode from which none return!
You shall lie sprawled in the open steppe;
You are the Kyyaz who is to die!
Just you wait, brave Kyyaz!
Because of you, brave Kyyaz,
If I do not don my white-swan plumage,
If I do not soar through the sky,
If I do not destroy you utterly,
If I do not muster my father Akun’s army—
Drawing my bow till arrows rain down—
Why have I not seized and slaughtered him already?
This slave’s appointed death has come;
Now I shall take his head,
Drag him along the road,
And hand him over to the Dangyts!
I shall gouge out his glaring eyes
And slaughter the rabid slave!”
Saying this, brave Kyyaz
Seized his black-edged battle-axe
And swung it around;
He struck down Choro,
The hero favored by fortune.
When mighty Kyyaz struck,
His strength failed and his body weakened,
And brave Choro fell.
Before Khan Kyyaz,
The powerless Choro collapsed;
All his ribs
Were shattered by the blows.
Grunting, Kyyaz kicked him
And tortured Choro.
As brave Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
Was about to show his might,
And was about to cut off
The unconscious Choro’s head,
Aichürök, the lady in the palace,
Heard the newcomers say
That Almambet’s Choro
Was being killed
By Khan Kyyaz before their eyes.
Her heart sank and broke;
Your sister-in-law’s tears poured forth.
From her quail-like eyes,
Tears fell one after another;
From her blackcurrant-dark eyes,
Streams of tears fell a whip’s length.
Unable to stop her running nose,
Unable to wipe away her tears,
She burst into piercing cries
And wailed in bitter anguish.
