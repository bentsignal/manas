If they suddenly overrun us,
If they unexpectedly make war,
They will pass through
And slaughter all Afghanistan here.
Their army can enter over ridge after ridge;
If we say we will not give you, sister,
Their renowned great Kozho
Will advance and overwhelm us!
The army of the Zhediger
Will overrun us together
And destroy us all together;
They have mighty Toltoy,
Who, once aroused, spares no soul.
If we do not agree to what he says,
If we do not give you as tribute,
If we stand opposed across from them,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
And clash against them in battle,
While arrows whistle,
And strike their marks with a sting,
And the people perish screaming,
What would become of us?
Why not heed
The words I have spoken, Aychürök?
He too is a father's son;
Why not go
To that hero Toltoy, Aychürök?
Shining in a coat of mail,
You have no choice but to go;
After facing them and fighting,
We have no choice but to give you
To those barking dogs.
Our strength is spent and our power gone;
We have no means left to take counsel.
Chürökbay, if we fight
On your account, blood will flow.
After sitting deep in thought,
We have resolved to heed your word;
We have resolved to find the means
To give you as tribute to Toltoy.
Without taking offense or resisting,
We have now found the means;
Finish all your objections!
From over there we have found
Your husband, lion Toltoy!
When all recourse is slipping away,
Toltoy of the Zhediger
Is also a father's son.
Now heed our words;
The people will be slaughtered for you—
Go to brave Toltoy!"
When they had spoken thus,
Chürök then said this:
"Azhybay and Tümönbay,
My two dear elder brothers,
I shall heed what you say.
My father is old and I am a girl;
What choice have I, what can I do?
I shall go to your Toltoy!
My dear two elder brothers,
Your manhood has become clear.
Why not agree to what he says,
Take from Toltoy of the Zhediger
Livestock enough to make the earth sag,
And only then give me away?
Have you killed countless men
Among the Zhediger people
And become blood-debtors?
Have you become plunder-debtors
For stealing camels?
If you have no such fear,
Have you given me as a fine
Because you rode no dappled pacing colt,
Ate no knucklebone's meat,
Rode no spotted pacing horse,
And ate no rich joint of meat?
The Zhediger who would take a wife—
Why not raise a great tumult against them?
