For Chürök, daughter of Akun,
They said, “This is the feast given to the people.”
Where the great Akkan takes its source,
In broad, pine-clad Talas,
Along the banks where the people assembled,
On the plain of Üch-Kapkak,
Beside the Ukuruk,
Along the Aigyr-Jal,
At the memorial feast of Kökötöy, father of the people—
That warrior Kökötöy’s
Feast from which he would never return—
Before all the multitude,
With the people’s mightiest giants,
Bloodthirsty Manas and Er Koshoy,
And warrior Bakai at their head,
They slaughtered barren yearling colts,
Setting vodka, bozo, and costly food
Before everyone without distinction.
Er Töshtük, son of Eleman,
Jamgyrchy of the Eshtек people,
Khan Kökchö of the Kazakhs,
Chambyl and Muzburchak the fearsome—
These were chosen as impartial witnesses.
“In the high seat on Judgment Day,
Let this be a covenant for all time;
Let such great men as these
Stand as witnesses.
All who have heard, great and small,
And even the most insolent enemies,
Heed these words.”
Showing the whites of their teeth,
They slaughtered livestock fatter than horses
And divided the abundant wealth and animals received
Equally among them.
Barren yearling colts and white-gray mares
Were brought forward with the blessing;
So much wealth
Was spent and given away.
A white wether with a yellow head
Was sacrificed with a cry of “Baabedin!”
They devised wonders no man could imagine
And held a feast for all the people;
Great herds of all four kinds of livestock
Were lavishly slaughtered.
The poor and starving, beset by endless hardship,
Ate their fill at that feast.
White fat lay there like a lake,
Delighting even the eyes
Of those who merely stood and watched.
They became sacred in-laws and feasted on fat,
Gathering the people to honor her.
Such was the measure of Chürök,
Such the singular bearing of that wondrous beauty.
The people clashed and the nation stirred,
Her splendor beyond all reckoning;
The woman who was Akun’s daughter,
Whose fame stirred the people of the age,
That shimmering enchantress,
Whose magnificence lay serene.
The fame of this noble-born Chürök
Became renowned throughout the age.
Konurbai of the Kara-Kytai
Heard word of her;
That Konurbai grew frenzied,
And his whole world was shaken.
Livestock was gathered in the midst,
His foremost troops were slaughtered,
And turmoil and strife erupted there.
Her beauty became known;
All whose ears heard and eyes beheld it—
All the people were thrown into turmoil.
“If only that wondrous Aichürök
Were our wife,” they said;
Women and men alike raged like dogs,
And those dog-hearted people grieved.
The power of this wondrous Chürök’s service,
Her force and venom, were great;
Before this shimmering marvel,
Every soul in the world despaired.
“This girl is beautiful,” they said,
