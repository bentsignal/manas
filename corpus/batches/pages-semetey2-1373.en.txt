She spoke softly, yet firmly,
The moon-faced lady, in anguish,
And to brave Kyyaz and Kanchoro,
To those two she began to speak:
“Panther born of a mongrel,
Brave Kyyaz, born of a raven,
How can I take you as my ‘companion’?
How can I overcome my grief,
How can I lie beside a wastrel?
Panther born of a falcon,
Brave Kyyaz, born of a crow,
How can I bare my breast?
How can I share his bed?
This Kyyaz who struck you from above—
Is it right that he should take
The bed of the noble gray-maned hero,
Or that he should rise after striking from above?
And is it right that he should take
The pillow of the gray-maned hero?
Or rise after striking from beside him?
The two reins and the single lead—
How can I take them in my hands?
Without suffering disgrace, calamity, and strife,
How can I go to the Jediger?
After my magnanimous lord, great as Ala-Too,
How can I remain alive?
Hearing endless gossip among the people,
The pampered lady Chachykey
Will hear that I took Kanchoro,
Her heart will surge with grief,
And tears will stream from her eyes.
In her old age, Kanykey
Has been sustained by Aichürök,
Who has watched over her,
And sheltered the child left behind,
Keeping him beneath her protection,
Though her lovely face was wounded.
Her valor flared, her eyes flashed,
And the lone woman’s sorrow boiled.
Then Aichürök
Said this to Khan Kyyaz,
Telling him of dreadful calamity;
When she began, she spoke thus:
“Such words are unknown among kin.”
Then she addressed Kanchoro,
Her blood boiling as she stood,
Ready to spring like a white lion,
With no dear kinsman left,
And said this to Kanchoro:
“Upon Buurul, who would not yield when asked,
My choro, you cast your cold lasso.
His beloved Chürök, who would not yield to entreaty,
You have taken as plunder;
You have turned against your own kin,
Murdered your own uncle,
And, choro, become monstrous beyond all others!
You began a campaign against your elder brother,
And left sixty noble widows
To make the village desolate.
My choro, you brought misery upon your lord;
You left the few Kyrgyz of mighty Ala-Too
And your homeland desolate!
My choro, Jylgyn, Keng-Kol,
And broad Talas—
You have left your lands desolate;
The prosperous Kyrgyz and the few Argyn—
You have left your people desolate.
Now who but he will whet
Black-edged Zulpukor till it bites?
If the countless Argyn and many Kyrgyz
Are surrounded and fired upon by the infidel,
Who will lead them out of battle?
Who but he will whet
The daring, mighty Zulpukor?
If countless surging foes arise,
If enemies overrun Ala-Too,
Who will rally and lead
This people standing forlorn?
