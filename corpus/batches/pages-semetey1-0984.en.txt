From before the riverbank,
Wise elder, tell us:
We lost Akshunkar!
We saw a swan of wondrous power;
Akshunkar was carried away,
And we saw turmoil fall into calamity.
These were my elder Semetey’s words:
‘My exceedingly wise elder,
My elder, the tiger Bakay,
Will tell us
Where the falcon went.’”
After the boys had said this,
The aged hero Bakay
Sent for Jamgyrchy of the Esheks
Together with his wife,
And had Sarykan of the bowl-shaped eyes
Brought along with him.
They hurried and bustled,
Asking what disaster lay ahead.
“He flew his bird at a swan;
The swan is gone, and so is the bird,
And he has remained on the empty plain.
May God spare us such a sight!
When my age-mate Manas lived,
If Akshunkar was flown at a swan,
It would strike the swan down;
Not only a swan—it would tower above
And take the creatures of the mountains as well.
As for the falcon that flew away,
If what they say is true,
I must consider this very carefully.
Let all three khans gather,
With the three senior wives and ourselves,
And place the matter among us.
Let us go swiftly without delay,
Put the three old women between us,
And obtain an answer from the daughter-in-law.
The swan is gone, and so is the bird;
It must be a time of God’s punishment,
And poor Semetey
Must be standing there exhausted.”
Hearing the boys’ account,
And hearing that Chachykey knew
What had become of Akshunkar,
The lion was deeply stirred,
And blue-maned Bakay mounted.
They joined Bakay,
And all six rode out together
To Kanykey’s encampment,
To Chachykey’s gated fortress.
On the marsh of gathering,
They gathered in a circle;
On the marsh of assembly,
They all assembled together.
They placed the matter among them,
Conversed and questioned one another,
Matched their findings step by step,
And formed a khan’s council;
It became a great gathering.
There were wise and aged people,
And all the blue-maned champions were there:
The tiger Bakay and Jamgyrchy,
And your elder Sary as well.
Kanykey, the wife of Manas,
The wise, fierce woman, was also there.
When only khans had arrived,
They began the splendid proceedings:
They had tender lamb and sweet head-meat
Set boiling in broth,
Spread thick felt carpets,
And filled them with yellow butter.
They cut rump meat and laid out mane fat,
And set arak and honey as drink,
Preparing to receive their guests.
Pleading with Chachykey,
“Tell the khans the truth,”
They sent three people forward,
