So that none could retreat,
And whoever met it would know its true nature;
It had been tempered
With human tears.
Along its flank
It was covered with black horsehair,
A tempered spear fit for war,
Forged by Sarybay—
Such was the manner of this strife.
When it found the measured mark,
When it struck the mortal place,
He did not flinch though it rang and rang;
Even when death was at hand,
He counted it less than a trifle.
He neither reeled nor stirred,
The fearsome wrestler, mighty in wrath,
Never budged a hair.
Furious yet unshaken,
Strength and gigantic stature
The Lord Most High had bestowed
Upon that swine without measure.
Without blinking or twitching,
Sarybay stood utterly still.
When Sarybay was pierced,
The ancient tempered steel spear,
The precious heirloom from his father,
Splintered through the middle.
Then the prowess of brave, gray-maned Sarybay
Was made manifest
And revealed before the warriors.
He could shatter any mountain he met,
While the white steed broad as a house
Flew thundering over the plain.
There was no time to ready another spear—
Damn this filthy world!
No other course remained.
When laid to turf it kindled fire;
Whatever it struck died in an instant;
Wherever it landed, blood flowed,
Wherever it slashed, life departed.
Life fled at its stroke:
The Zulfiqar inherited
From gray-maned Manas of old.
Seizing it by the hilt,
Drawing it from its sheath,
He reached him before one could shout, “Strike!”
And with it the noble lion
Slashed once again as he passed.
The other never saw the stroke, not even by a hair;
From the calamity of doom and death,
The swine saw no escape at last.
Roaring in menace,
Bellowing in rage,
Cracking like lightning,
Thunderous as a dragon,
He made every onlooker’s
Whole body shudder.
His wrestler’s form stood revealed
As he girded on his blue-mottled sword,
Sprang like a blue tiger,
Poured out his wrath and ground his teeth;
His attacking arm knotted with power,
His fury made the blood run cold,
And when enraged, misery boiled over.
The accursed monster’s son,
With his cast-iron head broad as a house—
Flee far away if you see his deeds!
He hacked down all who crossed him,
Gnawed apart all he encountered,
Destroyed all he set his eyes upon,
Reduced matchlock-men to ashes,
Filled his quiver with arrows,
And annihilated all who fought him.
That was the deft one, that was the lion,
That was the man endowed with courage.
He turned upon brave Seytek,
Raising his battle-axe and sword
Higher and higher,
His black eyes filling with blood.
