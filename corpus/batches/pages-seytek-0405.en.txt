And the quarrel will deepen in the end.
When this sly one runs out of schemes,
They will pitch their camp beside you.
Saying, “We were born as true brothers,”
They will swear oaths to each other.
Growing fat and thriving in secret,
They will gather armor that arrows cannot pierce,
And muster troops from the foremost peoples.
They will steep poison in your churn,
And thus bring about your end.
Your gifts will not be worth a coin
When hardship rises boldly against you.
Your kindness will melt like an ice floe;
Your great wealth will not be worth a scrap of iron.
May the crooked-hearted wretch go mad;
When he grows fat, he will ravage your borders.
All your works will become like charcoal;
When he grows strong, he will ruin your camp.
He will divide your fame down the middle,
And set your village ablaze.
He will turn your wife into an enemy,
At last destroying your honor,
Exhausting the reach of all your designs,
And utterly stripping away your worth.
Yesterday, when an army covering the earth arrived,
Külchoro, were you not there in the fray?
When mortal lives fell thick as flies,
When cunning men broke the ranks,
And many lives were shot away in battle,
When steeds wheeled under charging riders,
When the uproar split the sky,
And heroes met in combat,
From among your own men came
A double-tongued saboteur.
You two Jediger stand side by side;
May this accursed wretch sink into the earth!
This is the very face of treachery,
Broad-tongued and four-square,
This is the very face of deceit.
On the rise beyond the branching pass,
You looked askance across your ridge,
Upon your murderous, turbulent evil—
If only the earth would bear it.
You betrayed us by revealing the secret,
You burned the palace and spread the fire,
You laid waste to all and seized the frontier.
You turned your words toward treason;
Burn to death, you mad wretch!
You stirred up countless feuds and quarrels.
Whenever you wished, in this land—
Where is the blessed night, the night of joy?
Yesterday, when bored, we hunted roe deer;
When not bored, the dear people
Romped and played freely.
Where are the days we had before?
Ruling the people and keeping the nation,
You have become sultan of the Argyn.
Shattering all these people to pieces,
You have sucked them dry like a black leech.
You brought slaughter and seized the frontier,
You destroyed all and lit a fire within,
You sharpened your spear to excess,
You began your wicked frenzy.
Bent old folk and young children
You left without descendants.
You swaggering madman,
Your sin will fall upon yourself,
Your guilt upon your black-crowned head.
Can one prosper by harboring a traitor?
Wild in thought and foul at heart,
Strange in tidings and mighty in venom—
Can Kanchoro truly be a brother?
Would a son of man tend as a brother
A boar that gores from among the herd,
Or call a dragon his kin?
Without taking revenge on that burning wretch,
Dear brother-in-law who made him your guardian,
Can your deed be pleasing to God?
At heart he is our enemy;
In the end, the conflict will multiply,
