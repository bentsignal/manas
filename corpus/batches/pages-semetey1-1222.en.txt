From your ancient Kyrgyz people,

Will not leave you unharmed if you live;

Let it go, Kozho, my dear friend;
There is a great flaw in your words.
Will he agree to what you say?
Will he give us his widow?
Chynkozho, son of Shygay,
These words of yours are not right.
Semetey, son of Manas,
Is no slave who would give us his wife.
All the saints came together;
He is a son formed by their sovereign.
Kanykei and Manas together
Pieced this son together.
Before the multitude, he was a son
They asked God to grant.
He is the spring flowing from the valley,
The lamp that blue-maned Manas himself
Desired and received.
If we seize and take his wife,
Will he stand watching without a quarrel?
Kozho, you have blustering words
That bring you disgrace.
If we take his wife,
Semetey, son of Manas,
May bring a great slaughter upon us.
If we seize and take Chürök,
When Semetey comes tomorrow,
Will he not make
Your great Kyrgyz settlement boil in misery?
When Semetey sweeps down upon us,
Will he not show us wonders?
Let us not fall into calamity;
Poor Kozho, heed my words,
Let us not take Aychürök.
What shall we do with Chürök?
While we are safe and sound,
Come, Kozho, let us leave.
Let us not take the beautiful girl Chürök;
Let us not fall into calamity.
You have courage, laughable man;
You say you are Manas's equal.
Many Argyn fill Jeti-Özön;
You are truly accomplished,
So what have you become, commoner?
For now, while you swagger,
Let us not seize Aychürök.
Setting out to take Aychürök,
We are only a small people;
Let us not all fall into torment.”
