He began the quarrel with a deep growl,
Such again was Sarybay,
A man fully prepared for battle,
Those who heard with ears and saw with eyes
Called him Sarybay the Devourer,
Toward the poor and powerless,
He seemed never to look back,
Ready now, in his madness,
To consume the entire world,
His aspect strange, his venom strong,
“If you see his wrath, I am dead,” they said,
“Abandon hope for your precious life,”
His thoroughbred horse was Tookyzyld,
He saddled Tookyzyld,
Arranged his arms and armor,
And buckled on his flashing sword,
When the bloodthirsty man reared for battle,
His appearance utterly transformed,
He seemed ready to drive the whole world
Before him and slaughter it,
His shaggy hair stood bristling,
Then its wild rising subsided,
A hazy crimson fire
Scattered all around,
He screamed, pouring out his wrath,
Making the wrestlers flatten their ears,
And now committed a dreadful deed:
“My name is Sarybay,
Let harsh affliction be revealed,
Let blood and gore pour from combat,
Do not go on living—pass away,
Without leaving a single one,
Go and slaughter one another!
My mighty men, my warriors,
My lions who stand beside me,
Bold men girded for war,
Who destroy all they confront,
My champions, my finest men,
Those masterfully skilled with spears,
Stalwarts accomplished in cunning!”
Thus speaking, Sarybay
Set the battle order firmly,
Mounted his thoroughbred Tookyzyld,
Knotted its slender tail tight,
And, furious and prepared,
Drove out all his army,
The raging foe raised a clamor,
The sky resounded with the din,
The earth shook and mountains moved,
Piercing war cries arose,
Utter and final was the great conflict,
And mortals despaired of life,
The fierce fury of the swine,
His savage madness, became apparent,
Then Sarybay,
His eyes like hollows in a ravine,
Seemed ready to swallow his challenger,
To slaughter whoever he charged,
To ruin whoever crossed him,
And utterly wipe them out—
Such was the sight of his wrath,
He was a champion, a bulwark,
Sarybay was a mighty wrestler,
Who spared no life when grappling,
Who could not be sated with a colt's flesh,
Who mangled whoever crossed him,
And truly inflicted torment
On every mortal who confronted him,
Driving an army of tens of thousands,
He gathered all who remained
From among the dead and drove them on,
Whichever side met him was destroyed,
As he advanced and unleashed conflict,
A red haze rose like fire,
A great conflict of dire affliction,
Left every mortal full of regret,
That swift slave Sarybay,
A wicked slave in all he sought,
A baleful slave in all he observed,
His fury and lionheartedness
