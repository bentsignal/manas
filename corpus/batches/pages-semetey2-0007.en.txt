No vulture circles overhead;
My leopard Karagul,
There is none greater than me.
There is no boat upon the black water;
While my splendid head remains,
There is no one who can answer me back.
There is no boat upon the crosswise water;
While my falcon head remains,
There is no one who can equal me.
My argali lies upon my mountain pass,
My duck lies upon my lake.
My fox lies, Karagul,
Where I placed it in safekeeping.
Great Beijing is a wondrous city—
I traveled to its farthest edge;
All Beijing is an ancient city—
I traveled to its very foundation.
Giving Algara no rest,
I, a renowned great noble,
Came roaming back from the Kakan.
No pass remains that I have not crossed,
No land remains that I have not trodden;
Crossing passes and treading stones,
Algara grew lean,
His manifest blood thickened,
And Algara grew old.
I traveled through many lands;
There is no gray, ridged mountain left,
And no enemy left to face me.
No foe remains for me to grapple with;
I will turn Algara loose—
Let him grow stout, with no regret.
In winter, take the horses
Up to Kyr-Kayyn to winter.
For every horse herder,
Have marmot fur sewn upon his shoulders.
When autumn comes,
Graze them in autumn at gray Kürpüldök;
When spring comes,
Drive them along the Sary-Suu!
Great Lord Khan Kongurbai—
Have you heard,
Worthy lord, of the deeds I have done?
My brow is high and my venom strong;
While my splendid life remains,
I will straighten out anyone who challenges me!
Will anyone approach and escape me?
Will one who meets me pass without dying?
Beneath me is Algara;
I possess my courser Algara—
Will one who fights me remain alive?
While my splendid life remains,
Will anyone come swarming against me?
Will a man grapple with me and win?
Will any doomed man come before me?
Will he bend me and take me captive?
Will one who wrestles with me remain alive?
My saddlecloth is made of leather;
Known throughout this world
Is my valor, Kongurbai’s valor!
Yesterday—have you heard?
When chaos broke loose,
When the Bloodthirsty One came
And wrested away the khanate of Beijing,
When his undertaking reached Beijing,
Untouched even by Alexander,
When he took countless riches and girls
As plunder,
When he ravaged the throne,
And slaughtered many heroes,
Dust rose from his advance
And souls fled at the sound of him.
In his fury, Khan Manas
Drained the people of the Kakan;
That Bloodthirsty One whose home burned
At last died by my own hand.
Infidel and Muslim
Stood their ground and fought,
