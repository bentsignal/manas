He deceived the Kalmaks while urging the horse.
While champion Almam lived,
He deceived the Chinese while urging the horse.
The seething multitude
Entered among the Solon.
He raised the Solon battle cry
And urged past the Solon;
He raised the Shivei battle cry
And now urged past the Shivei.
While champion Almam lived,
He truly carried out a difficult stratagem.
Failing to recognize Akkula,
He threw the Chinese into confusion.
The heroism
Of valiant gray wolf Almam became known.
As he went farther on,
He reached the people of Islam.
Khan Manas's forty companions
Now remained shouting encouragement in ruin.
This creature Akkula
Had lowered his head to the ground.
The forty companions who urged him on
Failed to see
That Akkula had broken away.
They did not notice Akkula leaving.
Let us leave that there
And begin the tale
Of the open-hearted noble lion.
Upon Aybanboz, streaming along,
Noble lion Manas
Had entered the clamor.
Making his gray horse leap,
Hero lion Manas,
Within that tumult,
Caught sight of Achbuudan.
The lion raged;
His eyes blazed like fire,
His anguish smoked fiercely,
And bloodlust seized him.
Hero Joloy's Achbuudan
Ran out in front;
How could it be left behind?
Kökötöy's countless prizes—
How could the Black Kalmaks and Manchus
Be allowed to take them?
Rather than give the prize to China,
Let us die and cease living;
Let us sink away as though unborn.
Akkula, raced with honor,
That gliding creature fit for slaughter,
Was a beast unlike other horses;
Kökötöy's red banner—
This year was the time for him to win it.
Were his fetlocks only a handspan,
Had he become a frog?
Were his heels only a handspan,
Had he become a pig?
Yesterday, when we fled from Altai,
When I scattered my countless wealth,
Against the Black Kalmak people,
Lying on opposite banks,
Exchanging musket fire,
Waging fierce battle,
When Toruchaar fell to a bullet,
When the great Chinese and Manchu host
Surged upon us and brought turmoil,
When Toruchaar was shot down
And I was left looming on foot,
Khan Koshoy of Katagan
Gave Telkyzyl as a gift.
When I mounted Telkyzyl,
No tulpar could match him;
To preserve my life,
Stars were eclipsed in the sky,
The saintly spirits supported me,
And from the Black Chinese and Manchus
I barely escaped alive,
