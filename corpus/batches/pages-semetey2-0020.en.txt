With the moon as our streaming banner,
With clamor splitting the sky,
With the sun as our streaming banner,
With clamor splitting the blue heavens,
Then let us make war.
Stripping him of horse and armor,
Let us make hero Khan Kongurbai
Into such a spectacle.
Upon the Kalcha, khan of the monks,
Let us bring turmoil that cannot be crossed;
From that Kalcha, khan of the monks,
Let us exact our vengeance.
Let horses grow fat and ready,
And the gathered army stand massed.
May resolute youths and swift horses
Reach the time when they prove useful.
My son, if you heed me, do this.
May the road be blessed by saints;
For Beijing, let there be
No fewer than one hundred twenty thousand troops.
If you make war upon Beijing,
Let them be abundant, not few.
Let renowned giants come,
Heroes come to Beijing
Who do not retreat a hair from death,
Who never turn away from dying;
Let such warriors come to Beijing.
When the fierce gun cracks,
Let warriors come to Beijing
Who fight without flinching.
When the black gun bangs,
Let warriors come to Beijing
Who stand and fight without fleeing.
When we stand on opposite banks,
Shouting and leveling spears,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
Clashing together in battle,
With arrows whirring
And wounds burning,
May they perish, struck by bullets,
And spill blood in streams.
If you come to Beijing, come thus;
In the land of Beijing,
If you fight, fight in this way
Against the Chinese hero Khan Kongurbai.
Understand what I have said;
Your uncle Joodur has spoken.
My lion, my son, heed me.”
When Semetey heard this, he said:
“My uncle whom I regard as a father,
What you say is wise.
If I refuse to heed you,
May my six forefathers be infidels.”
Foolishness was struck by God
From father to grandfather:
His lion uncle Bakai was absent,
And he let himself be deceived repeatedly
By Karagul himself.
Karagul, son of Katal—
Here was black calamity.
That raging swine's
Deed was done in this way;
Upon the young son of Manas
He laid a singular corruption.
Foolishly born Semetey
Said he would obey him;
To the deceiver he said,
“I shall give you my mother.”
Then Karagul—
God had now struck the slave—
Took the gold-cheeked battle-axe
And braced it at his waist,
And now invoked Manas too.
Saying, “This is my true kinsman,”
God now struck the prince.
