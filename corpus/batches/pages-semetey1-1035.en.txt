Sixty anxious thoughts came upon her at once:
"May the striking God strike him—
Would he take a woman here in the open steppe?
He has made my words into an idle diversion
And put me to shame.
I, Aichurok, daughter of Akun,
Will employ a stratagem
And escape somehow
From this swaggering unfortunate man.
Do not mock me, my gray-maned hero!
There are bearded men and elders here;
All the people are present,
And people of sound judgment are among them.
Some people go about everywhere
Occupied with gossip.
The sun will set before we notice.
Restrain your desire;
Do not loom over my breast—
Take your hand away!
You swaggering unfortunate man,
Can a bridegroom behave as you do?
Is it right for a bridegroom to arrive roaring
And keep pressing himself upon me?
Watch what lies behind you, gray-maned hero;
For scolding your Choro so harshly,
I shall give you a taste of your own affliction!
This conduct of yours is foolish.
Gather your senses firmly
And stop lunging forward!
Your courage is known in the pleasure house;
If you would make sport, your Kyrgyz people
Are subjects inherited from your magnanimous father.
Your Argyn and Kyrgyz people are not here;
Do not keep pressing without cause!"
Having said this, Aichurok
From her breast, the lord's
Hand she pulled away.
The overbearing lion
Was left ashamed.
His father Manas had pursued enemies;
Kanykei had borne the hero,
Yet he had never before been so distressed
Or suffered such shame.
Aichurok, the daughter of Akun,
Removed the lord's hand from her breast.
Your overbearing lord
Was left ashamed.
He stamped about in fury
And brought down a dark day.
The lion grew enraged
And began dark speech;
Let alone Churok—he spoke
As though he would slaughter Akun himself.
If you would see a marvel, see it now:
Raging and surging with anger,
The lion let loose
And scolded Aichurok:
"I shall become a companion
To maidens gathering reeds.
Come here, you bitch;
Give me the falcon, and let me go!
I shall become an equal
To maidens gathering dung.
You are one of the lowborn;
Give me the falcon, and let me go!
To an old crone like you who has taken no husband,
To a broad-soled hag,
I shall keep pouring out my contempt.
Give me the falcon, and let me go!
You stripped the weariness from your horse
And flew wandering
Throughout the whole wide world.
There is no place you have not worshiped,
And no man you have not visited.
Old slave woman who has taken no husband,
Wretch who blocks the traveler's road—
Until your hair becomes white as a swan,
Until your teeth become grains of wheat,
