And bewitched the sun and weather.
Every living creature
Whose death was due was overtaken,
While all whose hour had not come
Entered their dens and escaped.
All the jagged mountains
Were caught and wrapped in white snow,
While the grazing wild asses
Fled into the desert and escaped.
His force numbered a hundred men,
And they loaded the warriors’ steeds
With provisions.
They drew arrows loosely from their bows,
And the accursed one whose soul was spent
Took all his sworn companions with him.
Some were skilled at finding the road,
And among the champions he had tested,
Each possessed the strength of a thousand men.
Their courage was mighty and their venom strong;
Such is the nature of a holy war,
And hardship came upon precious lives.
The heroes rode in splendor,
While strings of inverted beads
Glittered upon their heads.
Precious stones hung there,
Revealing their Chinese appearance,
And glass lenses gleamed before their eyes.
Black hair like a resting camel
Shone across their broad shoulders.
Their long trousers and ample breeches
Were fashioned from the hides
Of sixty he-goats,
With the trouser legs plainly visible.
Chasing wild asses and shooting ibex,
The frenzied swine
Plunged deep into their sport.
They watched the approaches and surveyed the land,
Raising a clamor when they climbed a ridge.
They held a rank above ordinary men,
For bloodshed alone
Those ill-omened ones had prepared.
Those who had ridden out from Beijing
Were infidels of uncommon courage,
Each a warrior worth a thousand men.
They raged at the very word “enemy,”
And rejoiced at the mention of death,
Heroes who roamed the world,
Warriors who could find no rest.
Along Kapsang grew black juniper;
They threaded their way beside the black junipers,
And with their dark mass of countless souls
They roamed through the gold mines.
They skirted the mountains of Chai-Algan,
Following the course of Karshan,
Crossing many a humped mountain
And fording many a bare ravine.
They wearied their horses and surveyed the land,
Covering vast distances and raising a clamor,
Chasing wild asses and shooting ibex,
And reveling deeply in many pleasures.
Along the myriad roads from deepest Beijing
They came traveling by night,
From gloomy Beijing
They came traveling by day.
Through gorges trodden only by wild boars
They journeyed without fear,
Across flats where even foxes never roamed
They rode their horses hard and far.
The moon waned and the days passed,
And many days went by.
The horses grew restless and pent up,
While all the heroes found their rhythm.
They never halted by day,
Nor took their daylight rest.
A dark ridge appeared,
A crumbling slope where no goat could pass;
They descended the broken slope,
And along the hills lay At-Oinok.
They crossed At-Oinok and now made camp,
