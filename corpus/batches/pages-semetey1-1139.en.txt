Helmets of blue iron—
So the heroic youths would not stand idle
If an enemy came from a known place,
But would go to face him;
If they stood brow to brow,
Struck with battle-axes,
And fought with cries,
A battle-axe might strike them
And death come needlessly.
The forty helmets she made for the forty
Made their strength known;
She overturned them onto their heads.
The kandagay she made for the forty
Was of Ashagan-Mashagan hide,
Fashioned by your cunning sister-in-law.
She had it steeped in dressing,
And saturated with measured dye,
Immersed in many preparations,
And laid in the shade.
She gave a horse for each month,
And gold and jewels by the measure each day.
From men who came without peer,
Marksmen of evident perfect skill
Who could shoot an eye,
She had provisions loaded upon bil
And sent them to survey the earth.
Hearing from many people
That the hide of the ibex of Too-Kuygak
Was thick,
She placed the sewing of the kandagay
Under Aruuke's direction;
Exactly ninety-five champions
Worked the hide for ninety days.
She had its inside lined with silk
And gathered skilled women and girls
To bite and soften it.
She had its unfit parts cut away;
So that bullets would glance aside,
She had plate upon plate of steel
Poured into its middle.
The kandagay was fashioned
To fit the forty companions exactly.
It satisfied the heart when seen;
Whole beaver skins
She split open,
And had a full span
Set at the ends of the sleeves.
At the sleeve ends,
The fearsome beaver trim
At the bend of the knee,
So it would never slip,
Was embroidered with pure gold.
Let whoever jousts stagger;
Infidel and Muslim alike
Would know it was Kökötöy's feast,
And whoever saw it would marvel.
It was named kandagay;
Come, boys, put it on openly
And know the gray-wolf courage
Of your unfortunate sister-in-law.
The matter is hard as I tell it;
I shall give another article.
Come, boys, put it on.
If you ask its origin,
It is costly leather.
I thought my forty falcons
Would recognize this labor;
I knew of this matter long ago.
At Kökötöy's feast,
Before the great gathering,
I thought you would wear
The ceremonial boots.
Passing them from master to master,
Having experts assess them,
For the price of one boot
I had given one horse.
For all forty I gave forty horses;
