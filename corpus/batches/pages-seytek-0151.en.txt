He seemed somehow different,
With the look of one who would interrogate,
Yet he did not seize the reins
And turn the mouth of the sorrel, hardened by trials.
Saying, “It is the funeral feast of my father, Khan Bakai,”
We did not take charge of the feast;
We did not break open with axes
The countless treasuries and myriad stores.
At Khan Bakai’s funeral feast
We did not scatter riches lavishly.
We did not throng the lower road,
Infidel and Muslim alike,
All crying out, “Khan Bakai!”
We did not honor him with a funeral feast;
Infidel and Muslim alike,
We never gathered everyone together;
For my uncle, sovereign Bakai,
We did not hold a splendid feast.
Under the enemy’s bullets of steel,
In the cruel snare of suffering,
My aged kinsman must be enduring torment
In Kanchoro’s hands.
Suffering much abuse from mighty men,
Weary Bakai must have grown;
His reason fled and senses lost,
Wretched Bakai must have grown.
To the appointed place of death
Bakai must have gone;
Stripped of mane and tail,
Alas, old Bakai
Must have been left hornless,
His horn broken, brought to shame,
He must have been left helpless.
The people did not cry out, the nation did not stir;
For my dear uncle Bakai
They held no honored funeral feast.
The land did not thunder, the people did not clamor;
My forefather, old Bakai,
Saw no such splendid rites.
Though grief must have smoldered and flared,
We cast no handfuls of earth;
We witnessed none of the rites,
And my warrior uncle Bakai
We did not lay in a deep-dug grave,
Saying, “The father of the nation is dead,”
Nor bury him with our own hands.
We did not gather at his hands and feet;
Upon my warrior uncle Bakai
We cast no handful of earth.
For my lionhearted uncle Bakai
We held no fitting funeral feast.
With six fleet-footed horses
And letters broad as a palm,
To all four corners of the world
We did not ride four circuits.
We did not summon the noble peoples
And hold Khan Bakai’s funeral feast.
The streamers of his great banner
We did not cut in motley red;
Saying, “My father Khan Bakai is dead,”
Infidel and Muslim alike,
We did not gather every last one.
At Bakai’s solemn funeral feast,
Infidel and Muslim alike,
With all the people gathered round,
At my father Khan Bakai’s feast,
Their weapons laid ready,
Bare swords belted on,
Matchlocks slung at their waists,
Raging like hungry lions,
The clamoring people fell silent;
Semetey, lofty as a mountain peak,
Slung his white musket over his shoulder.
The match of the white musket,
Flashing in many colors,
To an enemy watching warily
Appeared like a towering mountain.
When he opened his cavernous mouth,
From the gray wolf’s jaws
Blue flame came pouring forth.
