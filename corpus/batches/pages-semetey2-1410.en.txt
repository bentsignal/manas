It champed its bit,
Its eyes shining like a deer's;
Broad-chested, with a low mane,
Exceedingly fit to ride into combat,
The gray five-year-old was a splendid champion steed.
Though only an animal, the creature
Was a true friend to Karadöö;
It grew lean from running,
And when fully roused
It gnawed upon its bit.
His mount was the gray five-year-old;
Karadöö rode out for the spear contest.
If fate decreed it, he would die;
If Karagul had the strength,
Your hero had come out to contend,
To give the crowd a spectacle.
The dear hero who lived as booty,
Against a host of Zhediger men, stood alone;
Their strength could not match his assault.
Look at the child of the childless man:
Living among captors, he entered the contest,
Sparing not his precious life—
Look upon Karadöö!
Burning and blazing like an ember,
After Kyyaz took his sister-in-law,
How could poor Karadöö
Possibly remain still?
Shuddering, he sought an omen,
Walking in tears and sobbing.
When a young man grieves,
Is there a night like the Night of Power?
Will there be a day when I return
And see my land of Talas,
My Kyrgyz people in Talas, again?
O Almighty who raised a dwelling
In Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
Will I recline there again?
Twelve charges and eighty bullets
Have wound themselves around my breast;
Distraught and finding no recourse,
I live as booty
In the hands of the hero Kyyaz,
And grieve in shame without dying.
Twelve scouts in forty directions—
When I think of flying away,
Of reaching the place I long for,
I remember the fierce lion who supported me,
And how I was parted from my khan and elder;
I was thus parted from my sheltering dark ridge,
Thus have I been parted,
From a lion like Kanchoro.
If he is alive and has not perished,
The poor man I took as my refuge,
That precious hero called a lion—
If my elder Külchoro survives,
He may kindle the extinguished fire
And restore the soul that seemed dead.
If honored fortune swells and becomes full,
If the one my eyes once saw returns,
If the time comes to see my elder Kül,
The anguish in my breast may ease,
My clouded brow may open.
After six months of clamorous battle,
Ah, this ruined age!
If Kyyaz's grave is dug,
If the captive slaves and maids
Can open their brows in joy,
If wasted horses grow fat
And hungry stomachs are filled,
If Kanchoro, who ruined the people,
Has his dark eyes gouged out,
If all our sworn enemies
Are slaughtered as they deserve—
When I consider my heart's desire,
The knotted fetters will come undone;
May the Creator let me see again
My plundered Kyrgyz people.
Then the anguish will burst from my breast
