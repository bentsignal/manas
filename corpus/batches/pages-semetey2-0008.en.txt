When hero Kozhozhash, the great marksman,
Performed immeasurable feats of valor,
When Almambet and Chubak,
Baichoro and Syrgak,
Were all slaughtered in succession,
When Akkula fell to a bullet,
And yesterday, when Almambet died a martyr,
The blood-drinking Bloodthirsty One
Lifted his saddle and tack
And was left on foot in the field.
The lion’s courage at the edge of death—
The courage of that desperate
Burut doomed to die—
Was shown then.
His rage began in earnest;
Beginning with Kozhozhash,
He shot down eight
Of the treasury marksmen.
Gathering courage and making ready,
Six thousand Chinese and countless kaldais
Drew bows like pouring rain
And shot arrows like hail;
With my splendid self at their head,
We made ready and closed in to seize him.
Then powder spilled from his powder flask;
He despaired of his grieving life,
Stepped ponderously and braced himself,
Leaned upon Akkelte,
And that Bloodthirsty One stood there.
Before one could say this or that,
In the blink of an eye,
Riding a horse like a blue courser,
Wearing a blue lynx-fur cap,
His gray-wolf companion Bakai,
The Bloodthirsty One’s true confidant,
Opened the great slaughter.
Before one could say this or that,
In the blink of an eye,
He put the six thousand Chinese and countless kaldais
All to the sword and slaughtered them.
He revealed his might;
Then mighty Bakai
Mounted the champion Manas,
The Bloodthirsty One, upon Arbuurul.
His eyes blazed like fire;
The bloodlust truly seized
The accursed Burut,
And his rage began in earnest.
He utterly slaughtered
The Chinese as numerous as stones.
In the battle that day,
The mighty men in armor died,
And the stone-countless host was wiped out.
He mounted Taibuurul
And drove the teeming Chinese
As far as the Kakan.
His rage began in earnest;
Of the tightly barred gates,
He smashed five at once
And demolished them completely.
When the Bloodthirsty One withdrew,
I reconnoitered the road behind him;
I deliberately stayed and stalked him.
Azhybai, whom he posted as lookout,
Had fallen into a deep sleep;
I went straight toward him
And struck the thick shaft
Hard against solid bone as I passed.
My spear reached him,
And he went away wounded.
Concealing his wound without speaking,
The Bloodthirsty One let it fester and died, they say.
His own kin turned hostile,
And divided his possessions
In the hands of the orphaned child, they say.
That bitch Kanykei of old
Carried him away along the border,
And scattered much of her wealth, they say.
