Can anyone prosper who calls you father?
Let me burn off your remaining hair,
Pour in poison and see what happens
To your head, my khan-father!”
From blood-drinking, gray-maned Manas,
Your dear one was born a blood-drinker.
Seizing the bowl, he said:
“Wear your bowl on your head!
I am not grateful at all
For this food of yours steeped in poison.”
He overturned the bowl
On Khan Jakyp's head.
Then Jakyp's
Hair burned with a hiss.
Did you see how strong the poison was?
His beard and mustache
Burned together, crackling.
His hair was gone; he was bald,
Without mustache or beard.
Poor Khan Jakyp was dazed.
The skin of his head split;
His dear boy Semetey grieved.
To cut Khan Jakyp down,
The boy could not bring himself;
Was to take his distant vengeance.
All the assembled people,
Khan Jakyp had livestock;
Kulchoro to pass slowly,
The fool bore the name of “father.”
Unable to raise a hand against his father,
Semetey was in turmoil.
Semetey's newly arriving—
Jakyp's being half dead!
Blood-drinking Bakai, the leopard,
To Khan Jakyp's home
Had come to see him.
While tiger Manas was alive,
While he was Manas's companion,
Beijing with its khakan—
When they drove on to besiege it,
When they spread havoc,
Thrusting spears and breaking ranks,
Those whose time had come fell to shots.
There were salt marshes to ford,
There were great trenches dug,
In swarming China, many ditches;
Stirring it from the very roots,
When they slaughtered its swarming people,
Esen Khan's treasury—
When the Kyrgyz took it as booty,
Your dear Bakai, the gray-maned hero,
To his friend, gray-maned Manas,
The gray saddlebags he had offered—
Among Khan Jakyp's stacked belongings
They lay put away.
As soon as mighty Bakai saw them,
The dear man grew sorrowful.
Inside those saddlebags,
Rubies and gold were mixed.
“These saddlebags left by Manas!” he said,
Clinging to them, refusing to let go.
“My possessions left by King Manas!” he said.
Bakai struggled with the gray saddlebags.
To take them away as booty
Semetey set his mind.
When he saw those saddlebags,
“My possessions left by my father!” he said.
Grabbing him by the collar to train him,
Bakai and Semetey,
Both taking hold of the saddlebags,
To Kökcholok's side
Dragged them out.
Bakai stood pleading,
“Put them on Kökcholok!” he said,
Begging Semetey.
Semetey, the gray-maned hero's son,
Could not load those saddlebags;
A curse on his childishness!
He could not even move them.
Khan Bakai grew angry:
“Children nowadays
Drink butter dregs and shit grass.
When I was a boy in the old days,
Unheeding, mad and drunk,
I made white deer into cattle;
I threw the whole world into turmoil.
Wearing armor no shot could pierce,
Not saddlebags, wretched boy—
I grappled with the whole world.
Children nowadays,
May the damned brats perish!
They have not the strength for saddlebags.
Barefoot and bareheaded,
There was a time when the earth shook.
My dear youth has passed away.
When I was a boy like you,
I would roast whoever challenged me.
Not saddlebags, wretched boy—
A birch tree growing there,
I would tear it out by the roots.
Until they reach twenty,
Children nowadays
Are still mere children, it seems.
One boiling still left within them,
They are still half-cooked, it seems.”
So saying these words,
His elder Bakai worked himself up,
And spoke at Semetey.
Seizing the saddlebags,
Lion Bakai, the saint,
Blessed by Khizir from his roots,
A peerless wrestler, a saint,
To Father Manas, the lord,
The elder who had been a companion,
Coughing with deep rumbles,
The saddlebags left by Manas
He lifted with one hand.
In anger he swung them away;
Your unfortunate one, born capable,
Threw them onto Kökcholok.
Pleading with the Creator,
Those gray saddlebags there,
Your elder Bakai put them on Kökcholok,
And mounted himself as well.
Semetey followed him.
From malicious Jakyp,
Khan Manas's saddlebags
He had seized and was taking away.
Warrior Bakai's Kökcholok,
Under the weight of the saddlebags,
Ah, that Kökcholok—
Its fetlocks bending,
His pasterns bent,
His belly hung down to the ground.
“What will become of the dog?”
Your elder Bakai looked about in alarm.
The horse's spine curved like a sickle;
His ribs stood out.
His back bent and sagged.
Kökcholok stepped forward,
That worthy noble steed,
With a supple, ambling gait.
The wind could not match his trot;
No beast could match his walk.
Steam billowed from his nostrils.
The black stones he trod upon
Crumbled worse than charcoal.
Lord Bakai, your warrior elder,
(Kayipchi's dark cave,
When leopard Manas lived,
The very cave where he hid treasure),
To the cave where the treasury was gathered,
He tipped and lowered them.
To that helpless one left by Manas,
He handed over the gray saddlebags.
“The saddlebags left by Manas,” he said,
Entrusting them to Bölökbai,
Teaching what was unknown,
The learned elder Bakai,
Making the unperceived understood,
Falcon Bakai, the saint.
As they rode farther on,
Yellow foothills came into view.
Seeing the ridge, he wept;
Jilgynduu, Keng-Kol, broad Talas—
Seeing the land, he wept.
Kara-Buura and Chong-Chatkal—
Seeing their mountainside hollows, he wept.
The multitude of Kyrgyz, the many Argin,
Seeing where they had dwelt, he wept.
Your elder, leopard Bakai,
The dark ridge where he used to stand watch—
Seeing its peak, he wept.
God's sun grew overcast in the sky;
Upon Khan Bakai's head,
Sixty thoughts came at once:
“My dear orphan,
Seen today, gone tomorrow—
Such is this world of shit.
Orphan, listen to my words.
In those days, when my caravan Manas lived,
In a Kyrgyz cap of blue-gray lynx fur,
When I tossed my head,
Knowing the world,
When I lived in delight,
Lying on my back, acting like God,
I would slice and eat rump meat.
When my soul's companion Manas lived,
I would say, ‘Where is the angel of death for me?’
After that Manas died,
I was humiliated, herding camels.
While his lord was alive,
Your elder, Lord Bakai—
The marten cap upon his head,
Who did not wear it and toss his head?
When they heard of your elder, Khan Bakai,
Great China, the khakan's people,
The mighty peoples trembled.
My dear orphan,
When I reckon up my past,
My heart is sorely troubled,
Tears spill from my eyes.
The overturned, treacherous world
Seems to have gone without a backward glance.
The goodness that lay before me,
I failed to see it.
Bruised black and blue, wretched man,
While fierce Manas was alive,
I failed to die.
Kara-Shaar and Kambil,
