A foe could never pass us,
We were a rugged, towering rock,
Untouched by wind when storms arose.
Among this Kyrgyz people,
I was one who stood his ground.
No wind touched us when tempests raged,
We gave no livestock to claimants.
We yielded no sheep when wolves came,
We yielded no ground when foes came.
In courage, a lion and a tiger,
I had the strength of any man.
You were a crag like a mountain host,
Yielding neither pass nor road;
I was a lofty, rocky mountain.
While my broad nation endured,
Whenever hunger came upon me,
I would carve off a haunch and eat.
While this dear head of mine remained,
Of every human being born,
Throughout the eighteen thousand worlds,
Of any mortal who confronted me,
Of any champion who faced me,
I would say, “Let him bring his grievance!”
Whenever hunger came upon me,
I would slice off rich fat and eat.
“What enemy would dare to come
And face me in combat?” I would say.
The wild game upon the hills—
If I caught one with cloven hoof,
“I shall make it tame as cattle,” I would say.
“If I assail the whole wide world,
I shall make it submit,” I would say.
Chasing the blue-gray maral,
“I shall make it livestock,” I would say.
If a hostile, predatory foe came,
“I shall humble him,” I would say.
In those days my strength and courage
Had risen to their highest flood.
The forty saints, Khizr and Ilyas,
Were far away from me then;
It was my time to flee.
The reason I stood afraid was this:
Against the infidel, the ogre’s son,
I went to face him brow to brow.
Swinging axes and thrusting spears,
We fought with savage roars.
Heads no bigger than an ankle-bone were struck,
Armor impervious to bullets was torn,
And the ringed iron helmet
Was dented by the axe-blows.
Exerting all our strength,
Sparing not our reckless lives,
We stood confronting one another;
With Zulfuqar, the sword that cleaves a camel,
Striking back and forth,
We remained locked face to face.
Firing guns and drawing bows,
We battled in a bloody clash.
All the blood was swallowed up;
My kandagai trousers and Akolpok armor
Were utterly shredded away,
Yet nothing I did availed me.
For seven months I fought without rest,
But Sarybai, the ogre’s son,
Never once so much as staggered.
When I struck with a club, its point would not pierce;
That grim-visaged swine—
When I thrust my mace, its point would not pierce,
When I fired my gun, its bullet would not pierce.
Striking at him, I could not prevail;
Grappling with him, I could not conquer.
My sleeves burned and my heart was scorched;
Without dying, my eyes closed,
And my weakness became plain.
The moon waned and the days passed
As wrestling wore me down.
He terrified my people
And gave my breath no respite.
How many times he doubled my back,
How utterly he drove out my wind,
