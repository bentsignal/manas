"By contriving his way along, this Kozho
Will slaughter all the people!"
They split apart,
And whispered among themselves.
Was this the edge of the Zhediger,
Or the shameless face of those people
Who spoke their words openly?
If you take heed, Chynkozho
Had heard the edge
Of those sly words just spoken,
And had heard the end
Of the ominous words now uttered.
Having heard them, Chynkozho
Grew angry;
Chynkozho pounded the earth.
Surrendering wholly to his courage,
He shouted out his words:
"Vast black Zhediger,
You have seen my courage;
All of you came here
Asking me to obtain a wife for you.
Speaking two by two,
What did you just say?
Sons of bitches, Zhediger—
If you flee, you will eat shit!
Vast black Zhediger,
Let your bearded elders come;
Come, all you Zhediger!
Let your finest spearman come,
Your strongest battle-axe wielder come,
Your ablest deviser of means come.
Let your elder who has seen much come;
Come, every one of you with sense.
I shall place the matter before you
And find the means to settle it.
If the old man will not give his daughter,
I shall ride headlong against
Those sons of bitches, the Afghans,
And find the full means to settle it.
Yesterday Tümönbay, son of Tülkü,
And Azhybay, son of Azhy,
Carried wondrous words between us:
Before six days had passed,
They left promising to give Aychürök.
Vast black Zhediger!
What do you say to Toltoy going
As bridegroom to Akun?
What do you say to Toltoy staying,
Immersed in a wondrous pleasure,
Lying in one bed,
Eating meat from one dish,
Facing each other over one dish?
What do you say to Toltoy coming?
O Zhediger, vast multitude!
Rise and speak plainly;
Without groaning as though struck ten times,
Speak quickly.
Think of what comes after and speak;
Let the matter remain clear.
Hold back no evident secret—speak."
Chynkozho, son of Shygay,
Growled and surged;
He spoke to the vast black Zhediger,
Demanding that they answer.
Kozho stood roaring.
Among the vast black Zhediger,
There was no one to speak.
Chynkozho's shout
Sounded like lightning;
Of all the Zhediger,
Not one soul raised himself.
Their heads sank into the ground;
They looked down and whimpered,
Scratching the earth and groaning.
Even their strongest, old Tölök,
As though slaughter had struck,
Collapsed into whimpering;
All the Zhediger did the same.
