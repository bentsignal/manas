Into that towering palace
Chürök deliberately brought Choro.
Once he had entered the palace,
She sealed its entrance fast
And took Choro under her protection.
Sunlike Aichürök,
With her old maid who knew many things,
The noble-born heroine,
Her moonlike face gleaming,
Sixty buttons and forty keys
Jingling at her skirts—
Had you seen your sister-in-law Aichürök,
Her face shone like the sun
And swayed supple as fine leather.
Seeing Choro, she cried aloud;
The full-figured, luckless woman
Sobbed and wailed convulsively.
It seemed her ribs were breaking,
And tears streamed from her eyes;
She cried out and beat herself.
Burning with grief, Aichürök
Pleaded with Külchoro:
“You were our protector, our lion;
When this head was taken captive,
You alone were our guardian.
Though in anguish I said, ‘Let me die,’
The reason I remained alive
Was that you, my son, still lived.
Though in anguish I said, ‘Let me depart,’
The reason I stayed alive and safe
When Kyyaz took me captive,
When he heaped torment upon my head,
When my plight became dire
And he gashed my side—
Your sunlike sister-in-law Aichürök,
Bound as though guilty,
Lamenting with no escape,
Her head trapped in a narrow gorge,
Her precious self disgraced,
Wandered out this way,
Delirious as though possessed.
From the one Creator, Almighty God,
It was you I prayed for.
So I refrained from spilling my blood
And could not bring myself to end my life.
I did not kill myself,
My valiant, cherished son,
And today I have seen your face.
You were terribly late in coming;
Today I entrust to you
The sole survivor left in our need.
The foal left by the great stallion,
I reared until he grew—
The young hawk left by the falcon.
Take your ill-fated younger brother
As your companion,
And spread terror among the foe!
Destroy all sorrow;
My son, become your former self!
With broad ornaments and ample hips,
I was once your stately sister-in-law,
Who drove white deer and tamed them like cattle,
Who subdued the whole wide world,
Who confounded everyone she met,
Broke roads through narrow places,
And attacked many lands.
You were the peerless eagle.
When we remember your elder brother,
We mourn and wail without end,
Yet pray in hope, saying, ‘Choro remains in his place.’
We live on, offering our prayers.
You were the Morning Star of the people,
And I was the mother of the nation!
While you lived, my son,
We burned and blazed with longing.
Wondering, ‘When will my son return?’
I, black-faced Aichürök,
Lived yearning for you.
