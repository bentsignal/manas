When blood falls upon snow,
Look at the blood, and you see her face;
Look at sugar, and you hear her speech;
Look at the morning star, and you see her eyes.
Her waist was sheath-slender, her face moon-bright,
That renowned beauty, poor woman.
She stepped forward in radiance;
A little gold-covered amulet
Floated upon her breast.
Maidens and daughters-in-law stood together;
Mighty Churok among them
Made merry.
Her laughter made the sun set,
And her revelry brought the dawn.
Behind the swing
They played the game of Shabyyat;
Maidens and daughters-in-law mingled
And played in growing excitement.
Among the daughters-in-law was Kermekash,
And eloquent beauty Karachach;
At the head of the maidens was Kynymjan,
And formidable beauty Tynymkan.
There was Sugalak of the irrigator,
Among those maidens,
And Birmoyun of the grain grower;
Thimble-mouthed Oyumkan,
And among all those maidens,
Proudly pacing Kanymjan;
Camel-calf-eyed Bolpokan,
And incomparable beauty Kanymjan.
Your sister-in-law with her matched companions
Placed her arm around Kolkunay;
Altynay sang laments beside her,
And Kermekash shared her counsel;
They walked with stately bearing,
With eloquent beauty Karakash.
She herself was still a young child,
Her hair not yet long enough to braid,
In a red dress with a crescent waist.
From the shimmering beauties
She had gathered the finest.
Upon a wondrous plane tree and birch
They had hung the swing.
She appeared like a noble maiden;
Poor Churok stood at their head,
And thirty daughters-in-law and thirty maidens
Had begun their games.
In their midst, your sister-in-law
Played and raised laughter.
With renowned Churok at their head,
The companion watching them
Felt his heart carried away.
Kulchoro, who had never seen a maiden,
Saw them and grew excited;
He began the whole commotion.
With red Buurul beneath him,
Kulchoro turned at an angle
And plunged straight
Into the fearsome Urgench.
Kulchoro had seen the maidens;
The poor man, who had never seen a maiden,
Did not look all around him,
And in the fearsome Urgench
Did not spare his threadlike life.
They set aside the swing,
And played the game of Shabiyet;
Maidens and daughters-in-law mingled,
With mighty Churok at their head,
And played their difficult game.
Flattering Churok,
They excitedly told their dreams,
