You have brought the day of death upon me.
At the champion son of the Bloodthirsty One,
At the misbegotten foe who fixed his malice on me,
It seems I will suffer humiliation while alive.
If by chance I fall into his hands,
Before my allotted day has ended,
It seems I will die upon the open plain.
Could such misery, such a ruinous affair—
Algara running like a wild beast—
Ever have existed even in a dream?
I wear a bright red and green coat;
Its trim will not tear when I pull it.
I was deceived by a strange road;
If I fall into his hands, that Burut
Does not look like one who will turn aside.
It seems he may catch me;
That calamity-bringing Burut
Will drag me along the road
And make me fit for the Danggits, it seems.”
Thus Karagul
Grieved in this way.
How could Semetey hold back?
He pursued Karagul bare-backed.
A hero born surpassing
His father Manas, the gray wolf,
He bore down upon
The great plain, broad as the moon.
If you ask of that plain,
Haze descends and makes corners upon it,
And it ends in a dim and shadowed point.
When one looks, nothing catches the eye;
In this accursed world,
One cannot tell where anything lies.
The sight arrests the heart;
Jutting up in stark ridges,
The mountain’s summit cannot be seen.
Semetey, who had been deceived,
Pursued the infidel son of Katal
Bare-backed and without tiring.
A mighty plain beyond the reach of sight,
No small plain, but a vast one.
Across the face of that plain
There was no hillock as large as an ankle-bone,
No sunken hollow or ravine.
No ordinary creature could endure it;
Unless it were a swift courser
That would not tire when ridden for six months,
No other creature
Could withstand the journey.
None who encountered it remained alive—
Kyzyl-Kum, beyond the reach of sight.
This is the burning plain:
If a creature traveled there, its ears burned;
If a wild ass stepped there, its hooves burned.
It was a scorched desert,
With no grass to pinch off and eat,
No water to sip even a drop.
A goose in flight grew exhausted;
When a black vulture flew,
Its wings drooped
And it tired midway across.
It was an exceedingly harsh desert;
The many Chinese in Beijing,
From that day to this,
Called it Dyngsha-Talaa.
When they reached that desert,
Karagul, son of Katal,
Whipped Algara
And fled onward in haste.
Semetey’s horse Buurul
Redoubled his surging stride
And swept onward.
When Kongurbai’s Algara
Reached descending ground,
He ran far ahead, shimmering like a mirage;
When Semetey’s horse Buurul
Started up the slope,
He was about to reach his heels.
