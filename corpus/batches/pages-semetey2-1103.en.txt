For twelve days the meat would not cook—
Such a dire calamity had befallen them.
Here and there the cauldron’s
Middle heaved and bulged;
The rim and sides of the cauldron
Quivered almost imperceptibly;
Along the cauldron’s outer edge
Blood-red foam surged and seethed.
Külchoro, finest of heroes,
Was distraught, finding no remedy,
Twisting this way and that,
Despairing, “We are doomed!”
Unable to find a way, Külchoro
Went to his elder kinsman:
“My guide upon the righteous path,
My uncle, sultan of countless hosts,
My ocean spring, my broad river,
My steadfast khan and uncle,
Will you never have your fill of sleep?
Will your hip not tire of lying there?
There is no sentry posted on the tower—
Wake from your sleep, uncle!
Your former vigor is gone;
Do not sleep on without cause.
There is no end to your strange behavior:
The broth will not boil, the meat will not cook;
A day of dreadful trouble has come—
What can this mean?
O roaring leopard,
How do you interpret this omen?
The offering boils, yet the meat will not cook—
How can such a thing be?
Interpret this sign for me.
From some long-eared elder,
Have you ever heard of such a thing?
From all the attentive people,
From one who has worn his horse’s shoulders thin,
Grown old among nations and seen many peoples,
And journeyed around all four quarters
Of the dark earth with its hairy breast—
From my aged forefather Bakai,
Have you ever heard of such a deed?
We kindled a fire as great as Opol-Too,
And fed it with bent plane-tree logs;
Beneath the brimming cauldron
We made a fire fierce as hell,
Yet the cauldron never boiled,
And even now your feast is uncooked.
Is this what you are, wretched world?
Alas, uncle, arise!” he cried.
Having spoken these words, Külchoro
Awakened the heroic sultan.
He woke with a start from sleep,
His snoring ceased,
And that noble lion, proud as a cock,
Opened his eyes wide as bowls
And said this to Külchoro:
“That your feast will not cook
Means your strength is about to fail;
It means that from the noble uncle
Whom you honored as a father—from that gray wolf—
You, my warrior, are about to be parted!
When broth will not boil and meat will not cook,
No human undertaking can prosper!”
Thus spoke Semetey:
“As for this food that will not cook,
Let wolves divide it and dogs feast on it;
Let the dogs devour your feast!”
Then the bloodthirsty lion cried in anger:
“The feast you prepared with such honor—
Today Almighty God
Has bowed my head to the ground.”
So spoke Semetey.
Seizing the cauldron and swinging it around,
He sent it clanging
Off to the side.
The cauldron broke into six pieces,
