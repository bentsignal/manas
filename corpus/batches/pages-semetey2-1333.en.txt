At that time Kanchoro
Wore a black cap broad as a cauldron.
He had the will to slaughter
Not only the Kyrgyz but the whole world.
If Choro surged forward,
No mountain could withstand him;
Even the Kyrgyz in all their multitude
Could not defeat this Choro.
He sprang like a tiger;
No one who fought him would survive.
He made ready like a wild boar.
The great host was struck speechless
And simply despaired of life;
Unable to collect their wits,
The people clamored in bewilderment.
A calamity befell the multitude:
Fierce as a mountain, potent in venom,
Choro stood roaring.
Without speaking or saying a word,
The great people stood crushed.
He spoke like a storm and began his address,
Overwhelming the assembled people.
Jylgyn and Keng-Kol became a ravine;
Kanchoro arose from among his own
To become, for the Argyn and Kyrgyz,
An enemy worse than the Chinese.
The dear people sobbed and wept,
Grieving in utter anguish,
Bereft of their mighty warrior.
“What kind of age is this?” they cried,
And all the people stood lamenting.
At the wrath of Khan Kanchoro,
Khan and commoner alike were amazed.
Kanchoro began his fury;
The people who witnessed it
Renounced not only their livestock
But even life itself.
He donned his arrow-proof coat,
As though about to plunge deep into battle.
He split the tail of his mount
Diagonally and tied it in a hard knot.
He turned his tulpar's head;
The crested banner appeared in his hand.
The hearts of all the watching people
Were torn grievously apart.
The Grey Wolf's hardy sons cried,
“What age is this, what day?
What sort of night must we behold?”
The people clamored and grieved;
The birch wept and the willow wept,
Every living soul who looked on wept,
The men who claimed courage wept;
Look—the black earth itself wept.
The dear Argyn and Kyrgyz people
Moved among them, wailing.
While the populace stood thus,
What did Khan Kanchoro do?
He went jingling on, unconcerned,
Counting the clamor of the people
As no more than the cuff of his trousers.
He did not regard a dusty black ridge
As even a mountain pass,
Nor did he regard the people's clamor
As so much as grass.
The goatskin war drum—
He beat the great drum.
In Jeti-Özön the Jediger,
The vast, eager-standing host,
He roused every one of them.
Damn you, Jediger!
Obey the words of Khan Kanchoro;
Let all the people enter upon his plan.
In Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
The many herds of horses left in sight
Must be grazing there in sweeping ranks.
The sunny side of the horse range
Lies on the ridge of Jorgo-Küyük;
The shaded side of the horse range—
