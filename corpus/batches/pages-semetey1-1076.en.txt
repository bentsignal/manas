I stand asking it of God.
May the blessing remain with the boy.
My time is cruelly divided;
If brave Toltoy is skillful,
It seems he will seize
The twelve-year-old boy.
Thinking of this, my wretched soul,
My time is cruelly divided.
The twelve-year-old boy—
Will Toltoy seize him,
And, before he can say the Bismillah,
Slaughter him as a sacrifice?
He has no mane against which to scratch;
To the Argyn and Kyrgyz,
Will the prince return slack and helpless?!
Before he reaches maturity,
Will my indispensable Külchoro
Die a needless death?"
Speaking and grieving so, Semetey
Stood there as a sorrowing prince;
The common people wept, the khan wept,
All the gathered multitude wept,
The beauty Aychürök wept,
And the young women and maidens cried aloud.
Brave Semeң, son of Manas,
Born of his father, gray-maned Manas,
A hero greater than he—
Your prince did not stop then.
Taking the inner shortcut,
He brought out the gray mare
Of Khan Akun, barren for six years,
Leading it by the halter.
The leopard born of a gray-maned lion
Made it rear, crying, "Amen!"
He now grasped together
Its forelock and its ear,
Then took in his hand
The white saber with mottled neck.
Holding the mare upright,
He now cut off its head:
"Make this an age without grief;
If God created him as a living soul,
Keep Külchoro safe!"
Your heroic gray-maned lion
Now sacrificed the white-gray mare;
Spreading his hands, saying, "Amen,"
He now performed the rite
And sent the boy on his way.
Then Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Girded on a silver belt,
His sunlike face touched with radiance,
Dressed in a coat no bullet pierced.
When told he was going to Toltoy,
Without frown or knitted brow,
The boy smiled openly in joy.
He was as large as half a mountain;
An ancestral spirit settled upon the boy.
He wheeled Arbuudan sideways,
And, angling close
To his elder Semetey,
Gave leave and began to speak:
"Gray-maned elder brother, leopard!
Who would rejoice at this?
Why do you keep grieving
For the servant you raised?
You have kin, you have your beloved;
You have every enemy before you,
My falcon elder brother—farewell.
God knows whether I shall die
At Toltoy of the Zhediger's hands;
God knows whether I shall remain alive.
You stand in needless worry
And keep grieving in vain
For an orphan boy like me.
I am a bound-headed boy,
As though I had come here in resentment;
From the beginning I was a servant
Who girded your waist securely—
