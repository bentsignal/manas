An immeasurable flood engulfing them—
Unless the heroes come out to meet it,
It is coming to trample Afghanistan
And reduce it to nothing.
The road-hardened Jediger
Are coming to engulf Afghanistan
And grind it to powder.
The army split at the front;
Lion Kozho, who brought great strife
Upon the world, was at the flank.
Carrying a gold banner with a crescent,
Raising every kind of clamor,
When Kozho drew near,
The sight made the heart recoil.
His majesty was fierce, his wrath strong;
The leopard prince then
Looked as vast as Ala-Too.
A blue-mottled cloud appeared in the sky;
Semetey, son of the blue-maned lion,
Made Buurul bound and thrash,
Rearing and lunging,
And visibly rode out to meet him
Before Chynkozho.
Arbuurul beneath him,
A creature beyond ordinary livestock,
Pricked its extraordinary ears.
The white blaze on its brow, broad as teeth,
Seemed covered in steel;
The breadth of its pelvis
Was like a ravine filled with firewood.
His fearsome waist girded,
Semetey charged
Into Chynkozho's army.
His father was a surpassing blue-maned lion;
Lion-like Semetey
Unleashed turmoil there.
Girding a gray kise belt at his waist,
He charged, swaying heavily.
His hero's gift,
His renowned red banner,
He planted wherever he faced;
Semetey drove straight in,
Slaughtering the side he faced.
The playful blue-maned hero
Wrought destruction there.
He made the white standard his banner,
Filled the side he faced with clamor,
And set out to drive these people away.
When Semetey entered,
He darted in like a falcon;
The great black Jediger host
Fled shrieking like crows.
Making Taybuurul dance,
Those he reached then
He measured out with his spear.
Many were witless with panic;
Many ran in confusion.
Saying, “Elder, we submit,”
Many could not gather their senses
And ran away.
Many threw down their spears
And stampeded.
They threw away every one
Of their swords, guns, and weapons.
Saying, “Come, let us preserve
The lives entrusted to us,” many fled.
In terror they could not pull in the reins;
Their horses all bucked,
And many could not stop them.
Saying, “We shall die without seeing
A wicked wife or young child,”
Many fled weeping.
The saddles of many swift horses
Slipped beneath their bellies;
Many went careering about.
Saying, “My dear lost brother
Died in such-and-such a place,”
Many went weeping.
