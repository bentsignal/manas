He placed a sword in my hand,
And spoke at length of its thousand-fold lineage:
“Take heed, my child,” he said,
“You are my joy, you are my root,
You are the heir who carries on my legacy.
In this worthless, deceitful world,
Do not be vainly tempted, my child;
Do not chill the people’s hearts.
Do not lord it over those beneath you,
And never treat the poor, the orphaned, the wretched
With contempt, my son!
My forefather Manas said these words:
‘Here is Zulpuqor before you;
I give it to you, my son—take it.
A son as keen as Zulpuqor
Will come into your arms.
When you take this sword in hand,
Whoever faces you will swiftly perish;
When you strike straight, it will blaze like fire.
The land you charge against will be laid waste,
And its people will despair of life.
Of every hostile enemy,
It will level the innermost fortress.
When you draw it, its edge will glow red.
I give it to you, my son—take it,
Bind it firmly to your arm,
Do not loose it toward whatever lies before you,
But guard it securely and wait.
Solitude is suffering,’ he said.
‘All the cares that weigh upon your life—
May you sweep every one of them away.
God willing, a son will be born;
Name him Kenen.
If that son survives,
My child, he himself will inherit from you.
That son will be of the ancient line,
A lofty plane tree, a proud banner to his foes,
With eyes like deep and boundless springs,
The very image of a gray-maned wolf.
Prize the sword and place it in its sheath;
Then go against the enemy
Who flaunts his might and boasts,
Spill his blood and turn him back.
If a hostile foe advances upon you,
Break his back when you meet,
Still his heart when you clash,
Spilling blood and scorching the earth.
Wage the struggle fiercely!’
Thus spoke my grandfather,
And when he spread his hands and said, ‘Amen,’
There were eighty men behind him
And eight men before him.
When my forefather gave his blessing,
They all gave their blessing too.
Their white beards gleamed like pale silk,
Their shining teeth were like copper,
Their blue eyes were round as great bowls,
Their splendor seemed enough to swallow one’s soul.
All were gathered there together:
Almambet and Chubak among them,
And the elders Koshoy and Bakai.
With a great roar they raised their hands—
All the companions
Of my lion-hearted father Manas.
Having received the heroes’ blessing,
I awoke with a start from sleep.
It was a dream seen by grace,
With my forefather Manas at their head;
It was a day when blessings were bestowed,
A wondrous and auspicious thing,
A dream from which I woke in alarm.
What might this dream of mine portend?
Let uncle Akjoltoy interpret it now!”
Then his uncle Joltay
Readied himself like a hawk,
And prayed to the spirit
Of the hero who had passed into the next world:
“May your dream, my child, come true.
