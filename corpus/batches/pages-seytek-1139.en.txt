For noble, valiant Joltoy
I shall give this life held only in trust!
Stricken with sorrow and distress,
Fire blazing from his wrath,
As the sun darkened and the weather closed in,
Seytek pressed onward without pause.
With all his gear made ready,
He circled the hills and knolls
Sixty times.
Below lay Echki-Ölbös,
And at the head of the Ili, Üch-Aral;
He forded the edge
Of the river divided into three branches.
When the sun reached noon,
A towering peak appeared;
Its heights were shrouded in haze.
The earth seemed split and water plunged down,
As though the land itself had been destroyed;
Mist covered everything around it,
And Seytek saw it looming ahead.
The zamburak guns boomed,
The firearm’s bullets shrieked,
The great war drums thundered,
And battle cries rang out in front.
From the sound of those cries,
It seemed the enemy was advancing in force,
Now shouting the war cry “Kakan!”
Ninety giants swarmed there.
They had neither rope nor cord,
No road by which to overrun him,
No net to cast upon him,
Yet they raised a tremendous clamor.
They had seized your elder Akjoltoy,
Binding him without any rope;
They had plunged him openly into misery
And embroiled him in boundless calamity.
The time had come for him to behold
The true road from which none return;
Accursed death had drawn near,
And its appointed hour had come.
All means exhausted, Akjoltoy
Stood utterly without recourse.
Unable to find any shelter,
My noble elder was undone.
Kulansur was reduced to a lark,
My doomed elder to a willow switch;
His shadow remained, but strength was gone—
Such, indeed, is this worthless world.
My elder had met the infidel’s bullets
And found himself cast
Into the very embers of hell.
The noble hero had fallen
Into the hands of the merciless.
The eagle hero was caught
In the enemy’s inescapable net
And seemed to have fallen headlong
Into a boiling pit of Chinese troops.
Seeing this, brave Seytek thought:
“If fate has caught me, let me die;
But if God grants me another day beyond death,
Against these fierce-armed warriors,
Against this dreadful enclosing net,
Against the champions displaying their strength
And this host relying on sheer numbers,
Let me unleash a great tumult.
I shall whirl my spear and break their ranks;
If my days are done, I shall fall to a bullet.
If the Creator’s decree
Descends upon my head, I shall face it.
To my elder, left to me by my forefathers,
To that mountain-like shelter,
Let me become a companion.”
So saying, brave Seytek advanced,
His cry splitting heads,
His shout splitting stones,
Like an ember that scorched the eyelashes,
Coiled to strike like a dragon.
The forty holy guardians and Khidr-Ilyas
Raised their cries and shouted his battle call,
Surging from every ridge:
