The grateful people seized them all,
Until they lay in heaps like burial mounds.
Kuyaly’s vengeance was not sated;
She too was a surpassing gray wolf.
Kuyaly performed a fearsome deed
And issued her command.
The thousand wrestlers under her
She now assembled.
She marched them back and forth,
Then formed them into a long column.
From the countless wrestlers
Kuyaly selected one,
From the thousand wrestlers
Kuyaly brought forth another.
One by one from every group
Kuyaly chose them out.
When their number reached ninety,
Now put the words in their proper place:
“Troubled times have befallen us.”
With bull necks and camel-like frames,
Their struggling could destroy a life.
Some of the wrestlers there
Were broad of chest but short in stature;
Some wrestlers were such
That their whole bodies seemed black stone.
Such were some of the wrestlers.
Their bristling mustaches
Were like the hafts of battle-axes.
When you looked at some,
Their finely fashioned noses
Were like the sheaths of sabers.
When you looked at others,
Their lower jaws hung loose,
Like great leather sacks.
Some heroic wrestlers
Were splendidly formed.
“Obey whatever you are told,”
And each raised his noble head,
Ready to perform his service.
If told to dam a river, they could shatter stone
And turn the river from its course;
Crushing all without raising their heads,
They could grind stones into meal.
Such wrestlers were among them.
They had been born into this world
And come, it is said, as wrestlers.
They had faced grievous, burdensome trials
And endured much hardship, it is said.
They were stationed beneath the battle standard,
Ready to tie the firmest knot.
They could swing mattocks with sweeping strokes,
Fill their broad bellies,
And sustain their mighty bodies.
She gathered ninety wrestlers,
Choosing them from the strongest.
That infidel Sarybai
Was made to pay his true account.
She had the ninety mighty wrestlers
Hack his body into pieces,
Making a great spectacle,
And gather firewood from the forest.
To settle his true account,
They piled it mountain-high and set it ablaze.
The contentious villain Sarybai,
Who had shown his savagery,
Was cast into the fire and burned.
They shouted the glad tidings,
Burned him and scattered his ashes.
For having defeated Sarybai,
Maiden Kuyaly and hero Seytek
Had no regret at all;
There was no regret that he had died.
Their wish had fallen into their hands,
And fortune had entered their path.
The offspring of the honor-bound lion,
Receiving a blessing from Baabedin,
To follow the path of their lion forefather,
Received the blessing, “Moon-hoofed one.”
