At Karakan's in Bukara
They sought the child.
Dwelling in Ruined Chambyl
Was Buuday Khan;
His people were so numerous.
They gathered them all,
Made their young children file past.
Unable to find the child there,
They turned back.
Their destination was Samarkan;
Now they came to Samarkan.
They demanded a child from each household,
Gathered them without leaving one.
Their mothers raised an outcry,
Throwing the people into turmoil;
Their fathers wailed,
Breaking down the irrigation ditch.
The entire people cried out;
For seven days the children filed past.
Chong Eshen in Samarkan
Was a man of sacred gifts.
In that Eshen's household
Was a boy named Manas,
A great strapping boy.
“One who has been named Manas—
Is there such a child in your village?”
As they pressed the question,
“This is the child you seek,” they said.
Now God had struck the people.
“This child's name is Manas,”
The people began to say.
“We gathered and put it in writing;
For the boy's bravery
We named him Jar Manas.”
Sharpening his wicked tongue,
Among the mass of Chinese
An old man spoke at length:
“This child is named Manas;
His strength is great,
His bravery is considerable.
When he reached seven years,
He did whatever he pleased.
Of the children at play,
He slaughtered exactly twelve.
His father is the great Eshen;
You have found Manas without trouble.
My champions, your luck truly is good!
This child is named Manas,
High-browed, fierce in menace.
When you go to your khan, he will be pleased;
He lays low whoever challenges him.”
Heeding that old man's words,
The Chinese sorcerer,
Their diviner,
Their hero with second sight,
The great judge who had seen much,
Their champion named Döngö,
And the forty magicians of Beijing
Stood in a row.
They stripped the boy naked
And made him file past.
When the magician looked early,
When he sought the brave boy,
“This is the one named Manas,” they said.
Their discerning men examined him.
“His name is Manas,” they said;
Could the champions fail to rejoice?
Some examined him:
“Stout of body, broad-shouldered,
He is truly a hero.
He is indeed Manas himself,
More than himself, his very eye!”
Some said this:
“His right shoulder is broad;
He is the hero who will ruin the Oirat.”
Some said this:
“He has the bearing of a mighty mountain,
The whole world—
He has the will to slaughter it.
Karykan, the khan of Beijing,
Truly knew this,
That mighty one.”
Some said this:
“He will chase white deer
And turn them into cattle.
If he comes of age and becomes a man,
If he stands equal to a hero's son,
Beijing with its kakan—
He is the boy who will slaughter it.”
Some said this:
“This child named Manas
Is like a watching tiger;
Whoever clashes with him will not survive.
High-browed, fierce in menace,
He lays low whoever challenges him.
He is indeed Manas himself,
More than himself, his very eye!”
Some said this:
“He has a bearing unlike other mortals,
Chin-Machin and Kakan—
He has the will to slaughter them.
Has the enemy fallen into our hands?
Truly God has given him to us!”
They bound the boy,
Thinking he would die.
The champions all gathered,
Held a feast for twelve days,
Cast aside all sorrow,
Slaughtered their mules for the feast.
All the executioners grew excited,
All the champions grew frenzied;
Your Muslim people bowed down,
