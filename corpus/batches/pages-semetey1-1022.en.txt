Khan Akun, khan of the Afghans,
Was a khan possessed of sacred power;
He had given his promise
To Manas as a sworn in-law.
You speak too many words, sister-in-law,
And make me babble far too much.
What inheritance will you gain
By making mockery of a servant?
Why should I listen to your taunts?
Give me the falcon, and let me go!
The mount beneath me
Is Semetey's courser;
What I lost and wander seeking
Is my bloodthirsty lord's falcon!
Do not turn my errand into ridicule;
Do not idly mock
A man whose head is bound by duty!
What I wear upon me
Is my poor lord's Akolpok;
Though worn by a servant,
The armor fits Kulchoro exactly.
The coat I wear belongs to another,
The horse I ride belongs to another;
My uncle, who might strike in a fury,
Is in a dangerous temper.
Now agree to what I have said;
Do not mock me, dear sister-in-law,
And give me the falcon now.
What wish can I fulfill
By trading words with maidens?
Dear sister-in-law, give it now,
And let me take the falcon away!"
When Choro had spoken thus,
Churok stood and said:
"Oh, what shameless nonsense!
Your Argyn and Kyrgyz people are not here.
You are young, my dear;
Do not carry on without cause!
You have accused me of ranging
Across the whole world in search of a husband;
You say that at the edge of Ala-Kol
I took your Akshumkar,
Hurling empty slanderous words
And assailing me from this side!
When I wandered, my dear,
Did I traverse all the roads of the world?
Did I go demanding of your openhearted prince,
'Take me for yourself,'
And force myself upon him?
Did I take the falcon from your hand,
Saying, 'Give me its strength for six months'?
You stand saying, 'Give me my falcon';
How did I become subject to your claim?
You are young, my dear,
Yet you lay claim to your falcon.
As though you had taken a wife,
As though you were filled with joy,
You keep calling me 'sister-in-law.'
Whom do you think you are mocking?
You come right up to me
And begin addressing me as 'sister-in-law';
If I let you return to your own affairs,
You will still remain demanding the falcon.
What sort of outrageous boy are you?
As though you had taken a wife,
As though you had placed a bridal scarf upon my head,
You persist in calling me sister-in-law.
Do not lose your sense of propriety;
Set your words in order!
My husband is Chynkojo,
Tough as hardened red iron;
The husband who will take me is brave Toltoi,
Whose vastness equals half a mountain;
My nominal husband is Kadyrbai.
He is no enemy who merely stands and watches;
He rains arrows from the flank.
The husband I rely upon is like a mountain ridge,
And his courage is like blue steel.
