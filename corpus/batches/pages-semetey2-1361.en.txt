She wished to see her dear head there.
Her heart burning, her eyes blazing,
The enraged Chürök
Was truly seized by bloodlust.
She grieved, saying, “I shall die;
Rather than become Kyyaz’s spoil,
May I see my dear head
In the true abode from which none return.”
Aichürök’s heart was divided;
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Appeared before Aichürök’s eyes
Like a dragon.
“If slave Kyyaz comes near,
I will draw my sword from its sheath
And bring utter calamity
Upon brave Kyyaz;
I will cut short his age.
I will find a way to deal
With this would-be hero Kyyaz;
I will strike him with Zulpukor.
The sun of God darkened in heaven,
And sixty cares at once
Descended upon Aichürök’s head.
If Kyyaz draws near,
If Kanchoro comes beside me,
I will heap trouble upon them;
Whichever one approaches,
I will take his apple-like head
And bring utter destruction
Upon the two villains.”
Aichürök stood thinking this:
“Let me take delight in slaughter,
Sparing no accursed soul;
Let me sever someone’s head.”
A capable maid with a man’s bearing,
A maid who knew the enemy’s devices,
A mighty maid,
Who skimmed over the horse’s back,
Who had flown and journeyed
Across the whole wide world.
The mist dispersed from the sky;
Taking the form of a man,
She had observed all the giants,
Young and old,
Their warriors and lions,
Their many kinds of men,
Their rivers and lakes,
And those whose fortunes were complete;
Your sister-in-law had judged
The black-hearted wretches
Who fought women over the cauldron.
The accustomed fierce Aichürök,
With a man’s bearing and fiery eyes—
Keep far away if you see her form!
Bloodlust seized her firmly;
Her even teeth, like ruby stones,
Teeth unlike those of other women,
She ground with a loud crunch.
Her eyes blazed in torment;
She writhed like a white snake
And gathered herself like a lion.
The blood left her face pale;
Her eyes reddened like a wolf’s.
Her dear hair, thick as a hillock,
Streamed down across her breast.
The Jediger were a host, she was alone,
Yet she did not look around,
And spared no living soul.
Though the Jediger ringed her,
She did not reckon that she would die;
She did not look about her,
Nor spare her thread-thin life,
And counted the shouting Jediger
As less than a settled people.
Her heart burned to embers;
She flamed and blazed fiercely.

The keepers at the gate-mouth,
