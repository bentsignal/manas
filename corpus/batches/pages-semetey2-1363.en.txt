And dragged them off stumbling.
With repeated surges the Jediger
Lay plundering everything;
They smashed whoever resisted
And stripped the place utterly bare.
The heaps of gold filling the vault
They divided up and carried away.
They attacked every part of the gate;
Whatever remained,
The Jediger plundered and burned.
The young women who had been out strolling
Lost their dowries and were lined up;
The red-robed, slender-waisted,
Quivering beauties
Were plundered by the Jediger.
Countless goods and myriad possessions
Were scattered, and a feast began.
Into your wretched sister-in-law’s mind
Came a merciless design
That no person could unravel or nation know.
Inside the palace Aichürök—
The vast host of black Jediger
Could not force their way in
To the excited beauty.
The Jediger could not reach
And plunder your sister-in-law,
Though their vast army bent the earth;
They could neither loose bows nor fire guns.
The many Jediger could not brave
That accursed lioness of a woman.
The reason they could not enter:
Look at the moon risen over the mountain;
Look at the moon, then look at her face;
Look upon Chürök, whiter than the moon,
Upon her form of perfect grace.
There is the morning star in heaven—
Look at the star, then look at her eyes;
Look at sugar, then hear her words;
Look upon the gleaming form
Of that wretched fairy-born woman.
Her white breast exposed,
Her precious body gleaming,
The ill-fated woman of manly bearing
Worked out a plan in her mind:
She meant to leave the palace dead.
Thus the wretched woman’s purpose formed;
She clenched her teeth with all her strength.
“Let their heroes draw near;
Let me divide and judge
Their would-be lions,
Their fighters and their men;
Let me strike them with Zulpukor.
The skilled spearman,
Kanchoro who destroyed the village,
That mighty man and others like him;
Warrior Kyyaz, their khan,
That fatherless, ill-starred man—”
Taking courage, she clenched her teeth,
Counted her resources and formed her plan;
Your sister-in-law Aichürök prepared herself.
Their fighters and wrestlers,
Their men who boasted of strength,
Brandishing battle-axes and thrusting spears,
Closed around the wretched Chürök in the palace.
Their fighters and lions,
Their men who boasted of strength,
Swarmed all about her.
The Jediger pressed in
To bind her at last.
Their executioners and lions,
Each separate man,
Their many kinds of wrestlers,
Had only just surged forward
When valiant Aichürök
Caught hold of Zulpukor,
Which stood ready in its sheath,
And drew it by the hilt—
