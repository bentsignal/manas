God, cast the infidel beneath him;
God, raise Manas above him.
He is the lion upheld by the patron spirit;
Creator, hear my words
And do not let my back be broken.
God, grant victory
To my panther Manas, my lion.
Do not raise the Chinese above us,
Do not cast the Kyrgyz beneath them.
The morning star stands in the sky;
God, do not scatter those behind him.
If You created the black Kalmyks and Manchus
As living souls, O God,
Do not let their eyes ever look straight upon him.
If by mischance he falls from his horse,
The lion prince, the panther,
If by accident he should fall,
They stand ready to write the line,
'He delivered his Manas into our hands.'
With Khan Kongurbay at their head,
Against the Muslim people
They stand ready to make a spectacle.
Do not let the Chinese day dawn;
If You created my people as living souls,
Do not let them be slaughtered
And driven back toward their homeland.
The black Kalmyks and Manchus
Have a migrating host they once shattered,
And have long borne a grudge against us.
If Manas were no longer before their eyes,
The arrogant heroes' heads
They yearn to take with their swords;
The Chinese go about nursing vengeance,
Longing to bring slaughter upon us.
This infidel has a fearsome look
And has long been possessed by rage.
The Chinese eagerly seek
To bind and capture the faithful,
And to make all the Muslims
Into slave laborers.
The ravenous black Chinese—
If people like us fall into their hands,
Their custom is to slaughter every one.
At Kokotoy's memorial feast
We have happened to gather together.
Make this an age that finds its meaning;
If You created us as living souls, O God,
Keep Manas safe for us.
Oh Khan Manas, my dear colt!"
So then the Muslims prayed;
The elders Koshoy and Bakai,
Those saints, gave their blessing.
Raising their hands and sobbing,
The handful of Muslims
Loudly cried out their blessing.
The Muslims stood in an uproar:
Is not Manas the backbone
That braces the Kyrgyz waist?
Unshaken and courageous,
Is not Manas their lion?
Is it not Manas who brought slaughter
Across the whole wide world?
Is it not Manas who freed
All of the Muslims
From the black Chinese and Manchus
And gathered them beneath his hand?
Looming like Opol-Too,
Your saintly gray wolf Manas
Grew wrathful and made himself ready;
The blood-drinker Manas mounted his horse.
Twenty rode on his right,
Worthy comrades, matchless men;
Twenty rode on his left,
Bodyguard companions of the blood-drinker,
Matchless men who brought devastation.
Kalkaman's Black Horse
The blood-drinker had mounted,
While cow-rumped Sarala
Was led beside him bareback.
