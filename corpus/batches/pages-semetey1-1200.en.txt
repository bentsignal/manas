Kongurbay charged and thrust;
Khan Manas did not sway.
The leopard thrust from the side;
Kongurbay did not sway.
They thrust in turns,
Shouting as they leveled their spears.
Kongurbay and Manas then
Broke sixty spears.
The teeming Chinese roared,
Crying their Kakan battle cry.
The Muslim faithful roared,
Crying their Islam battle cry.
May God never show us such a sight:
Their spear points struck,
Driving each other up and down.
When they fought with spears,
The spears broke and splintered;
Shafts thick as cauldron handles
Flew about with a clatter.
Chasing and thrusting at one another,
God had struck them.
The madman's Black Horse
Had grown warm down to its flesh.
Thrusting three times for every single thrust,
Kongurbay meant to prove himself.
The copper lance gave a shrill cry;
In the broad ravine where no bird flew,
No enemy escaped the spear,
No person escaped death.
Alöökö's Kongurbay
Showed the form of a lion;
His courage was unlike another soul's,
And it was plain he was a hero.
Shouting as he extended his spear,
Crying the Möndü battle cry,
Kongurbay now reached
Blue-maned Manas himself.
When he reached him, the bronze spear
Struck him and drove through.
Did you see that he was
The strength of Beijing's Chinese?
Did you see that renowned Kongurbay
Was a lion?
Kalkaman's Black Horse,
Its pelvis narrow and its strength slight,
Seemed about to fall backward.
When it rose upright
And was about to topple over,
As Manas neared the ground,
Manas seized
The Zulpukor in his hand
And cut the infidel's bronze spear
Clean through the middle.
His spear broke and vanished;
Was Manas not upheld by Shai?
The lion Manas righted himself.
Fastening his clasps,
He put on Akolpok.
Sarala, who had accompanied him—
He sprang bareback
Onto the bow-like thoroughbred.
Roaring like Opol-Too,
Noble Manas caught him.
Before the teeming Chinese host,
Before he could dodge away,
The mighty khan Manas
Struck the Kamar-like Kalcha
And passed on.
From atop Algara,
As though a mountain had collapsed,
Alöökö's Kongurbay
Fell crashing down.
Khan Manas raged,
Roaring like Opol-Too.
When noble Manas came,
The Manchu people wailed,
Bowing like young brides,
