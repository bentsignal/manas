Beneath a plane tree as large as a house,
Your elder, old Bakai,
Had removed the saddle
From Kökcholok beneath him,
Hobbled him with double fetters,
Pillowed his head on the blue saddlecloth,
And lay with his head toward the qibla.
The old man sleeping there
Started awake from sleep,
Turned onto his right side,
Hastily performed his prayer,
And saddled Kökcholok.
Their elder Bakai, the bringer of good fortune,
When the dawn shone white,
And light fell upon the earth,
Led Aktaigan behind him
And perched Akshumkar on his hand.
Sent before you, he brought good fortune;
If you followed him, he was like a myriad troops.
He made known what others did not know—
The wise old Bakai.
He made men perceive what they had not sensed—
The true prophet Bakai.
At the empty pasture where no soul moved,
“Where has Semetey gone?” he said.
On the broad foothill ridge,
Bakai stood leaning and watched.
“Ride toward Agala, toward Kyzyl-Kum,
The fatherland of Almambet;
It was his father’s pasture.
When the soaring black bird,
The lion Almang, went away,
He left it behind long ago.
When hardship and an evil day befall you,
He said it would be your refuge.
Instead of camping at Kyzyl-Kum,
What word shall I hear of
The inheritance Manas left behind?
I will ride out to meet
The lone one who is my shelter!”
Saying this, hero Bakai
Climbed the ridge and looked.
Hoping the boy was safe,
He raised the hare-foot spyglass in his hand;
Their elder, old Bakai,
Opening and closing one eye,
Looked for Semetey.
He saw him driving the herd with a roar,
Having raided it entire and whole.
Across Kaspang’s Kara-Talaa,
Countless black shapes like ants
Spread glittering over the plain.
The accursed one had taken many animals,
Not merely a few, but an abundance.
Their hoofbeats made the heavens resound;
He had taken an endless mass of stock.
He had begun his fury fiercely,
And slaughtered all the feeble ones,
Leaving them along the road.
Swaying and streaming onward,
The barren mares led the herd;
He had sent the foals shivering
And killed them in the water.
The whinnying brood mares
He drove along with heavy steps.
At Echoing Zhar-Kyya,
They began to file out one by one;
They streamed over the great ridge.
When they came into that procession,
Not one horse remained outside the file.
Their elder Bakai examined
The horses Semetey had brought.
He flared up in anger,
And in his old age mighty Bakai
Found grief waiting before him.
His wail split the stones,
His cry split heads,
And he halted at Kapka-Tash.
