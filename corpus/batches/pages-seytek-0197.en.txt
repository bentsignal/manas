I see I was destined to suffer,
For rushing in heedlessly,
I see I was destined for disaster.
Cruel Aichürök, fair as the moon,
You are my treasure and my strength,
A bride lovely as the moon.
Delay brings grievous trouble—
I entrust Tootoru to you.
I gave you your choice from the herds;
Take my swift steed Tootoru.
I have been somewhat shamed, Aichürök;
Pour out my blood and take it in your leather flask!
Go to the herdsman and return;
Bring me quickly that poor fellow
Whom I found only when death was near.
Let my eyes behold him;
Bring him quickly back to me.”
Thus brave Kyyaz spoke,
Beginning a quarrel and hiding his intent,
Stirring up still greater trouble and strife:
“You were a lake, Chürök—overlook my fault!
Go safely and return unharmed;
Come back quickly and do not make me yearn.”
Thus he spoke to Chürök,
Striding away with wicked thoughts;
To the hapless woman fair as the moon
He entrusted the horse Tootoru.
Brave Kyyaz turned back,
Kyyaz the warrior with the fierce mustache;
When you looked at his mustache,
It resembled a sack from Bukhara.
Each of his shoulders was immense,
As though two men sat upon them;
When you looked at his two cheeks,
They were like two sated Dungans.
Such was his aspect when you saw him,
Like a bloodthirsty khan,
Like a mighty ruler of nations.
Kyyaz turned and remained behind,
Entrusting his bay horse:
If you will not ride him, here is the road;
If you would push him, here is the ravine;
If you would bind him, here are his legs.
Many are the cares within my heart;
God stands above your greatness.
Kyyaz, your anger has brought no good;
Time and again,
You have not curbed your violence.
Because my beast spoke,
You fly into a rage.
Let us pass before him,
Ask your beast to speak,
And, hero, though we are women,
Let us hear.”
She turned Tootoru’s mouth,
And before brave Kyyaz
Your dear sister-in-law Aichürök
Stood pulling the bay horse’s reins.
Wearing a robe patterned with blue flowers,
Brave Kyyaz, had you seen him,
Raged like a blue-maned lion;
He ran forward, heaving and panting,
His knees bending toward the ground,
His weapons set in order,
A naked sword strapped to him.
He pulled the reins
And turned the tireless steed Tootoru’s head.
Such was brave Kyyaz when you saw him;
As the bay horse swayed and plunged,
He stood stroking its forelock.
The great bay horse neighed,
But its forelock did not part,
And it showed no sign of speaking.
He turned it this way and stroked it,
But the great bay horse said nothing.
The bay horse had no words to speak,
And brave Kyyaz, enraged,
Was disgraced before Chürök,
The woman lovely as the moon.
