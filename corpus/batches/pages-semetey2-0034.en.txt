After they had crossed that plain,
The tale and tumult grew wondrous.
When they journeyed farther,
They neared the host lying at Dyngsha
Of Kichi-Beijing, in the place
Called Chok-Tabylgy—
The force belonging to the infidel son of Kaldai,
To hero Khan Kongurbai.
They had drawn close to it.
It was a tower built for keeping watch;
He was the mighty khan of the Kechil.
If a man died and left his ox behind,
Kongurbai divided
The dead man’s property equally.
They raised their seter goat,
And the mount they rode was a sacred horse;
Such was the rite of the Kechil:
All of them went without trousers
And slept with women.
Upon the hazy ridge of Bozorgo—
Have you heard?—
Khan Kongurbai was in the fortress.
Such was the nature of Kongurbai:
He had eighty-four armored men,
And whatever side they struck became red fire.
There was a gate with doors of steel;
On this side of the gate
Were wonders beyond measure:
A tigress had borne fifteen cubs,
And no person approached
The fearsome Kalcha.
There were sixty dragons;
And on the farther side of them
Was a sword-snouted bili.
Three taibaz had been raised there;
Within a single taibaz
Was the strength of ninety tigers.
Such was the renown
Of valiant Khan Kongurbai.
Yesterday, when Manas took Beijing,
When the warriors threw themselves into the fight
And wreaked terrible slaughter,
Kongurbai saw the worth
Of beasts of prey as sentries
And had prepared them, that mighty man.
Drawing near to the gate,
When they came within one arrow-shot,
They looked upon the gate:
No person could approach it;
Whoever approached that fearsome Kalcha
Would not remain alive.
No person could come near;
Whoever encountered it would not remain alive.
Looking below it,
There was a pit dug
So that a person falling in would die.
Going farther on,
There was a prepared snare
That would seize a man.
Passing beyond that,
There was misery set boiling for him.
Beijing is no easy land;
Kongurbai is no easy hero.
When Kongurbai received warning,
He quelled a chaotic assault
Before anyone could even order, “Strike!”
At the mouth of that gate,
A club as large as a house was mounted.
If truth be told, we cannot know the dawn;
His champions of many kinds
Stood ready
At the mouth of that gate.
As they were about to cross the threshold,
Your champion, son of the gray wolf,
Seized Kongurbai’s Algara
By his slender tail.
Praying, “May God deliver the slave,”
He gripped the tail roughly,
