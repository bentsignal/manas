Will I be left in disgrace?
I would sooner die broken than be shamed;
Ümötöy is fierce—
I have never seen the like of him among the dead.
Without stiffening in anger,
I shall go to my khan and elder.
My silver-eyed, sun-faced,
Thunderous lion of an elder—
I shall go and seek his counsel.
Before anyone can cry, ‘Stop and turn aside!’
Saying, ‘He is your close cousin,’
In the blink of an eye
I shall tell Semetey.
If he says, ‘He truly is a Kazakh;
Leave none of his army standing,’
Then, raging to my very roots,
If the fury given me
By gracious God seizes me now,
I shall rise in wrath
And attack one of them.
I shall choose and mount a charger;
Without demeaning sword or gun,
I shall charge those accursed Kazakhs
With only a whip.
If they swarm me, I shall make them pay with their lives,
And force them to cry, ‘Mercy, dear brother!’
I shall test their vaunted courage,
Felling them with my whip,
And drive the wailing Kazakhs
Toward Sary-Arka.
I shall drive the sprawling swine
Into Sary-Arka.
I shall pen them among the reeds,
And gallop straight at
The raving Ümötöy himself.
Whether I should attack or not,
I shall seek my bloodthirsty elder’s answer.
Then I shall wreak devastation
And turmoil among the Kazakhs.
Ümötöy of Khan Köksö,
It is true that you are a grim warrior;
Like thoroughly tempered iron,
It is true that you are strong.
Known among the people,
It is true that you are a lion;
It is true that you can
Deal with multitudes.
Warrior, it is true that you can
Ride against the Kyrgyz and wreak slaughter.
Black-maned leopard,
I shall go to my khan and elder,
My noble elder Semetey,
And firmly seek his counsel.
If my elder speaks
And says, ‘I will pay the blood-price
For your father, gray-maned Köksö,’
We shall accept his word.
With gold by the foot and precious ornaments,
We shall pay your father’s blood-price.
I have many full-grown beasts,
Their fore-saddles sagging,
Their twin humps bulging;
I have many fine animals of great worth.
To those who understand, this is my honor:
Let us be friends and not revile each other!”
After pondering in this way,
He said to Ümötöy:
“I have means to pay the blood-price.
I will not strike you on this little ridge;
Wait here, valiant gray-maned warrior.
The man I must face is Semetey;
I shall not be gone longer than a mare’s milking.
My gray-maned elder Semetey—
I shall go to Semetey.
I have my lord Bakay,
Blessed by every saint,
