Looming vast as a gorge-riven mountain,
His two eyes bulging,
As though he would suck out any soul he struck,
As though, without a backward glance,
He would slaughter any living being—
Such was Sarybay’s appearance.
His immense weight scorched the earth,
His ferocity scorched the people,
And he began a great upheaval.
The wretch, despairing of life,
Caused many another soul
To perish outright.
Writhing in rage,
The people despaired of their lives,
While divine power struck the madman
With all its force.
Toward Külchoro
He leveled his blue-steel spear.
Before he could arrive ahead of an arrow,
Before he could reach and transfix
The warrior favored by fortune,
His mind crowded with grave fears,
The poor youth beset by many troubles,
The hero Seytek, son of Semetey,
Leveled his spear and charged;
Keeping him from reaching his uncle,
He cast his fly-like life aside.
He too was a falcon and a lion;
His valor and leonine courage
Had now come to the fore.
The playful hero Seytek
Had come out onto the plain
To shield his gray-wolf uncle.
His earliest forefather was Tügöl Khan;
The poor youth’s roots ran deep,
And all his forefathers were khans;
From birth, Kydyr had blessed him.
He was the offspring of my hero.
“That buried swine will reach
My uncle, whose strength is nearly spent,” he thought.
He is the black eye of the demon;
When he strains, he sucks out life,
Calamity incarnate.
Sarybay had turned deadly earnest
And unleashed surpassing fury.
Dust rose everywhere,
And this wretched, faithless world
Seemed utterly overturned.
He lunged in bitter desperation,
Clawing the earth like a polar bear.
Leaning back, he drew his mace,
And when his violent rage seized him,
He seemed capable of shattering the blue sky.
Behold the monstrous Sarybay;
See how his fury raged.
May your renown pass away, cursed raider;
May you die and be laid in the earth.
May your fortunate city be bound,
May you die and be bound beneath the ground.
He advanced, bellowing and snarling,
Clattering in fury,
Making ready like a tiger,
Whose charge none could survive.
He flared up in raging fury,
Changing through countless forms without pause.
He stormed and thundered in wrath,
His towering hair and beard bristling,
As vast as the Üңkür-Koo gorge.
Anyone who entered his reach,
He seemed ready to seize and swallow;
Such was Sarybay’s nature.
Sarybay rode out prepared,
With all his weapons fastened.
Like a returning blast of bitter cold,
Like snow and ice that tormented living souls,
The wretched hero Sarybay,
When you beheld his greatest might,
Was like an icy wind sweeping down the valley.
Raging together with Jelmayan,
He strained as though to swallow and devour,
