If he carries on like this, God will strike him;
He will unleash chaos.
The accursed wretch has gone wild
And will take my wife away;
Thus he will bring me to ruin.
That brazen wretch
Will carry off my wife.
He will put an end to my laughter;
That wretch will scatter
All the possessions I own!
Though only a boy, the little brat
Has cast his chilling lasso
Over the languishing Churok.
He tethered Buurul far away;
Where maidens played Orompoi,
He remained absorbed in play.
God has taken all sense from Choro;
Where maidens played Shabyyat,
He made merry without haste.
Without saying, 'I have my hulking uncle,'
With maidens who had hung a swing,
He has stayed there frolicking!"
Seeing this, your lord could not remain still;
Smoke billowed from his mouth,
And flame streamed from his eyes.
His fire-like eyes blazed;
The gray-maned lord bristled,
And bloodlust seized him.
Semetey grew furious,
As though Kulchoro had taken
His beautiful wife Churok:
"If I do not sacrifice
Those two across the river!
If I do not take every vengeance
Upon Akun on the farther side!
If he does not return the livestock of my lordly father Manas,
If I do not trample upon the head
Of that self-styled lord Akun
And scatter all four kinds of his livestock!
If I do not drive off his mares in pursuit
And bring out all his hidden spite;
If he comes near, may I rain blows
And dash out his brains!
Now he has struck my fury;
Let alone Akun—may I not rest
Until I myself have slaughtered
Both Churok and Kulchoro!"
The lion grew furious
And burned with jealousy for his wife.
He loosed a lion's voice
And shouted menacingly;
Unable to contain his anger,
He called out for Kanchoro.
Hearing his voice, Kanchoro,
Saying, "What has happened to my lord?"
Flew like an arrow
And reached the gray-maned hero's side.
He raged and swaggered in fury;
God had struck the bloodthirsty man.
As though his khan-father Manas had died,
He stood there enraged:
"Come here, Kanchoro.
I have seen a marvel and a spectacle;
I will spare no wretched soul,
For I have met a grievous calamity!
If a khan provokes my anger, let him die;
Today, let every soul
Who opposes me die!"
He shouted in fury;
Your gray-maned hero, agitated and hurried,
Called to Kanchoro:
"When I climb, you are my support;
My anger has risen and made me rigid,
Kanchoro, my little brother!
Drive the fourteen horses here;
From among those fourteen horses,
