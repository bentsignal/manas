Your warrior companion could bear no more,
And mountain-like Kyyaz would not be stopped;
He would come to Aichürök,
And keep humiliating her, calling her a slave,
Thinking God had delivered him a slave.
On the road by which Kyyaz would come,
His courage and lionhearted valor
Had now risen above all others.
Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
The fierce, illustrious hero,
He had gone out to intercept.
Eager to enter the struggle,
To avenge his prince,
He made his weapons ready,
And buckled on his naked sword,
Hanging the golden-cheeked battle-axe
From his bare forearm,
And praying to the spirit
Of Manas, departed to the other world.
Against Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
He went to wage a furious fight.
If his strength boiled up and proved enough,
He would drink his blood.
If his fate arrived and his days were done,
“Let me die at brave Kyyaz’s hand;
But if we clash unexpectedly,
And fortune grants me strength,
May I suck and drink
The black blood within him like kumiss.”
He swiftly invoked Baabedin,
And, honoring his father Manas’s way,
Called upon Aituyak by name.
The lion-born warrior
Swiftly uttered every prayer
And went to the road where Kyyaz would come—
To the mouth of the dark ravine,
To the ridge where the banner vanished,
To the blunt projecting spur,
To the broken Aksai pasture,
Upon the gaping mountain shoulder,
Thinking, “If the warriors meet face-to-face,
Will their bullets miss?”
Its lining was worked in silk,
Stitched by a wretched, diligent craftsman,
Layered, riveted, and sewn,
So that merely looking at it
Made a man’s eyes slide away.
Trimmed and bordered with sharpened steel,
When it was readied for gunfire,
A vast fortune had been spent.
Its weakest places were cut away,
Then filed and cast from steel,
Its edging was drawn out in gold,
And whole sable skins
Were fitted around the cuffs.
Its collar had folded ornament,
Its cuffs were reinforced
And embroidered with gold.
A musket ball could not cut it,
Nor could a rifle ball penetrate it;
It was sewn throughout with sharpened steel,
So that in close combat
A man’s hand slid off it.
Showing her care as a sister-in-law,
She dressed Külchoro in the kangadai,
The armor no bullet could pierce.
The battle garb Külchoro wore
Had not the smallest hole anywhere;
Its fastenings numbered nine,
And he buttoned it and made ready,
Buckled his sword in its sheath,
And prepared all his weapons—
Ready to die if true death came,
Ready to challenge
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger,
To grapple with him,
And clash thunderously in battle,
Ready to die if fate decreed,
And make the vile swine Kyyaz
Suffer torment at his hands.
