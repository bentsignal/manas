I would increase my feats each time;
Setting hoes in a row,
I would shoot them with my bow.
My arrow would be blunted,
And the faces of the hoes gouged in.
I girded on crimson steel,
And precious Khan Bakai tested me,
Saying, “His strength will suffice for the world.”
Thinking, “He will become someone’s spokesman,”
My dear mother Kanykei
Had me raised with sacred rites.
“The strength of this wrestler is unique,”
Said the saint who led the people,
The collar of the precious robe,
The father of the peoples of the age,
The elder sustained by the sacred spirit,
The sage who had seen many lands—
My uncle Koshoy approved of me.
“Choro’s valor is great and his strength supreme,
Though he is unruly,”
Said heroic Koshoy, approving me when he saw me.
My prowess became known;
“He is a fearsome warrior,” they said,
And when they saw my courage,
Many heroes despaired of their lives.
Wondering whether I was the greater warrior,
Our grandfather Chylaba
Questioned a sorcerer.
When he heard that I was a hero,
He was astonished and amazed.
Lo Ma, the khan of Beijing,
Heard that my valor was exceptional,
And my strength beyond measure;
As the son of Khan Almambet,
My fame became known among the people.
I loaded the great musket,
And only the hardest part
Of the unyielding black earth stopped its shot.
Where I stood became a fortress;
Every enemy I met became a fool.
Life burst from his breastbone.
Such was his prowess:
He played with his ornamented spear,
And every enemy he met crawled away.
He was a hero of surpassing stature,
A panther, a gray-maned warrior—
Such was Külchoro.
Brimming with strength and spreading broad,
Your hero Choro, attended by the sacred spirit,
Your Choro, a hero surpassing the world,
Whose thoroughbred could carry him for six months,
Kinsman of the magnanimous khan—
He too was one of the greatest.
Would the lion-like gray-maned hero stay still?
He summoned Kyzylsart, Seytek, and the Black Giant,
The three of them, to the spectacle;
Would he refrain from wreaking havoc?
He summoned up his former strength.
When the sword Zulpukor
Hung from my arm,
When I wore trousers
That no bullet could pierce,
When I crushed the whole wide world
And held it in my power,
When the hungry blade Achalbars
Swung from my arm,
When I crushed the whole wide world
And took possession of it,
When I brought disaster upon any foe
Who crossed my path,
When I destroyed everyone I encountered
And seized all before me,
All twelve parts of my body were whole.
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
A full-limbed, bold young warrior,
I made much blood flow across the plains;
Such was my stony courage and strength.
While my sovereign lived,
While I possessed my former strength,
