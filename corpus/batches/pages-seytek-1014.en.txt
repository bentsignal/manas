At the age appointed for me.
Six thousand horses at the head,
Drawing the gaze of the whole wide world;
Upon a gray horse bright as moonlight,
White cotton as broad as a yurt’s path
Was displayed upon its forelock.
A thousand red-tailed camels,
Their faces turned toward the qibla,
Were loaded with red gold and jewels
And made to kneel facing the pole star.
The mouth of every treasury
Was opened for Khan Akun;
Abundant wealth beyond all counting
Was scattered before Khan Akun.
If cast into water for forty years,
Not the slightest edge would spoil;
If soaked in water for eighty years,
It would spring back without rotting.
However grave the hardship,
Not the faintest edge would spoil;
Though it endured forty years of famine,
Not the slightest thread would tarnish.
Carpets gleaming like gold
Were spread in splendor.
Abundant wealth and countless livestock
Were bestowed upon the pledged in-laws.
Bowing in homage and feasting on fat,
With abundant wealth and myriad livestock,
The pledged in-laws opened their hands in blessing.
With my princely father Manas at their head,
Abundant riches filled the place.
Thus our father-in-law, Khan Akun,
Entered into this marriage alliance.
The aged father of the people,
Dear holy Koshoy,
Saying, “Between this world and the next
I shall stand as impartial judge,”
The saintly Koshoy arose.
Receiving the blessing of “Amen,”
Our forefather Bakai sealed the bond.
“Manas and Khan Akun—
These two are now in-laws;
The foundation of their kinship
Shall be known among so many people.
Here are heroes and khans;
All the discerning people are here.
Here are eloquent men of sound judgment,
And others who know the finest ways
Of marching against the foe.
We have heard it beforehand,” they said,
“And are satisfied by the words spoken.
Not only in this world, but in the next,
On the Judgment Day of Resurrection,
Khan Manas and Khan Akun
Shall remain inseparable in-laws;
Abundant wealth and myriad livestock
Have been laid between them as a pledge.
To this service that has been performed,
Great and small and elders—
We all have borne witness,” they said,
Recalling the words of truth.
“We will not say it a second way.
Before the true God, all of us,
Great and small and elders,
Will not suffer true disgrace.
These are sufficient words, O hero.
Whoever denies them
Will have both cheeks blackened with soot;
If the words of one who turns away
Are accepted as true,
Can a human beast know wisdom?
Is he an infidel standing between us?
For twisting these words,
May a curse descend upon his neck,
And may his shroud burn for nothing.
Manas is Akun’s son-in-law;
And to Manas shall become a bride
Akun’s moonlike Chürök.”
“These are the heroes’ words,” they said,
And the assembled people surged in uproar.
