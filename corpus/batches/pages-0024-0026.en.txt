There is no forest I can flee into,
I have no strength to do battle,
I can find no road of escape.
To fight and strike them down,
And only then to die,
I have no gathered army.
I have no sure support,
No one to bind beside me,
To fight that Chinese dog;
My Karakan is gone as well.
I have no strength to clash with them,
No means to grapple with them.
What we see today is gone tomorrow.
Ah, alas, so this is it:
This world is shit!
How shall we find a way?
“A child whose father has died”—
My Jakyp, dear to my heart,
We look to you all for support.
Listen well and mark my words:
“A child left by a valiant man—
We hold you as a precious gift.”
Akbaltay said this in lamentation,
Telling of what Alöökö had taken,
Telling of the horror.
As your elder Akbaltay stood there,
Tekechi Khan and Shygay Khan
Came stumbling up as well,
In utter confusion, covered in dust,
Their heads awash with blood.
Their unguarded route had been raided;
Their great wealth and countless possessions
Had been taken by the Chinese.
They had been made to suffer terribly;
Of their champions on guard,
Alöökö had sixty shot down.
Pale and drained of strength,
They came running, staggering,
As though they were about to die,
Despairing of their poor lives.
Stumbling, the two of them said:
“The Chinese took my horses,
The Kalmyks destroyed my property,
The Torguts shed my blood.
They will not leave me even
My little life, no bigger than a fly.
The countless Chinese, a deeply rooted people,
Have the people of Kakan with them.
When your Karakan died,
“Let us slaughter them all,” they said,
And that mighty man made ready.
Everyone who challenges him—
It is his custom to drink their blood.
If we turn aside, that damned man
Will destroy and slaughter us together.
If we do not obey his commands,
If we do not give what he wants,
He will leave our people without descendants.
That warrior Alöökö,
And his khan, the unbeliever Molto,
Have wonders and possessions.
The sufferings of six months ahead
Can be foretold this very moment
By the old sorcerer they have.
That swine whom fire should kill
Has still more wonders:
He has rhinoceroses, he has elephants.
Do not talk foolishly, my children;
This time, heed my words!
That unbeliever of fierce wrath—
Let us do as he says.
Everything he demands,
Let us prepare and give him.
At this time Alöökö
Has champions, has an army;
To slaughter every one of us,
His champions are all assembled.
His ways are strange, his wrath is fierce.
My children, if you let him approach,
Abandon all hope for your lives!
Alöökö, whose ways are strange—
His tigers have borne fifteen young.
No man has dared come near him,
No man has faced and defeated him.
At this time Alöökö
Has sixty dragons;
Beyond them stand
His elephants with swordlike trunks.
No mortal could advance against him.
My brave children, heed my words!
Alöökö’s army,
When I look closely, my children,
Appears in all its many forms.
When I see those champions,
My mind reels and scatters.
Even if the Flood swept over them,
They seem able to stand against it.
Girded with iron thick as a leg,
Carrying a mace the size of a travel-yurt,
Striking down whoever comes their way,
His champions lie tethered.
Anyone who comes to face them
Is torn to shreds.
We lived among the snowy mountains,
Among the gaping gorges.
To you children without a father
I pour out my many sorrows.
Their customs are strange, their wrath is fierce.
Truly God has struck us now:
The Chinese people have swept in;
They will lay every one of us low!”
When Alöökö came to surround them,
Argyn and Kyrgyz together,
Their poor lives thrown into turmoil,
That handful of Muslims
Were hard pressed, at their wits’ end.
There was Akbaltay of the Noygut,
Tekeche Khan and Shygay Khan,
There were Ulak Khan and Jakyp Khan,
Karakan’s eight sons,
All bewildered, finding no way out.
At the marsh of Üyürmö
They gathered round in council.
Upon the hill where the people met,
They held a great assembly.
Tekeche spoke at length:
“Against those damned Chinese
Let us fight to the death, my people!
To the Torgut and Manchu peoples,
How can we submit to their commands?”
Shygay stood and said these words,
Telling horrors beyond human hearing:
“Against Molto and Alöökö,
Have we the strength to struggle?
Against warriors of towering might,
Against unbelievers swarming like black insects?
If we follow the old traditions,
If we ask those who know,
The Chinese people, untouched by the Flood,
Have brought hell upon us many times.
Before this they raided the Kyrgyz
Exactly ninety times.
When my forefather Ogoy arose,
He stood against the Chinese.
