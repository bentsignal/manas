Son, have you forgotten this?
When they buried you beneath a haystack,
Cut open your shoulder and gouged it,
Enslaved your heroic soul,
And made you gather firewood
For forty hardened herdsmen,
It was Kanchoro who inflicted that disgrace,
And valiant Kyyaz who seized your hands;
It was he who gave the command,
A foe more sinister than any mortal.
Will you not repay the slaughter
And humiliation, Külchoro?
Will you not offer the madman
As a sacrifice, Külchoro?
Let such a traitor disappear,
And let your affairs become clear as glass!
From rocky, cliff-bound Keng-Kol,
We have come to recover our people;
From the pig swollen with wrath,
We have come to take revenge.
From the scattered, ruined Kyrgyz,
We have come to gather counsel;
From the dragon-shaped pig,
We have come to exact vengeance!
The finest among us are our khans;
Drinking red blood and swallowing dust,
Let us all perish together.
Let us snap in a row like threads of hair,
Our precious lives we would not spare.
Let our blood flow like water;
How can our souls find rest
If we swallow grief without revenge?
How can our souls find rest
Without fighting and drinking blood?
The past disgrace and great calamity—
If you are a man, Külchoro,
Take them to heart.
God the Creator made humankind;
If every soul were weak,
All would wander aimlessly.
In the remote tract where no horse could travel,
Upon you and my noble uncle
They drew their bows like pouring rain,
And shot arrows like falling hail.
Stirring total turmoil and strife,
They drove the people into terror,
Seized the land where the nation made its living,
Passed the heights and took the mountain belt.
Desiring wicked turmoil,
Your two uncles Chynkojo and Toltoy—
Claiming, “Those two uncles slaughtered them,”
They incited valiant Kyyaz.
They camped along the Kök-Ötök,
And reveled together with Kyyaz.
Their blood-grooved iron-shafted spears
They gripped short and brandished,
And without sparing a single one of you,
They unleashed a massacre.
They drenched their spears in blood,
And every precious, worthy life
They felled and laid upon the earth.
Blood-red calamity Kanchoro
Planted his steel against the ground,
And never once regarded
The kinship of distant relatives.
With their matchcords glowing red,
Shayymbet and Baytaylak
Destroyed all the khans’ sons,
Those fire-scorched swine.
They pierced them through and took the khans,
And took the lives of those who deemed themselves mighty;
Red gold and blue silver,
And countless herds they seized as plunder.
Climbing a hill, he shouted,
And summoned an army of ten thousand.
In long boots and leather trousers,
With a pacing mare and crimson sheath,
Slave Joydur urged them onward,
Craving ever greater turmoil;
