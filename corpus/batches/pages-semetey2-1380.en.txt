With her stately, flashing eyes,
Was the finest of women.
She was Khan Zhamgyrchy’s daughter;
If she wished, she was as sacred as the Night of Destiny.
For the warrior who had served,
Your sister-in-law was the maid destined for me.
Because I had avenged our father,
Because I had struck down Kongurbay
And laid him flat upon his back,
Zhamgyrchy’s Shayymbet
Clothed the lovely Akbala, my beloved,
In patterned gray silk,
And mounted her on a smooth-gaited pacer,
Making the hem of that silk robe
So wide that it swept the ground.
She had grown graceful as a doe,
Her hair coiling around her head;
She had matured like a young deer,
Her hair winding about her feet.
Her dowry was complete and displayed,
And she stretched proudly like a white doe,
Too reserved even to speak.
Without holding counsel
About that unfortunate beauty,
Kanchoro did not give her to me.
When God struck the bloodthirsty man,
He stirred the nation and gathered the people,
Then decreed that the son of Almambet
Should take that lovely Akbala.
When he decided it so,
He exalted Külchoro as a lion
And burned me, leaving me diminished;
Thus he began my grief.
Of that radiant beauty,
The companion I thought was mine—
Listen, my friend Kyyaz—
Thus he left me bereft.
Unable to equal Akbala,
Unable to shatter the world and prove myself no lesser,
Rather than remain without a wife,
Rather than become one with the black earth,
I raged and rushed away,
Offended by this deed
Of Manas’s foolish son.
I meant to slay men and scatter the people,
And depart;
Hearing that Kyyaz of Zheti-Özön
Was a true hero, I meant
To come away to you.
I meant to reach
The very root of that bloodthirsty man.
I have many things to tell;
Not once, but a thousand times,
That ill-starred butcher,
That deceiver who ruined the people,
Has stripped me of honor.
When we campaigned against China,
When we seized and slaughtered
The finest armor-clad Chinese,
When we drove forth and killed
The strongest Chinese in their jackets,
Zhamgyrchy’s daughter Akbala
Was brought by Shayymbet, and we took her.
We hoped to gather khojas and mullahs,
And have them wed her to me;
While I cherished that hope,
That arrogant deceiver
Refused to heed our words,
Refused to give Akbala to me,
And gave her to Külchoro.
My heart burned and turned to embers.
Unable to become a mighty champion who breaks armies,
Unable to prove Akbala’s equal,
I blazed and burned with anguish;
That was when my heart first turned against him.
