And make her a wise and honored wife.
If Chürök will not obey,
Strike her across the neck;
Do not leave the infidel alive,
But slaughter her for sport.
We shall seize her in the palace
And wreak boundless ruin;
Let us overpower and capture her
And bring disaster upon her.
The woman whose lord is dead,
The infidel fallen into your hands,
The woman whose husband is dead,
That frenzied infidel shrew—
Let us storm her palace
And scatter and exhaust together
Her countless goods and boundless wealth.
Let us plunder all there is to plunder
And firmly crush all resistance.
If Chürök struggles,
Let us offer her as a sacrifice.
Let us heap humiliation on her like sheep;
Let us cut off her head without mercy.
If the shrew refuses to see reason,
Let us be rid of that woman;
Let us massacre her people,
Bring misery upon their ruler,
And gather up the whole nation.
Of those older than seventeen,
Let us leave not one maiden,
And of those younger than thirty-three,
Let us leave not a single woman.
Let us spread savage calamity
And round them all up together;
Let us submit the many matrons and maidens
To the appraisers.
If Chürök turns away from us,
Let us kill her and not leave her alive,
And together bring about her end;
Then let us take another beautiful maiden
And enter the palace.
Let us make the revelry magnificent;
Let us find a wife among the maidens.
If Chürök remains stubborn,
We shall wage war without restraint.”
So Kanchoro spoke,
Addressing brave Kyyaz,
Like a lion whose words could not be contained.
Bridling and bristling with rage,
As though God had struck him mad once more,
Kanchoro sprang up in fury.
A thought came to him as he rose;
His face darkened beneath a cloud,
And his shout split the rocks.
He put his weapons in order
And girded on his unsheathed sword,
Like a cloud returning with rain,
Like a host marching to slaughter,
Like a champion who drinks blood,
Like a hero who will spare no one.
Rage seized him and his eyes blazed;
He ground his teeth with a crunch—
Such was the warrior you would have seen.
He seemed ready to settle along the blue hills;
Had you seen Kanchoro,
He looked ready to devour all before him,
As though he had occupied Chatkal.
If crossed, he seemed ready to flay a man alive;
He charged like a wild boar,
Raged like a tiger,
And none who fought him would survive.
He gathered himself like a champion,
And his muttering was like that of a demon;
The warrior’s fury
Was that of a wrestler who ruled a nation.
Whichever feature you observed,
He seemed a monster that swallowed men.
He broke away like a wolf,
As though ready to devour anyone who opposed him.
