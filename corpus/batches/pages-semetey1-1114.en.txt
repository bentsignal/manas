Sanchybek of Samarkand
Also came along the road;
He came rolling onward
With an army of some ten thousand.
Biting their whips firmly,
Urging on their lone horses,
Kozubek of the Kokand khan,
Malabek of Margalan,
Alabek of the Six Cities,
And Temirkhan, khan of Bukhara—
From there came armies in abundance.
The armies of the four khans mingled,
Coming together in great numbers.
An army of some twenty thousand came.
The four divided into four,
Their princely bearing apparent.
Among those four
Were khojas in motley robes
And mullahs in white turbans.
They had great komuzes broad as an embrace
And had their dutars played;
They had some sixty young men
Sing their akysh songs.
From beyond them,
Turning toward the great din,
Spears striking against spears,
Those with spears growing excited,
Those with battle-axes exulting,
From the heights of Chambyl-Bel,
From the banks of Belendi,
Zhanaaly and Karachal came.
Calling it Khan Kökötöy's memorial feast,
Calling it the brow of the great gathering,
They came by the Kyzyl-Kyya road
With an army numbering ten thousand.
From beyond them,
Carrying an iron-rope net,
Taking shelter like blue iron,
Carrying a spear without a tassel,
Riding the racehorse Kuu-Azyk,
Its hair tail tied tight,
Banner and spears rattling,
Kökbörü came along the road
With an army of some ten thousand.
From beyond them,
The war drum deafening ears,
Their polished spears made of sharp steel
Thrust forward from every side,
With an armful of weapons and gear,
Riding a horse like Telkyzyl,
Muzburchak, son of Buudayyk,
From beyond the heights of Bukhara,
From the ruined land of Chambyl,
Saying he would go in ceremony
To see Khan Kökötöy's great feast,
Saying if the Black Chinese and Manchus fought,
He would spill their blood,
He too came down then
Along the banks of Karkyra,
To Khan Kökötöy's feast.
Crossing passes and treading stones,
Hurrying in great agitation,
From the land of the Lop River,
From the Oygut people opposite,
Carrying a gray-mottled banner,
Raising a clamorous cry,
Choosing a horse from among the thoroughbreds,
Bearing a letter from young Aydar,
Along the banks of Karkyra,
To Khan Kökötöy's feast,
Karacha came along the road
With an army of some nine thousand.
If you look toward the qibla side,
Brave Ürbü of Eybit Khan,
Who knew many kinds of languages,
With gathered good fortune,
With a people assembled from many tribes,
Along a road branching to the side—
