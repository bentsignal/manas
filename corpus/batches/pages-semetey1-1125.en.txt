He himself was the people's blessed elder.
He was an exceptional hero.
He had stubborn joints
That would not heal in six years.
His flesh was raw and bruised,
His blood remained but one spoonful.
Until his rib bones filled out,
Until seven years had passed,
If we fought, I would not be sated with blood;
I would not spare this Töshtük.
With weapons filling an armful,
There was Muzburchak of Khan Buudayyk,
Watching the uproar;
He tested him as well.
What could dear Burchak do?
His fierce eyes were like Venus,
His moonlike ears were shields,
And his neck was wholly thick.
Looking over his whole body,
He had the strength of ninety men.
An exceptional man had been born;
In figure, dignity,
And appearance on horseback,
He matched Orokkyr,
Khan of the Shibee.
His axe was unusually grim;
Considering his substance,
He was one of the heroes.
Then hero Kökchö, son of Aydarkan,
Came before him,
And he assessed him:
"His blue-mottled horse stamps noisily;
His great lungs wheeze.
Thin, clattering ayran, a narrow sieve—
The shrill-voiced hero Kökchö
Is a chatterer with a maiden's courage.
One who tucks letters in his hem—
What fool gave this man
The name hero Kökchö?"
Seeing Kökchö, Ezkara mocked him,
Tormented him,
And trampled the many Kazakhs.
On this nearer side
Was elbow-sized hero Agysh,
His whole body sinew.
He looked upon him
And assessed him closely.
Every soul who saw him felt content;
A champion on foot, useless on horseback,
He set fire to those who grappled with him.
Bull-necked, with thighs like a stallion,
His build differed from a man's;
He ruined those who wrestled him.
When he thrust his spear and shouted,
He possessed great skill.
When he gripped the trigger and drew the bow,
He possessed great prowess.
With the chest of a hungry lion
And the eyes of a hunting eagle,
He raised high at the feast
A fine steel-patterned spear,
And threw down and crushed
The champion who faced him.
Spear shafts bent at the middle
If they unexpectedly quarreled;
In Beijing of Kakan-Chyn,
They would joust this way for six days."
He took a slight liking
To elbow-sized Agysh.
Raging upon a pale-gray stake of a horse,
Surging straight before him,
Kökbörü appeared;
He assessed him.
His fortune was complete;
Kökbörü, son of Urumkan,
Was a merciless hero.
Broad-chested and wide-breasted,
Both champion and lion.
