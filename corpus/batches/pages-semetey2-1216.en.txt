Licking his lips.
Both champion and lion,
A hero poised like a falcon,
He made the whole world submit,
And threw all he faced into turmoil.
When he seized his sword with a shout,
Those who came against him
Were drenched in black blood.
Diviners judged and foretold
That he was the mightiest in the world.
When he swung his sword with a shout,
A great many
Of humankind perished.
Trembling at the sound of his name,
Even the people of distant Beijing withered.
When he faced them and leveled his spear,
All mortals fell beneath it.
Of all whom ears had heard and eyes had seen,
None was as mighty as Choro.
If you ask what Külchoro was like,
He was the red ember of the saxaul,
The tempered steel bullet of the firearm.
Both champion and lion,
Among all sons born of men,
No hero could equal him.
Armored against bullets,
From beyond the forked pass and high ridge,
Without letting them reach his heroic uncle,
He met the earth-covering Jediger
And attacked them in return.
Stooping low, he speared warriors.
The peerless champion, that gallant man,
Impaled twenty or thirty at a thrust,
Spearing all within his reach—
Where now were their leaders?
He speared all who assailed him—
Where now were their bloodthirsty men?
While peerless Manas lived,
So that worthy men might receive gifts,
Seven banners given as tribute
Were planted in seven places.
Such was the consummate warrior,
Making those he reached fall slain.
Six banners given by nobles
He planted in six places,
Driving them down and slaughtering them.
With a steel shaft and sharpened point,
The keen-tipped ornamented spears
Were thrust as they charged,
Striking the measured ground,
Clashing against one another.
He sent his spear whistling,
And hurled the Jediger heroes
From their saddles with his spear.
Making them cry, “Jakey, mercy!”
He drove the Jediger
Headlong before him.
Those places that had boasted of their strength,
He subdued one after another.
Leaving none who fought him alive,
He laid them low as he advanced.
Driving the Jediger headlong,
He came onward, felling them.
The lionhood of noble-born Choro
Was plain to see.
Spears lay scattered on the ground,
Dead horses appeared
With teeth bared wide.
Warriors lay stroking their mustaches,
And heroes’ blood poured out.
Many more saw the matchlocks and commands,
Watching from afar;
Many more fled in panicked terror,
While many lay dying.
Many more rushed about in deafening tumult,
And many were struck with panic.
At the forked pass and high ridge,
They could find no way of escape.
