He had grown hardened from drinking blood,
Seasoned by constant combat.
Having pledged himself to the twelve holy patrons,
He was named Külchoro the Lion.
Among spearmen, Külchoro
Was a surpassingly renowned and able man,
Who knew better than any
How to outwit the enemy he fought.
A man wise in all the world,
A hunter who seized like a falcon.
Breaking through dead ends and opening roads,
Against the infidel son of a monster,
Against the hero Er Sarybai,
If fate decreed that he should die,
If God truly willed it,
He would surrender his fly-like soul.
He would go and thrust his spear,
And sacrifice his reckless life,
Trading blows if he had the strength,
And stand face-to-face with him.
Then Choro resolved upon this:
Against the grim-faced infidel
He would charge straight in and level his spear,
For God, for the people,
For the rugged Ala-Too,
For the Kyrgyz people of the north,
Who dwelt in the Ala-Too.
Choro, noblest of kinsmen,
Choro the hero, upheld by the saints,
Terrible in bearing, mighty in wrath,
Choro the hero who spared no one,
Choro, favored by perfect fortune,
Who straightened all who challenged him—
Such was Külchoro’s nature.
Taking his steel spear in hand,
He attacked Sarybai,
Power radiating from his presence,
While between his two cheeks
Grew whiskers five finger-widths long.
Sarybai confronted him,
And Külchoro, a blazing ember,
Prayed to the Creator:
“Grant us abundance while we live,
And grant us faith if we die.
We are not mere brushwood—do not make us a road.
We are sons of princes—
Do not disgrace us before the dog Sarybai!”
Ask no more about Külchoro:
He was a glowing crimson ember,
A bullet of steel against the foe.
When he charged in fury,
There was none like him in all the world.
Choro, proud of his valor,
Against the infidel son of a monster,
Buckled his battle sash around his waist,
And against that earth-smeared swine
He went alone and attacked.
He pulled the reins
And turned the mouth of his fluid-swift Surat.
Against the roaring infidel
He leveled his spear with a roar.
Such was Külchoro’s way;
Sarybai charged,
And this was how he prepared:
Tall and broad-shouldered,
Both wrestler and lion,
Both mighty and heroic,
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
A reckless man of boundless courage,
Who throughout the eighteen thousand worlds
Had stirred up great turmoil.
His lashes were flame, his eyes embers.
No other man possessed such dreadfulness.
He brought turmoil upon his foes;
If hosts came flooding against him,
He never panicked or hurried.
If a formidable foe fell into his hands,
He crushed him into a patch the size of a rabbit.
Though ninety armies grappled with him,
He never opened his flank in fear.
