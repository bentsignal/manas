The earth itself shook and turned.
Bölökbay, the consummate craftsman,
Who had befriended the prophet David,
Took up a file and worked the iron.
Over a treasure beyond comprehension,
He and David took counsel.
He fashioned the battle-axe,
Perfecting every curve;
Its socket was steel and its edge was sharp.
So that it would never recoil from the foe,
He laid steel along its blade,
And David bent and tempered it to perfection.
“So Choro may be its master,”
He had a holy elder bless it.
While he was making it,
The strain upon his forge was so great
That tribute fell upon the Kyrgyz,
And many young bulls were slaughtered.
The strain upon his forge was so great
That a blue bend of forest was felled.
He hammered out its keen edge,
So whatever it struck would not survive,
But die doubled over,
Its appointed doom fully met,
Yellowing for want of any cure,
And swiftly departing this world;
He quenched it in human tears.
He made every piece of equipment ready
And buckled on his battle-axe.
Fate’s line had now been drawn:
Brave Kyyaz was destined to meet
Fortunate brave Choro.
At a spur of the shouldered mountain,
At the tip of the Sekichek ridge,
His strength and courage
Had now surpassed all others.
He had lain in wait for brave Kyyaz
Of the Jediger, saying,
“This is the path a hero must take.”
He would fight and test his strength,
His fame reaching distant Alay,
His name known throughout many lands.
Great and wise fierce hero,
Your battle cry stirred an uproar;
Your courage and lionheartedness
Surpassed those of all other mortals.
To bar valiant Kyyaz’s road,
He summoned all his courage,
Ready to die if death arrived,
And, if fortune allowed, to make
A spectacle of Kyyaz.
His threadlike life turned to ash,
His mind surged into frenzy;
He gave no thought to dying
And did not spare his life from death.
Lion Choro, guarded by the spirits,
Embarked upon a terrible feat of courage.
His precious, sweet life
He bound to a hair and cast away.
The golden-cheeked battle-axe—
Such was the nature of that axe:
Its span-long blade gaped broad,
Spreading blood wherever it struck;
Its crown was like a mace-sized fist—
Such was the nature of that weapon.
If you ask about the battle-axe,
It would not rebound even from stone;
If it struck iron in fury,
It would not spin back.
When swung straight at the foe,
Its blade flamed red with fire.
When its handle was grasped and swung,
Its shaft lengthened forty fathoms.
Set upon grass, it started a fire;
Whatever it struck died instantly.
When it struck stone, sparks flew,
And fragments burst like bullets;
It crushed whatever it bit into.
The lame smith Bölökbay
And great master David had forged it.
