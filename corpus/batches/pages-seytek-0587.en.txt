“Let us enter the grave alive.
My younger brother and my sons,
My irreplaceable souls,
My formidable ones who stand before me,
My mighty figures, vast as mountains!
From Sarybai’s venom
I am burning and consumed!
My mountain guarding the herds,
My sheer black cliff
Above which no black eagle circles,
The cliff where lions make their lair—
The lineage of these Kyrgyz
Has never been defeated by a raging foe.
Your fathers and forefathers
Never yielded their place to another;
Though lungs burst and gall spilled,
The enemy could not defeat them to the death.
If Sarybai presses us hard,
Let us grapple all the harder.
One of the seven days brings death;
Let us sacrifice our precious lives.
How could we submit
To the strength of that overbearing swine?
How can we give the ancestral people left by our fathers
To a fool?
This is the narrow land of gorges,
The land of every courageous man;
This is the road on which warriors have marched,
This is the host they led with them.
This is the land of pillar-like Talas,
This is the treasured Kyrgyz nation;
For this multitude of Kyrgyz people,
I am the one created by Almighty Allah
To become a sacrifice!
Sarybai has attacked us;
Against the enemy who grapples with you,
Charge and level your spears,
And truly sacrifice your precious lives
For the sake of the people!
Wheel your spears and break the foe;
From the conflict that brings black night,
From the villain who ruins the people,
Deliver your multitudes.
If fate has come, then die;
Swing your swords and thrust your spears,
And test yourselves in combat.
Fight without yielding,
Stand face-to-face
With the dog who spares no life,
And wage battle abundantly;
Save the white-kalpak Kyrgyz, the noble people,
From the hands
Of the wrathful dog!
If hostile death comes, let us die.
While our precious lives remain,
Our beloved people will raise their banner;
Sarybai is the enemy—
How could we give them to this slave?
How could we raise as khan
The infidel son of an ogre, the world-exhausting warrior,
Calling him “my lord” and “my lion,”
Calling Sarybai “my champion and hero”?
How could we go before him,
Saying, “Submission to Sarybai,”
And make him khan?
Rather than raise him as khan and submit,
Prize all that he says,
Turn our necks and call him “Master,”
And yield to his decree,
Let us first attain our inheritance;
Rather than leave a hair unharmed,
Let us depart this world without looking back
And perish in slaughter!
Let us advance and begin the conflict,
Enter the battle head-on,
And overcome Sarybai’s host.
Let us test in combat
His strength and lionheartedness.
If our power proves unequal in battle,
Rather than live on in shame,
