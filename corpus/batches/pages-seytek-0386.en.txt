Tell the warriors—tell your men!
Tell the executioner who, when the khan commands,
Takes human noses and ears
And wears them as amulets;
Who drinks and is never sated with blood,
Who grapples and never spares a life,
Who drinks poison when told to drink water,
And scoops blood when told to scoop souls;
Bull-necked and camel-thighed,
Who wrings the life from those he grapples—
Tell that savage wrestler and headsman,
Tell him who stands beneath the banner!
Tell him who, when word comes from the khan,
Levels his spear without delay;
Tell the marksman who never misses,
Who destroys whoever attacks
And delivers punishment with his own hand.
Give the order in your wrath;
Upon the head of the maddened slave
Bring utter and final destruction!
When the wrestlers close in,
Let his head fly off with a crack;
From this vagrant rebel
Let life depart with a shriek.
Let them fit their spearheads,
And begin the combat in full;
Destroy this monstrous villain
And grind him beneath the earth!
You bluster without cause,
Bull-chested and rash-tempered—
Can a hero be like you?
You are no man but mere earth;
Your courage is great, but you have no sense,
You are utterly lacking in wisdom.
Yesterday the crescent-shaped golden banner rose,
And tumult split the sky;
The honored fled and khans died,
And all who fought were slain;
The earth thundered and the people roared
When the fierce battle raged.
He will grimace, thirsting for blood,
While turmoil closes over his head;
“What can a lifelong comrade do?”—
As his soul departs, Kanchoro
Will plead, “Oh, my comrade!”
Your noble blood will turn to ash,
Your good fortune turn to night,
Your throne be filled with tormenting demons,
And grief become a day of calamity.
Firing his gun and drawing his bow,
He will take away your arrows,
Drive back your host,
And mount and seize your throne.
He will cut down and shatter your fortune,
Bring spectacle and chaos to your camp,
And openly launch rebellion.
Roar out your command
And take the sword in hand!
That crooked, vile traitor—
Finish him utterly!
Understand all the words I have spoken;
Cut off this slave’s head
And from the top of a post
Hang it dangling!
Let your troops close in from every side,
Let Külchoro’s head be struck off,
Let the orphan Seytek be subdued,
And let the women, children, and remaining herds
Be scattered once again!
He clings to you as kin,
While enemy blood is spilled;
Let the crooked one’s schemes collapse,
And let no ill-willed, inwardly treacherous
And openly hostile enemy
Stand against this accursed palace of yours.
Tell the sentries, tell the guards
To seize and slaughter this slave.
Tell the good men who raise you high,
Tell the shaman who strikes the lungs,
Tell your wrestlers and your lions,
