How long ago his temper flared!
If anyone raises a hand in resistance,
If anyone quarrels in defiance,
Beat his brains to pulp.
Make war on them like this;
See how numerous this people is.
Search every one of their bundles.
Act according to their bloodline;
Slaughter all their people without leaving one.
Violate their beautiful women,
Scatter their honor like ashes.
Press the attack fiercely;
Take revenge for that ancient grudge!
They speak their own stammering tongue,
And harbor hatred toward us.
Their heads are black and their feet are cloven;
Treat those wretches like cattle,
Humiliate them and make them slaves.
Slaughter every last hero
Who might strike back.
Plan well and start the conflict;
Rip open their pregnant women.
Burn them until no smoke remains,
Slaughter them and leave no descendants.
Gouge out the eyes with spears
Of the heroes who stand against you!
Find a way to settle their account;
Strike down the ancient devil's great rage
Until he loses his senses!
Separate out their strongest men;
Inspect their treasury and goods.
Bundles, saddlebags, and packs—
Take all these as plunder;
Bring ruin upon them in this way.
If there is a hero or lion among them,
If there is a man who boasts of strength,
Come out and face him in single combat!
Thus spoke Chyntengir,
And issued a harsh command.
Such was the khan's command.
Onto the vast field of battle,
To the place where they would clash,
Odukan came out for single combat—
A descendant of the Black Kalmyks,
An heir of mighty Joloy.
His strength was extraordinary;
He traveled on foot without mounting a horse—
Who had ever known such a giant!
Do not ask what Odukan was like:
His eyes were those of a black demon,
He was the very embodiment of calamity.
His head was like a cauldron
On the body of a dying boar;
His brows were like a dog lying in wait.
When he attacked, he swept men away;
When giants charged, he destroyed them.
Once a one-eyed giant came,
A khan who knew no peace.
They fought savagely,
Standing against each other in battle.
He drank and was never sated with blood;
When he struck, he spared no life.
He blunted blades of steel,
And destroyed invincible giants.
He grew up in the desert, reeking of poison,
And on the cliffs, reeking of blood.
He roamed about cutting off heads,
Killing men and laying waste to peoples;
He had shaken many lands.
He was himself a bloodthirsty scourge.
At the sight of him Kakan and Chyn,
And at his valor Bakburchun,
Uluntu and Dynsha lost heart.
His strength and courage
Were renowned throughout these lands.
When he faced a man, he spared no life;
Even half a boar's carcass
Would not half-fill his belly.
He gathered his courage and prepared;
Inspiration came to him from the unseen.
When his khan gave the command,
Would Odu merely stand and watch?
Would God strike Odu down?
He mounted mountain-like Kongkul,
Divided Kongkul's tail in twelve,
And tied each part in a sharp knot.
He girded himself in mountain-like iron.
Hanging at his arm
Was an iron mace as large as a yurt:
If you take fright, flee far away;
If you meet it in fury,
Your poor head will be smashed to pieces.
He lashed the horse Kongkul,
And thundered out his challenge:
“Let not merely one of you, but a thousand
Heroes of the Burut people charge me!
If you would fight, the Kakan host is ready;
