With an army sixty thousand strong,
The blessed Manas, the great lion,
Was said to become your companion.
Sarybai, whom I shall fight,
May you bar his path like fog.
Hero of all the people,
Morning Star of the cherished flowers,
Shelter from the stormy summer,
Hero of the valiant Kyrgyz,
The most formidable of spearmen,
The mightiest of renowned lions,
Who surged with fury at the sight of foes,
Whose forefathers
Encountered the sacred night,
In the ravine where they stayed overnight,
Wrapped in an ancient plane tree,
Protected at its roots by Khizr.
He released whomever he set his eyes upon,
The dervish named Khizr
Always accompanied him as a companion.
Father Manas, protect me,
Here along the Talas,
Noble steeds and broad-blazed racers,
Almambet and Chubak, names spoken together,
Whose names are heard though they are gone,
Heroes whose patron spirits live,
Who shattered ranks with thrusting spears,
For the great common people,
Who fell to bullets in Kakan.
Struck down in their blooming prime,
Laid low while still young.
Even Bakburchun of the Six Gates
Marveled at their shooting.
Almambet and Chubak, names spoken together,
Heroes whose patron spirits live,
May your spirits look upon me.
Saying, “He is the descendant left behind,”
From the hands of the wicked,
From the infidel’s grievous snare,
From the burning embers of hell,
May you deliver and save me.
Azhybai and Bakai, two khans,
May you also lend me support.
Syrgak and Sergek, the twins,
Boundless champions of the wild,
Warriors and lions against the foe,
Auspicious heroes who have passed away,
Sarybai, whom I shall fight,
Look upon me with favor.
From this infidel Sarybai,
Protect and save me yourselves.
The one who came to fight the infidel,
The one now facing him,
Is I, Külchoro,
From the day Külchoro mounted a horse,
Sired by my father Almambet,
And borne by my hapless mother,
The fairy maiden Aruuke,
How many fearsome foes have I seen!
How many dire afflictions have I faced
From those who would slaughter every last
Descendant of Khan Manas!
No man dared confront me,
No enemy I faced defeated me.
Khans who rode against me died,
Many mighty warriors died.
Saying, “I will eagerly wage war,”
Khans of China fell.
Coming to grapple with me,
How many giants and ogres lay slain!
Orphaned of mother and father,
I wandered until I came of age.
Joining the people as best I could,
I matured and became a man,
Becoming the equal of heroic sons,
While our uncle was the sovereign khan,
And watchful foes were laid low,
I went on wrestling,
And no champion who came could defeat me.
Our fathers and forefathers,
