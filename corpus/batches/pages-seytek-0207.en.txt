Our beautiful hair was shorn,
And we ourselves became plunder.
Once precious, we were ruined;
Our proud and noble heads
Were branded with names of shame.
Your mountain-like father passed away,
And we were left in disgrace.
Wailing and weeping in sorrow,
Deprived of the true falcon,
They wrecked and shattered the royal camp.
The red-gowned, slender-waisted,
Gracefully swaying maidens
They seized wholesale as plunder.
They bloodied the mouth of my pen,
And ground my honored ones like grain.
They turned my silk into wool,
And made my warrior a slave;
Noble brides and white-haired matrons
Seized your mother
And imprisoned her as a bondwoman.
I survived my bondage and recovered,
While you were born in exile and grew;
Ninety beasts were slaughtered for your feast,
And you were given the name Seytek.
You became a hero, became a lion;
You became the one who shelters us.
The haughty Jediger who drove your caravan
From broad Keng-Kol—
Now take your vengeance upon them.
They scattered my people from Talas;
Release from Kyyaz
My long-festering pain and anguish.
Bind your heroic uncle Külchoro
Firmly to your side;
Make all who confront you bow,
Pursue the hero’s vengeance,
And bring stern calamity upon the foe.
Take divinely guarded Külchoro
To stand as your champion.
If you ask what became of Külchoro—
From one sign I see,
He seems to have passed into the unseen;
From another sign, your father
Seems merely to have gone upon a journey.
The wide world became narrow,
And for your majestic father
We were left grieving like this.
Khan Bakay, father of the people,
Lost the one he cherished
And languished sorrowfully, blaming himself.
Your grandmother endured bitter grief,
Deprived of her camel-calf.
Her back broke and bent double,
And the two eyes of my heroic, fierce mother
Bulged from their sockets.
Her breast must have festered,
Her entire body must have trembled;
She must have suffered all that fate could show her.
Crying “My foal!” and wailing long,
The deeply afflicted matriarch
Must have endured a living death.
She must have kindled an inextinguishable fire,
And, from sheer need to survive, tended
Kanchoro’s calf.
The wide world became narrow;
Grieving and wailing, she turned to snow,
With no means to rise again,
And must have remained there, frozen in misery.
She must have suffered all that fate could show her,
Her ears deafened, her eyes still seeing;
If you consider it, such must have been her state.
When your heroic father vanished,
We suffered all that fate could show us,
And at the hands of the invading enemy
We endured punishment and died.
The peoples of the whole world gathered,
And all the noble ones were slaughtered.
Khan Sary died first among the khans,
The shepherd of the people of Sandyrga;
All the heroes died.
