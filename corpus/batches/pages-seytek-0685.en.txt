“My looming presence at the fore,
My sheltering shadow, Külchoro.
My coat to wear against the cold,
My host of forty thousand warriors.
If I send him ahead, he brings good fortune;
If he follows behind, he is like a countless host.
He rides onward against the foe,
My deep-rooted plane tree at the fore,
Unyielding when he mounts for battle,
My dark-blazed steed, my refuge.
Striking at the shouting foe,
My battle-axe of keen white steel,
My bulwark when I turn to God,
My mighty support on a month-long journey.
My looming presence who takes the fore,
My plane-tree shadow when he rides.
Fire cannot pierce it though tamarisk burns,
An arrow cannot pierce it though fired from a matchlock,
Its collar gold, its sleeves brass,
Double-ringed mail with lark-eye links,
Unwearied when he enters the shooting,
My host of sixty thousand dark-clad warriors.
My cherished Külchoro,
Father, my dear foster-brother,
I turn the mouth of every steed toward you.
From Almighty God,
For you yourself, my valiant one,
I make a special plea”—
So Semetey spoke,
Raising his hands in blessing,
Entrusting him to the spirit
Of Manas, departed to the other world.
He spread his palms and strode forth,
Entrusting Külchoro
To Almighty God:
“May God set this deed aright—
The hero protected by the spirits,
Valiant hero Külchoro,
The courser that races across the desert,
The falcon worth the whole world,
My people who once lived free!
Do not let it be said that Sarybai took them,
Do not break my pride.
Do not destroy my fortune;
Each time we seemed spent, we flared anew,
For we were sons of deep-rooted Manas.
We were servants beloved by God.
Guarding the land and tending the people,
We were sons of gray-maned Manas.
Should a prince’s descendant
Ever fall into misery?
Is it right for a nobleman’s descendant
To suffer such distress?
When Sarybai is his adversary,
Is it right that he should fail?”—
So he cried out in anguish,
Imploring the spirit
Of the magnanimous hero gone to the other world.
To confront the infidel, son of a monster,
To meet him face-to-face,
To sow havoc if he turned,
He urged Surat beneath him,
Throwing the whole world into alarm,
Making him stride high,
Making him gallop with pounding bounds,
Making him pace with sweeping strides.
When your warrior mounted his horse,
Saint Khydyr himself
Seemed to lead it by the bit,
And all appeared as vast as mountains.
From the well-led Kyrgyz people,
What else could the poor warrior do?
He rode out alone from their ranks.
Watching him, Semetey
Shed tears from his eyes,
And his dark eyes, like a merlin’s,
Filled with tears without blinking.
Though he had mustered so many troops and hosts,
His heart smoldered heavily,
And your hero wept, his voice ringing:
