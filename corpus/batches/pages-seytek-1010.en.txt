They left the settled nation, the teeming people,
Thus abandoned to desolation.
When the warriors departed on their journey,
Who, without wearying his jaw, will whet
The black-bladed battle-axe?
If the vast Kyrgyz nation remains,
Who will lead it without bringing grief?
Who will keep the red-shafted Zulfiqar spear
Sharpened without stirring it?
If the age-old Kyrgyz people remain,
Who will lead them without wavering?
If the precious lion closes his eyes,
There are Chinese on the frontier,
Russians on the right,
A mass of guns before us,
And many hostile foes.
How shall the villages pitch camp?
What remedy will remain
For the few scattered, wandering people?”—
The nation said this with sobs,
And all the heroes grieved.
Everyone old enough to understand
Said, “Now disaster has come,”
And despaired of life.
As the people stood mourning,
The dear lion whose courage was mountainous,
The doomed hero, heavy with grief,
Prepared himself like a hawk.
Into the dear lion’s mouth
Wisdom came from the unseen;
His heart withdrew and turned away,
And his gray-wolf valor showed.
Leaving this wretched world behind,
He bade farewell to the countless people:
“Great and small, old and young,
Elder sisters, sisters-in-law, and younger sisters,
All of you stand here.
White-bearded wise elders,
You too stand here.
My thick-armed tigers,
My stout-limbed heroes,
When the black musket cracks,
You enter battle without tiring.
When the fierce gun snaps,
You enter battle without fear.
Thick-armed and stonehearted,
Stout-limbed heroes;
Warriors who sow wisdom, whose wrath is fierce,
And whose cunning is complete;
My host arrayed before me,
My heroic Kyrgyz bulwark,
My stronghold, you stand here.
My inheritance from my forefathers,
My inexhaustible treasure—
My people, you stand here.
Kara-Kyr, where the people summer,
My stronghold, you stand here.”
The heroes Bakai and Semetey
Spoke softly, leaving solemn words.
From among the five warriors,
The hero Semetey began to speak:
“My dear people, fare you well.
Our journey has wearied and reached its end;
Our hearts have chosen and turned away,
For this wretched world, vain to behold,
Appears ready to lead us onward.
From the mountain I made my landmark,
I clasp your hands in farewell,
Saying, ‘Farewell, remain in peace.’
Our journey has grown old, our eyes shine;
From the fleeting pleasures of the world,
We prepare to ride away, our eyes shining.
Grim death has entered my thoughts;
Those destined to depart are going
To the land of no return.
Our hearts have chosen and turned away;
This vain world, brief as a handspan,
Appears ready to lead us onward.
We have begun to reckon our journey,
