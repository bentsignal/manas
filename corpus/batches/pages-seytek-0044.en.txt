You have called him another man’s son,
Though the young hero came from your own flesh.
It has been eleven months since you took her,
And good fortune and prosperity have settled upon you:
Your wife has borne a son.
Consider the reckoning, hero Kyyaz.
If you destroy your own child,
And in Aichürök’s heart
An unresolved grievance remains,
If dark suspicion lodges in her mind,
May she not put on her swan plumage,
May she not go seeking Khan Akun,
Gathering an army from Akun,
And mustering a mighty host.
When summer comes,
Let us not mount mares with flowing manes;
When autumn comes,
Let us not drive lambs with streaming tails.
Let us not face one another across the slopes,
Clashing and fighting,
And fall into some extraordinary feud!
Let us not load the camels,
Nor, by attacking her child,
My hero, bring some strange torment upon ourselves!
If we attack her child,
If we become needlessly entangled in disaster,
This unfortunate Chürök,
Capable of wondrous upheaval,
Has the strength to gird her waist,
And a place of refuge already secured.
This keen-eyed woman knows a great deal
And commands respect among the people.
Will she not summon those who rule all the earth,
And gather every lord?
Will she not gather every hero
Who boasts of strength and giant stature?
Will she not turn the quarrel bitter?
Sparing not her life, will this woman
Not rise and fight us?
Kara Döö, whose fame reaches across the world,
Stands there grinding his teeth.
The blessed and impartial hero Kutu,
Cried out like a young goose;
Your poor uncle Kutu’s
White beard gleamed.
His counsel was wise, his words were honey;
Once he grieved, no man like him remained.
Speaking gently but with firm resolve,
Dear Kutu began his speech.
Hero Kutu spoke these words,
Saying this to hero Kyyaz:
“My son Kyyaz, you have honor.
Let blessed Kutu speak one word;
Hear it and understand, gray-maned lion.
My son Kyyaz, heed my words!
When God strikes a khan,
He clashes with his wife
And battles with his people.
They grasp spears and bind themselves for war;
He becomes a stranger to his kin
And an enemy to his own nation.
When calamity is about to engulf a khan,
He begrudges each task he gives his servant
And counts every morsel eaten in his house.
Good fortune departs from the khan’s head,
The golden throne vanishes beneath him,
That mountain-like support disappears,
And then, crying out in a little cluster,
His people leave him too!
A good man’s head remains unharmed.
When God is about to strike a khan,
Let alone his people—even the wife in his embrace
Becomes his enemy.
Kyyaz, you were the hero,
And I was the people’s orator.
Your thoughts have become divided:
You have called him another man’s son,
Though the child came from your own loins.
You have treated as a stranger, hero Kyyaz,
The spring that flowed on the Night of Power.
