Let us not be caught unawares!
If we set out for Kakan-Chin,
Let dust fly into the sky,
As the army gathers in a mass.
The spearheads clashing,
Each intending to go on holy war,
The army racing with a roar.
While your lion father lived,
He chose seven heroes,
Stationed them in seven places,
Had them called sons of the accomplished,
Brought great conflict upon Beijing,
And ravaged every place they reached.
All the fine-bred horses,
He made prance in the mountains;
The seething Chinese,
He made their wives and daughters weep,
And destroyed the cities
Of the black Chinese and Manchu.
Bringing hardship upon China,
Your adversary Kakan-Chin—
If we wage holy war against Beijing,
Leveling it flatter than a palm,
Making springtime seem no more than autumn.
The army marching in a mass,
The earth hidden in haze,
Dust flying into the sky.
The march becoming a wondrous road,
The whole world stirring,
The army surging and spilling forth,
In the eyes of Kalcha Kongurbai,
Appearing like a monster—
Thus let us go to Beijing.
Against the seething unbelievers,
Against hero Khan Kongurbai,
Thus let us wage war!
When Muslims set out on holy war,
My only support, hear this:
Such is the nature of battle!
Raising the white sacred banner,
Shouting the cry of “Manas!”
With the white-mottled standard as our banner,
With plentiful shouts and uproar,
When we meet the Chinese,
The bronze cannon booming,
When the hosts meet in full,
When the general battle begins,
The battle-axes clanging,
Sharpened swords flashing,
Spears breaking apart,
Human blood spilling,
Ah, my foal,
A man's valor
Appearing like a lion.
When the facing enemy is scattered,
My only son of a hero,
Is that not a mighty marvel!
While my hero Manas lived,
With hero Bakai before us,
We brought calamity thus;
Crushing the enemy we faced,
We took them within a single day.
If death came, we died;
All those we faced,
When we advanced, we burned like fire.
While my gray wolf Manas lived,
I reclined in armor no bullet pierced,
And when they provoked me,
I crushed the Kyzylbash.
While bloodthirsty Manas lived,
I reclined wearing my shield,
And when they angered me,
I smashed and ruined the Chinese.
While your comrade lived,
Their gold, silver, and daughters—
Alas, what a woeful, dreadful day,
Why did I not die while plundering them!
Chinmachin and the Kakan,
