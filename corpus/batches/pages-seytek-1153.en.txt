He made him understand life’s preciousness.
He revived the life
Of the sobbing Seytek, who had been near death.
Declaring his authority as guardian,
He pulled him back toward home.
Seytek, growing angry and impassioned, said:
“Ah, my uncle Akjoltoy,
A brave man may indeed be reckless;
When young and drinking liquor,
His mind is thin and he is drunk.
But does a man turn back from death?
Can anyone retreat from his appointed doom?
A valiant man, a heroic man,
Without contending for land or tending a people—
Let such a living soul sink into the earth,
For does not every soul die in the end?
Feeding in hollows and passing his days,
Even the most cowardly creature
Still lives out its allotted time,
But its days pass in fear,
And even a sheep dies among the reeds.
For honor and for dignity,
Does not a heroic youth die?”
So spoke brave Seytek,
Arguing grandly before calming down.
Having gained knowledge through the prize,
He obeyed Akjoltoy,
For Akjoltoy was his appointed guardian;
They separated out and took the horses,
Then turned back and took the road,
Coming at last to camp in Talas.
Riding colts and flying hunting birds,
They ranged the hillsides and hunted deer,
Lived at ease without disquiet,
Played bagalak and raised the dust,
And made broad Talas their summer pasture.
He broke the raiders’ strength,
And set his kinsmen’s hearts at rest;
He kept Talas and made it his land,
A lion of surpassing fortune,
And there he remained.
Let us leave that matter there
And begin the news from elsewhere:
At the edge of Great Beijing,
In a wondrous planted garden,
Upon the throne of great Kite—
Kite’s throne was like this:
A tower raised for pleasure.
Ask not of that tower—
A grove of plane trees surrounded it,
And at its center stood a golden throne.
Before the golden throne,
Upon a mound adorned with jewels,
On a platform built for repose,
Stood a white pavilion with jeweled pillars;
There great Kite lay.
His blue-steel weapon hung at his side,
And a blue-collared robe covered him.
His outstretched legs would not draw in,
His closed eyes would not open;
For many days and many nights
His snoring never ceased.
Unwearyingly he occupied the throne,
Doing entirely as he pleased.
When the pale dawn broke,
And wind moved through the valley,
When stars scattered from the sky
And the earth opened in glimmering light,
As though some man would come bearing news,
Then Kite’s
Snoring subsided,
His eyes opened from sleep,
His outstretched legs drew in,
And his heart heaved in turmoil.
He put on his battle gear,
Set his battle-axe in readiness,
And prepared himself for the hearth of war:
