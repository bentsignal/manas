He has commanding presence too,
The resourceful companion
Has strength as well.
Eloquent of tongue and masterful in speech—
Can there be another man like this companion?
Would it be right to speak so
Of that wretched companion?
Immeasurably swift,
The keen and valiant companion.
His prowess is beyond measure;
Named the companion of companions,
He has hardened himself on blood.
Among all the heroes
Known by ear and seen by eye,
He has won renown as Külchoro.
A mighty-born lion of a companion,
Perfect in every limb.
Have you ever heard
Of a hero who could annihilate
Not only China, but the whole world?
His forefathers, who chased white deer
And herded them like cattle,
Were the ancestors
Of the companion who made battlefields his domain.
They chased blue deer and herded them like cattle;
This Külchoro, now lying buried,
Made every place he sought his domain.
From the aged patriarch
Khan Koshoy of Katagan he received a blessing;
Joining all twelve limbs in strength,
This incorrigible companion
Came to be called the bloodthirsty warrior.
Ninety beasts were slaughtered for his feast,
And he was granted the name of Lion.
His quality as a swift steed was plain:
Beneath him was the racehorse Buurul,
And even in old age this leopard
Could still deceive the Kalmyk.
He is Semetey’s kinsman,
One who can never drink his fill of blood,
Another lion of the Kyrgyz.
Orphaned of his father,
Born without ever seeing his face—
Such was the companion’s fate,
Yet he stirred turmoil throughout the world.
Wearing an amulet around his neck,
He was raised by Kanykey;
They kicked and frolicked together like foals,
And, like true brothers,
Contended for milk at the same breast.
These words are no falsehood:
If you ask who the boy is,
He is bloodthirsty Semetey’s companion.
And Semetey’s affair stood thus:
The Kyrgyz and Chinese, the two peoples,
Hurried to declare that they had made peace,
While between them Buurul reared and plunged
As though his master had been slain.
They made him pace back and forth,
And drove all the Kyrgyz and Chinese
Into frantic commotion,
As though a slaughter had struck them.
Amid them Külchoro
Rode forth as an envoy,
With all his weapons made ready,
And a sheathless sword at his waist.
Like a cloud returning with rain,
Wrath spread across his face.
Ah, thick-armed and stone-hearted,
A powerfully built daredevil,
When vast armies closed in combat,
He never opened his flank in fear;
When he entered the fray,
He crushed men to earth like rabbits.
The boy was surpassingly noble,
And he was a lion besides,
A wise and discerning hero
Who knew every stratagem of the foe.
