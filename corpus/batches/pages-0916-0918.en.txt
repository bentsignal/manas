If hardship threatens your entrusted life,
My lion, at your side
I shall be your companion.
March in holy war against the unbelievers,
And keep me in your thoughts!
If you find yourself hard pressed,
I myself shall come to your aid;
If fierce strife and turmoil arise,
I shall be your companion!”
With these words, Shai Ata
Turned Kayypboz's head.
With Ajybai, Bakai, and Chubak,
And generous Manas—all four—
Again and again Shai Ata
Clasped their hands.
The five holy elders who clasped hands
Were Shai Ata himself;
They looked, but could not see him,
None knew where he had gone:
He vanished from their sight.
For the generous prince Manas,
Zhelmaian had been found,
And his honor was thus secured.
Taking a shortcut along the narrow path,
Ajybai and Bakai led the way.
Father Shai had come and given his hand:
Khizir's blessing met him in the wilderness.
Through this, the hero Manas
Gained the title “Lion”!
Wherever he spent a night,
Leafy plane trees and willows sprang up.
For the blood-shedding leopard Manas,
God had been a helper from the first.
Wherever he made camp,
A plane tree stood to keep watch.
The champion favored by Khizir
Was supported by Khoja Khizir.
His forefathers were all khans,
Favored by Khizir from their beginnings;
His earliest ancestors were all khans,
Favored by Khizir from the first.
When he attacked in fury,
No enemy could stand against him.
He was that noble lion himself;
None who opposed him escaped alive:
He was the very eye of the leopard.
Zhelmaian and Chubak had been found,
Seven saints had given him their hands:
That was when the lion Manas
Was touched by their blessing.
Leaving minds astonished,
The generous prince then
Loomed like the Ala-Too mountains.
At one glance, a thousand men;
At another glance, one man:
The wolf-maned hero of great fortune
Had powers to bring to bear on many.
While valiant Manas was there,
Could any mortal face him?
Could an enemy confront and defeat him?
Could anyone bring suffering on his people?
Could those who clashed with him survive?
The mortals who obeyed his words
He brought to submission with words;
The fools who would not obey
He subdued with fury.
In tamarisk-covered Keng-Kol, broad Talas,
And Uch-Koshoy he spent the summers.
Whoever provoked his anger
He took between his fangs and chewed.
Wherever his reach extended,
Of the prince favored by Khojosan
There was no mortal who did not ask.
His companion was the hero Bakai.
Of dear Prince Manas,
Which deeds could one begin to tell?
Mighty Bakai led the way;
Ajybai of the Argyn khan,
And Akbaltay's son Chubak
Sought hunting expeditions, searching for game,
Hunting argali, pursuing wild asses,
Never weary of seeking game.
On the open yellow plain,
Wild asses, six at a time,
They shot and brought tumbling down,
Then loaded onto Zhelmaian.
They crossed the Mediyen desert;
Kayyp-Mazar, Kara-Kyr—
Past the shrine of Kayypchy
Manas had just gone.
From Zhanggaktuu's grassy upper slopes,
With Manas at their head,
The hero Bakai then,
At the place of their ablutions,
Put their hands into the water;
They washed their hands for prayer,
Put their feet into the water,
And washed their feet for prayer.
The generous Bakai then
Raised the ringing call to prayer.
As they recited their prayers,
And had just offered a blessing,
On the ridge of Kum-Bulak,
At the place of their ablutions,
Something appeared from the unseen:
A gray puppy the size of a hand,
Its eyes not yet open,
Only just newly born,
Was found by Manas Khan.
To Manas, favored by Khizir,
All the finest creatures came;
To Manas, favored by Baabedin—
The other day, at Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
While the Kyrgyz lay at ease,
He went out eagerly hunting.
To the champion favored by Khizir
Every precious thing came his way.
His fortune blossomed anew;
Upon the champion of surpassing fortune
God's light of blessing shone.
Zhelmaian, the gift of Shai Ata,
For wolf-maned Manas himself,
Was a mainstay for carrying loads.
His Chubak, who had come from afar,
Was a fortunate path when sent ahead,
An entire host when driven into battle.
