With an army of some six thousand,
To Khan Kökötöy's memorial feast,
To the edge of the great gathering,
Ürbü came down along the road
With an army of some six thousand.
From this side of them,
Dwelling in Sary-Arka,
Men called formidable and grave,
Their horses splendid dun racers,
With white blazes on their brows like amulets,
All of them peerless thoroughbreds.
Their racehorses pranced and snorted;
Their men were of another order,
Their mottled breasts heaving.
Dust billowed overhead;
The many Kazakhs of Aydarkhan
Came shouting and singing ölöng songs.
Wearing a coat of blue iron,
Kökchö, khan of the Kazakhs,
Rode a blue-mottled racehorse,
Waving a blue-mottled banner.
Kökchö came along the road
With an army numbering forty thousand.
After the Muslim army arrived,
Dust curled into the sky.
The Muslim army assembled,
And set aside the old and aged livestock
For the Kalmyks.
Hearth pits were hollowed out;
Milk-fed camel calves and barren yearlings
Were slaughtered for those who came.
The arrivals hastened about,
And saying, “May a son be like this,”
They bowed gratefully
Before Kökötöy's son Bokmurun.
Alas, Kozho, my soul's friend,
Those were the eyes that saw it;
Those were the words that were spoken.
That was where infidel and Muslim
Crossed paths.
May God never show us such a sight!
Before one could say this or that,
In the blink of an eye,
The people burst into alarm.
They seized their spears;
All the Muslims
Turned their minds to battle.
They ran to their horses,
Despairing of their entrusted lives.
The reason they despaired was this:
There was Khan Koshoy, leader of the elders,
And dear Töshtük was there as well;
Among the assembled Muslims
There were lions besides Manas.
The Black Kalmyks and Manchus
Had reached the appointed place;
Kalmyks countless as hairs on a cow
Had covered the land.
Seven standard-banners came;
A din that split the earth came.
One flank of the Kalmyks
Skirted Ketmen-Bel,
While the upper part of that army
Pressed against Aygyr-Zhal.
When they asked whose army it was,
It was Great Zholoy's army.
Because the Kalmyks were so numerous,
They began to cause havoc;
The army that came from the Muslims
Was ready to flee.
They halted in agitation,
Cursing one another.
Saying brave Bokmurun had gathered
A strange people no nation had seen,
“Let them plunder if they wish;
They are guests who have come to us,”
They exchanged formal courtesies
And endured the trouble,
