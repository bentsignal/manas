Kanchoro rode out,
Calling the clamor of Khan Manas.
His broad red radiant face
Was hidden rather than shown.
His courage was no ordinary thing;
In bearing and appearance,
He was no less than hero Chubak.
There was the lion named Kanchoro,
Who conceived crooked schemes
And had a dark-hearted nature.
Were it not for his treachery,
His fortunes too were complete.
His nature was to endure six months of riding;
Raised and protected to maturity,
He was Semetey's own wing.
He had fed him the fat of the rump
And caused the sacred gray-maned hero
To call him Chubak's son.
Kanykey had given her breast
To Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
And nursed both of them.
When Kanchoro sucked the breast,
His voice reached God;
Kanykey lost her strength,
And from her breast then
Dark crimson blood flowed.
"In the end this one will be an enemy;
Kill him outright," she said.
Poor Kanykey wept.
"The lone boy Kanchoro—
What could he possibly do?"
Young Semetey would not consent.
He was a dark-hearted lion;
Otherwise Kanchoro
Was a lion who knew the enemy's devices.
He too prepared against the Jediger,
Praying to the Creator.
What he chose and mounted from Talas
Was Kobosh Khan's tulpar.
The kasaba standard floated in his hand;
A golden-cheeked battle-axe
Flashed upon his arm.
The sight made hearts waver;
Kanchoro of Khan Chubak
Looked like his father.
Against the teeming Jediger,
To charge and thrust a spear,
To take his ancient revenge,
To unleash a great tumult,
To die if death came,
Or, if he survived, to break
The Jediger into fragments.
If death came, he would fall to a bullet
And die at the Jediger's hands.
Kanchoro rode out surging,
Taking a white sacred banner
And a clamorous battle cry.
He emerged from the gate
And came apart from Afghanistan.
Black-bearded hero Tolok
Set his course toward hero Tolok's army;
Kanchoro rode out panting.
Baytaylak, son of Bakay,
Kalyk of Sary Khan,
All rode together to war.
Agybay, son of Agy,
Brought a great army from Afghanistan,
Bringing troops in abundance.
As they prepared and gathered the people,
His white turban was cauldron-sized,
And when he shouted, his voice was like the call to prayer:
Akun Khan's son stood apart.
The command drum was beaten;
Akun's people closed in,
Dividing into many kinds.
The red banner with golden finial
Appeared from his horde.
