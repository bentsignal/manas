AICHÜRÖK SHOWS SEYTEK
TO KÜLCHORO
Then Külchoro,
Whose wings had been clipped,
Who had lost his mighty lord,
Had passed his days in slavery
And withered in servitude.
Now his sister-in-law dressed him in fine clothes
And filled his soul with joy,
Yet the awaited moment seemed late:
To see Seytek, heir of the blue-maned wolf,
Just once, Choro’s
Two eyes yearned as though they were four.
His heart burned within him,
His arms had grown thin as reeds;
He longed to look upon
The orphan left by the hero—
This was now his dearest wish.
Burning with anguish and humiliation,
He felt the time long overdue
To see brave Seytek.
His senses reeled in helplessness,
The bones of his frame went slack;
His two eyes yearning as though they were four,
Külchoro stood staring imploringly.
This was why he implored:
The one who would support him climbing,
The one who would steady him descending,
The spring flowing down the hill,
The beloved of his sorrowing soul,
The young falcon left by the great-hearted hero.
His support in every trial,
The baby left by his mighty lord—
Could your hero die without seeing the child?
He was the wing on which Choro would circle and fly;
To see him once was
Külchoro’s consuming desire.
His heart burned like embers;
Enslaved and humiliated,
He longed to see just once
The child left by that fallen man.
His heart was pierced and torn,
His many torments rose before his eyes;
Overcome and distraught,
He readied himself like a hawk,
Found inspiration from the unseen,
And pleaded with Aichürök:
“My dear sister-in-law Aichürök,
Show to my eyes
The spring flowing through the valley,
The lamp left by the hero!
The plump gray falcon,
The morning star, the gray goshawk—
Bring him to me, sister-in-law,
That I may open my eyes and behold him,
Open my mouth and kiss him,
Press him to my breast,
And scatter all my sorrow!
Let this precious soul be born anew,
As though its former self had died;
Let Külchoro, your kinsman and khan,
Be as one newly born.
Let heroes race over hill and mountain;
Today let my fallen elder brother rise again!
Let the tears streaming down both cheeks
Pour to my skirts like a flood;
Let my swelling heart become a lake.
Today let long-buried Külchoro
Rise grandly like Ala-Too,
His sorrow gone, a warrior once again!
Let me bind Seytek, born of the hero,
Securely to my waist;
I shall confront brave Kyyaz
And exact payment for my heroic elder brother’s blood!
