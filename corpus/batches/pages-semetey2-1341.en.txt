From the fury of my curse
The black water dried away.
Wishing my curse to prove false,
I spat upon a black stone;
Unable to withstand it, the black stone
Split clean apart.
It seems my curse struck like a whip;
It seems the leopard sultan has died;
It seems God has punished me openly;
It seems my noble lord has died;
It seems the enemy has swarmed upon us!”
Poor Aychürök, the woman,
Lost heart and came apart;
The tears poured from her eyes.
Those dear eyes like a quail’s
Shed tear after separate tear;
Those dear eyes like black currants
Shed tears a whip-length long.
“My bloodthirsty lord!” she shrieked,
And sobbed until she wept blood.
“How can I live without the sultan?”
The proud beauty screamed;
Weeping blood, she lamented,
And Aychürök stood there wailing.
When she saw the enemy’s siege,
Her black hair untouched by comb
She tore out strand by strand;
Her black hair untouched by braid
She flung across her breast.
Her white face, like the risen moon—
Curse this womanhood of hers!—
She clawed open through the middle.
Her hair thinned like meadow grass;
The blood flowing down both cheeks
Poured across her breast.
Upon the woman plunged into mourning,
The enemy surged ravenously.
Her close-fitting dress and quilted coat
Were torn apart like saddle straps;
Her dear hair, fine as raw silk,
Was thinned strand by strand.
The tears flowing down both cheeks
Poured over her collarbones;
Blood covered her pallid face,
A film clouded her black eyes.
Every joint in her body shook;
She moaned, wept, and screamed.
Her ten vertebrae and eighty bones
Seemed to separate layer by layer;
Misfortune and grief, great affliction,
Could plainly be seen, if one looked.
Her insides burned into embers;
The gray wolf was gone from the world.
It could be seen that she was left behind;
It could be seen that the massed Jediger
Had surrounded and taken the place.
Whatever the host desired,
When your black-faced sister-in-law looked,
Gray-mottled banners became their standards,
And cries rang from rank to rank;
Pale, hazy banners rose,
And piercing shouts became a tumult.
Around the precious white palace
The forty-two companies thronged;
There was no remedy to be found,
And bitter misery boiled over.
The heroes and lions
Of the Jediger raised
Piercing cries and rallying shouts.
Without looking up or down,
When they came and besieged the stronghold,
Without reckoning that they might die,
Without glancing around them,
The Jediger, eager for plunder,
Did not spare their lives at all;
Their spears bristled,
Their swords flashed,
Their tasseled standards swayed.
