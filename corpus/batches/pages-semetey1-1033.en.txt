He has equipment and ammunition;
Near us,
In China, stands a rogue named Muradyl,
An enemy gathered for mutual destruction.
You begin a terrible uproar;
You deceive me and take my horse,
Then leave me standing like a stump!
You do not understand your own circumstances,
Nor do you travel upon your own road!"
As your lord advanced in a surge of power,
Your poor sister-in-law Churok
Stepped apart from the maidens.
Her mail shirt gleamed;
Radiant, she walked out
Before your hero.
At the sight of her, his body melted,
And your lord's heart was overwhelmed.
The moonlike face
Of Churok, the daughter of Akun, appeared.
Waist like a sheath and face like the moon,
That renowned and doomed beauty
Wore a golden cap upon her head.
That renowned and doomed beauty,
The wretched daughter of Akun,
Displayed her snow-white throat
And overwhelmed the lion's heart.
She stretched with the grace of a lynx,
As though reluctant to spend words in speech.
Her blue leather boots, stitched in paired patterns,
Her double-soled boots,
Gleamed upon her feet.
She walked forward, sleek and stately;
When she laughed, her teeth shone broad as shovels.
Aichurok smiled,
Her face clear as a mirror;
Supple as dressed leather,
She walked smiling and graceful
And came out to welcome her lord.
Whimpering like a puppy,
The heroic lord, the lion—
These were the words Churok spoke;
This is what she said:
"Your words came out in anger.
You have arrived safe and sound.
Your Argyn and Kyrgyz people,
Your elders who have grown old—
I ask after them with longing:
Are the people safe,
And is your aged lion of an elder brother well?
Your land of Keng-Kol, rich with oleaster,
Does it remain safe and prosperous,
And your fortunate Kyrgyz people with it?
Are the possessions inherited from your father
Still safe and sound?
If they are safe, my sovereign,
When will your laughter return?
What shall I do, my sea-like sultan?
I spoke on every side,
Yet you have utterly devoured us with words.
You said, 'They gathered dung.'
Sea-like lord and lion,
Can a bridegroom be like you,
Assailing us from this side?
Lion-like lord,
You displayed your gray-maned fierceness;
Calling us 'maidens gathering reeds,'
You struck us cruelly with false words!
On arriving, you poured out chilling speech;
You called us 'maidens riding a swing.'
Wrapping us in words of torment,
You assailed us from this side!
Do not surge like a cuckoo;
Do not scold Kulchoro!
Do not let your scalp contract in anger;
This conduct of yours is coarse.
Do not quarrel with your servant!
The younger brother you came berating
Has every fortune in balance.
