The women wailed, the people wept,
The multitude stood in turmoil;
The throng that once strode proudly
Now stood there in this state,
Become like witless cattle.
Thus was the great host struck down;
They cried, “My bloodthirsty lord, do not die!”
How could his honor be hidden?
The Argyn people’s hearts sank;
Though they stood there lamenting,
Though they grieved and wailed,
The valiant gray wolf could not be found.
Let us leave that matter there,
And begin with this calamity:
No human should suffer such violence,
No such humiliation exists in all the world.
There, at the dark eye of the spring,
Beside the very straight plane tree,
Below Tört-Kül,
In the garden planted by noble Manas,
On the plain facing Bart-Köl,
Along the Üch-Koshoy,
Within the walled enclosure,
Stood Aychürök’s white palace.
Its strength was of this kind:
Stones were heaped into a mountain,
Cliffs were raised all around it;
Spears struck it and were blunted,
Enemies despaired of taking it.
And at the palace entrance—
Behold all its wonders—
Fire was drawn from six channels of earth;
That luckless palace, matchless in beauty,
Had been fashioned by magic—
Behold the wonder of its making.
Sixty matching black camels
Could not reach it against a raging foe;
None who fought there left alive.
Going farther on,
Fifteen mares had foaled tigers there;
No person had ever approached
A palace of such design,
No enemy approaching it had prevailed.
Those who heard its fame marveled;
Those who saw it wandered dazed.
The white palace stood in a renowned place,
Built at the poor woman’s command;
Such was its strength.
It was bound about with iron,
Sheathed in silver,
And gilded with gold;
If two people spoke,
It sang out by itself.
Lions guarded it all around;
Its gate was made of steel.
The keepers at that gate
Held clubs as large as houses.
Its grim wrestlers—
If even one wrestler was unleashed,
A man’s shoulder would be torn away.
Whenever they saw a stranger, they made ready.
There were seventy executioners
Who wore human noses and ears
Strung upon them as talismans.
Seen from far away by any man,
They raged like white male camels;
They prepared well in advance
For enemies who came to fight.
The white palace stood in a renowned place;
Its strength was of this kind:
No man dared advance toward it.
While that accursed bloodthirsty one was there,
Whoever saw his aspect would panic;
No enemy came near him,
But, losing heart,
Would flee without approaching.
Toward that stronghold with its steel gate,
To seize and plunder the women,
