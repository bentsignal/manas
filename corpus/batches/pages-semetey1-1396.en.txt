His four legs moving swiftly,
Whenever he simply set off,
The tips of the grass whispered.
When he trod upon black stone,
It scattered like millet;
A great bustard, large as a house,
Shrieked above his head.
They traveled without sparing their horses,
Going far, no little distance.
Crossing rugged ground,
Passing over mountain passes and stones.
The sight made the heart divide;
Ask not how the gray wolf fared—
He loomed like Opol-Too.
He rode Buurul, making him surge;
Buurul, restored to full flesh,
He mastered and made leap.
The sight made the heart divide;
His beauty appeared mountain-like.
Crossing a pass no phantom roamed,
Treading land no man reached,
The thoroughbred Bakai led the road;
How could the warriors hold back?
They reached Buurul-Tün.
There lay Buurul-Tün,
Its rugged hills in view,
Making a man's heart divide.
It was a land lying free,
Called Buurul-Ata.
No man traveled there;
Its beasts and creatures
Made any beholder marvel.
Its saplings were like plane trees,
Its birds like snowcocks;
God had truly created
And placed them there.
Its lizards were like snakes,
And its snakes were of every kind;
God Most High in his power
Created living creatures of every kind!
It was a land lying free;
Every beast and creature was there.
A cold wind roared from the valley,
Its breeze fragrant with violets;
Its grasses grew in separate kinds,
Fescue and dark sedge,
Gleaming when the wind blew,
Divided into countless varieties.
It had trees and branches;
Everything the ear had heard of
And the eye had seen was there!
On the plain on this side,
Its tortoises were as large as bowls.
It was a land lying free—
Would the Kyrgyz not settle there!
Would Argyn and Kyrgyz turn away,
As though it were a land unfit to camp?
Koumiss by day and girls by night—
Could such things fail to be there!
Wearing paired sable caps,
Could it be a place not to sing,
Drink koumiss, and court girls—
Could it be a place not to rejoice!
Could it be a place that could not contain
Wonders, festivities, and delights,
Where men could not revel in games,
Could they not set up an ordo here
And make it their abode!
It had hills and passes,
And countless varied highlands;
Projecting headlands came into view,
And looking on them divided the heart.
The finest things in the world
Appeared as visions before the eyes.
There lay Buurul-Tün;
Looking across its low hills,
There were bull-like and camel-like deer—
What wonders there were to see!
