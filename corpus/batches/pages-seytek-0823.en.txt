The enemy who faced them,
They routed with heroic might,
At the sight, the mind reeled,
They loomed like lofty blue mountains,
Their movement seemed an army of forty thousand,
Crouching like lions,
Wherever they surged past,
No mountain could withstand them.
Like a flood that shattered mountains,
Like innumerable hosts engulfing the land,
Though all the world’s peoples attacked,
The great khan’s descendants
Could not be bent or conquered for an age.
The reason they could not prevail:
Before them strode a gray tiger,
A lion like the magnanimous hero,
Throwing the mighty into confusion,
Making the dun horse beneath him
Stride proudly onward.
The holy wanderer Kydyr
Led it by the bridle;
Good fortune and prosperity flourished,
The steed’s dark shape remained,
Its cry splitting stone,
My father, upheld by the forty saints,
Shouted his battle cry,
Raising the cry of “Manas!”
“Boy Seytek and Külchoro,
And Kuyaly, the lion-maiden—
This swine has challenged you!
Seize him, seize him!” he cried,
“Grip your spears and thrust!
Meet him and grapple,
Strip the swaggering swine
Who came so boldly
And bring his death upon him!
With piercing shouts and ringing cries,
Fight wherever you meet,
Cut down all you encounter,
And take revenge for old grudges.
Through the wrath of the turmoil he raised,
Some innocent souls
Died before their destined time.
The land of no return, the true abode,
Many a meek and living soul
Reached because of Sarybay.
Khans died though guiltless,
Many wrestlers and giants died;
He quenched the fire
Of the Kyrgyz in Talas,
Drove them all away,
Slaughtered multitudes,
And brought misery upon many.
Such grievous hardship and great turmoil
Had befallen the Kyrgyz.
Have you heard tell of yesterday,
When Khan Semetey, the falcon hero,
Dwelt in his palace in Keng-Kol
And tethered his fourteen steeds?
Whenever hostile foes came shooting,
He drove them before him.
Whoever provoked his anger,
He crushed between his fangs.
In valor and lionheartedness,
Strong-souled and fiercely brave,
A master wrestler, a peerless hero,
He threw the whole world into confusion,
Making the white courser beneath him
Stride proudly onward.
He robbed every confronting enemy
Of strength and fortune.
When he made arrows rain like a storm,
No enemy ever returned.
The great Manas, surpassing lion,
Appeared in spirit;
The minds of mortal men
Reeled in astonishment,
As he made the matchless steed Akkula
Stride proudly onward.
