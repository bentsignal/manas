The poor, grieving soul at life’s end—
Once they had encountered one another,
Would I not wail myself to death,
Would I not surrender my fly-like soul?
When all who grieve come together,
So this is how it must be.”
Thus the people raised an uproar,
Their hearts crushed as though by lead;
Mother and son were reunited,
They came face-to-face at that place,
A place the mighty Creator
Had truly bestowed.
Gathering the host and assembling the troops,
Stroking his white beard,
Sobbing, “O my only moon!”
Your magnanimous old Bakai
Stood there lamenting.
Surging like embers,
Burning fiercely like fire,
Your heroic grandsire Bakai
Turned his face toward the qibla,
Ready to surrender his poor life.
The old man who had suffered many torments
Could not restrain his tears:
“Alas, blue-maned leopard,
Your living form has appeared.
What more could I want in my grief?
My orphan whom I cherished,
Here, right before you,
Let me sacrifice myself for you.
Yesterday, when my bloodthirsty prince still lived,
When the towering city of Kakanchy
Lay beneath my siege,
When I was beating down
Its khans and nobles,
Did I descend upon some enchanted ridge?
Thirsting terribly in the night,
How else have I survived until now—
Did I drink the water of immortality?
While the bloodthirsty lord, her son, stood there,
She wailed, unable to reach him;
The reason for her wailing was
That she could not pass beyond
The spellbinder’s blinding sorcery.
Weeping bitterly and beating herself,
Poor, withered Kanykei stood there,
Pleading with her only cherished Semetey,
Begging Semetey.
Then Kanykei said:
“My pale-gray falcon,
My hazy, tranquil morning star,
The one for whom I braided my servant’s hair,
The one I waited so long to see.
If my racehorse dies, you are my hoof,
You are the talon of my falcon,
You are the hoof of my racehorse.
My falcon, you shall be lightning…”
His mother stood weeping,
Her heart wrung with anguish.
The blood flowing down both cheeks
Fell upon her breast.
When Kanykei stood weeping,
The birch and willow wept;
The cherished one appeared before their eyes,
And every soul who saw him wept.
The people sobbed and wept,
The newly cherished shore wept,
The precious ones wept, the lions wept,
Countless people wept in anguish,
The heavyhearted lion wept.
All who were weeping
Wept, consumed by their own sorrows.
O bereft and grieving soul of bygone days,
O never-ending tears from the eyes;
Each one weeps, consumed by grief,
Whenever sorrowful misfortune strikes.
When mother and child are overcome,
When a child meets his father again,
The dead return to life,
And the extinguished flame is kindled anew,
