Who had traversed this world—
Was that not Bakai?
Your uncle, champion Bakai,
Started awake from sleep,
Rolling onto his right side,
Starting awake, he recited a prayer,
Saddled Kökcholok,
Readied his arms and armor,
And girded on his unsheathed sword;
Fuming in anger,
Your uncle Bakai the leopard—
Behold his bloodthirsty fury!
Your uncle, valiant Bakai,
Making the ignorant know,
Making the unperceptive perceive.
Crossing rugged passes,
Traveling the lone mountain road,
They journeyed a long while,
Crossing remarkable passes,
Ridges, passes, and mountains.
How could your heroes hold back?
They crossed winding mountains
And forded winding waters;
All the heroes grew eager,
All the coursers grew spirited—
How could the heroes hold back?
They reached and halted at
A gray-hazed yellow steppe.
It was a fine day's journey across.
Your uncle, the gray wolf, had named it
The Fescue Steppe.
Do not ask what that steppe was like:
It had no hill the size of an ankle-bone,
No hollow the size of a saddle,
No furrow touched by a plow,
No hole dug by a marmot.
It gleamed without a trace of haze;
It was the Fescue Steppe,
Its rich growth waist-deep,
A place where the people lay at ease.
When they reached that place,
"My river and spring in the valley,
My young champion found late in life!
Here upon this yellow steppe,
Let us ride without growing weary;
Let us race along the road
And open up the horses' eyes!"
His uncle Bakai said this,
And Kökcholok opened his stride.
Dust billowed like a mountain,
And he quivered as though about to fly;
Semetey and Bakai raced,
They grappled with the mouths
Of the coursers beneath them,
Making them rear and strain,
Making them buck as though their riders had died.
How could your heroes hold back?
At Echoing Jar-Kyya,
Where a single horse-track wound on,
Bakai halted there.
Onto the broken, narrow ridge
Bakai rode his thoroughbred.
Yesterday, your leopard champion Almambet—
There is the pass of Dust-Whirlwind,
There is a lake called It-Ichpes.
Holding Lake It-Ichpes,
There stands on watch Kuuördök,
May the wretch burn in fire.
