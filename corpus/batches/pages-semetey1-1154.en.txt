Never spared one who wrestled him;
The meat of one whole pig
Did not fill him at a sitting.
Was he not capable Zholoy,
Whose fame spread through the world?
Was he not Zholoy who shattered
And took so many lands?
The great Kalmyk who towered over you
Could knock down a camel with one punch—
Was he not the champion Zholoy?
When he met a man on foot,
His brow was like the reedy edge of a lake,
As though he would swallow whoever appeared.
Wearing yellow leather trousers,
Zholoy of auspicious hour prepared.
If you had seen great Zholoy,
He raged like a blue boar:
"If a bastard Burut
Comes to wrestle, I shall throw him;
I shall seize him
And thrust him alive into the grave.
I shall take alone
All the Muslims' great prizes.
When the jostling turmoil begins,
I shall unleash a single havoc."
The spirit of those who saw him failed;
Zholoy came out from the Kalmyks,
Appearing like Mount Opol.
When the handful of Muslims
Saw Zholoy's form,
Their courage failed badly
And they lost their wits.
When the would-be Muslim champions
Saw Khan Zholoy,
They fled from among the people.
Manas too grew anxious,
Wondering which champion would go out
Against the enraged infidel.
Dear elder Koshoy
And hero Töshtük, son of Eleman,
Stood in a row then,
As did all the Muslims.
With a black hat large as a house,
The tiger, bloodthirsty Manas,
Born as the black stallion of honor
For the Muslim people, cried:
"Is there a giant among you
Who will take the infidel champion?
Who will seize his trouser cuffs,
Wrestle like a champion,
Tear whatever comes to hand,
Butt like a blue bull,
And bring merciless havoc
Upon the foremost black Kalmyk?
Is there such a man?
Is there one who will win
Honor for the Muslims?"
Gray-wolf Manas, the tiger,
Mounted a blue racehorse:
"To wrestle once,
To provide the people a spectacle,
To learn what sort of hero is seething there,
And let the black Kalmyk and Manchu people
Tell forever
What the Burut are like—
Hero Töshtük, son of Eleman,
Youngest of nine sons,
Eleman's cherished one,
A mortal of the one God,
How are you against the champion,
To grapple with
That dog who boasts of strength?"
When Manas spoke thus,
Töshtük stood and said:
"My time to wrestle
The boasting Kalmyk has not yet come;
My strength has not yet filled my body.
My flesh remains raw and bruised.
