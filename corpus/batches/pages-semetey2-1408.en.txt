Making their great teeth jut out,
Their lower lips sag,
Their necks rise like ducks',
Their two humps bulge,
Their long hair trail—
A sight to gladden the soul.
Nursing secret resentment and pressing on,
So the bald men might win the prize,
Hero Kiyaz's attendants
Led a thousand riding camels,
Urging them forward with cries.
Hero Kiyaz's attendants
To add to the prize
Led a thousand dark blaze-faced mares.
In the great arena amid the people,
A herald stood upon the tower.
A wrestler came and struck
The great drum that summoned the people.
Black Taz of Kapchalchi
Came out to clash;
Yellow Taz, the judge's son,
Came out to fight him.
Taking lungs in their hands,
Barefoot and bareheaded,
The two bald-pated paupers—
When those two madmen fought,
The people who saw them drew back.
Their two heads shone
As though moonlight struck them, making people laugh.
They staked a great prize
And fought with raw lungs.
The stronger pounded the weaker
Until his strength was gone;
They made a marvelous spectacle.
If you looked upon the great games,
Heroes and wrestlers seized each other's cuffs,
Clashed with one another,
Butted like gray bulls,
And tore at each other like hungry stallion-camels.
Like hungry wolves,
They mauled whatever part they reached.
One of these wrestlers
Had lived in the bloodthirsty leopard's house;
There was a great sorcerer there,
And born of that sorcerer
Was the man called Karadoo.
By what had once been promised,
He was an inheritance
To the bloodthirsty lion himself.
Do not ask what Karadoo was like:
When angered, he could tear
A man's head from his body.
He looked death in the face;
When his fury truly rose,
He struck a camel and split it open.
His wrestling was extraordinary;
His heroic bearing was formidable.
Karadoo was a wrestler through and through;
Such was the champion's form.
Bull-muscled, with stallion-camel thighs,
When he grappled he tormented souls;
Broad-chested, with a lion's breast,
He was a splendid man.
While gray-maned Semetey lived,
He had not yet worn the bulletproof coat,
Nor charged wildly against the foe.
He was still a young man;
He had not yet become the bloodthirsty hero's companion,
And his longing was great.
He had joined Aichurok
And come away as spoil.
Kutchechen of the Zhediger
Had raised him as an adopted son.
Because no rumor had yet spread
That he had been taken as spoil from Talas,
The people did not yet know
What manner of man Karadoo was.
