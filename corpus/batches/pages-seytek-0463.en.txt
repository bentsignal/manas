Without speaking a word or uttering a sound,
You cast embers into my fleshly heart.
You brought great suffering upon my soul,
You drained away much of my strength,
You seared my soul again and again,
You utterly chilled my breath,
You broke my back.
Again you did a wondrous thing,
Setting my very core aflame,
You sought to make my misery boil,
Uncle, I shall die by your hand,
I shall not let you go unless I die.
If I do not see your true resting place,
If I do not die together with you,
If I do not hew off his head with an axe,
If I do not cut through his neck with a sword,
If I do not see the face of the world beyond,
If I do not die locked in your embrace,
May I die unworthy of the name Külchoro,
Let me see whether you will speak.
Just utter something by chance,
And I shall take it as a sign.
If you will not speak to me,
If you will not heed what I say,
I shall disfigure you now,
I shall unleash my towering rage.
I said I had fallen into the embers for you,
I was scorched and became an ember myself.
I burned and burned until I was gone,
I wore out my heroic head,
I aged my strongman's soul.
I was blinded and became as a grave,
I became level with the black earth.
Every ordeal and mighty torment,
I was the one who endured it all.
For you, bloodthirsty villain of the dark day,
Only a spoonful of my blood remains,
My flesh is left bruised and raw,
I fell from my course and was left bereft.
When the mighty gray-maned hero was lost,
They said, “The valiant man has seen
The true abode from which none return,”
They would wash him with koumiss,
And wrap him in brocade,
They would wash him with Zamzam water,
And bind him in cloth of gold,
Entrust him to the wise for safekeeping,
And have singers praise him,
Entrust him to men of honor,
And have poets sing his praise.
If I do not bury him with my own hands,
May I die unworthy of the name Külchoro,
May I sink into darkness as though never born.
Turn your neck toward me,
Uncle, while you are still alive,
Reveal your inmost secret.
The song you spoke in truth,
Speak it sincerely as your creed.
Tell me the secret within you,
Tell me the truth.
When spears are gripped and ranks broken,
And doomed men fly before bullets,
When the great turmoil arose,
What calamity did you wrap yourself in,
What affliction befell you,
Uncle, driven from your people,
What ordeal overtook you?
I have burned and smoldered in anguish,
Long ago I lost all hope,
Then I encountered you on the open steppe,
And found you again for myself.
You turned affliction into a grievous disease,
And renewed my sorrow once more.
Your upland dwelling was a white palace,
Yet I endured much of your hell.
I suffered servitude of every kind,
For food I took blows upon my knuckles,
All the while calling you my princely uncle.
And now, meeting you upon the road,
Finding you on the open steppe,
