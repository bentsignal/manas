They advanced with noses running,
Their weapons numbering eighty-four,
Their onslaught blazing like fire.
When my father, the Ogre,
Saw Manas’s bearing,
He found no ridge to flee across,
No black earth to enter,
No grave in which to lie.
When eagle Bakai and hero Koshoy
Blocked the valley of Kerüüchö
Without letting anyone stir,
When Almambet wrought havoc,
And Chubak and hero Syrgak speared
The champions who shot in ranks
And plundered my countless wealth,
When my father, the Ogre,
Was slain by hero Manas,
They broke open my treasury and made a road through it,
And subdued my warriors,
Loading all my heaped-up riches
Upon my own elephants.
They tormented my very soul,
And plundered all my livestock.
They bewildered my people,
And bent my back many times.
They tasted much of my salt,
Then plundered and carried away
My needle-mouthed, camel-eyed,
Restless dark-eyed
Daughter, beautiful as this,
The strength around which I girded my belt,
And my people, diminished sixfold.
After long ruin I recovered,
I grew up while still in swaddling clothes.
I am a warrior and a hero;
I am Sarybai the champion!
I crush whoever challenges me,
I devour whoever meets me;
I am the black scourge itself!”
Skilled in the ways of war,
Külchoro said this to Sarybai,
And fierce fury seized him.
He ground apart
His broad, gate-like front teeth;
His eyes became burning embers,
His lashes blazing fire.
The warrior could not restrain
His surge, like a rushing river:
Sarybai was ready to fight.
Would a gray wolf hold back?
After Külchoro spoke thus,
The Ogre’s son answered,
Utterly dreadful words:
“If I do not slaughter your whole lineage,
If I do not do all the harm I know,
If I heed your words when you plead,
If I fail to avenge myself,
May I die unworthy of the name Sarybai;
May I sink into the depths unborn,
As though never born of the Ogre!
I have many words to tell,
And if you consider the horses wearied and lives tormented,
You will see how great my grievance is.
In Zhelpinish lived Jarmanas,
Whose wife was Karjygach;
She and Khan Manas were siblings.
They camped along the cliffs,
And, coming to Karjygach’s aid,
Gathered an army from the Kyrgyz.
The noble Bakai and hero Koshoy
Led an army such as this,
Loosing arrows in punishment,
Hiding by night and not marching by day.
They were warriors of many kinds,
Great Manas at the army’s head,
And Karjygach behind the host.
Plumed standards rose and banners thronged,
A dark and dreadful tumult arose,
Throwing the whole world into panic.
