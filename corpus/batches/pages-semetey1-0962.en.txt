When her lord dismounted,
His knee bent to the ground;
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Ran as swiftly as an arrow
And reached the weary Buurul,
Taking hold of him.
At the cast-iron-rooted copper post,
He tied the wretched man's Buurul
With an especially firm tether.
Kanchoro ran up
And took his White Falcon.
Your dear mother Kanykey
Had fashioned an ornate golden perch;
He set the White Falcon upon it
And bound it securely again.
The pampered wife Chachykey,
Her silk with gathered folds,
Her flowing silk rustling,
Stepped backward, hanging back,
Acting coyly like a young child.
Semetey came toward the encampment,
Leading Chachykey by the hand.
He brought her inside with affection,
And set aflame every unburning place
Within Chürök, who watched them:
“His unbearably pampered wife
Struts about, the infidel!
I shall find the right way to speak with her;
If the woman comes to terms,
I shall accept her as an elder sister.
If the woman will not come to terms,
I shall settle our account together;
I shall take her husband away
And cast her into suffering!”
The wretched woman had studied Arabic
And become expert in the language.
When the sun quivered toward setting,
When the people were preparing to sleep,
When the livestock had been tethered,
And the people's arrangements were complete,
Twilight entered and deepened;
Stars of every kind crowded the sky.
Without appearing before a single person,
Without being noticed by anyone,
The pampered wife Chachykey
Went to have a private talk.
If the woman agreed to reason,
She would become her companion;
If the woman would not listen,
She would bring about her utter ruin.
If Chachykey found the proper way,
She would take her as an elder sister,
Serve beneath her,
And, if the poor woman came to her senses,
Stand before her with many entreaties,
Her knees bent to the ground,
Bowing her head in submission.
A sheer cliff rises in the valley;
The wretched daughter of Akun,
An old bondwoman who had seen much,
A sharp bondwoman skilled in transformation,
Stretched herself like a lynx.
Reluctant to spend words in speaking,
She moved obliquely like a white doe.
The unfortunate daughter of Akun
Set aside her pride and began to speak.
On the bank of broad Keng-Kol,
Where twin plane trees had grown,
Aychürök lay in wait
At the place where the pampered wife
Would go to relieve herself.
At Chachykey's side were forty
Attendant daughters-in-law:
Twenty married women and twenty girls,
Drawn from the Argyn and the Kyrgyz.
Weeping wildly in extreme distress,
She had devoted those forty
To accompany her at her side.
