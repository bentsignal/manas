Its roof-ring was gold, its ropes were copper—
Karagul's house broad as a millstone.
Swaying like a blue tiger,
The youngest of the herdsmen,
Bozbala, awoke in fright.
To the tent with golden poles,
He resolved to tell what he had seen
To hero Karagul.
Bozbala rose and spoke,
Telling a horror no one knew,
Speaking no lie but the truth.
When Bozbala entered,
Karagul, son of Katal—
Here was the dark hour.
Look at that raging slave!
Both cunning and a hero,
His name and fame were heard
Throughout the Kakan people;
He was a greater hero than Kongurbai.
He made his old age harshly known:
When his bladder ached,
Forty-two nobles
Lifted that accursed swine
And carried him out to urinate.
They slaughtered a barren, milk-fed foal,
Cut horse-rib fat and set out mane fat,
And laid out food and provisions
For that wretched slave himself.
Black liquor from a leather flask
Was what that accursed slave
Drank to quench his thirst,
Liquor mixed with küpsör.
That ruined slave,
As though he were ill,
Sat enjoying luxury
Like Prince Khan Kongurbai.
Look at the accursed one,
Drinking liquor and heaving;
Do not ask about the accursed one,
Flapping like a blue vulture.
He prolonged the conversation,
Making some sixty herdsmen
Tell their tales in turn.
He had someone who angered him
Tan fresh hide,
And made twelve Kalmaks
Dance awkwardly before him.
Karagul, source of ruin—
Look at his appearance!
That was the wretched slave himself;
Those were Karagul's eyes!
Before Karagul
Bozbala came.
His knee bent to the ground,
And he bowed like a bride,
Straining his throat as he roared,
Shouting in alarm,
Calling, “Abygyi!”
“Last night as I slept, I dreamed;
Abygyi, I shall tell you.
