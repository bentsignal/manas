If only I could appeal to the Dangyts!
Saying nothing and offering no counsel,
He pondered this in his mind,
Writhing like a white tiger.
Wrapped in his blue coat of mail,
Hero Kyyaz, if you had seen him,
Raged like a blue bear.
Hero Kyyaz stood dumbfounded.
Clutching his thigh and working himself up,
So monstrous was Kyyaz’s arrogance
That the hair on his crown stood on end;
Your lord Kyyaz bristled,
Writhing in frenzy.
Dear Kyyaz himself
Hit upon a devious scheme.
Growling and working himself into a rage,
He cried, “All the countless black Jediger,
Come, let none remain behind!”
Unable to bear his anguish,
Kyyaz conceived evil
Against the child born of the womb,
Against the spring flowing from the ravine;
The madman plotted evil
Against the young hero in chain mail.
His fiery eyes blazed fiercely,
His bloodlust truly seized him,
Strength surged through his body,
And on that dark, ill-fated hour,
Kyyaz, destroyer of nations,
Grew black hair across his face.
His two sunken eyes truly blazed,
And he violently ground
The great teeth in his jaws.
He drew in his throat and clenched himself,
Taking breath with a growl.
The countless black Jediger,
Despairing of their precious lives,
Fled away in frantic haste.
Without speaking many words or uttering a sound,
Hero Kyyaz was struck speechless.
Twisting his face and groaning,
Hero Kyyaz’s countenance changed.
That day Kyyaz thundered,
And flew into a fierce rage.
Not sparing a single soul among the people,
This Kyyaz scattered his wrath,
As though he would swallow them whole.
When his wife gave birth, hero Kyyaz
Roared in fury,
And summoned, without exception, those skilled in cunning,
The mighty and wise, eloquent in counsel,
Calling all his people together,
The elders of the Seven Valleys,
Gathering every last one.
The multitude came in turmoil,
And dear Kutu saw
How dark Kyyaz’s face had grown.
Kutu was the blessed elder of the people,
An eloquent man whose mouth poured forth words,
A healer who revived the fainthearted,
A good man who raised others up,
A renowned master of speech,
An accomplished and capable man.
Kutu came and stood there.
That mighty giant Kara Döö
Had formed a plan that day:
If my father, hero Kutu,
Should fall into an argument with him,
And they should fight until both turned pale,
I will draw my sword from its sheath,
And raise a dreadful tumult.
I will crush them like grain,
Striking high and low,
And without killing the child,
I will wrest him from Kyyaz.
What began in the morning lasted until evening;
His foster father, hero Kutu,
Put Kara Döö between them,
And watched with desperate vigilance.
