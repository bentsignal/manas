To the tent upon the riverbank,
We shall go and grapple
With the blood-drinking Kalcha hero!
The people you will fight are the Kalmaks;
Because the Kalmak raided the horses,
Is it right to despair?!
Because the Chinese raided the horses,
Should we seethe in anguish?!
Because the Kalmak took the horses
And brought us suffering,
Should we keep letting them be taken?!
If I remain alive, just watch:
I shall drive my caravan from Kerüü;
Unless I die, I shall take my revenge
Upon the Kalcha khan of the monks!
Because the Chinese took the horses,
Should we seethe in anguish?!
Does a warrior not encounter hardship?
You are children of a dark day;
Does grief not divide one's reason?!
Does a brave man who rides on patrol
Not encounter every kind of peril?!
Can grief serve as payment?
Because the Kalmak took the horses,
Should we go on grieving?!”
He spoke words of courage to the boys.
The dear hero stood towering;
The dear warrior did not stir at all.
Standing erect, he paid it no heed,
And counted the boys' grief
As less than the hem of his robe.
He fastened on his golden belt-pouch,
And radiance like moonlight touched the face
Of the gray wolf, pure-born from his father.
Your gray wolf grew wrathful,
Standing tall without distress;
He mounted Buurul and rode out,
Your dear prince looming high.
In the broad valley of Üch-Karkyra,
Unable to find the trail here,
He now turned back.
Straight across Charyn and Chara,
Your gray wolf descended
Into the Lower Charyn plain.
Nothing caught his eye;
Where the powerful weather-worker Muradyl
Had driven them away
Could not then be determined.
He searched along the mountainside;
They say he examined in turn
Ylailuu-Kyr and Aigai-Tash,
But found no trail.
He searched Kyygach-Kyr,
And followed the winding Kara-Suu
Along its course.
Unable to find the trail here,
He searched across a great distance.
They say he crossed Üch-Kaiky,
And passed through Ükürchü;
He searched down through Üch-Kapkak,
Along the flowing waters
Of the Tekes River.
No trail appeared to his eyes;
Where the mighty weather-worker Muradyl
Had driven them,
And which way he had gone, remained unknown.
The earth billowed into dust,
And Semetey's head reeled.
Through rugged ground and a great valley,
He searched up the great valley;
Your gray wolf, touched by Khizr,
Searched across a great distance.
At Kara-Bulak and Moinok-Tash,
He now turned back.
Turning back and making ready,
Receiving wisdom from the unseen,
The lion son of the bloodthirsty hero
Now went to where the horses had been raided.
