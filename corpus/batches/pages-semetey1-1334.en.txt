If you face the khagan and go,
Against the grim Black Chinese,
If we enter straight and wage war,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
If we exhaust one another,
Choose a people to serve as support;
For the face-to-face battle,
Choose your support from among heroes!
Leave the many and choose the one;
Choose one fit to be a tiger against foes,
A champion who knows the enemy's stratagems!
When the black gun cracks,
Choose a warrior who will not flee;
When the fierce gun snaps,
Choose a hero who will not flinch!
My foal, my son, if you heed me,
If you go to Beijing, go this way:
Take a marksman who shoots the eye,
Bull-necked and camel-thighed,
Who ruins those who grapple him;
Take the exceptional man who does not turn from death!
Who shoots what even a bird cannot reach;
Gather coats bullets cannot pierce.
Gather an army by calling every father's son;
Gather no small force, but a great one!
Against infidels like black worms,
If you grapple, grapple thus;
Against hero Khan Kongurbai,
If you wage war, wage it thus.
Let this year pass over you,
Then go in the following year!
Send word and let us gather
Kanykey's remedies;
Let us fatten fully
All the thoroughbred chargers.
Let the horse grow fat and ready;
Let the hero grow stout and pent with force;
Then go to Beijing!
Your father Manas was a lion;
I was his uncle Bakai,
Who never crossed what he said.
I was Bakai who wore
A bulletproof iron shield!
I was Bakai who knew
Kakan-Chyn's Beijing!
Traveling on campaign for six months,
Going no little way, but far,
To Chinmachin's khagan—
Those people are Chinese of ancient stock,
Their multitude thick as black worms.
The more depleted, the more they smoldered;
They are a people rooted deep.
Fighting until all were exhausted—
Know that I am that Bakai!
The more they died, the more they multiplied;
They are those accursed Chinese people.
My roar splitting stones,
Parted from my own companion,
I am the one now going toward death!
Know this grief of mine!
My only one left by the lion,
Grant me this journey and heed me!
Beijing is no easy place;
The Chinese are a fierce and unruly people,
No people from whom five or six riders
Can exact vengeance!
It has a pass on which it relies,
And a place lying fortified.
Broad-booted and cotton-sashed,
Kongurbai, khan of the Kechil,
It has that swaggering lion.
He still lives, neither dead nor gone;
Bakai has words to tell of him.
Do not act imperiously, my foal;
The ill-starred bloodthirsty man
Has black eyes watching.
What your father destroyed,
What your lion accomplished,
