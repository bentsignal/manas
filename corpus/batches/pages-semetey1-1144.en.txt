The giants of the Muslims
Could find no composure;
When they saw Khan Manas,
As though they had seen their father,
I saw them weep.
“Where is Manas, where?” they cried;
I saw them panic.
The Black Kalmyks and Manchus,
Saying, “The bloodthirsty Burut has come,”
Abandoned their violence;
With Ezkara and Zholoy at their head,
I saw where they fled.
I saw the stone-carrying Chinese
Bow like brides
And fall into turmoil.
Ezkara, khan of the Manchus,
Boasted among the people;
I saw him assess Manas:
“He has a black hat as big as a cauldron.
If infidel and Muslim alike
Attacked him together,
He has the power to swallow them all,
Counting them less than a hair.
If we show hostility to Manas,
We will all meet our end together.
Even if all Beijing came as one,
He would slaughter every one.
The massed Chinese gossip
And call him ‘the bloodthirsty Burut’;
The havoc he brought upon Altai
Was fitting for a gray wolf.
For the Burut who dwell in mountains,
Mountain-like Manas is a fortress.
He himself follows the faith of Islam;
In his heart he means to bring hell
Upon Beijing, where no flood can pass.
He is an imposing lion,
The one lion born
For these Burut.
His wrestling strength is of another order,
A peerless champion born among the Burut.
His battle cry is ‘Burut!’
You are a hero, brave Manas;
Let the Burut destroy these people,” he said.
Speaking many words in grief,
Ezkara drew back.
“Beyond these people lies the Kakan land;
Opposite them lies Bakburchun.
I have seen all of these people.
Even one whom the Chinese call a hero
Is the work of God.
If all Beijing gathered,
He is a man who could slaughter it in one day;
We came as guests and plundered meat—
This too was the work of God.
If resentment enters his heart,
He is a man who could slaughter us all
Here at this very feast.
If Manas roars and gathers strength,
What can we say we have done?
We began the lawlessness
And ate all the meat.
Does he perhaps know
The cowardice of the seething Manchus?
If Khan Manas grows wrathful,
Perhaps he will drive us as far as Kakan.
Beneath him is the dun horse;
Bloodthirsty Manas the lion
Has strength that reaches across the world.
He has armor no bullet pierces;
Mighty Manas has great power.
Seeing his young horse, we attacked;
We erred in demanding Maaniker,
And made ready to seize it.
Beside him is Almambet,
Who made his golden throne into grease
And laid low his father, Khan Azazil.
He follows the faith of Islam,
