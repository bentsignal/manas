His shout split stone;
His roar split heads.
He began a vast tumult
Such as men might know and nations behold.
The Jediger faltered
And began to flee.
“Great black host of Jediger,
You are merely hands that were just fighting,
Merely mouths that were just boasting.
Jediger who claimed to be heroes,
If you flee now, you are finished!
Looking back again and again,
What were you saying just now?
If you are heroes, if you are lions,
If you are the finest people—!”
When Kanchoro spoke thus,
The Jediger rose as one
And all marched thundering.
At the valor of that host,
The earth split with a roar.
Trumpets deafened the ear;
Pale banners became their standards.
A clamor split the sky;
The people cried out and the host swelled,
Countless and abundant.
The attacking Jediger
Divided into surging ranks;
Their savage aspect appeared.
They mounted stallions from the herd,
Took single poles from the woods,
And became wildly excited.
Their strongest men, their lions,
Broad-chested and wide-breasted,
With long poles and pale clubs—
What well-formed wrestlers they were!
The Jediger had grown frenzied,
Bent that day on plundering the horde.
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
They meant to break the horde, seize plunder,
And unleash a great upheaval.
Darkening with grim resolve,
The Jediger entered the fray.
With a cry that deafened ears,
The Jediger entered the tumult.
Into an affliction with no remedy,
The Jediger entered the tumult.
They made their spears ready
And prepared their battle-axes.
They surged, roused to fury,
Saying to one another,
“Mount up, Jediger!”
Heroic Kiyaz thundered;
This was when he became inflamed.
“Break and take his horde!
Raid and take his horses!”
The slippery Kanchoro
Was rousing them at that moment.
When their khans issued a decree,
How could the people not be stirred?
If they refused the khan's word,
Would God not strike the populace?
The leaders of ten bustled,
The leaders of fifty strutted,
The leaders of a hundred ran,
And all the leaders of a thousand
Thus despaired of life.
Their heroes and brave men
Took battle-axes in hand.
Boasting as heroes and lions,
They simply swaggered in warlike pride.
They pounded the earth with a roar;
Their clamor split the sky.
The host filled broad Talas;
The dread and calamitous affair
Was not small but immense.
Clubmen formed the front,
Fighters stood at the flanks,
Swordsmen formed the front—
