Turning their faces toward Berish,
The chosen heroes rode forth.
Lest rain should soak it,
They loaded the white pavilion,
And many heroes secured it upon a camel.
The six horse-tending stewards,
All six equally valiant,
Set out with all the falcons,
Riding with longing toward the maiden.
Akjoltoy led the way,
His swift horse leaving only a streak,
As he left hills, ridges, and winding gullies
Far behind.
Akjoltoy, surging with vigor,
Led them onward.
Whenever they reached a suitable place,
They sported upon their tulpars,
And the restless heroes raced.
At other places
They grappled from the very hem,
And the reckless men seemed
Ready to destroy one another,
Until others seized their hands.
They charged, leveling spears,
And struck one another with whips.
Across countless hills and countless deserts,
They rode tirelessly onward.
Whenever they reached another place,
They began astonishing games.
They immersed themselves in sport,
Shooting when they saw wild asses,
Chasing gazelles and overtaking them,
And playing kök-börü,
Revealing the secrets in their hearts.
The treasures of their dear lives
They spent upon delight,
Immersing themselves in every pleasure
Until all their longings were eased.
As the heroes journeyed,
Across the boundless plain
They wrestled for the goat carcass from the ground.
They never traveled an ordinary journey,
But turned it into a hunt.
They plunged into immense,
Unending revelry.
They rode hard without sparing their horses
And raced in a sweeping free-for-all.
They crossed nameless mountain passes
And deserts no mortal had ever seen,
Beholding wondrous beasts—
Wild bulls and camel-like deer.
Upon the broad plain of Keng-Özön,
They camped in the pleasant valley,
And became guests of Keng-Özön.
Ah, wondrous Keng-Özön,
A valley blessed with perfect abundance.
If you ask what that valley was like,
Apples ripened until the boughs bent;
It was a land beloved by God.
Its springs boiled up and overflowed,
And nightingales sang mournfully.
When a sorrowful mortal heard
The nightingale’s voice, he wept.
Wild beasts inhabited the land,
Where no human being had ever come.
Even the bears had grown tame as cattle,
And its tigers bore twin cubs.
Wolves and foxes dwelt there,
Wild sheep streamed down the hills,
And every creature rejoiced.
Across the marshy islets,
Geese and ruddy shelducks mingled,
Calling loudly to one another.
They rejoiced at the sight of a human;
Who had ever seen such creatures?
Whoever saw them had every longing satisfied.
Great apes and butterflies
Filled its forests.
Ah, wondrous Keng-Özön,
A valley blessed with perfect abundance.
