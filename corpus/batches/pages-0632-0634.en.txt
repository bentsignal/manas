I shall lead you with me and make you a hero,
Make you the equal of a hero's son.
By kneading, I shall make you a house;
By planting, I shall make you an orchard.
If you ask what I am like:
I am a ridge rising high,
I am an utterly fierce-maned lion.
The one a fortunate man will find—
I am your princely elder Bakai.
Upon my wrist, Achbolot—
I am Bakai who hung it there.
The whole world like little birds—
I am Bakai who drove it before him.
My name is Bakai; I am an elder.
I have come from afar, roaming about.
If you heed my words, my Manas,
I have a line to draw,
Many upheavals I could set in motion.”
So spoke great Bakai.
A traveling cauldron with golden handles,
Beside the lion-hero,
Bakai brought and hung up.
They slaughtered a white-gray mare
And ate their fill of pure white fat.
A white Quran as large as a horse's head
They brought out then
And held against their breasts.
As sharers of their gains in this world,
Their bond had now reached fullness;
As brothers in faith for the next world,
They now stood united.
The white Quran, large as a horse's head,
They set between them, they say.
Magnanimous Manas and Bakai
Embraced each other, they say.
Then Manas said:
“Encountered through the unseen,
My khan and elder, there is no hero like you.
For lifting one's brow and taking joy,
There is no land like broad Talas.
Until I go to the other world,
Until this precious life, worn down,
Comes to the dust,
Turn my steed's head for me.
My hawk-like elder, hero Bakai,
Be a father to me;
Stand as my support.
The two reins and one leading rope—
Take the rule of the people.
Me, a spoiled and unruly fellow,
Contrive to set upon the path.
Bakai the hawk, my khan and elder,
Be a steadfast presence for me.
The black-cheeked battle-axe—
Fit its handle so it will not tilt.
Of the Kazakhs and Kyrgyz,
Lead the way yourself.
Take unity in hand;
Set us on the road you know.
Come and settle in Talas,
Fortunate elder Bakai.
Against the Chinese I shall do battle:
Be my steadfast companion!
Placed before me, you bring good fortune:
You are my lion Bakai.
Placed behind me, you bring good fortune:
You are my hero Bakai.
Recognizing Talas as the land for us,
Go up and settle there.
The champion of the forty chosen men—
Be Bakai, favored by Khizir.
Whose blessing enriches the people—
Be like a prophet!
You are my Bakai, my lion;
You have appeared as my refuge,
My hero who is like a prophet.”
When Manas said this,
Bakai, his khan and elder, agreed.
They ate bread of white wheat
And together held the holy Quran.
Their sheep came to share a pen;
Their meals became shared feasts.
In this world, sharers of their gains;
In the next world, brothers in faith.
Like flour of white wheat,
They were kneaded together.
Like the hairs of a white saiga,
They were drawn out together.
For Manas, visited by Khizir,
The champion Bakai had been found.
For Manas, visited by the chilten,
A valiant man like himself had appeared.
They embraced and became friends,
Lying in the open steppe.
Your elder Bakai, the tiger—
His tales comforted the soul;
His deeds delighted the soul.
His words were sweet and sorrowful;
Such a person is not met again.
Your dear elder Bakai
Found the word at an unexpected moment;
Found sight in the dark.
He would not be carried away by ill speech.
In the dark, a corsac fox's
Trail he could follow without going astray.
Even when going alone, that wondrous one,
Wolf Bakai, the leopard—
He was the hawk; he was the support.
Making people grasp what they had not grasped,
That was the hero Bakai, the hawk.
“Of Sultan Manas, the leopard,
I had known that he was a lion.
From God Most High, the One,
‘Grant me Manas!’
That is what I kept asking.
You have chosen to lodge your guest,
Chosen to have him tell his tale,
Chosen to have him draw his line.
My magnanimous one, met along the way,
You have chosen to hear the wonders.
You have chosen to draw from within me
The festering, pain-filled turmoil.
Let me sing; listen to my song.
I shall tell my secret as faithfully as my creed.
My friend, listen to my words;
Know that what I tell is true.
