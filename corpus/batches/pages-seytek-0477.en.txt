See how he burns and cries out.
Father and son have met;
See how they surge with emotion.
To embrace each other once,
Making heaven and earth resound,
To greet each other just once—
See how they burn with longing.
The hero Semetey
Had no words to speak,
No eyes with which to gaze,
No potent medicine to restore him;
Such filth is this world.
The generous lion, led astray,
Met his own son,
Yet stood there without recognizing him.
Semetey then spoke these words,
Telling of horrors before their meeting:
“Born of a mother, I became a lion;
I brought the whole world beneath my rule,
And became a gray-maned lion of a man.
Led astray by the enemy,
I became one who fled his people.
I took a cavern for my home;
I turned back after my people were scattered,
And was separated from Külchoro,
Who had sat there as my protector.
I scouted the land and grieved;
I was parted from mother, child, and wife,
From my Kyrgyz people with their cities,
And from all those who remained.
A person longs for one to ride;
She lured me and bound me.
Kökmonchok, daughter of the peri,
Aichürök’s younger sister,
Grew up and blocked my road,
Bewitched me and bound my hands,
And showed me many wonders.
I am not dead but alive,
One of those who have not died.
She bound my eyes so I could not see,
And chewed me between her teeth.
Those she stabbed were countless and turned to ash;
Her army numbered tens of thousands.
The steel city was a great fortress;
By my reckoning, my father was mighty.
Defeated by the Jediger,
Why was my noble father, that dear lion,
Reduced to such humiliation?
He filled and dammed the broad yellow valley,
Growing stronger each time he died,
Smoldering anew whenever he was spent,
And gaining strength from his very roots.
Each time he died, he flourished;
He was both sovereign and lionhearted.
My oceanic, gray-maned lion father
Was blessed with perfect fortune;
Indeed, my father was so great a hero.
That I was made Semetey’s son
Leaves me no grievance against God.
I heard tales among the people,
But now I have seen my father’s word made flesh.
Left an orphan, I grew to manhood,
Managed to rejoin my homeland,
Matured and became a warrior,
And became the equal of heroic sons;
Now I have seen my father himself.
More than his teeming host,
I have seen my father’s own eyes.
My soul is aflame, my breast an ember,
Yet my heart is content with the Creator.
I longed to see my father,
And now not the slightest regret remains.
My heart is content with the Maker;
I have seen the mountain that is my support,
I have seen my valiant father.
My towering presence, like the Ala-Too—
My heart surges, my insides boil,
And I am filled with true joy:
I have seen my father’s form.”
So saying, his son,
