Calling the battle cry "Khan Akun!"
The karnay blared,
The brass horn clattered,
And dust curled into the sky.
The great host, frightened at first,
Gathered to the last man.
Would the deftest with battle-axes,
The strongest Afghans,
Stand watching without fighting to the death?
The gray-piebald banner became their standard,
And a wailing clamor arose.
The finest spearmen stood apart,
And the best men appeared.
The inside of the city closed down;
All the bloodletters gathered.
The old men stayed bowed at home;
All the Afghans
Set their army in motion.
From the treasury horde
Khan Akun's army appeared.
Tumenbay, son of Tulku,
Was a hero with a Turkic spirit.
At Jeti-Ozon, the Jediger
Would meet them face to face and fight;
That was where they would reach their end.
Little wisdom and much courage—
That Tumenbay, son of Tulku,
Was a harsh-tempered hero,
A lion who stood out plainly.
He fought trusting fate
And was one who broke formations.
He girded the jekene at his waist;
If the seven divisions of Jediger
Suddenly engulfed him,
He attacked alone.
He did not fear death by a penny;
Barely having escaped death,
The teeming Jediger
Had surged in yesterday;
Before this, Tumenbay
Had fought them many times.
Driving after him,
Because of desire for Aychurok,
Yesterday Toltoy of the Jediger
Drew his bow like rain
And fired bullets like hail.
Saying, "He will not obey my demand,"
He began a great tumult;
Saying, "He will not give his old daughter,"
He slaughtered the people.
They stood in single combat;
Not once, but seven times
The Jediger fought.
They fought horse to horse,
Jousted with spears,
And struck with swords.
Because of Aychurok,
They found much slaughter.
The truest of them was Chynkojo,
Like tempered red iron.
His courage was different, his venom strong;
He could devour whomever he met,
And his bulk was half a mountain.
Kojo, son of Shygay,
Had a black cap like a cauldron.
He had the will to slaughter
Not only Afghanistan but the world.
He had received instruction
From blessed Manas's spirit.
That was the hero and the warrior,
A cunning and wise man,
A lion who knew perfectly
The enemy's devices.
Yesterday, when Almambet fell to a bullet,
Did he not unleash
A merciless turmoil?
Chynkojo the expert
Had taken Almambet's magic stone,
