The true source of prosperity,
The root of all abundance.
He was a hero who never grieved,
One of the fierce black-maned wolves.
May God preserve Joltoy!
If I do not see him alive,
If I do not come to shield him,
They will say, ‘The wretched son of Semetey—
Weak-backed, feeble Seytek—
Knows well his own helplessness;
He surrendered me into the hands
Of infidels without a protector,’
And he will hold my death against me.
For failing to be his shield and support,
God will strike Seytek down!”
So spoke that lionhearted man,
Grieving and lamenting, that brave hero.
Beneath him Kulansur
Sped ever farther beyond the eye’s reach,
Pinning back his ears like a wild ass,
Sweeping the earth like a whirlwind.
The black steel bit
Struck against his throat;
As the beast ran straight and true,
His willow-silk tail
Lashed against his flanks.
He seemed as slender as a spear;
Over the broken black earth
The creature flew with a roar,
Leaving all others behind.
He flung his forelock toward the sky,
Driving his hooves deep into the track,
Racing onward without pause,
Hurling clouds of dust as large as houses
High into the heavens.
The flesh of his haunches rippled,
And the unmistakable excellence
Of the racehorse bestowed by Perizat showed.
Across the black earth’s open plain
He thundered onward,
Like his father, that valiant gray wolf,
Throwing the whole world into alarm.
Following the yellowed trail
Of the two giants in pursuit,
He chased until he himself turned pale.
Following their ashen trail,
He chased until all color left him.
The mountains vanished into level ground;
So grievous was his ordeal.
He leaned against the saddle’s ridge;
No firm ground remained upon the plain,
And he was engulfed in deep mire.
His mouth gaped wide,
And white foam mixed with blood
Scattered across the breast
Of mighty, peerless Seytek.
Rough hills became mere ridges;
Traveling onward without sparing himself,
He satisfied Kulansur’s yearning
And unleashed the horse’s full strength.
His fury rose fiercely;
The hero, precious as an eagle,
Hung his life upon a single hair.
Tormented by worry for Joltoy, he thought:
“Where shall I find that noble man?
If noble Joltoy is lost,
How many years must I wage war
And raid the Kakan?
How many years must spears of pine be bound
As I battle the enemy,
Making blood flow through the valleys?
If I do not renounce this borrowed life,
May I die unworthy of the name Seytek;
May I sink away as though never born.
I shall journey as far as Kakan
And charge upon them in fury.
If he has died, I shall load his body
Upon my horse and carry it home.
If Joltoy is alive,
I shall see him safe and well;
