The strides of the dark steeds
Were like the winter wind across the uplands.
Their splayed hooves were broad as Chatkal;
Upon the bones where three joints met,
A twelve-handled cauldron
Could seemingly be hung.
Broad-bellied and wide-backed,
Peerless Artoru—
Such was Artoru’s nature.
The bay belonged to gray wolf Semetey;
This was the bloodthirsty hero’s heavenly steed,
The horse he rode into battle
After Arbuurul.
He saddled Artoru,
Fearing for his borrowed life,
Buckled on Zulpukor,
And readied Akkelte.
He tied the sacred gray pouch
Around his waist,
But it gave no sign.
Formerly, when the bloodthirsty leopard
Tied the gray pouch at his waist,
The Forty Chiltens and Kyzır Niyaz
Would form forty thousand troops,
Advancing from the ridges;
They became forty thousand men against the foe,
Rising from the earth as an army—
The heart of anyone who saw them would fail.
Such was their protection.
Dividing into countless hosts,
They would join him and level their spears;
The Forty Chiltens, united as companions,
Would raise their battle cry.
Now not one of those signs appeared.
What is here today is gone tomorrow;
One day it shows its back,
Another day its face—
Such is this worthless world!
When the enemy came in a flood,
And the signs of battle appeared,
The black-mottled leopard
Would spring up from beneath him,
Carry word to the enemy,
And go forth fully roused.
The stunted gray wolf-lion
Would seem ready to rend anything he reached,
Ready to unleash calamity,
And would lunge wildly about.
Now not one of those signs appeared.
Despairing of his borrowed life,
His breast burned and his heart was an ember.
The mighty black bird Zymyruk,
Whenever it saw the opposing foe
And gave its rumbling cry,
Whenever the war drum sounded once,
That mighty black bird Zymyruk
Would seem ready to seize the enemy at once,
Thrusting its taloned feet out before him.
On seeing this sign,
The enemy would panic, lose their senses,
And flee without facing him.
Now not one of those signs appeared.
Damn this deceiver—
This worthless world has betrayed me!
The white male camel with a black head
Would appear ready to kneel and crush all beneath it;
When it faced a hostile foe,
It would rear and lunge wildly.
Now the eye could see nothing;
No one knew where
The sacred power had gone.
Such a deceiver is this world—
No one can tell what it truly is.
He climbed the ridge and gazed ahead;
Seeing no sign, his heart burned.
Swaying as though struck from this side,
The lion Semetey stood there.
May God never show us such a sight!
