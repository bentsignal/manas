Her name was heard throughout the world;
Many who had heard with their ears and seen with their eyes
Knew well
That none like her had ever been.
She charged straight at Sarybay,
Raising her weapon like Zulfiqar,
Her spear made ready,
Straining and crouching for the charge.
“I shall reach him before an arrow,” she thought,
And rushed forward with leveled spear;
“I shall smash him utterly.”
No sooner conceived than done:
Power boiled and strength swelled,
And the formidable champion transformed,
Shouting at the madman
As she unleashed her terrible wrath.
The life given by Almighty God
She valued not even as a hair, the wretched girl,
But cast it away as though tied to a thread.
When she turned her charger’s head
Toward her foe, brave Sarybay,
And now leveled her spear,
She bent low at the waist.
Her two eyes blazed with fire,
Bulging and gleaming through the gloom;
Her great spine and ribs
Stood starkly apart,
And blood-red embers mixed with gore
Poured like flame from her mouth.
Her prowess and courage
Appeared extraordinary to the eye.
Breaking free of all restraint,
Shattering the ground beneath his hooves,
Zhelzhetpes flew like a cloud,
Racing with the thoroughbred,
Breaking a path with his hooves
Toward wrathful Sarybay,
Striking at the marauder.
Then Kuyaly,
Tall and broad-shouldered,
A mighty champion and keen warrior,
Was herself one of the great heroes.
Before one could cry out,
In the blink of an eye,
Her Zhelzhetpes grew wild,
Soaring like a cloud in fury,
And reached him sooner than an arrow.
Kuyaly, a master of the spear,
Spoke the words she had long kept,
And to the hero Sarybay
She said this:
“For as long as anyone remembers,
You have plotted violence,
Roaming among nations and spilling blood,
Never ceasing your strife.
If you continue so, madman,
You will never prosper in this world.
Even a dog is far better than you;
You have never ceased your doggish ways.
Your violence has reached many;
Many innocent souls have died.
This one is human, and you are human—
What you have done is truly evil.
You have put out the hearth-fires
Of peaceful folk dwelling in their summer camps,
And driven them all away.
You destroyed the trees of their homeland,
Left their little children orphaned,
And cast them into misery.
You have no ancestral feud to avenge;
For what fault of the Kyrgyz
Have you tormented their lives so cruelly?
For as long as anyone remembers,
Your frenzy has never ceased,
Nor have you had your fill of drinking blood.
Plundering tribes and slaughtering people,
You have never ceased your strife.
You steeped your spear in blood
And would heed no word spoken to you.
You crossed ridges and mountain passes,
