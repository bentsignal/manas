If we attack her child,
This infidel’s strife
Will never be quelled.
If you judge it rightly, Kyyaz, it is an evil dream
For a man to kill his own child;
Never in all time has such a deed been done.
Do not let Chürök suspect a thing;
If you are a hero, Kyyaz,
Do not plunge your head into turmoil.
You have challenged a bloodthirsty gray-maned lion;
Do not lay an ambush within your village.
Hero, do not bring sedition into your village.
Set the sorely afflicted, wretched woman’s
Troubled mind completely at ease,
And hold a feast for this child!
Slaughter ninety black beasts for his feast,
Declare, ‘He has become my son,’
And name the child yourself!”
Saying this, hero Kutu
Sharpened his eloquent tongue.
Dear Kutu spoke on and on.
He uttered words of every kind;
His plain and truthful speech
Was like the thrust of a spear.
He was a quick-witted orator,
Broad-jawed and fluent-tongued,
Gentle in nature and truthful in speech,
A man endowed with eloquence.
What was coming and what was passing
He understood in full.
Against Kyyaz, who had turned bloodthirsty,
This Kutu, as you may see,
Harbored great hostility.
He offered the people fitting counsel,
And the people obeyed, crying aloud:
“The poor man’s counsel is wise;
We consent to what he says.”
It suited the multitude,
And when the multitude gave its assent,
What could hero Kyyaz do?
To slaughter the great herds,
Your poor Kyyaz made ready.
To give the child a name,
He slaughtered ninety beasts for the feast,
And the people surged about in commotion;
The time had come to hold the celebration.
Shouting, he gathered his people,
And Kyyaz summoned his whole nation:
His diviners and wise men,
His elders who had seen much,
All those of great knowledge,
His keen-eared heroes,
The heroes who knew many things—
He gathered his far-flung people.
A great abundance of worldly treasure
He firmly committed to the expense.
Among the people stood a tower—
Ask not how magnificent that tower was!
It rose many fathoms high;
In those days, extraordinary indeed
Was the learning of your hero Kyyaz.
From the tower, hero Kyyaz
Roared out a mighty cry,
And to all the vast assembly
He loudly proclaimed the news,
Announcing to the Jediger nation
That Chürök had borne a child:
“Hear me, my countless black-clad people!
The fairy-born Chürök,
Whom I took and bound in wedlock—
I counted every passing day
And recorded each one in writing.
