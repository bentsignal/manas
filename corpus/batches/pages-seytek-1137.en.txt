The reason he had lost all hope:
His two arms that once gave strength
Hung motionless and paralyzed;
His noble head, the guide of life,
Was drenched in crimson blood;
His entire body
Lay utterly still and helpless.
The dear Kyrgyz folk in white felt caps
Yearned desperately to see Joltoi.
They had no crag beyond the wild goose’s flight,
No mountain to shelter them.
They had no ridge from which to face the foe;
When all recourse was gone,
Their champion Seytek was nowhere in sight.
“Has the marauding Chinese triumphed?
Has he crushed my flank?
Our mighty wrestler Akjoltoy—
Father, by God’s decree,
Must he die at an infidel’s hands?
Must he take the road of no return?
Must a golden life, dearer than the world,
Be snuffed out by a firebrand?”
Biting their thumbs and shaking their heads,
They all spoke of the dreadful news:
“Alas, what can I do with this grief?
Oh, this loneliness!” they cried.
“Have I met these raiders
Only to meet my death upon the ridge?
Will these fortunate Chinese
Settle freely upon Jonok?
With blood drained and senses reeling,
When every means is exhausted,
There is no kinsman,” he said faintly.
As Joltoy stood in this plight,
Brave Seytek, the hero’s son,
Could scarcely rein Kulansur’s mouth:
“God, preserve him!
Where is Joltoy, my joy?” he cried.
The gray-maned hero could not remain still.
Clad in his splendid golden coat,
Mounted on his sleek, water-smooth steed,
He traveled day and night,
Exhausting his horse as he searched the land,
Seizing his spear in wrath,
Setting his firearm ready,
Scanning the passes and searching the ground,
Driving the sweat from his horse’s coat,
He followed the trail where the two giants,
Akjoltoy and Bilgich, had given chase.
Growling and gathering fury,
Taking on the aspect of a hungry lion,
His majesty swelling into rage,
He resolved to ride as far as Kakan,
To discover whether Joltoy
Was dead or alive.
He aimed the wretched steed
Along the very road of the chase:
“If Akjoltoy, whom I seek,
Has not died but still lives,
May I stir up a mighty conflict behind me
And win myself renown.
That bravest hero, worthy of the name,
That peerless falcon,
Was accomplished, a true man;
His valor was unsurpassed.
It seems he went forth, sparing not his life,
Chasing after the cunning Bilgich;
Ill-fated Akjoltoy
Seems to have gone as far as Kakan.
He was a kinsman who gave us shelter,
A man untouched by sorrow,
The bulwark of our dear people here.
If enemies came from right and left,
If challengers encircled him tenfold,
In the crush of clashing battle,
Where blood was spilled,
His presence always shielded us.
Almighty God who created living souls,
May God preserve Joltoy!
Joltoy was the very eye of fortune,
