He was the mighty khan of the Argyn,
A champion and a lion;
When he entered battle against a clashing foe,
He was the man who bore the banner.
He never panicked on hearing tidings,
Nor stood watching without joining battle.
He never lay quietly upon the earth;
Thrown into fire, he would not burn,
Thrown into water, he would not sink.
His valor was extraordinary;
He was truly a mainstay.
Sary Khan, the champion bound to one’s back,
Was truly a warrior.
His wisdom reaches far;
He is a surpassingly gifted sage.
Whenever he assails an enemy,
He emerges alone at the head of the race.
Sary Khan, the champion bound to one’s back,
Has the thoroughbred bannered charger.
Sary Khan has strength enough
To subdue the whole world.
His eyes see from afar;
Many enemies have wearied themselves fighting him.
He reads the stars in the heavens,
That companion of mighty Semetey.
Damn you, brave Kyyaz—fire at the spot
I have just shown you;
Fire at the lone mole as large as a pail.
Do not look around;
If you are a man, if you are a lion,
If you truly are the hero Kyyaz,
Shoot at the very spot I showed you.
If Sary Khan dies—
If that Sary Khan dies—
It means God has granted him to us.
But if Sary Khan does not die,
They will fight like immortals
Until the brittle reeds ripen
And the ashes of judgment scatter;
The maddened slaves will keep attacking.
No man can defeat him by rushing him,
And he never yields his companion’s life—
Such is the nature of Sary Khan.”
Carried away, Kanchoro spoke,
Standing there and roaring,
While Kyyaz merely stood.
Kyyaz had lost his senses,
And his whole body trembled.
He was distraught and agitated,
And, unable to find his bearings,
He pleaded with Kanchoro:
“My dear Kanchoro,
Here is a rich city, and here is its land;
If you would take my head, here I am.
Here is a land filled with cities;
Hero, if you would spill blood, here we are!
If you would strike like hail, here are our heads.
I have spoken—accept my words.
Devise whatever conflict you know best;
If you would shoot, choose the way yourself.
Find the means yourself,
And form the plan yourself.
The entire people of Jediger,
Who dwell in Jeti-Özön, were gathered here,
Yet such terrible calamity
I have never seen before.
Take the heavy musket and lie in wait;
Fire it as you see fit.
If you send a man against a foe whose ways he does not know,
That will become grounds for reproach.
Sary Khan, whose ways you know,
Has proved a formidable champion against this;
Devise the means yourself.”
So brave Kyyaz spoke,
Bending his knees to the ground;
From atop the bay horse
He sprang down and ran,
