The people had lost all recourse.
Blue haze parted in the sky;
With a rumble like a mountain collapsing,
Hero Semetey appeared.
Over rugged hills and passes
He rode out fiercely,
His Buurul beneath him
Covered with an abai cloth.
The sight overcame every heart;
He loomed like Opol-Too,
And the flame from his mouth
Showed a spear's length.
Your gray wolf, the son of Manas,
Had put on Akolpok;
When they saw the gray wolf's form,
The Zhediger stood grieving.
Your thundering sultan
Girded on a silver belt,
His face radiant as the sun;
He girded on a golden belt,
His face radiant as the moon.
The leopard supported by the spirits
Stood erect and untroubled;
He reckoned a kinsman like Chynkojo
As less than the hem of his robe.
The dear hero, proud as a cock,
Stood ringing without stirring;
The sight arrested the heart,
And blood dripped from the point
Of the inlaid spear in his hand.
His ravenous nature showed;
Buurul beneath him
Gathered himself more fiercely than a stag.
Semetey, thoroughbred prince,
Had put on Akolpok.
The kasaba banner floated in his hand;
Akkelte, inherited from Manas,
Gleamed upon his right shoulder.
Zulfiqar rang in the sheath
Of the poor sultan-born hero;
Semetey was a sultan,
And came onward ringing.
The heart flinched at the sight;
The Zhediger were overcome.
In his right hand the battle-axe
Shone as though struck by moonlight.
When he came up onto the pass,
The valiant hero appeared in full.
A black-mottled leopard
Raged at his flank.
The watching Zhediger cried,
"Accursed day, now we are dead!"
And prepared themselves for death.
The poor people lost heart,
Their eyes darting wildly;
Saying, "No mortal can be like this,"
Their joints went slack.
A lion with a cropped blue mane
Made ready at his right side;
The Forty Chiltens and Khizr Niyaz
Became an army of forty-two thousand,
Closing in from every side.
The giant black bird Zymyrik
Stretched out its feet
As though about to carry them away.
"His spiritual support is exceedingly strong;
If he moves, he will reach us.
We are a people without guilt;
Will he ignore the guilty
And destroy us instead?" they said.
The Zhediger stood trembling,
Suffering and grieving.
Semetey, son of Manas,
Raised the pale-mottled banner,
Shouted the cry of "Khan Manas!"
And, paying no heed to the great host,
Had drawn aside.
