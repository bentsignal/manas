He seated both upon his knees,
His mind filled with boundless thoughts,
And fondled the young men:
"My matched pair of lions,
While your heroic elder still has life,
For the two boys left among the khans
Like crows upon
A stake of black steel,
It is not merely my wife, young men—
My own dark life is yours in sacrifice!
Young men, leave my kinsman and take my household;
Leave the two of us and take the one;
Leave my wealth and take my encampment;
If you wish, take the wife you struck!"
Raging and distraught,
He pleaded with the two servants.
When he heard what had happened to the falcon,
He shouted, "Baabedin!"
And raised the battle cry, "Manas!"
Semetey rejoiced greatly,
As though his gray-maned father,
Tiger Manas, had returned to life.
He had a pale white mare caught
And sacrificed beneath the crescent hoof.
Semetey hurried with all his might,
Your hero, born more precious
Than Manas of the forty holy companions,
A hero who took after
His father, gray-maned Manas, without deviation.
The tent with golden cords
He had folded splendidly;
Striped felt and provisions
He had loaded upon Akborchuk.
He took a barren three-year-old horse for slaughter,
Rich fat for their provisions,
And red tea for their drink.
He had every piece of equipment packed,
And sour liquor and excellent food,
Saying, "Let the young men drink on the road,"
He loaded upon the packhorse.
The bulletproof Akolpok armor,
Armor fitted for Semetey,
He strapped behind
The companion who was Almambet's son.
The sight of it gladdened the heart.
With weapons and equipment upon his body,
That hesitant, poor fellow
Appeared as vast as Opol Mountain.
Driving the train behind him,
Because of his longing for Akshunkar,
And because of Aychurok's deed,
He drove fourteen horses from the Kyrgyz
At a pace beyond all measure.
The attendants he took as companions
Were all sons of khans.
The hesitant poor fellow's
Longing rested upon Churok.
His heart yearning and his breast aching,
That renowned hero, the great warrior,
With six grooms tending horses there,
And four men tending the cauldron,
Exactly twenty-five souls in all,
Set out, your hero, for Afghanistan
In pursuit of Aychurok.
The sight of him gladdened the heart:
Your gray-maned hero, taking after Manas,
His forefather and khan,
Looked altogether like Manas.
Of the champion chosen by Karan,
Which distinguishing mark shall we tell?
His brow was high, his wrath was strong;
The descendant of the tiger
Straightens all who challenge him.
He made that Buurul, seeming born of the unseen,
Step forward with a shimmering gait.
The sight opened every person's heart;
The poor man, lion-born,
Appeared surpassing in ancestral spirit,
