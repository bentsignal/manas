That true gray wolf, born imperious,
Is not your brother-in-law himself!
You babbling poor women,
How much must I tell you?
If you ask the lineage
Of the young man on the farther bank:
Long ago, when my gray-wolf father-in-law,
That hero,
Was in the fullness of his time,
The father of the young man across the river,
A gray-wolf lion,
Strayed from the straight road.
He bitterly contested
The khanship of Kakan-Chyn and Beijing;
Barely escaping death,
He fought with his own close kinsmen.
His heart turned away from the Chinese,
And he migrated from Beijing.
He found a land like broad Talas
And a hero like my father-in-law;
He rendered true and surpassing service,
And his purpose brought good fortune.
This is the son left as a legacy
By a lord like Almambet.
If you saw the son, you would bow;
If you saw the hero, you would tremble."
Churok spoke and fell silent.
The maidens and daughters-in-law grew enamored
Of the young man across the river;
The thought, "If only he were my husband,"
Brought great trouble into their minds.
The maidens and daughters-in-law marveled greatly
At the young man on the farther bank.
They gazed at him with longing,
Placed wishing necklaces around their necks,
And, for the young man on the farther bank,
Saying, "If only he were my husband,"
All the maidens and daughters-in-law
Made their wishes in agitation:
"If only Argyn and Kyrgyz gathered
And camped on both banks;
If only that dear young man
Were among our people
As an honored lover and companion.
To the young man standing across the river,
May this dark life God created for me
Become a sacrifice."
All the maidens and daughters-in-law
Leaned out, staring in wonder.
Some maidens said,
"If only that young man across the river
Were the one I took as husband."
Others spoke and said,
"That howling, ill-fated wretch
Is a shameless poor man."
All the maidens cried out together
And entered into the clamor;
They fell into an immense uproar
That would not subside.
Some of them said,
"The moment he crosses, he will die."
Others spoke and said,
"Will he take his own life?
Will God strike the young man?
He will turn back."
Others spoke and said,
"While crossing, the young man will tumble,
And his head will be torn off."
Some maidens said,
"He will cross the Urgench and come;
His horse is formidable."
Then Churok said:
"Do not howl, poor women!
Whether he will cross and die,
God alone knows."
