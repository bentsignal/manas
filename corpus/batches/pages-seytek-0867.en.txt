And faced him defiantly,
Along with their comrades—
Thirty tigers who had closed around them,
A fold no wind could penetrate,
And thirty-five companions—
He left their arms hanging limp,
And, without killing them, left their eyes staring;
Tormenting them while still alive,
He bound them and drove them away.
Those wretches who would not submit
He prodded with his spear
And drove away.
At the sight, the heart was overwhelmed;
They loomed like a blue hill,
The lions Manas and Semetey
Appeared like a deep blue valley.
They wounded all who took up spears,
Cutting through the sixfold host;
When within reach, they speared them,
And those who came close enough to strike
They smashed with battle-axes.
Those they faced they stretched out dead,
Those they met they felled;
Those who came face-to-face
They destroyed with swords,
And those who ventured even closer
They struck down with battle-axes.
The earth turned green-black, filled with blood;
For the return of peaceful, joyous times
The vast army yearned.
The people cried out in uproar,
Fleeing ridge to ridge for shelter;
Hero Külchoro and hero Seytek
Moved among the people like tigers.
Those fated to live escaped by cunning,
Those doomed to die could not escape.
Hero Seytek hewed through their center
To right and left,
While through their very midst went Külchoro
Upon his red-roan horse—
A blooded courser that had witnessed much,
Its champion rider a master of the spear,
A man seasoned from his youth,
Who had seen the turmoil of combat
From the age of twelve.
Your capable elder Külchoro
Drove Sarybai’s vast army
Ahead of him,
While hero Seytek
Struck down their warriors.
The people gathered with cries;
What vast turmoil and mighty conflict!
They attacked and were themselves destroyed.
Delighting in Zhelpinish,
The ogre’s son, deeming himself a hero,
Reaped men down as if at play.
“You have stirred up boundless strife,” they said;
“He who attacks first must not weep—
Go now to your God,” they said.
He swung and thrust his polished spear,
Breaking apart the ranks
Of that turbulent, ruinous conflict.
The hapless people were needlessly trapped;
The ogre’s son was ruined.
All the heroes assembled,
Wrestlers and giants were slain;
Their vision dimmed and clouded,
The warriors’ path was blocked.
Such was the state of the battle:
They began the shooting and slaughter,
And when the two lions
Fought beneath the moon,
The two of them utterly renounced
Their lives held only in trust.
The consummate warriors, the noble lions,
Speared everyone who came,
Crushing six thousand.
Hero Sarybai, of great fame and terrible might,
Kept pressing onward,
Crushing everyone he encountered,
