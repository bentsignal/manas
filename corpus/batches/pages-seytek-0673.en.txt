He who set fire to grassy lands,
Who stirred the stars in the heavens.
With the fortune of holy Kyzyr
He had been friends from the very beginning—
He was that lion himself.
None facing him had won,
No foe had dared confront him,
No man opposing him had prevailed.
“Forefather Tügöl Khan,
Keep your all-seeing eye upon me,” he said;
“First forefather Baary Khan,
Come and be my companion,” he said.
Praying to his ancestors,
Your mighty wretch, brimming with strength,
Prepared himself with Kerkula.
His opponent was Sarybay,
An infidel of perfect strength,
A hero of boundless courage.
If fate had decreed his death,
He would ride straight forth and thrust his spear;
Against the raging infidel
He would stake his life and wage war,
And save all the people
From death.
Thus he rode out for single combat.
Hero Seytek stood upon the rise;
Sarybay charged and struck.
When Sarybay thrust his spear,
It could not withstand the contest:
Sarybay’s spear
Burst apart into ten pieces,
Shattering utterly.
Hero Seytek did not even lean;
Sarybay swerved aside.
He struck Kerkula on the thigh,
Then took the ornate spear in hand.
Your hero Seytek stretched tall:
“This side of the belt,” he said,
“The lower edge of the lung,” he said,
“This is the measured spot,” he said,
And staked his life upon a hair.
His opponent was Sarybay;
His only prayer was to God—
Such was the nature of the clash.
His adversary was Sarybay;
To face him and test himself,
On the good path granted by the Creator,
To surrender his precious life,
To stand face-to-face,
In battle where blood would flow,
To behold a hero’s courage,
And, if God so willed,
To give up his dear life,
He made ready all his weapons,
And bound on Ajybay’s sharp steel blade
Without its scabbard.
Arming and preparing himself,
He prayed to the spirit of the noble lord
Who had passed into the other world.
Behold the valor inherited
From his fathers and forefathers.
His forefather was Tügöl Khan,
Blessed by Kydyr from the beginning;
His first ancestor was Baary Khan,
Blessed by Kydyr from birth.
The more they were spent, the more they flared;
From the beginning they grew ever stronger;
The more they died, the more they multiplied.
He poured blood into the black waters;
Behold how the son inherited
His father’s bloodthirstiness.
He broke through sands and made a road,
And attacked the frenzied foe.
He never retreated before death,
Nor turned back if gunfire meant dying.
Told to drink water, he drank poison;
Told to spill blood, he spilled his very soul.
Told to take a cap, he severed a head—
The offspring of the lion,
Who set the grassy steppe ablaze.
