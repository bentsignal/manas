He has fourteen thoroughbred steeds;
Watching him, I see that he is
That celebrated son of Manas!
Raging to pursue a blood-debt,
Straining to raid the people,
It seems his time to come has arrived.
When I look upon his bearing,
He seems to be Semetey himself.
To Beijing of the Kakanchin,
This very year the bloodthirsty one
Seems destined to bring an upheaval!
The one who splits lungs and severs heads—
He seems to be that accursed man.
More than Semetey himself,
Even his dark, staring eyes resemble him!
He has brought fourteen horses;
Their coverings are spread in place.
All fourteen horses,
Each is tethered separately.
Their white halters are tied in the okoro fashion;
Every one is alike, made of horsehair—
A halter fitted to each of the fourteen.
Smoke rises from the tent,
And the state of their camp is plain.
Of the Buruts who came to Beijing,
When Almambet died in the pursuit,
And Manas went away defeated,
I heard that in Chin-Beijing, among the Kakans,
A descendant had remained;
I heard that Almambet left behind
A single son to carry on his line!
When that Manas went away,
Manas gained a child
Who swept away all his sorrow, they say,
And named the boy brave Semetey, they say.
When that champion was born,
He consumed the Russian feast;
If this child lived,
He would destroy the Oirot, they foretold.
He consumed the Chinese feast;
If this child lived,
He would overturn the age, they foretold.
They say he scattered all his wealth.
His fierce mother Kanykei
Took up her child
And fled toward her own kin, they say.
Until twelve years had passed,
He lived out his days in Bukhara, they say.
When he reached the age of twelve,
He returned again to Talas, they say.
When he returned to Talas,
Whoever challenged him died, they say.
All the sedge-covered grasslands—
I heard he greased them with blood.
When his wrath was provoked,
Beginning with his own father’s foes,
I heard he laid them all low!
I heard the tale from those who came.
He poured blood into the black water;
I heard that a dark hour had arisen!
Over the Argyn and the Kyrgyz,
I heard he became the master;
Taking possession of Talas,
I heard he became a giant.
His father, the hero Manas, had a comrade,
That confidant Bakai.
That Bakai must have died
And seen the face of the other world.
Had that Bakai not died,
Had he not seen the other world,
His fortune would still be whole;
He would have led the bloodthirsty one’s son
And brought him here.
That looks like Bakai himself;
If what I say is no lie,
Then that is Semetey himself,
Come this year
To make a spectacle of slaughter!
