Loaded upon each bay horse.
When all was ready,
The dear hero stood tall;
The steadfast, beloved warrior
Made his weapons ready,
Prepared to confront the Kakanchin,
And remained ready to depart.
The inheritance left by his father—
Lion Bakai, the keen warrior.
He put on a blue lynx-fur cap
And flourished his blue-gray shield.
Its crown was a life-protecting coat of mail,
Its foot was shod with a thousand pieces of gold.
He who flourished the blue-gray shield
Never tired of the foe he faced.
Bakai Khan, the rich man’s son,
Had rinsed his mouth with honey;
He found sight in darkness
And found words in sudden peril,
A confidant when taking the road,
A comrade of tiger Manas.
Bracing himself like a leopard,
Your dear uncle Bakai
Received wisdom from the unseen.
His coat of ring mail gleamed;
Your uncle, lion Bakai,
Had a white beard that shone,
And cried like a visiting goose;
Your heroic uncle Bakai
Thundered like a prophet.
He raised the gold-crescent banner;
Your uncle Bakai made ready,
Raising the cry, “Aziret!”
A safeguard for his entrusted life,
A gift from my sworn father Almambet—
Tall and broad-shouldered,
Both wrestler and warrior,
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
The keen hero who could shake the world.
His form, his noble bearing,
His appearance upon a horse
Stirred the heart of whoever saw him;
To your uncle’s eyes
He looked like Almambet.
When Bakai turned and looked,
With broad mouth and cliff-like brows,
He spoke like that gray wolf.
Lion-born Külchoro
Had Almambet’s very eyes.
It was as though, on man and head alike,
The skin of gray-wolf hero Alman
Had been laid over him;
It was as though the Almighty
Had matched nose to nose
And turned them from the same mold.
Tall and broad-shouldered,
He was a blazing red ember;
Though Japar the Creator
Continued creating souls,
There was none like Külchoro.
Full-limbed and imposing,
The sight of him stirred the heart;
To bloodthirsty Bakai the falcon,
To the poor man sunk in sorrow,
As he watched Külchoro,
How much he resembled your uncle Ala!
Beneath him was Surkoën,
With solid hooves broad as bowls;
For the campaign against Kakan,
That spirited champion Külchoro rode him!
Do not ask how Surkoën was:
Short in the forelegs, long in the hind,
High-backed and broad in the chest,
His pace like the wind of the open steppe,
His muscles like the back of a bull,
His tail sparse and his mane scant;
If he chased a kulan, it could not escape.
