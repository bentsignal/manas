Like men already struck by slaughter,
I artfully deceived them all
And displayed my lionhearted nature.
The infidel son of Kalday,
The warrior Khan Kongurbay,
I deceived and brought around to my word.
I approached him from the Kirma side,
Claiming I had fled from the Kyrgyz,
And deceived him by saying I was Chinese.
‘Our first ancestor was Chylaba,’ I said,
And invoked his ancestral spirit.
The blue-gray-bearded Chinese warrior
And his interpreter Ezkara—
Such was the state of those accursed men;
I deceived every one of them.
My princely elders, my sultans,
I have shown you my resourcefulness
And delivered the Kalmyk hero Kongurbay
Into your hands.
Torment him as you would a sheep;
Do not spare the Kalmyk’s life—
Cut off his head.
Drag him through the people
And expose him to the Danggits.
Let the black blood flowing from within him
Cure the pain in your heart,
Heal the sickness inside you—
Scoop it up by the handful and swallow!
May the place that aches be soothed;
Once you capture Kongurbay,
Alas, my lionhearted elder,
May your clouded eyes open wide.
May your scorched heart
Be calmed as though sprinkled with water,
And may your painful yearning be healed;
May the grave of the Kalmyk
Who subjugated the whole world be opened.
Send Ezkara into the grave,
Displaying your valor to the utmost,
And make the few, long-suffering Kyrgyz
Rejoice and laugh.
Let us fulfill the wishes
Of this nation left desolate;
Let us distribute gold by the foot and worldly treasure,
Saying, ‘This is the blood-price of ten generations
Of your forefathers.’
Let us set every hero’s heart at ease,
Make these Kyrgyz rich,
And exalt our valor.
Let us kill the champions
Of the red-tasseled Chinese,
Then rest, my precious elder.
Let us crush the finest men
Of the stone-buttoned Chinese
And make merry.
Let us capture the strongest
Of the jacketed Manchus
And cure every brooding sorrow.
By calling the Chinese my kin,
I planted open hostility
Right beside them.
I sowed a feud that will bring disaster,
Deceiving the aged Kalmyk;
Like men struck by slaughter—
Listen, my precious elder—
Today I shall slaughter them all.
I have brought down the old Manchu,
Silver-eyed and sun-faced.
My thundering elder,
I shall leave them swollen as though poisoned
And slaughter them all.
I drew out the boundary line;
Among the Chinese I have done
A deed no ordinary Kyrgyz could do,
And brought to their khan
Mischief no common man’s father ever witnessed.
I have secured all their roads
And learned their plans.
