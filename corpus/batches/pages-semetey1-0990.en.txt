We, all sons of khans,
To Opol standing here
Will go ourselves.
If we return, there will be trouble:
We have beaten his wife and left her.
We shall take your Bloodthirsty Manas with us
And travel onward from there."
Having settled it thus, Kulchoro
In Kanykey's encampment
Prepared a deep blue-green saddlebag.
"If an enemy comes out shooting,
He will be left powerless," he said;
The saddlebag held abundant powder and shot.
He had it folded and made ready
And loaded upon Akborchuk.
A great golden tent for sleeping,
A bronze field cauldron for cooking,
And six grooms to tend the horses—
Exactly twenty-five souls in all—
Were setting out for Afghanistan.
Driving fourteen horses from Talas,
Akborchuk bore the provisions,
And Kokborchuk carried the liquor.
Driving fourteen horses from Ogoy,
These young men, seeking their lord,
Spurred eagerly and rode hard.
They had their equipment folded,
And all their armor and weapons
Loaded upon fourteen horses,
With ample food and ammunition.
Your dear mother Kanykey
Had made for the fourteen horses
Special white ammunition cases,
Each fitted to its horse.
The young men eagerly drove them hard,
And to their lord, lofty as Opol Mountain,
Their hearts opened in gladness;
Before their elder's eyes
They appeared like an army of six divisions.
Kanchoro and Kulchoro
Raced side by side,
Their stirrups striking together,
And before their elder
They came proclaiming good news:
"Ah, elder brother, a reward for good news!
Akun Khan is your father-in-law,
And Aychurok is your wife.
Aychurok, daughter of Akun,
Changed herself into a white swan.
She listened closely to your words,
And took a great liking to you.
When she rose over the ridge,
My hero, she reached you;
But after speaking sternly with your wife,
She departed deeply offended.
She changed herself into a white swan
And scattered her venomous fury.
Saying, 'Let him seek and find it,'
She carried Akshunkar away
To the city of Akun Khan!
If you are a man, my heroic elder,
Let us find a way to seek and recover
Churok of old and the falcon.
If you are still a mere boy, come,
Let us go back to your wife.
