When this post was planted,
An immense part of the hero’s treasury
Was spent upon it.
Akbermet’s forty brides
Tied the horse with jingling ornaments,
Their nimbleness beyond compare,
Swiftly fastening it with a clatter.
Their splendor surpassed all others;
Fragrant perfume billowed from them,
They spoke serenely without laughter,
And the brides smiled;
The mortal soul of anyone
Who beheld them trembled.
With matchless agility,
They now drew open the door.
After Akjoltoy entered,
The forty nimble brides
Bustled about observing every custom,
Set down slabs of rich mutton,
And from potent black liquor
Poured a brimming ox-head bowl
Until its surface stood still.
The forty brides laughed in a row
And, approaching dear Uncle Joltoy
With graceful steps, offered it to him.
They also offered him
Fresh yellow foaming kumis.
When brave Joltoy drank it,
He readied himself like a hawk,
And from dear Joltoy’s lips
Came wisdom as if from the unseen.
His eyes could see afar;
Wise gray-maned Joltoy
Spoke slowly and with gravity:
“Oh, my child Akbermet,
If the famous rumor is true,
If it is sound and not a lie,
I have heard word from those who came
That your proud gray-maned champion
Has had a dream.
I have come and seen how you fare;
My child, let us interpret the dream you told.
My child, if a black golden eagle takes prey,
If a collared greyhound takes prey,
If it tracks across the black earth,
Then for the numberless Kyrgyz
Your noble gray-maned Seytek
Has become a stout house with walls
Through which no storm or wind can pass;
My child, he has become the ruin of the enemy he marked.
For the sons of the white-capped Kyrgyz,
My child, your prince Seytek has become khan.
Guarding the court and ruling the people,
He shall possess broad lands.
My child, if a black eagle takes prey,
If poison flows from its claws
And whatever it seizes dies,
Then, breaking treasuries and taking livestock,
Your prince shall become renowned.
A dark-blue son greater than himself,
Dark-bellied and blue-backed,
Who stirs conflict among the nations,
Who breaks through mountains to make roads,
Whoever dares resist,
He drives before him and disgraces,
Who brandishes a steel spear
And jostles the whole wide world—
Such a son shall be born to him.
He will subdue the Muslims who can speak
By the power of his tongue,
And drive the speechless fools before him
Until they beg for mercy.
He will wield a spear of steel,
And bring down whomever he marks,
Set the grassy steppe ablaze,
And burn his vengeful foes;
His fortune shall never leave his door.
My child, Bermet, when you give birth,
Bound in destiny to your hero,
A tiger will come into the world.
