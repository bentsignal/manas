He had a tail fastened to the bay horse,
Lest the summer heat strike him,
And kept him in the shade,
Saying, “He will be my companion,”
Leading him about and training him,
Saying, “He will wreak slaughter upon the foe,
And vanquish every enemy he faces,”
Tempering him like white steel.
Ill-fated in your bereavement,
My dear Külchoro,
Take me with you—do not leave me wailing.
While my heroic uncle lived,
I ate my meals as his pampered child;
Against the eighteen thousand worlds,
If my devastating uncle were here,
I used to say, “I shall wreak carnage.”
I was a lofty white peak, but now I am laid low;
Parted from my magnanimous lion,
I have fallen into utter misery.
I entered battle, then turned back;
My own kinsman became my foe,
And I lost you, my khan and uncle.
Among the encircling hosts, I stand alone;
A bullet struck and tore through my shoulder,
And I cannot turn around.
Amid the thousands surrounding me, I stand alone;
A bullet shattered my ankle,
And I cannot move.
Helpless and growing weak,
My strength gone as I bleed,
My soul screaming, my vigor spent,
All power fled, steeped in grief,
Parted from my dear uncle,
Here I sit in this plight.
The wings meant for flight were crooked;
My true fortune was meager.
A deep pit was all I was destined to see;
My own kinsman Kanchoro
Had no trace of brotherhood in him.
My own kin became my enemy;
Parted from my beloved lion,
My sorrow will not leave me.
A fire burns within my core;
My own kinsman became my foe,
And bitter misery became my lot.
The sky is clear, yet the earth is overcast;
This is a woeful, calamitous day.
I am left wandering, ill-fated;
Alas, my uncle, take me with you.
My aged grandfather Bakay is gone;
My mountain-like uncle is gone.
So this is what you are like, treacherous world—
Damn you, worthless world!
Wandering in grief,
My beloved Semetey—
Parted from Semetey,
I am about to fall to a foe
Who spares no life, however one pleads.
Am I to plunge into a calamity
No man can wade through?
Shorn of my wings,
Am I about to be captured?
Pleading, “Spare my blood,”
Are we to die a wretched death?
These pressing Jediger
Are not the red, misty mountains;
Those pressing in to take my life
Are not an enemy come from China,
Nor are they the dark, rugged mountains.
The Jediger swarming over me
Are not an enemy sprung from the Kalmyks.
Their lungs bursting, their hearts aflame—
These Jediger are surely mad.
Though Kyrgyz, they fired upon us
And did worse than the Chinese.
Where no enemy dared advance,
The Ala-Too was our land;
