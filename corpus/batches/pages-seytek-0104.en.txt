His heart was torn apart,
And tears poured from his eyes;
Taken captive and disgraced,
He longed desperately
To see the afflicted Choro just once.
Serving as a bondmaid,
Passing his days in slavery,
His hands secured and bound,
He suffered humiliation and lamented;
His senses failed and all turned dark,
And his days became unbearable torment.
Kyyaz thundered in his might,
While wretched Külchoro was humiliated;
He had fallen into an iron cage
And endured a sea of misery.
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Had become a deep-dug pit for him;
Grieving, unable to find a way out,
Külchoro remained there,
Deprived of all six faculties.
Once Choro had been mighty,
But such was this calamitous horror
That might gave way to his disgrace.
Though he said, “Let me die,” he could not;
He could not take his own life.
He lived his days in slavery
And drank the bitter water of anguish;
His strength failed and left him helpless,
His former thunderous fury gone,
And he remained staring in supplication.
Had that Choro not come,
The true order would never have returned.
The destined hour had come:
Implacable death had reached Külchoro,
And it was time for him to die.
Choro’s beauty had faded;
His eyes stared wildly,
His legs hung limp,
And his living soul cried out.
His abundant, jewel-bright courage
Had wholly shattered and fled;
His vigor was spent and his strength gone.
Against Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
He had no deed left to attempt;
He scarcely seemed alive.
Choro, champion of the world,
Had no strength to act against Kyyaz.
Once Külchoro
Had been a lion favored by fortune,
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
Powerfully built and fierce of face—
Such a warrior he had been.
Now he yellowed with wasting sickness;
His destined hour had come.
After enduring countless torments,
It was time for Külchoro to die.
His black-collared felt coat
Had come apart layer by layer,
And lice spilled from its collar.
The torment of accursed humiliation
Was plainly visible there.
As for moonlike Aichürök,
That tumultuous woman of a thousand devices,
That headstrong woman—
How could we know what scheme
The nimble-witted infidel might devise?
In a prominent place stood a white palace,
Formidable in its strength;
A vast, resounding palace,
Immeasurable in its size.
It was encircled by ramparts,
Its gates were made of iron,
Its pillars were finely hewn,
And all around the cavernous building
Was brightly painted.
Its strength was plain for anyone to see;
Across its roof, side by side,
Three carts could have passed.
