Crowding until there is no room on earth,
Let them not end up beneath the earth,
Starting quarrels and causing turmoil,
Shouting as they run about with nowhere to stay,
Screaming as they quarrel.
Take full account of it,
My dear uncle;
Find a spacious place.
If an army spreads across the land,
May there be ample firewood to burn
For the boys who serve them.
When the cauldrons come to the boil,
May steam hide the ground;
So the people do not go thirsty,
May there be a thousand springs of water.
May the place where we hold the feast
Be level ground with a river valley,
And its country broad and wide.
I will entertain the people;
If wrestlers step forth,
Let them seize each other by the cuffs,
Tear whatever flesh they can reach,
And butt like blue bulls.
If heroes ride out to joust,
Let their lances bend.
When the people have gathered
And watch in a circle,
May their fortune be complete;
May it be so broad no dust rises.
If we place Khan Koshoy at the head
And hold Kökötöy's memorial feast,
That is my great desire,”
Bokmurun said, and fell silent.
My elder uncle Koshoy—
A saint who untied the knots
Of ten thousand tangled words,
An elder who became judge of the Katagan,
Dear holy Koshoy.
His white beard gleaming,
Honking like a young goose,
He gathered force and spoke firmly,
Speaking truth without falsehood:
“If we camp in Alay and hold the feast,
And send six swift horses
With a palm-sized letter,
If we strike a Koroson fire-steel
And invite every one,
Infidel and Muslim alike,
When word spreads and the people come,
When a flood comes covering the land
And the people seethe and sway,
Unable to cook and serve the food,
If they hurriedly search for firewood,
And demanding guests beat
All the servants,
What would become of us?
Men whose languages we do not know will come;
From their multitude the black earth
Will barely bear them and will sway.
Those who come making demands
Will recline, calling the feast their due;
All the servants
Will carry dung upon their backs.
Your Alay has no surplus firewood;
That will be a great hardship.”
Your uncle Koshoy went through
All the celebrated places.
“Your celebrated land is Sary-Arka;
Let me tell you its condition.
It has no hill as small as an ankle bone,
No hollow where a saddle blanket could sink.
It has no reed marked by a plow,
No burrow dug by a marmot.
A crow grows old before it can cross;
When a black vulture flies,
By the time it reaches the very center,
Its wings tire and grow weary.
Your Sary-Arka is a waterless desert;
If the people gather in crowds—
