Let us remain friends even in the world beyond.
Do not be reckless, Kanchoro;
Let us each return safely to our lands.”
At that, Kanchoro
Flared up in fury,
And spoke wildly
To Kyyaz of the Jediger,
Gnashing his broad white teeth
Until they loudly ground,
His eyes blazing like a lion’s,
Blood-flecked white foam
Bubbling from both corners of his mouth,
His two eyes reddening,
Glittering like a raging fire,
His head broad as a great cauldron,
His black brows like a crouching hound’s—
At such a sight, you would flee far away.
He thundered in his rage,
Darkening with fury,
And spoke to the Jediger:
“I had thought your Seven Valleys
Were a land worth calling a land,
And, Kyyaz, you wretch fit for the grave,
I had thought you were a man!
I called you Kyyaz the hero,
But you are Kyyaz the woman.
You brood over far too many things,
You miserable Kyyaz!
Taking a spear without a tassel,
And girding on my naked sword,
I shall break through your gorge and make a road;
I shall attack you, Jediger.
If I do not first make peace
With Külchoro upon the ridge,
If I do not make the slaughter glorious,
If I do not spare so much as a hair
And destroy you, Jediger,
If I do not scatter your myriad herds,
Leaving you nothing at all,
And wipe out your people utterly,
Then rage as you will, hero Kyyaz!
If I do not raid your six valleys,
And thus devise your ruin,
If I do not massacre
All your boastful people,
Bring the death of vengeance upon you,
And turn your honor into ashes,
Setting right your arrogance
And stripping you of power,
If I do not slaughter multitudes of you,
Leaving not one hair’s breadth of a man,
And destroy you, Jediger—
With his keen, Zulfiqar-like sword,
Does Kanchoro, who fears no bloodshed,
Fear to drive out
You, the massed black Jediger?
This slave fears no hero;
Do you think
Your warrior fears to lay you low?
Know well what I have said;
My hero Kyyaz, heed my words.
Jylgyn, Keng-Kol, and broad Talas
Are no lands where we can linger at ease.
What do you say, Kyyaz, alas,
Of Külchoro, Almambet’s son?
He is more than a match for both of us.
If by chance we leave him untouched,
If by chance we do not assail him,
He will recover from his sickness
And turn again toward battle;
He will recover from his wounds
And return firmly to the fray.
If we leave that warrior to himself,
His aching wounds will heal.
A wounded man who spares his foe
Will see Kyyaz’s blood spilled.
