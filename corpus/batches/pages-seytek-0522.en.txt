Yet he had no thought of dying.
The steel shield upon his head
Shattered and burst into sparks.
That infidel son of the Ogre
Survived even this and stood fast.
Sarybai grew fearsome
And began his true scourging.
Overturning mountains and draining lakes,
He wrought fierce deeds without pause.
He consumed all who crossed him;
Only the unbroken black earth
Halted the mass of his force.
He leveled every hill,
And tore up forests in his might.
He reduced to dust the very seed
Of both the living and the lifeless.
Furiously making ready,
The infidel Sarybai
Came charging straight ahead.
He roared in wrath,
Making the whole body
Of every onlooker tremble.
Blazing outright and stirring strife,
He bristled in fury
And began a savage assault.
The solid black earth beneath him,
Before the scourge of Sarybai’s rage,
The infidel’s bitter venom,
His crashing fury,
And his utterly dreadful aspect,
Seemed about to break apart.
Raging and running mad,
He began a savage assault.
He overturned earth and sky,
And swept them away.
He cursed the beast beneath him;
The teeming army on campaign
Surged away in panic and stopped,
As though ready to flee before him.
Yet Choro was a champion and a hero,
And possessed every weapon.
The vigilant, noble hero Choro
Let the golden-cheeked battle-ax
Swing from his wrist,
Then gripped it firmly.
If you ask about the battle-ax,
Its broad edge was a handspan wide;
Wherever it swung, blood spread.
Its steel socket was as thick as a fist;
Such was the battle-ax.
Placed upon grass, it set it ablaze;
Whatever it struck died at once.
When swung in battle lust,
A head flew broadly away,
And blood followed after it.
When the blood flowed, strength departed,
And after the blood, life departed.
The champion who wielded it rejoiced;
It was honed to cleave stone when swung
And pass through without glancing aside,
So that when brought against an opponent,
The blow would reach his very roots.
Thus its edge was sharpened and refined.
When men learned how keen it was,
Many companions embraced him as friends
And begged him for it.
He would not give it for a life;
No living soul had seen such cold cruelty.
Whoever it struck fell and crawled;
No marvel like it had ever been.
The golden-cheeked battle-ax—
The agile hero Choro
Gripped it in both hands and swung it around.
In less time than one could cry “Ah,”
The golden-cheeked battle-ax
Was drawn back with both hands
By the hero Choro, upheld by the spirits.
He turned Kulansur’s head,
And the mighty warrior watched closely
The infidel son of the Ogre.
