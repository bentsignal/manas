A blue lynx coat and a black cap—
Such was his splendid attire.
There he made merry;
What else could dear Agysh do?
He was enjoying himself.
The twelve choros, led by Kyrgylchal,
Had gone to bring the news.
As though slaughter had fallen
Upon brave Agysh,
Old Kyrgyl, their leader,
Collapsed in ruin.
When they came to brave Agysh,
Speaking softly but gravely,
Kyrgylchal began his speech:
“Hero Agysh, hear my words.
It is Khan Kökötöy’s funeral feast,
And all the people have gathered there.
Infidel and Muslim alike—
Bloodthirsty Manas is their leader.
The infidels’ champion, Hairy-Sole,
Has brought the end of the age.
His eyes are like the sockets of a shield;
Tükübai is a gray-maned wolf,
As though he would devour whoever he sees.
Manas has sent us to seek you.
He has six fleet horses,
And generous lord Manas
Has sent a palm-sized letter.
You bear the name of a young warrior;
Choose a thoroughbred and ride.
Kanykei saw you from afar
And fixed her special gaze on you;
She has taken a great liking to you.
If your death has come, go and die;
But if fortune favors you, brave Agysh,
Split an infidel like Tükübai
Cleanly into two.
Hook him and trip him from within;
Tükübai is formidable,
But win us a prize from the Chinese.
There are infidels like black beetles,
And hero Khan Kongurbai is there.
He has gathered many champions,
Saying, ‘I will throw down the Buruts.’
The wretch has come for that purpose;
Tükübai has entered the wrestling.
Against a palm-sized Burut,
He has come to settle matters.
Elbow-sized brave Agysh,
What are you like as a wrestler?
Hear my words, Agysh.
Wondering, ‘Can he win the prize?’
Your own kinswoman sent us.
Against that hideous infidel,
Stand and wrestle him.
For the palm-sized Muslims,
Win the true prize.
If your strength begins to fail,
Endure disgrace from the wrestler.
Never let them say Agysh was thrown;
If the prize is lost,
Do not live on—die there.
If you are a champion, Agysh,
Tear apart the ribs
Of an infidel like Tükübai.
If you heed me, know that
I myself have come to you, brave Agysh.
To put you into the wrestling,
Your time has come, brave Agysh.
Go grapple fiercely
With that swaggering swine;
From the hulking, mountain-like infidel,
Win us one prize.”
Kyrgylchal, leader of the Forty,
With the twelve choros at his head,
Selected Agysh
And drove him to the place
Where all the people had gathered.
