Of all the people born to me,
My wretched mother comes out before them,
Saying, “Her holy patron is the Messiah,”
My wretched mother simply comes out to meet me.
A khan’s wife is his wing;
From time to time,
It has ever been my unfortunate noble mother’s
Custom to perform such rites.
Father, companion, hear my words;
Oil your bright steel.
Do not truly provoke my wrath:
When he roused my enmity,
Though he was my own uncle,
I laid Abyke low.
And did I die?
Did retribution overtake me?
Did it strike me through the ribs,
Or make itself felt within my lungs?
Has Semetey died from it even now?
Scorching everyone who crossed my path,
Making your fierce valor known,
When Köbösh greatly enraged me,
I sent him to the grave.
And did retribution overtake me?
Did I close my eyes and stretch out my legs?
Does the fierce wolf lie dead?
Has your fierce wolf, even now,
Suffered the torments of the grave?
When have I ever heeded a warning rite?
When he stirred my enmity,
I devoured my own father, Khan Jakyp.
And did I die?
Has this precious head, even now,
Beheld the face of Judgment Day?
I made my uncle Chyybyt scream;
I overthrew the six rich lords.
I sent Kölbay to the grave,
And showed all the people who saw me
The fierceness of the wolf.
I sent many men to the grave.
And did I die?
Though retribution came upon so many,
Where have I ever encountered it?
When have I died from bloodguilt?
Those who provoked my fury,
Those who kept crossing me—
I slaughtered the forty companions.
They loosed their bows like pouring rain;
Though I pleaded, they would not spare my life,
But shot their arrows like hail.
When I drew my steed Buurul forward,
Though I folded my hands in submission, it availed nothing;
Thus they seared my soul,
And set my blood boiling.
Though I pleaded, my kinsmen would not relent,
But drove my soul to desperation.
They were my father’s legacy,
And your valor was tested before the people—
Yet I slaughtered the forty companions.
I unleashed an impossible madness;
I cut open their pregnant women,
Shattered the poles of their lattice walls,
And made their brides and daughters weep.
I smashed every roof pole,
And made their sons and daughters weep.
I massacred their old,
Leaving not so much as a hair:
I destroyed them all.
I devoured my own kin;
And did I die?
Did retribution overtake me?
Has your uncle, even now,
Let his feet dangle into the grave?
Has he departed this world?
Ümötöy, son of Köksö—
Go and meet Ümötöy.
If that swine will listen to reason,
There are eight caverns of treasure;
