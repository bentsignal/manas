When wicked men unleashed disaster,
Your father’s blood-red Akolpok,
Armor fitted for your lionhearted father,
Those six vile ones who had grown up among us
Put it on in this manner,
And wore it after defiling it,
Then scoured the face of the earth.
They dressed him in a Jekeyboz coat,
Mounted him on the swift Jelmaian,
And lavished livestock along the way,
Sending men through many cities,
Making camels kneel in every place,
Seeking fine cloth from city to city.
They searched the city of Bombay
And paid gold to obtain
The cloth of Bakburchun,
Mending all that was damaged,
Repairing all that was chipped.
Whatever proved unfit was broken apart,
Steel was filed and fitted
Into the spaces between.
With awls made of steel,
Bent and shattered by the work—
“Almighty God of power,
A grievous calamity has come,”
Karimbay wept when he saw it.
The women twisted the backing threads,
And champions among all living men,
Warriors, examined and tested it.
They repaired its woven backing;
My noble mother Kanykey,
Sleeping not at night,
Resting not by day,
Your suffering grandmother restored it.
She strengthened its backing with gold,
While sixty women and widowed sisters-in-law
Grew weary and exhausted by the task.
Its links were fine as a hen’s eye,
Its rings flickered like a lark’s eye,
So tight that a needle’s point could not enter.
Ten patterned saddlebags and ten carpets—
I know the customs of the Ogoy.
Forty embroidered saddlebags and forty carpets—
My child, I know the customs of the Kyrgyz.
Our Talas is a precious land:
Deer bellow in its rugged hills,
Cuckoos call from its sunny slopes,
Its flowers laugh in crimson bloom,
Though my darling knows me not.
Its springs surge and overflow,
Its nightingales sing sweetly—
Such is our land of Talas.
There they sow autumn rice,
Cuckoos sing among the willows,
Wild asses gather on the plains,
And argali graze upon the hills—
Such is our famed land of Talas.
My young hero found when death was near,
When you go to your people, be content;
Go safely and return unharmed,
Come swiftly and do not leave me longing.
If you meet your grandsire Bakai,
Rescue him from his grief!
Your true enemy is Kanchoro;
Go to Kanchoro, who now rules as khan.
Ask him to return as your inheritance
The countless treasures left by your father!
If he confronts you and refuses your words,
If he will not give you your inheritance,
Return without trading insults,
And come tell wretched me,
“Mother, he refused to give it!”
When they ravaged my people,
When yesterday those villains wrought evil,
When they plunged us into torment,
When they slaughtered the heroes,
When they slew my blessed protector,
When they massacred so many of mine,
When they destroyed my shelter,
Those quarrelsome, accursed kinsmen—
