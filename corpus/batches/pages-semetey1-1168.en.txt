Accursed old age!
Your uncle closed his eyes
And now fell into deep sleep.
When your uncle fell asleep,
Joloy lifted him with a grunt.
Boasting so greatly,
He headed toward the strongest
Of the jacket-clad Chinese:
"Now the Burut is dead;
He has seen the face of Judgment Day.
Such a Burut cannot survive;
He has seen what none have seen.
Even if he had eighty lives,
The accursed Burut is dead.
Now I will throw the Burut down
And drive his head into the ground," he said.
Poor Joloy swaggered,
Boasting and striding proudly.
This boastful infidel
Coughed with heavy grunts,
Hoisted Koshoy of Katagan,
And carried him
To the village of Ay Jang-jung;
He carried him again
To the village of Kun Jang-jung.
He carried him onward again.
Ezkara, khan of the Manchus—
Before Ezkara
He now carried him once.
Then he rushed onward,
And as he passed farther on,
He was about to carry and fling him
Before Kongurbay.
The Muslim people
Lamented as though their backs had broken:
"Old Koshoy is dead," they cried,
Wailing for their honor.
Joloy carried Koshoy
And ran toward the finest
Of the stone-bedecked Chinese.
Leopard Manas then
Stood watching Koshoy
And grieved in anguish:
"Accursed old age!
You have experienced
The torment of Judgment Day;
Your strength has gone, poor man,
And you have died."
He seized the black-jawed rawhide whip
Whose strike deafened the ears,
Swung it around,
And charged toward Koshoy.
Your gray-maned hero Manas
Struck the fatty flesh
Of the fat tail.
Blood sprayed from the tail
Of your sleeping uncle,
And his eyes opened wide as grain mash.
He seized the tail,
And from Khan Joloy's hands
Made a camel-like bound.
Your uncle, hero Koshoy,
Fell down from the back
Of lordly Joloy.
Your practiced uncle, giant Koshoy,
Performed the degey throw;
Turning Joloy around,
He slipped inside his stance.
Koshoy tripped Joloy.
"Our Joloy will throw him," they said;
The Black Kalmyks lamented,
Shouting in separate groups,
Calling their battle cry in waves:
"Burku-burku-burku!"
