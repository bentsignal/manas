I will wage war for three months!
Against the advancing Black Kalmyks,
While strength and life remain in me,
I will find some means!
If the Kalmyks press upon us,
Roaring, I will thrust my spear;
Taking no rest by day or night,
I will fight each day without cease.
If I do not show boundless valor,
If against the monstrous Black Kalmyks
I cannot withstand their onslaught,
Let me die instead of living;
Let me sink away as though unborn!”
Speaking thus to his people,
Manas the leopard, the bloodthirsty lion—
Strength surged from both his hands.
Between his two cheeks
Bristles grew enough for five felt boots.
He blazed forth in fury;
The falcon touched by Khizir
Was seized by his thirst for blood.
Sparing not his own life,
Gripped firmly by bloodlust,
He unleashed his terrible wrath.
“Those Kalmyks who have received their orders—
Let me go meet them myself.
Against that swine, the Kalmyks' khan,
Let me level my weapon and fight;
For all the wrongs of times gone by,
Let me take a full, crushing vengeance!”
So spoke the bloodthirsty lion,
Writhing in his fury.
Your hero readied himself without delay;
Such was the champion's way—
Rousing his courage, girding himself.
Such was the destroyer's way—
Swaying like a mighty mountain.
Against Kakanchyn, his adversary,
“I will charge and wreak slaughter,” he thought;
“The Black Chinese and Manchus
I will meet face to face,” he thought.
His people put their trust in that hope.
Across his breast he drew
Shot-proof armor, a coat of mail.
Never shrinking back from death,
Never fleeing his appointed doom—
That was the lion himself.
Against the hostile Black Chinese,
That was where he made his stand.
The noble lion who dared all
Girded his waist without delay,
Struck Toruchaar once on the flank,
Took his lacquered spear in hand.
It drew the eye to see it:
The fitted armor no shot could pierce
Gleamed upon his breast.
His strength was extraordinary;
Such was the hero's way,
Unlike any other soul.
None he charged escaped alive—
That outstanding hero who drank blood.
No mortal could equal him face to face;
Such was the champion's way.
None he faced departed living;
His prowess was renowned.
None who provoked him survived.
In the eighteen thousand worlds,
Against valiant, bloodthirsty Manas,
None remained to turn a catching-pole.
Coming forward to face him,
No mortal could do him harm.
His fortunes stood complete;
The unseen patron guarded that lion.
His fortune was different, his fury mighty.
Know by this that he was a lion:
He laid flat whoever clashed with him!
Showing his fierce bearing,
Against the Chinese host, his many foes,
Your bloodthirsty hero rode out alone.
How could your champion hold back?
Forty boys followed him.
Now your fierce hero reached
The foothills of Altai's dark mountains,
The spur where the mountain ended,
The blunt projecting headland.
When Manas came up beside it,
A little gorge lay straight ahead.
From that little gorge came shouts;
There were noble Baltay and Kutubiy,
Their shouting split the rocks,
Calling “Manas!” as their battle cry.
One could offer oneself to God in thanks:
Those thought dead were alive;
They were among those who had not died.
Earlier, when they had met,
His father Jakyp had said:
“Akbaltay and Kutubiy
Have departed this world,” he had said.
Such had been the word about Akbaltay:
Into the unbelievers' iron-rope
Net he had fallen, they said.
Into the Black Chinese and Manchus'
Pit he had fallen, they said.
Into Black Kalmyk Zholoy's
Hands he had fallen, they said.
Yet he was still alive and well;
It had been a time of deception!
When he drew near to Manas,
Noble Baltay then,
Resplendent in his ring-mail,
Rode the horse Kulager,
His white beard shining,
Crying out like a white falcon:
“With the Black Kalmyks and many Manchus
I grappled—was that badly done?
You who pledged yourself to twelve spiritual guides,
Oh, my lion Manas, are you well?
Manas the leopard, hear my words!
The torments of Judgment Day—
I returned without seeing them, do you see?
Against the swarming Chinese,
Forty-two times I fought with spears;
I returned without dying, do you see?
Look at the deep trench they have dug;
Look at the misery they have set boiling!
Wave after wave of people came.
Oh, my foal, brave Manas,
A flood came sweeping over us!
Not merely a match for us two, my foal—
A people to match the whole world came!
Marksmen arrived in swarms,
Warriors who never missed their shots.
Earth flew as they surged;
The Chinese filled the place, seething.
Giants kept tethered came;
All came abreast on foot.
Those mighty fighters looked extraordinary;
Every one was a formidable champion.
They brought calamity upon this head.
When Zholoy of the Kalmyks
Was about to overwhelm us with disaster,
When Ezkara of the Manchus
Was about to bind us now,
Their mighty fighters were extraordinary,
