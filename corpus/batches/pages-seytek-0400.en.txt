My whole body turned to blood,
And both my arms were palsied.
This wretched one, unable to drink kumiss,
Longed even for muddy water.
A sorrowful day arose and became my fate;
My mouth, which could not hold a grain of wheat,
Swelled to the size of a bull’s head.
My mouth, which could not hold a grain of millet,
Swelled to the size of a heifer’s head.
My strength waned as the days passed,
And I endured a dog’s humiliation.
I wished to die, but could not;
I sobbed, unable to give up my soul.
Suffering was heaped upon suffering,
And yesterday the tears from both my eyes
Streamed down over my breast.
The wide world became narrow;
This once-magnificent soul
Longed even for muddy water.
My valiant son was lost,
And Külchoro departed in grief,
His two hands bound,
His neck pierced through,
His back slashed by the foe.
My Chürök wept and beat herself;
Her fair breasts, round as apples,
Were split open by the lash.
They rode wretched mares, jolting along,
And hung loosely in their felt cloaks
When they were carried off as booty.
Our villages stood side by side;
Our birth cords were cut and our swaddling washed together—
From our roots onward, we were inseparable.
When we rode against the foe, our cries were one;
When we went to battle, our banner was one;
When we fought, our dust was one;
Our souls were close, our lives were one.
I told you to heed my words;
I told you to cast your eyes upon us.
The treasure left as a legacy,
Your bloodthirsty uncle and lion,
You joined with Er Kyyaz to destroy.
You burned and cast aside Keng-Kol,
And set a grievous fire among your kin.
You took Chachykey as booty,
And took the mourning widow for a wife.
You broke open the treasury and took the money;
You divided the Kyrgyz and took slaves.
You seized the mountain passes, spreading terror,
And laid waste to the people.
My son, you stained your spear with blood;
You destroyed your own uncle
And fastened this deed securely.
You burned my plane tree and raised an uproar;
You bound my sapling in black.
You exhausted my treasury and possessions
And brought all my laughter to an end.
Well indeed you honored your lord,
You bastard, by withering
The mother who gave you her breast!
I was tormented and beaten;
I pleaded with the wretch,
Parting my snow-white hair,
Letting milk flow from my white breast,
Showing the suffering I had endured,
And shedding tears from my eyes.
I exposed my scorched brow in humiliation,
My dress torn into countless strips,
My honored head brought low.
“You once stood as my eldest son;
Show me a little mercy,” I said.
Raising my sinking heart,
“Here is your nourishment, my son,” I said,
And showed him my breast.
I screamed, wept, and beat myself:
“Look upon this helpless old woman;
Take your wretched, humiliated mother
Under your sheltering shade.”
I went begging for mercy,
While black blood ran from my calves.
