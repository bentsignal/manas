Before one could say this or that,
In the blink of an eye,
If one broke its halter,
It looked ready to devour everyone.
Neighing and straining,
Heaving against one another,
Pinning back their ears,
They flung the black earth's turf
Up toward the sky.
When they pawed the ground with a roar,
Where their feet struck, the black earth
Split layer after layer.
To the two men, senseless with fear,
Their animal form could not be discerned;
They appeared in the form of tigers.
Tümönbay lost his senses
And spoke to Azhybay:
"If one of these tigers
Gets loose, it will eat us;
Then our misery will truly boil!
If one of these tigers breaks free,
It will chew us without leaving a carcass!
Can toothed tigers
Really have been tied here?
Was it wise to act as intermediary
For a bitch like this?
What a waist she has girded!
Where did this toil-stricken bitch
Find such a husband?
If one of these breaks free,
It will surely eat us!
Can anyone prosper who devises
Every kind of lawless deed?
Can anyone prosper who gathers
And ties up fourteen tigers?!
She has worn down horses' flanks,
The wretched woman of consummate arts,
Flying about and traversing
The whole wide world.
Of Chürök's forty arts,
Which art can we know?
Is one of these the mountain pass lying closed,
Or is it the wretched Chürök's husband
Who came from Beijing?!
We knew it from the beginning:
A sidelong look was always in her eyes,
A crooked edge was always in her words;
Given the chance, Azhybay,
She was always ready to trip me!
A slant was always in her eyes,
Perversity was always in her words;
When the moment suited this Chürök,
She was ready to work her Tümönbay trick on you!"
Azhybay and Tümönbay
Stood rigid, unable to walk,
Quarreling with one another:
"We must have come to experience
Torment like Judgment Day;
We must have come to die
At the hands of maiden Chürök!
When Chynkozho strikes with a roar,
Will he spare a soul?
When fourteen tigers gather and maul us,
Will they spare a soul?!
Chürök has raised
This calamitous turmoil;
We have died before living out our lives,
And suffered disgrace from the infidels!"
When Azhybay and Tümönbay
Saw those tigers,
The strength left their arms,
And courage left their hearts.
Azhybay cowered against Tümönbay;
From inside Azhybay's trousers,
His urine streamed out.
Words deserted Tümönbay's mouth,
And the staring light deserted Azhybay's eyes.
