He had brought them before him,
And drawn them close to his side.
Speaking softly but firmly,
Champion Kyyaz began:
“There are seven chambers in the treasury.”
The Jediger’s brave Kyyaz
Opened the treasury doors.
Heavy bolts of silk brocade
And all manner of coins he lavishly
Scattered among the old women,
Yielding to their demands.
Bright gold and white silver—
Without making them ask, brave Kyyaz
Gave the accursed old women
Sacks filled with treasure.
What else could wretched Kyyaz do?
He did not cease his villainy:
He smeared poison on their edges
And prepared seven razors.
On each razor’s edge
Was poison fatal at a touch.
“If Aichürök bears a girl,
Wrap her in pure white,
And saying, ‘My khan, your wish is granted,’
Bring me the glad tidings!
It was a girl I desired;
If you fulfill my desire,
By your fathers’ graves, seven mothers,
I will grant whatever you ask.
If you truly believe my words
And truly serve me,
But she bears a son,
He will fall into servitude in the end,
And ultimately harbor an enemy’s hatred.
In the end he will do no good,
But bear immeasurable enmity.
If she bears a son,
Cut his umbilical cord with it,” he said.
The “razor steeped in poison”
Was given to the brood of the accursed,
The seven sorcerous old women.
Such was the nature of those crones:
They had reached ninety without dying,
And never suffered earthly torment.
They were witches like ogres,
And their people followed them into wickedness.
At the hands of the seven old women,
Many infants who might have grown
And come to be called children
Had died and vanished.
They were drunkards who meddled in disputes,
Greedy schemers who plundered the world.
The seven sorcerous old women
Were never satisfied, even in death;
Even with their heads beneath the earth,
They would not cease corrupting the people.
At every step they plotted,
And never abandoned their great wickedness.
People trembled at the sight of them;
Great spell-casters and enchanters,
All seven were nothing but calamity,
Practiced in uncanny arts.
They were the very eyes of seven corrupters,
Calamity itself.
Each was a true scourge.
Whenever a woman went into labor,
They would blindfold her,
And someone would take her child.
They gave no thought to its tender age,
Nor ever had a day when they feared God,
But simply killed it.
Once the people had gone, brave Kyyaz,
Showing them to no one
And letting no person know,
Led the seven sorcerous old women
To a place amid encircling plane trees,
Where brave Kyyaz’s golden throne stood:
To the palace of the golden throne,
Into brave Kyyaz’s fortress,
He brought the seven sorcerous old women.
