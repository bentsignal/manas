No one she fixed her gaze upon remained unharmed;
That woman of invincible wishes
Had a seal upon her tongue.
May God take Ümötöy—
It is his mother I fear!”
So Kanykey
Spoke amid the turmoil,
Amid merciless great distress
And clamor.
She urged stubborn Semetey
To make peace.
She urged Ümötöy and Semetey
To shine upon each other
Like mist-veiled stars in heaven,
To bind themselves for eternity
And become friends.
But then Semetey,
Without saying yes or no
To Khan Bakay and Kanykey,
Refusing to heed their words,
Giving no open answer,
Grew furious and scowled,
His eyes flashing wide.
When he thought of Ümötöy,
His thirst for blood seized him.
To the grave with this world!
Whenever one foe is destroyed,
Another rises from our own village,
And our troubles multiply daily.
Ümötöy, son of Köksö,
Now we have seen what you are.
Is quarrelsomeness what he loves most?
How can we offer words of conciliation?
Let us beat the madness out of him this time;
If he rages and scowls like that,
Let us find a way to settle
With this ill-starred scoundrel.
If that Kazakh loves conflict so much,
Let us strike him with every man at once.
Always, my mother,
Ah, my dear lady,
Wanting her kin to live without fighting,
Will take their side.
Her blood relations and kinsmen—
She keeps saying how worthy they are
And praising them.
He drew back the blood-drinking Choro,
Turned again toward him,
Bit his lip and narrowed his eyes,
And somehow led Choro outside.
Külchoro flew like an arrow,
Dashing forward in haste,
Springing and bounding like a roe deer,
The agile, lion-hearted Choro—
Matchless in valor,
The swift, white-browed warrior Choro—
Ran fleetly and reached him.
Semetey and Külchoro,
Letting no one overhear
And no person learn of it,
Spoke together in whispers.
Semetey told Külchoro this,
Standing there with a smile.
“Slaughter that Kazakh dog,”
“Put him to death,”
Semetey urged him on.
“Do not let him recoil even from stone,”
Semetey incited him.
“To the grave with it, my lion Choro,
Your strength can master the world.
If Ümötöy, son of Köksö,
Will not listen to reason,
Crush him and bring me his head;
Cleave your way through his Daңгыт warriors,
And return utterly sated with blood.
His heeled boots and leather trousers—
Bring them back as spoils!
But alas, my poor mother—”
