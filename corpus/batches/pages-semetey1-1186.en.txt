No remedy against Chalkuyruk.
Though I am young, I shall see;
At worst, my poor soul,
I will come behind three horses.
If only I had reached full age,
I would lengthen my stride
Against the tulpars racing now
And perform every feat."
His owner stood and wept greatly,
Consumed by grief as he wept.
"I have raced a yearling among horses;
God above, You alone know.
Unable to run strongly enough,
What will Kulatay endure?
Will Kulatay suffer hardship
And die upon the steppe?
Unable to move legs or body,
Will Kulatay be taken?
Will the stallion Kulatay
Be left upon the steppe?
While still a yearling,
Will Kulatay meet disaster?
If he can go no farther, that is ill fame.
God above, what can I do?
I entrust him to You alone."
Before much time had passed,
In the blink of an eye,
Before one could turn about,
A shout arose from the lowland;
Everyone on the plain
Cried, "Drive out the horses!"
A shout arose from the mountain;
Everyone on the mountain,
All pursuing without pause,
Cried, "Drive out the horses!"
Foremost of the forty companions,
Follower of falcon Manas,
Saintly hero Bakay,
Jelpinig, iron of the earth,
Your gray-maned hero who knew perfectly,
The four corners of the world—
The saint who knew their origins.
Wore a blue lynx cap
And wielded his mottled blue shield.
He chose that distant place
As Akkula's starting point.
Beyond that, among the horses,
No lingering regret remained.
He put armor upon him
And mounted him on Maaniker.
Young Aydar of the flowing forelock
Struck at the horses and drove them.
Beside young Aydar
Were exactly two hundred champions.
Old leopard Bakay
Shouted in anger,
Calling to young Aydar:
"Hey, young Aydar, drive the horses;
Drive all the horses swiftly.
If you drive on from here,
Ridge after ridge will come,
Rough foothills will come,
And a shimmering desert will come.
Where a raven goes, its ears,
Where a kulan goes, its hooves,
Will be scorched by the ground.
As you continue driving them,
Among saxaul wood and black sand,
This is where you must release them:
At that lower black ridge.
Foal of a bay mare,
Tulpar of the black herd—
When you release Akkula,
That is Akkula's country."
Young Aydar of the flowing forelock—
To young Aydar himself
He taught what he did not know.
