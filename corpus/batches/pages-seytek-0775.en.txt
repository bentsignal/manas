If the dear soul does not weary,
By the river stands a clustered poplar;
When they camped beside the clustered poplar,
When the revelry came into full bloom.
When the pale dawn broke,
When the constellation Libra vanished,
And sank behind the looming mountain,
When the cold wind of daybreak
Swept down the river,
When the pale dawn stood,
Stars glittered in the sky,
As the pale dawn opened,
And yesterday light spread over the earth,
Yesterday, noble Aichürök,
My days of honor had departed.
After Sarybai unleashed his wrath,
And ground Talas to dust,
After Sarybai laid siege to it,
Aichürök, Akun’s daughter,
Traversed the whole wide earth,
And wandered throughout the universe;
I drained away her sorrowful blood,
And aged her noble head.
Her heart pierced, she withdrew,
And from Sarybai’s wrath
Tears poured from her eyes;
Only a spoonful of blood remained,
Her fly-sized soul shrieked,
Unable to endure, she sobbed,
Her heart pierced by sorrow, she withdrew,
And tears poured from her eyes;
Her fly-sized soul shrieked,
Truly entering in, she was battered,
And pleaded with wretched me.
The reason for her pleading was—
The stars in heaven were dim,
And she, ill-fated like me,
Was my sworn friend unto the afterlife.
That accursed enemy Sarybai—
The swine’s cruelty has gone too far;
“Let there be a mighty exchange of shots,
And by Almighty God’s wrath
Deliver them,” she said.
God created him as a lion,
The doomed sultan Semetey,
Whose fortune was perfect.
If you looked into his heart,
It was broader than the earth.
Born of man and woman,
Such a dear lion was created but once;
His like could never be among mankind.
Take the sun-faced noble sultan
To be your husband.
Against the grievous foe Sarybai,
Wage the struggle with all your might.
From the hands of that savage dog,
Let Semetey’s Seytek
And Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Be delivered,” she said.
Chürök cried and wailed,
Speaking graceful, honeyed words,
And lamenting with bitter sobs.
From the spirits I heard the news
That my beloved Semetey
Was locked in enmity
With the hero, son of the ogre;
Of the fierce gunfire
I heard, and came to rescue him
From the magnanimous hero’s son.
In frantic haste I traveled,
Never resting by day,
Never taking a day’s repose,
Never lying down to ease my hips,
Never reclining to satisfy my sleep,
Crossing towering lofty mountains,
Wading through deep ravines,
Driving the horse relentlessly onward,
Crossing passes and treading stones,
Until at last I arrived and made camp.
When the horse grew as small as a lark,
