Do not by chance trade insults with them.
Coax and flatter them,
Using your honeyed tongue.
From the hands of that rabid dog,
Recover the Akolpok armor.
If he should suddenly act perversely,
If by chance he refuses to give it,
Come and tell wretched me,
“Mother, he refused to give it,”
Then return and let me contend
With that black-hearted dog.
The great inheritance left by your forefather
Brought on a mighty upheaval;
A heavy musket fell across my neck,
And blood was spilled into my bosom.
Great anguish came upon us;
We struggled like wolves,
Our coal-black hair
Streaming as we staggered along.
Our berry-patterned dresses
Were ripped apart at every fold,
While murderous blood poured in streams.
We screamed and tore our hair,
Our frail lives scattering like flies.
They were an army; we were alone.
When they came against us by force,
We wailed and wept in grief,
And lost your father’s Akkelte—
We were parted from Akkelte.
If you ask what Akkelte is like:
When aimed at an attacking foe,
It fires of its own accord;
The hostile enemy who faces it
Is plunged into torment,
And the doomed foe is struck down.
When the moment comes,
A bullet flies from its muzzle as it is raised.
Enemies struck by its bullet
Fall with a thud before the smoke appears;
Once its shot reaches them, they are gone.
Akkelte’s bullet could not pierce that armor,
Nor could the point of a spear,
Embers of burning hardwood could not scorch it,
Nor bullets from a heavy gun penetrate it.
Its collar is gold, its sleeves are brass,
True chain mail with rings like larks’ eyes,
Half a yard in thickness.
When they surrounded the stronghold,
Bringing ruinous calamity and great strife,
When those mangy dogs attacked,
Plundering all our possessions,
And looting the royal camp,
I, your mother, was seized first,
My black hair hanging loose,
My face torn and bleeding.
I fainted, my spirit extinguished,
With no son to be my champion.
When my fate turned utterly cruel,
The senseless infidel Kanchoro
Struck your mother to the ground.
My strength failed and left me,
Black blood streamed from my side;
I, your ill-fated mother,
Had every part of my body
Beaten dusty by the lash.
Your moon-faced mother’s fair countenance
Was bloodied by five clawing nails.
The treasured saddlebag upon my back
Was carried off as plunder,
And Akolpok remained behind
In the hands of the slave Kanchoro.
If you wear it, no bullet can pierce it;
It is the armor to wear into gunfire.
Go and see the field where I was disgraced,
And learn what befell your mother.
Coax and flatter him,
And from that wretched slave’s hands
Bring Akolpok back and wear it.
If by chance he refuses to give it,
Then take up against that stubborn swine
