To true Beijing, let us drive him!
There are Karykan and Esenkan;
Let us take him as a gift.
A great dungeon forty ropes deep,
Without killing him or making him vanish,
Let us put him in the dungeon-pit.
Without quarreling or fighting,
Of the bastard Burut—
Of the people called Burut—
Having locked Bilerik away,
Let us demand their khanate.
If they do not obey, let us destroy them.
The people's hero Alöökö,
Taking him to the edge of the land,
Let us place him there as a hostage!
Taking much tribute in gold,
Imposing countless livestock as tribute,
If their livestock cannot meet the tribute,
Taking girls with five braids,
Binding their giant on the steppe,
If the Burut do not obey the order,
Pounding his brains like ground grain,
Let us avenge ourselves upon the Burut
For wrongs from the distant past!
Chasing wild asses over the sands,
He has now gone mad.
Taking the measure of the Chinese,
Seeing many of these people,
Stretching a survey rope over this land,
This shattered Burut
Has been roaming the steppe,’
So Ezkara said,
And dealt a crushing blow squarely.
Brave Zholoy of swinish nature,
Fighting with crushing blows,
Sating themselves on blood,
Standing and battling without fleeing,
Seeing many a fierce encounter,
As though a spring were flowing,
They shed red blood.
Standing in confusion,
He made whatever trouble he knew.
His giant entered and used force,
The Manchus and Chinese humiliated them,
And slaughtered Bilerik's army.
Aykhoja's Bilerik—
To capture Bilerik,
Whatever trouble Ezkara knew,
He carried out after judging him.
He began the trouble he knew,
Making his warriors loom large,
He slaughtered them before him.
Crushing the mightiest,
He brought a terrible ordeal.
Bilerik was left alone.
The Chinese came and surrounded him,
And thrust out their spears.
The outcry deafened the ears.
Bilerik had his soldiers
Slaughtered by Ezkara.
When Bilerik suffered humiliation then,
When many of his troops died,
The lone hero endured suffering,
Lived through hardship;
His close companions had died,
He had known solitude!
They stood thrusting spears,
Bilerik would not yield,
Fighting for twelve days.
That poor Bilerik,
The Chinese crowded round him,
The wretched man was alone.
There was no support before him,
May God not give this to his servant,
No protector to ride out.
No support in which to put his trust,
No place where he could shelter,
No cliff too high for a goose to circle,
No unassailable black mountain.
No people to take as companions,
No nation to serve as support,
To set him free,
To bring tumult and slaughter,
No giants Koshoy and Manas,
He suffered exceeding humiliation.
He had no strength to trade blows.
As Bilerik traded blows,
God, do not let us see its face,
His nose was wholly ruined,
His ears all swollen and torn,
Ripped away from the eardrums.
When seen from every side,
A mortal's body shuddered.
Spears, swords, arrows,
Struck from every side with a roar.
The Chinese an ilek, he alone,
Had he done something for God?
They stood exchanging blows,
His strength went and he weakened,
Fighting fifteen days without respite,
His bosom filled with black blood,
The poor man longed for support.
His two arms that had shown courage
Could not rise, were paralyzed.
His precious head, worth more than the world,
Was a bucketful of red blood.
I asked those who knew,
About the tale of Bilerik.
He endured exceeding torment;
Could God's servant be brought to this?
Their warrior who wore a jacket,
Mighty Zholoy the unbeliever,
When he came pressing fiercely in,
Of Islam, the pure faith,
Of the sons of Muslims,
He yearned to see even one.
From those Chinese bullets,
May God not give this to his servant,
The black velvet, proof against bullets,
That he wore upon his body,
Was torn apart layer by layer.
When Bilerik moved,
Arrows, a traveling tent's worth,
Poured out from every part.
In that very battle,
His mount was Kylzheerde;
Kylzheerde swayed, unable to go.
The reason he could not go—
A bullet had torn his muscle.
Kylzheerde ran limping,
There was not a hair's breadth of recourse.
Bilerik despaired of life;
His strength gone, shaking his head,
When they stood exhausted,
The wrestlers of the Chinese
Attacked from every side.
When they pressed the battle hard,
Unable to match them in battle,
Aykhoja's Bilerik,
When his strength was spent,
The hero of the Manchus,
The battle-wolf upon my back,
The unbeliever of fearsome fortune,
The hero of a boundless people,
Most wise, a great judge of men,
Their valiant one, Ezkara,
The unbeliever among the kalday,
