The madman summoned
All his wrestlers.
He began issuing orders:
“Do not let the buttocks
Of that raging old hag touch the ground.
Whenever you see her, stir up turmoil,
And grant her no great comfort.
In the morning, make the old woman
Chew and soften
Tough rawhide with her teeth!
At sunset, make the witch
Dance like the Kalmyks.
When night has passed and day returns,
Strap a waterskin upon her back,
Put a crooked staff in either hand,
And condemn the wretch to breaking stones.
Torment her like this,
Heap many hardships on the coward,
Find two barren yearling calves,
And prepare the place of her death;
Between those two barren calves
Bind her fast!
Do not kill her quickly,
But kill her through humiliation—
Thus finish off the infidel!
Among the horses are piebald chargers,
A mixed herd of Argyn and Kyrgyz stock.
When evening falls, this hag
Must not slip away unseen
And race about like a greyhound;
She must not keep disrupting the people.
At the black heart of the boiling spring,
Right beside the tall plane tree,
Dig a great dungeon forty ropes deep
And make a place there;
When evening falls, cast the old woman
Into that pit!
By day, drag the old woman
From the pit and make her ready;
To the two barren calves
Bind her tightly by the waist!
Bind her fast and secure,
And across the paths she might walk
Dig deep, broad trenches;
Make the infidel suffer
Every torment under heaven!”
So Kanchoro spoke,
And when he commanded, the forty wrestlers—
Who cut off heads when told to take a cap,
Who spilled their very souls when told to spill blood,
Who drank poison when told to drink water—
All forty wrestlers,
Stronger than ordinary men,
With their precious swords,
Like men struck by slaughter,
At the sound of Kanchoro’s voice
All fell upon their task.
They never failed to do his bidding:
To the two barren calves
They bound her tightly by the waist.
While they abused your mother,
While they heaped misery upon her,
Before he had even finished speaking,
Before anyone could say another word,
Külchoro’s wife Akbala
Came running in anguish,
With the white dagger bought
For sixty camels and forty colts
Drawn ready from its sheath,
Praying to the spirit
Of her forefather who had passed into eternity.
She was the finest of women,
The daughter of Jamgyrchy,
One set apart from other mortals—
That wife of Külchoro.
She had heard a tale from a gossip,
Heard that her revered old mother
Had been savagely mauled,
