Sary Khan’s death,
The unyielding underworld
That noble man had seen.
Before they could strip off his armor,
Before death could reach
That hero Sary Khan,
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Was overwhelmed by countless cares:
Praying that his gray-wolf uncle would not die,
His tears streamed in ten directions.
Oh, Sary Khan, my uncle,
You were left dishonored on the ridge;
Alas, uncle, avenge this!
You were left dishonored in the hollow;
Oh, my uncle, avenge this!
Tugging at the matchlock,
Saying, “My noble uncle has died,
He suffered this disgrace
At the hands of that infidel Kanchoro,”
He wailed and wailed,
Beating his own head,
Sobbing and weeping bitterly,
Crying, “My mighty uncle!”
His ribs seemed torn apart,
His whole heart burned,
His fly-like soul nearly left him.
They were fortunate men and lions both,
My two uncles, Sary Khan and Bakai,
They were the banners raised in Talas.
Fallen at the banner’s foot,
Today my uncle Sary Khan lies slain.
Before meeting the one he was destined to meet,
My majestic khan and uncle,
Was this how your fortune hardened?
Before encountering the one you were to encounter,
My fortunate kinsman, my khan and uncle,
Was this how your road was barred?
Like the broad gray flood
At the confluence of Jeti-Too, my khan and uncle,
Do you now lie embracing the earth?
You were a pass no enemy could cross,
A hero who stood immovable.
Though men surpass men and women bear sons,
Uncle, another gray-wolf hero like you
Will never come into this world.”
Bracing himself on his right side,
He wept, “My mighty uncle!”
Bracing himself on his left side,
He wept, “My sovereign uncle!”
The world darkened beneath clouds;
Over Külchoro’s head
A darkened world descended.
When that noble hero grew wrathful,
His brow fell and his heart broke.
Death comes one day out of seven—
Damn this deceitful world!—
And grief fell upon him.
Oh, Sary Khan, my uncle,
Your white beard was like soft down,
Your sturdy bones were like brass.
You were noble and lionhearted,
My uncle, a hero born for battle.
“If you die in combat, Külchoro,
My son will bury you,” you used to say.
Among horses, the steed Tuuchunak—
I shall slaughter the horse Tuuchunak.
My khan, my uncle Sary Khan,
I shall bury with my own hands.
As his blood drained, his strength departed,
And his face had turned pale.
The renowned man of Kyrgyz and Kazakh,
My uncle, had met his death.
If my uncle Sary remains unburied,
His two round crimson cheeks
Will be pecked away by blue vultures.
My dear uncle Sary Khan
Will become carrion for beasts.
Flapping as they peck at his belly,
