If we let Ebegey fall to a bullet,
We may as well camp along the Kok-Ozon
And become like a fire gone out.
While the Chinese challenge us alone,
While the enemy pours out his rage,
While black calamity burns like fire
And overwhelms the poor hero,
If we conceal our comradeship,
Strike not a single blow,
Turn our faces away and close our eyes,
Will our fortunate elder
Not think us powerless?
Will they not say those two have no support
And mount them like black donkeys?
If true death has come, let us die;
Let us become a support
For the humiliated gray-maned leopard,
For elder Er Ebegey,
For a forebear dearer than a father!
Er Kenen gathered his courage,
As did the accomplished Jeljetpes.
The two giants would not stand idle.
To begin an unequal battle,
To free the captive Ebegey
From the iron lariat net,
From the black demon's bullets,
From the infidel's deep pit,
From the embers of burning hell,
From the hands of the two giants,
To rescue him,
They broke through the formation and opened a road.
The two splendid falcons
Came and attacked the pair.
Choyun Alp stood at the front.
Before that giant hero
Could say a word or turn aside,
In the blink of an eye,
Jeljetpes proved his strength.
Broad at the base and sharp at the point,
His spear bloodied any foe he thrust at.
Its iron was smelted and worked,
Its edge was sharpened,
Filed again and again with a file,
Hammered again and again with a hammer,
Hammered without rest for eighty days,
Tempered, crushed, and beaten;
Its charcoal leapt like sheep,
And it shattered every bellows.
His servant Dynshokor
Made the master smith weep.
Its solid workmanship was unsurpassed.
Yengkor, who came from Beijing
And had befriended the prophet David,
Was the smith who made
Jeljetpes's spear.
When its point touched a man,
He paled and wilted like grass;
When its point struck true,
His life left before he could draw breath.
It had been tempered in poison,
Heated twelve times red-hot
In the poison of an albasty demon.
Such was his spear and equipment;
Such was the stratagem
Of your elder Er Jeljetpes.
When the fully armed infidel,
The giant hero Choyun Alp,
Joined Odukan
To wage immeasurable battle,
When those two matching madmen
Were about to spill Ebegey's blood,
Plunge him into deepest misery,
And inflict on Ebegey
A torment no man could bear,
Your elder Er Jeljetpes
Came upon him from behind.
At the lower edge of his hair,
At the bare rim of his temple,
He thrust his spear straight through.
From Choyun Alp's head
Blood flowed like an irrigation ditch;
The spear passed through his brain,
And his life went out mingled with blood.
He whipped the bay stallion,
Shouting in anger.
The mighty, fortunate hero,
Who was like a host when he rode in front,
Cried to his elder Er Ebegey,
“Gather your senses!”
His gray-wolf courage revealed,
He thrust beneath the steel shield
On the head of the fully armed infidel,
The hero Khan Odukan.
He thrust and sent the shield flying.
The peerless champion Jeljetpes
Had his axe bound in leather,
Its handle fitted with twisted gold;
When laid on grass, it started a fire,
