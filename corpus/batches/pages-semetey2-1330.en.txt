You should stage much entertainment
And great games, Kiyaz!
Make this manifest infidel Chörök
A moon-crested banner,
A clamor that delights the people,
A sensation among all who see,
Proclaiming, “Chörök who raised the tumult!”
Halt everyone and stir their souls,
Delighting all who hear.
For that shimmering infidel,
That strange-minded woman,
Slaughter livestock of all four kinds;
At her feast, slaughter ninety sheep.
Say, “Chörök is Kiyaz's wife,”
And give her that name!
That is my counsel; understand it.
You were a simple-hearted hero;
My great Kiyaz, heed my words.
There is hardship and disaster;
Grasp the point of what I say.
If you are noble, if you are a khan,
Know that Toltoy is your elder.
Take the shimmering woman
In the red dress with ribbon waist!
If you incur her wrath,
That wild infidel woman
Who has bent the peoples of the age—
If you receive her well, she is Chörök;
If you fail to tend her this time,
She is a calamity that will bring gloom.
If you desire her, she is a wife;
If you depart from proper custom,
She is an ill-starred infidel.
That is my counsel; understand it.
Oh, heroic Kiyaz, heed my words.
You were a heedless khan,
Great in courage but without sense,
A rash and simple soul.
I drew upon the learned elders;
I asked the knowledgeable.
My account may be incomplete:
Within Aychörök
There is, perhaps four or five months along,
A child left by the mighty warrior.
When Chörök goes into labor,
Against that fierce black troublemaker,
Gather in full ninety witches
And forty old women.
Find one who knows incantations,
An infidel who knows sorcery,
And have them sow trouble beforehand.
When Chörök gives birth,
Make her drink a potion and grow drunk,
Then steal away her child.
Let not a single soul know;
Let not a single person see.
Before anyone can utter a word,
In the blink of an eye,
Steal away the child
Of that raging Chörök.
To a dungeon forty rope-lengths deep,
If her child is a boy,
Find a way to steal him away,
And put an end to him.
If it is not a boy but a girl,
Say, “My wife has borne a child”;
Slaughter ninety sheep for her feast,
And give her whatever name you choose.
Part and comb the hair at her temples,
And saying, “This has turned out well,”
Give her away where she will not weep.
But if the child is a boy,
He will not let you turn your plow.
If by chance you leave him alive,
If by chance he survives,
Then the Jediger of Jeti-Özön—
You are finished, valiant Kiyaz;
You will be slaughtered and eat filth.
