When the black earth swallows the world,
Does not its surface grow rich and fertile?
All else is false; death alone is true.
Cruel death has settled upon my heart;
Now I am returning
To the land from which none return.
We five have set out on the journey.
What my father, lionhearted Manas,
Found severed, he joined;
What was scattered, he gathered;
He rekindled the extinguished fire,
Brought the dead soul back to life,
Led a man onward and made him a hero,
Made him equal to the sons of heroes,
Made him a man of mountain-like weight,
And a khan over the whole wide world.
He taught the ignorant,
Guided them onto the path of knowledge,
Found words where there was no way through,
Found eyes in the darkest night,
And found an answer in sudden peril.
Wearing a cap of blue lynx,
He wielded a blue-mottled shield
And pondered many grievous trials.
He never stumbled over evil words;
Even in darkness, if set upon
A corsac fox’s tracks, he never lost the trail.
King at the head of heroes,
His eyes saw from afar;
Amid great assemblies,
He stood alone and won the race.
His valor was beyond compare;
Chief among kings, he was wise.
Delight and torment,
Suffering and death,
He foresaw six months ahead
And conceived abundant wisdom.
At the word “kinsman,” he grew gentle;
If an enemy suddenly appeared,
He girded his steel blade at an angle,
And prepared himself with wisdom.
The prophet-like elder Bakai
Was a saint who comprehended
All eighteen thousand worlds.
Whatever he called a sin was truly sin;
Lion Bakai was a holy man.
With my father, lionhearted Manas,
He kept the white steel ready.
Like a father, they protected
The white-kalpak Kyrgyz.
They quenched the burning fires
Of enemies who raised their standards,
And drove all of them away.
They broke Beijing and captured its banner,
Bringing turmoil upon every foe they faced.
My forefather Bakai was keen-sighted,
A master irrigator in the fields of wisdom.
With my father, lionhearted Manas,
Their sheep shared one fold,
Their food one fellowship;
They gathered their gains together,
Shared death and shared faith,
And were comrades of equal measure.
On journeys they shared the dust;
Entering battle, they shared one life;
Raising the banner, they shared the mirage;
Entering war, they took counsel together.
A seasoned mainstay to the warrior,
For my kingly father Manas
He had been counselor from the beginning.
My prophet-like old forefather,
Khan Bakai in his old age,
A saint whose very presence was precious,
Has set out upon the journey.
Bakai is gone, and our backbone is gone;
From this transient world
So mighty a hero has passed.
Even our father Adam passed likewise;
Cruel falsehood lasts but five days,
And strength has left the five heroes.
