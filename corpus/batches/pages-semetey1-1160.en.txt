The one among the Muslims whom I hold above all,
My saint who stands watching over me!
You brought down
The five pillars of fortified Beijing;
You laid low many
Of the Black Chinese who faced you.
His blessing enriched the people,
The old man who was like a prophet,
A saint born surpassing
All the sons of humankind.
My worthy child Kanykei knew
That you would grapple
With the infidel before the Muslims;
Saying, “My elder khan will wear it then,”
She had long kept it for you.
Its drawstring is secured with leather;
Its price is dear and its worth is great.
This forlorn kandagai
Is worth half the treasury's goods.
She tanned it and worked it supple,
Your sorrowing child Kanykei;
Saying, “At last he will give his blessing,”
She made it for you, elder.
If you accept the kandagai trousers
And give me your solemn blessing,
Here is why your blessing is needed:
I have many dark-ridged mountains,
And many foes with whom I fight.
I have many mountain passes where I gird myself,
And many places where I must face the enemy.
The Russians are on my right;
The Chinese are on my flank.
Guns confront me straight ahead;
I have many enemies with whom I contend.
Elder Koshoi, hear my words:
One who is here today is gone tomorrow;
Such filth is this world.
There is no way to resist
The Creator's command.
Though I have lived to this age,
I have no son behind me.
The horses on the ridge have mottled heads;
The swift argamaks have no master.
If my precious eyes close,
They have no owner to tend them.
Elder Koshoi, my old man,
My saintly kinsman who grants prayers,
Death may come on any of the seven days.
If I take up my spear and break the ranks,
If my allotted fate overtakes me
And death takes me in a bullet's flight,
Who will whet the black-bladed battle-ax
Without letting its edge grow dull?
If these people are left behind,
There is no son to be their protector;
Who will lead them without causing grief?
Who will whet the hawthorn-handled battle-ax
Without letting its edge turn?
If I pass from this world,
Who will lead these sorrowing people
And keep them from scattering?
Blue-maned elder Koshoi,
There is no way to resist
The Creator's command.
If my eyes close upon this world,
I have no son behind me as a protector.
When I reached the age of twelve,
When I did as I saw fit,
When I drove away all the Manchus and Chinese,
Root and branch,
When I unleashed the great slaughter
And ravaged their women and girls,
Taking them as booty,
Karabörk, daughter of Khan Kayyp,
Was the wife I chose and took.
How many years has she been with me?
It is plain what burden she has borne:
She droops and does not comb her hair;
