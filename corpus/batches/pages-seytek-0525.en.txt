Though you be mighty falcons,
You wretches shall die by my hand.
In my wrath I shall conquer;
Not only your people but your land
I shall swallow when I fix my hostile gaze upon it!
I shall seize every one of you,
Make your blood pour out,
And cut short your precious lives!
I shall shake off your filthy dust.
Felling the trees of your fortune,
Toppling your houses to the ground,
May I die if I do not gather your people as plunder,
Crush your nation to powder,
And slaughter you all cleanly together.
May I die and cease to be Sarybay!”
So declared Sarybay,
And as Sarybay stood there thus,
The times closed in with dreadful force.
He unleashed utter turmoil;
In his fury, Sarybay
Struck Döböchö once.
Sparks flew from his mace,
And fire burst from Döböchö.
As though he would envelop the whole world,
As though he would leave not one
Of those he met unsucked and undevoured,
Sarybay upon his mount
Flared up, the accursed madman.
His eyes blazed like a wolf’s,
And he ground his teeth like a boar;
His thirst for blood seized him.
Power surged from his bearing,
And through the fire of his two cheeks
Black hair sprouted thickly.
He made ready in wrath,
And all delight fled away.
Ah, the warrior who had conquered the world,
The infidel, was filled with rage.
He was madness itself,
One who spilled blood and severed heads,
Just as everyone had long rumored—
He proved to be exactly as they said.
He was a warrior and a lion,
A hero beyond all reckoning.
“I shall scatter their young heroes
And slaughter their Külchoro!”
So declaring, Sarybay
Brandished his many-fashioned spear,
Sweeping it from side to side,
And began a dreadful battle.
He summoned his courage and prepared,
Seeming destined to prevail.
He looked as if he would conquer
The whole wide world.
He seemed about to unleash
A vast and terrible calamity.
Your warrior, the Monster’s son,
Your infidel who scorched the earth,
Unmatched in valor, mighty in venom—
He was that accursed one himself,
The black eye of the demon himself,
Calamity incarnate.
He made ready in wrath,
Set all his weapons in order,
And prepared to enter the fray.
The road before him narrowed,
And his fury became crimson flame.
He roared as he rode toward the ridge,
The fortunate accursed one,
The scorched and raging madman.
He roared menacingly,
And cried out in anger:
“If you mean to come, send your hero!
Send your lion favored by fortune!
Send one skillful with the spear!
Send your strongest, one who will not flinch!
Send the brave man who has girded his waist!
Send your renowned champion and lion!
Behold this might of mine!
