From the sky came a six-fold hailstorm,
A driving sleet.
Choking every ditch,
A torrential rain brought down the night,
And just as it stood thus,
I saw a beauty among women,
With a thimble mouth and camel-calf eyes,
Roving eyes like a camel calf’s,
A crimson dress and slender waist,
Slanting, gleaming eyes,
I saw an extraordinarily beautiful girl.
Her attendants were seven thousand,
Her armored warriors eighty-four,
They cast spells by reciting their book,
Never among humankind had I seen such a beauty.
She threw my blood into turmoil,
And from all mortal men
She took away my strength.
She made my eyes unable to see,
Thus she transformed me.
She made my ears deaf,
Eighty sorceress maidens
Surrounded me three times each day.
They led me astray from the road,
No human shape remained to me.
If you saw it, such is the calamity;
She bewitched me beyond measure.
Can Bakai still be alive,
He who always found wise counsel?
Aichürök’s younger sister,
The fairy’s daughter Kökmonchok,
Has bewitched me grievously,
Bringing night over my eyes,
Never ceasing to beguile my soul,
She has spilled much of my blood.
She keeps barring my road,
Binding my hands with spells,
Stripping the coat from my back,
Bewitching me utterly—
My colt, through sorcery I took a wife.
My mind is greatly distracted,
But when I gather all my courage,
Your voice seems to be Külchoro’s.
I cannot leave my words unspoken;
Keep watch over me.
When all forty attacked,
When I was parted from the gray steed
And left upon the open plain.
Yesterday, when I slipped away to the white tomb,
Jaw sprang forth beneath jaw,
Kanchoro the swine emerged as a foe,
Tongue sprang forth beneath tongue,
He readied his sword tight in its scabbard,
Not one remaining behind, all alone
The people of Jediger rode out,
And the black stallion, the fleet racer,
The foal of the miraculous gray,
The carved beauty taken as spoil,
The cudgel inherited from Toltoy,
When Surkön could no longer go.
The teeming black host of Jediger
Drew their bows like falling rain,
And shot their strange arrows like hailstones;
The foe surged roaring like a flood,
The raiders overran the land,
And when the enemy came roaring,
There was no luck anywhere in me;
Parted from Taibuurul,
Your princely uncle had no fortune.
When Surkön could go no farther,
I seized both his forelock
And his ears together.
To follow my father Manas’s path,
I dedicated him and slaughtered him.
In the blink of an eye,
Before one could turn this way or that,
Clouds arose from the hollows,
And over the summits of the mountains
Black clouds closed in,
The grass shook and quivered,
