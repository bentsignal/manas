When the days of summer came,
It was pitched for excursions
Upon the blue-mottled field.
Its threshold, fashioned from ruby,
Was beautifully formed;
Merely to behold it
Satisfied the human heart.
The canopy of that ill-omened pavilion
Had a brocaded crown,
And that wondrous pavilion
Was embroidered all around.
Silver wire was wound
Through the eyes of its lattice walls;
The ends of the roof poles
Were ceremonially bound
With gold.
Every eye of the lattice wall
Had marvelous nails like polished vessels.
All the people and khans assembled
Seemed to stand gazing at the splendor.
Among the Jediger of Jeti-Özön,
All the bearded elders,
Those who picked up distant news,
The aged slaves who had lived long,
The jesters who made many laugh,
The yellow-haired bearers of hearsay—
All the Jediger,
Men who cultivated wisdom,
Champions quick to devise a way,
Within that royal yurt
Gazed in astonishment as a great crowd;
Young and old craned forward to look.
Some listened to tales
Of bygone times and precious deeds.
Some judged formidable Chürök,
Saying she was an extraordinary woman.
Some rejoiced at seeing Aichürök
And delighted in the sight.
Some, astonished by Aichürök,
Cut their own hands
Instead of slicing the meat;
Black blood flowed from their hands,
And some withered in pain.
At the sight of Aichürök,
Their souls leapt with joy;
The wrestlers who cut their hands
Had blood pouring from their sleeves.
Their eyes shone and blazed;
“She is a woman unlike any other,” they said,
And bit their fingers in amazement.
“She has reached the utmost beauty
And perfect grace,” they said.
“She is a woman who has brought
Great trouble among the people,” they said.
“With a face radiant as the moon,
She is Lady Chürök,” they said.
“She is a woman of calamity
Who has stirred strife throughout the world.”
The assembled Jediger craned to see;
Astounded by the graceful bearing
Of the unfortunate woman,
The great host of black-clad Jediger
Stood gazing and trembling.
“So this is the woman,” they said,
Marveling one by one.
Brave young men of courage
Stared until their eyes ached;
Some stumbled and fell,
And some seemed struck down.
They stood shoulder to shoulder, craning to look;
Able youths and promising sons
Gazed admiringly at your sister-in-law.
Good heavens, souls are fashioned in every way!
Everyone who beheld
That poor, fierce woman
Was struck with wonder.
