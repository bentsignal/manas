Turn your chargers' heads;
Stand ready, army, safe and sound;
Do not stand idle—lower your spears.
If death overtakes you, die.
For the warrior Seytek,
All the vast Kyrgyz host,
Stand as my support.
Fight with ringing battle cries;
Plunge into the tumult,
Thrusting and dueling with spears.
When the uproar begins,
Sarybai will ride out in single combat,
Upon his frenzied racing camel.
His valor is extraordinary,
His worth and venom immense.
That towering swine—
The accursed man's spears
Are wielded with skill.
Though Sarybai's devices are exhausted,
He still has strength.
He found no mountain to strike,
No enemy with whom to fight.
The power that enters great conflict,
The power that unleashes great turmoil—
When you go to face him
And confront him,
From the Kutbe side,
Surge forward on the gray horse;
Let the warriors enter from the flank.
Drive the young steeds through,
And pursue as far as you reach.
From Alakem's lower side,
Pour forth heavy fire.
Leave Sarybai's myriad host
Unable to bind our hands.
Raise the battle cry and reach them;
Charge all together,
And wage war as one.
Such is the way of battle.”
So spoke the champion maiden:
“Sarybai, who has ridden out alone—
I shall see how he rages,
Put an end to his panting,
Tear his eyes wide open,
Crush him if he falls into my hands,
Break his ribs,
Seize him and spill his blood.
That marksman who calls himself a champion,
Who made the whole world submit—
Your lone-ranging warrior—
I shall inflict great misery upon him
And avenge the ancient grudge.”
So saying, Kuyaly
Gripped her weapon fiercely;
Her bare white teeth
Gnashed again and again,
And her lion eyes blazed.
She slung the Adi-makpys bag
Across herself and sprang forth.
Zhelzhetpes beneath her
Was covered with a caparison;
She tightened the flank girth firmly
And drew the crupper tight.
Her eyes reddened like a wild ram's;
The beautiful Kuyaly
Shone like the rising moon.
Her human qualities were unmatched,
A peerless wonder surpassing the world.
Sarybai was the one she would face;
Such was her valor.
Time and time again,
They had shattered lances in combat,
Torn at each other many times,
Until strength left their apple-round heads
And blood drained from their fair bodies;
Thus had they fought each other.
They climbed the ridge and faced one another,
Calling and dividing their forces.
Time and time again,
They had battled to the death,
