When Seytek launched his attack,
The strongmen stood aghast;
The strongmen who had come blustering
Lay stretched out dead.
Not one champion stood his ground or escaped;
The sixty mighty caravan men
Were annihilated then and there.
They had assailed the lion-hearted hero,
And the strongmen at the front froze,
Entangling themselves in their own deaths.
By assailing the renowned lion,
They had ensnared themselves in disaster.
Their bundled money lay in a heap,
And with that, alas,
The sixty strongmen were no more.
He wrought a terrible slaughter;
Not leaving a single one,
He struck off their heads with his sword.
Your divinely favored falcon
Did not leave so much as a hair,
But massacred them all.
Silks and money piled up,
And every one of the strongmen was slain.
His fortune had turned full,
And a hero upheld by divine favor arose;
His eyelashes became tongues of flame,
And against the leopard hero’s son
No one had a word of defiance left.
His father’s inheritance,
Noble Buurul, had been found,
And the father’s son, alas,
Recovered his honor through this deed.
The strongmen who had challenged him
Had wielded strength surpassing all others,
Yet your hero’s fury
Proved mightier than them all.
By providence he encountered
His father the khan’s noble steed,
Found upon the road as he journeyed.
Before he could turn the stallion’s head,
Once brave Seytek had spoken these words,
His eyes blazed like a lion’s.
His bloodlust seized him,
And strength and power surged through his body;
From the fire of his two cheeks,
Hair a sock’s length seemed to spring.
Would he spare those he encountered?
Would the tiger hero’s offspring,
On meeting foes, not gorge himself on blood?
Would he spare those who grappled with him?
Would the leopard’s offspring,
When attacked, not glut himself with blood?
He snatched up the golden-cheeked battle-axe
And swung it round;
At Karasart, the caravan chief,
He brought it down upon the crown.
Karasart, head of the caravan,
Was struck by the young warrior’s axe.
His head flew off with a crash,
His life fled with a shriek.
When the sixty mighty caravan men
Saw that battle,
Courage drained from their hearts
And strength from their wrists;
Their eyes bulged in panic.
The sixty mighty caravan men,
Their tiny souls shrieking,
Scattered and fled in every direction.
Would he spare a fleeing foe?
If he did not attack the foe who challenged him,
How could the leopard Seytek prosper?
He took Joybolot in hand,
Struck his dark-bay horse upon the flank,
Raised a cry like brave Manas,
And charged the caravan men,
Striking them down as if in sport.
Ten died where he played,
Others died facing him at the ridge,
And five or six of them, if you looked,
Died where they had fought so fiercely before.
