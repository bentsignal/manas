Speaking softly and gravely,
And began to address Akjoltoy:
“Akjoltoy, who came between us,
Wisdom’s husbandman, equal to a vast host,
Are you our elder Akjoltoy?
You are our guiding saint who lends us aid,
Our deeply rooted, immovable plane tree,
A thoroughbred who would not weary
Though journeying for six months,
A kinsman who would never abandon us
Whenever hardship came.
Your words are sweet as sugar,
And your gaze is like a lion’s.
Wise master of understanding,
Akjoltoy sees from afar
The calamities of both kin and foe.
Seeking the broad land of Talas,
Seeking the noble hero Kenen,
We came to pitch our camp,
To raise the war cry against
The Chinese khagan in the uplands,
And become true allies.
We exhausted all our horses,
Consumed all our provisions,
And traveled onward in desperate haste,
Galling every mount,
Weighing heavily upon every heart,
Wearing out our colts until they aged,
Exhausting every horse,
Crossing passes and treading over stones,
And pressing mightily onward.
The forty tribes stirred like stars,
Becoming friends until Judgment Day;
We made firm our word of unity
And swore never to part,
And journeyed here of our own will
To settle upon his pastures.
Our wish to become Kenen’s companions
Had long been spoken;
Our two eyes had become four with watching.
We intended to stir battle against the foe,
To keep the heirs of the treasured people
From humiliation by the cruel,
And willingly receive and protect them—
That alone was our purpose.
If we camped beside Kenen,
Smashed the vaunting enemy
And spilled his blood,
And if hardship came to Kangkay,
Stood beside him as support—
That alone was my desire!”
What else could their elder Akjoltoy do?
After the heroes had spoken thus,
Ebegey and Kögöy, the two lions,
Having accepted his skillful counsel,
Resolved wisely and made a covenant,
Binding their promise firmly.
They slaughtered a mare,
And, to become unquestioned kinsmen,
Dipped their hands in its blood.
Reaching agreement in grave words,
Splitting even a black hair exactly in two,
Winning riches wherever they fought,
They became trusted kinsmen.
While matters stood thus,
With a swift and splendid horse
And a letter small as a palm,
Brandishing a tasselled spear,
Zhelzhetpes equipped a messenger
And sent him to hero Kenen,
Entrusting him with the news.
“All that was needed has been set underway;
Baabedin Kenen, baabedin!
Your warriors have joined the host;
Ebegey and Kögöy, the two lions,
Have become your companions!
Led here by God and destined to share your salt,
To settle in the upper reaches of Talas
And become your companions,
The heroes Ebegey and Kögöy have come;
The greatest fortune, prosperity, and happiness
Have come to be yours!”
So the messenger spoke,
Delivering the news.
When he heard these tidings,
The formidable dark-maned lion, the precious warrior,
The precious hero and protector of the people,
Prepared hereditary gifts
For the lions Ebegey and Kögöy,
And went to behold the heroes’ faces.
He chose warriors from among the finest,
Prepared horses from among the finest steeds,
Prepared richly ornamented robes,
And made everything complete and even,
Amassing abundant wealth.
He had the sealed mouth opened
Of the treasury inherited from his fathers,
And its countless worldly riches
