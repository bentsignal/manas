Is this not the finest of lands,
Where those who dwell never have their fill?
Its cow-parsnips are thick as an arm,
And when its startled deer bolt away,
They are like white-faced cows.
Its leaves are broad as hides,
Its currants large as thumbs,
Its lizards like snakes,
Its wild sheep like kulans.
Its ants are like greyhounds,
For Almighty God created
Wild beasts of every kind.
Lions, wildcats, tigers, and birds—
The grey-maned heroes’ day had dawned:
They stalked and shot the deer,
They shot and butchered the stags,
And the mighty men feasted
On bloody liver mixed with ashes.
The pleasure of roaming the hunt
Became a splendid pageant;
Such was the delight of the chase.
They remained there for six days,
Shooting bull deer and camel deer,
Bringing down every one of them;
Whenever a boar appeared before them,
They speared it through,
And reveled in abundant sport.
They took off their boots, stretched out their legs,
And slept soundly without stirring.
They remained carefree and untroubled,
Regarding bears and boars
As mere tusked enemies,
Not worth the hems of their robes.
Their bravery and lionhearted might
Almighty God in His power
Had bestowed without measure.
That noble hero, upheld by the spirits—
His fortune was complete.
He was a fortress with iron gates,
His wisdom broad as a river.
After Kuraaly and Kutubiy,
He was the hero born to surpass them.
When dawn began to glimmer,
And the cold wind before sunrise
Struck sharply against his face,
Then Akjoltoy
Awoke from his sweet sleep,
Rolled onto his right side,
And, unable to sleep, grew restless.
He stretched like a hungry lion,
And Joltoy rose from sleep.
He hurriedly performed his prayers,
Saddled Kulansur,
The steed with a kulan’s mane,
And without speaking many words,
His heart surging with joy,
He knew that the hapless peri-maiden
Had indeed arrived.
What had been foretold early had come late;
To behold the face
Of that hapless peri-maiden,
His two eyes became four with longing.
Accursed be mortal frailty:
His fly-sized soul cried out,
His heart surged and wandered,
The fire of love appeared before him,
And she seemed near to his eyes.
Your eagle-like kinsman Akjoltoy—
When he journeys, an army supports him,
A master of wisdom, a river of sense,
A seer who clearly knows
What is distant and what is near.
He is never trapped by the unknown;
Even in the moonless dark,
Once set upon a trail, he never strays.
His eyes can see afar;
When many gather for debate,
He alone emerges ahead in the contest.
He is the sage who unties the knots
Of every torment and tribulation.
