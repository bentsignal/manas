His father Almambet
Came riding forth from among the host.
From the side of the noonday sun,
All the forty companions,
With elder Kyrgyl at their head,
Appeared as though slaughter had struck,
Like arrows fiercely loosed,
As though returning to dwell among men,
As though they would drive and devour
All the world.
Renowned among the renowned were those heroes,
Illustrious, fierce-eyed champions,
Red-speared lions
Who had subdued the whole world
With their might.
While the lion Manas lived,
The forty companions were highly honored;
“Companion” was a formidable word—
Not forty companions, but forty comrades.
Each of the forty followed a different path,
All forty had broad white blazes,
Each of the forty had a different nature,
Each grim champion could shatter an age.
Words lose worth when said too often;
Among the forty companions,
There were all kinds, lacking none:
Some were so fierce that water could not penetrate them,
And there were heroic companions
Of gentle nature and open countenance.
There were also harsh companions,
Hot-tempered and abrupt.
There were heroic companions
Who never retreated from battle,
Never shrank a hair’s breadth from death,
Who turned their chargers’ heads
And stood beneath the banner.
There were clumsy companions
Who could not speak and had no words,
Who had chests but no substance,
Black heads and forked legs,
Human only in name,
Mere creatures born into the world.
There were cowardly companions
Who never stepped outside at night
And never uncovered their heads in sleep.
There were clumsy companions
Whose mottled flanks trembled.
All forty bore forty spears,
With curved swords at their belts,
And truly raised an uproar.
Now the spirits of the forty companions,
The heroic companions, brave Seytek,
And Kuyaly, daughter of Kayypchaal—
These warriors joined
The three as comrades.
Every side thundered,
With an uproar that split the sky,
As they joined them in companionship.
Looking toward this side,
One wore a blue silk coat.
His form loomed like a mountain,
His surge like a rushing river,
His bulk like a great elephant,
And such was his splendor.
His spear was steel, his arrow finely wrought,
His deeds were famed throughout the world.
The finest of the yellow-gray,
The most skillful of mountain game,
A fierce champion of the Kyrgyz,
He too was one of the precious few.
He was Chubak, son of Khan Balta,
With piercing shouts and ringing cries,
Another of the bloodthirsty warriors.
With a brass spear and mottled-headed mace,
In the echoing empty ravine,
He raised a piercing shout.
Chubak, son of Akbalta,
Also emerged from one side
And joined the rallying cry.
He strode proudly in his coat of mail.
