When I had the Kalmyks surrounded,
And struck them with resounding blows,
While I was beating them,
Why did I not die there?
Alas, when Manas lived,
When the swaggering Kojo stood before me,
My hand could reach him.
When I faced Kakan and Keng-Tung,
And had them surrounded,
And was beating
The bald men of seething China,
Each carrying a stone,
Why did I not die there?
When my wise Manas lived,
When I cast turmoil
Across the face of the earth,
Whoever stood in defiance,
I seized and offered up,
And slaughtered him as a sacrifice,
Why did I not die there?
This life I live is an evil name.
My courser Manas, take me with you!”
Bakay stood and said these words.
Your dear woman Kanykey
Took gifts and pacers for the three khans,
And grasped a golden staff.
She cried out in fury
And summoned the two twins:
“Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
You whom I nursed in turn five times
Before the five Argakar stars had set—
By your fathers’ graves, you two wretches,
Has almighty God struck you?
Sink down never to be born,
Die rather than walk alive!
When a horse dies, wretches,
Its hide is needed;
When an elder brother dies, wretches,
A sister-in-law is needed.
When an elder brother loses his sight,
And his wife becomes unruly,
His younger brothers restrain her fury.
Go and gouge out her blinding eyes;
Slaughter as a sacrifice
The wretch who insulted the khan!
I myself shall face
Whatever calamity comes from it.
She and I were born together,
Our fortunes born equal;
I myself shall give the rival wife,
And whatever disaster follows,
I alone shall face it!”
She girded her waist belt,
Shouted in anger,
And summoned Kulchoro.
Taking the golden staff in hand,
She struck Kulchoro once.
Having taken a blow from his mother,
Capable Kulchoro—
He was a hero of twelve years;
Do not ask of his blazing ardor.
He seized the black-jawed whip,
Bent it double in his hand,
Forced his knee toward the ground,
And ran toward Chachykey
To tell what had become of the falcon,
And to call the foul-tongued woman
To account before returning.
When he drew near the fortress,
Chachykey’s forty daughters-in-law
Had fastened the gate
And stood holding the door.
He bellowed and shouted,
Calling, “Open the door!”
And Kulchoro now arrived.
At the door they had barred securely,
The brave-born Kulchoro
Struck, broke through, and entered.
