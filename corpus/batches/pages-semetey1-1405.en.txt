Compared with that wild ram, this one
Surpassed it, I heard.
There lies the Kara-Kyya side;
Ah, my dear foal,
Curse the ram and all with it—
This is the dark hour!
If we shoot and slaughter that wild ram,
And feast upon its fatty meat,
It will be God's gift to us!
We shall scatter Kongurbai's livestock
And raid the accursed one's horses,
Fleeing with them from Keng-Shor!
If the wild ram escapes,
We shall see grievous trouble
And die fighting
Against that accursed swine!
The Chinese are forty tribes;
My mighty son, hear this!
Beijing is no worthless land,
And Alööke's Kongurbai
Is no hero who avoids a confrontation!
The renowned Kongurbai
Is the defender of this Beijing!
This is the building set upon Kerüü;
This is the mighty khan of the monks,
Your father Manas—
This is the hero who deceived him six times!
He himself must now have a blue-gray beard.
Kongurbai is no easy foe,
A servant more skillful with the spear,
The enemy's stratagems
He knows exceedingly well, that mighty servant.
That is the prominent building on the pass;
That corrupt, accursed man
Is the mighty khan of Beijing!
Of the exceptionally cunning Kalcha,
My dear foal,
Your wretched uncle Bakai
Will tell you much!
You halted your accursed uncle Bakai
In an empty ravine,
Brought his feats of valor to mind,
And made him lament hardship today!
In Bakai's old age,
Sorrow comes to mind:
With golden collar and copper sleeves,
A double coat of ring mail with larklike eyes,
A netted outer garment and a wide coat—
When worn in battle, no bullet pierced it;
It was the armor I used to wear.
Mounted on a tulpar and gripping a banner,
While my falcon Manas lived,
It was the road I used to travel!”
Your accursed uncle Bakai,
Your elder like a prophet,
Took the road he himself had seen,
Along valleys with springs and streams.
Uncle Bakai, the dear hero,
The dear warrior, reconnoitered,
Crossing uneven ground.
Kökcholok beneath him
Flickered his two legs;
When Kökcholok traveled,
The tips of the grasses trembled.
Tulpar above all, Kökcholok—
When capable Bakai cried, “Chu!”
No livestock could match his walking pace,
And no wind could match his trot.
His broad rump rolled,
The swift pacer flashed,
And when he opened his mouth, he snorted;
The black earth, hard as a kiln,
Burst apart beneath his hooves.
He did not tire when reconnoitering;
Your uncle Bakai's tulpar
Shone upon the road!
