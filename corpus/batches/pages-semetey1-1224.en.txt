No beast could match him in a race.
With a camel-stallion forelock and low mane,
His swiftness was plain;
When tethered, he pulled the post
Over with its head.
His muscles were like a bull's back,
And his gait like the wind of the steppe.
His eyes shone like an argali's;
When he ran at full stride,
No horse could keep pace.
Hero Toltoi's Surkoyon
Never tired when ridden scouting,
Nor suffered when ridden with the baggage train;
He did not weary if ridden six months.
That surpassing swift thoroughbred,
Until he reached sixty years,
Would not age or loosen his fangs.
To go as a bridegroom,
To raid the Afghan city,
To eat meat from one platter
And face one another upon one bed,
If Chürök proved kind,
To weigh out gold by the foot
And give it as the sister-in-law's fee;
If Chürök proved cruel,
He was about to enter the upheaval.
Toltoi's ninety youths
Were skillful spearmen,
Champions who knew without mercy
The enemy's stratagems.
When a black gun cracked,
They were heroes who did not flee;
When a fierce gun snapped,
They were heroes who did not start.
Hero Toltoi's Surkoyon
Was fed rice and rice-chaff,
And had his sweat drawn every day;
He was fed his fill of barley,
And had his sweat drawn every month.
He raced with birds in flight;
Hero Toltoi's Surkoyon,
His flank-flesh that would yield rump-fat
Clung close to the ribs.
At the sight of a shape, he darted;
When Surkoyon saw a figure,
At a bird's flight, he started.
Then Toltoi grew excited;
The dear hero grew wrathful
And spoke to the Zhediger:
"If I go today, I shall die;
Great Black Zhediger,
I shall behold the face of the other world.
I have left; now I shall not prosper,
Nor return alive and well!"
Surkoyon, saddled five times over,
Chewed a bit of steel,
His eyes shining like a camel calf's.
The afflicted courser Surkoyon
He saddled unsparingly
With a bearskin saddlecloth and white felt pad,
Warning the great Black Zhediger,
"I shall not return!"
He drew the rear girth tight
On the courser Surkoyon,
And shortened the crupper.
Having made Surkoyon ready,
Zheldet brought him;
Lion Toltoi saw
That his horse was prepared.
He put on twelve layers
Of his bulletproof coat.
He placed a blue-iron helmet
Upside down upon his head;
What could dear Toltoi do?
He donned the ribbed mail armor,*
Put on iron from head to foot,
Buttoning it over his whole body,
