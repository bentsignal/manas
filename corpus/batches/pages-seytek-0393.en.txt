The old guards blocking the road
Threw everything down in ruin,
Even the bravest among them.
The hard-pressed guards fled,
Scattering their swords on the ridge;
Bellowing and stumbling,
Many souls fled back.
When they heard of the hero’s courage,
The sentries ran away.
The many guards scattered in panic
And reached their separate homes,
Abandoning all their possessions;
The swaggering champions
Fled each to his own place.
“The hero’s son has come,” they said,
“The apple of Külchoro’s eye;
The bloodthirsty hero’s own young champion,
For whose feast ninety beasts were slaughtered,
Who was named Seytek—
The noble one himself has come!”
They swiftly invoked Baabedin,
Having heard he was the consummate champion.
They heard that divinely protected Külchoro
And hero Seytek had come.
The champions who had heard of them before
Invoked God and slaughtered sheep,
Shouting approval and slaughtering colts,
And began a mighty celebration.
They left Kanchoro’s gate
Deserted and unguarded.
They passed it contemptuously;
The gatekeepers at the outer entrance,
The keepers of the khan’s door,
The warriors stationed on watch,
And the brave sentries—
Every one of them fled.
The people raised a clamorous uproar
And had long since reached
The very seat of Kanchoro.
Her champion and protector
Had been wounded in sixty places.
The blue mountain pass had become a marsh;
Little true valor remained
Of that lineage now exhausted.
Few of the bloodline remained;
Most of my son’s strength was gone.
“May that roaring madman
Not suddenly bring humiliation upon him.
While my hair is white as down,
As he sets out upon his journey,
At the hour when he cannot turn back,
Now that the end draws near,
Let me die before fate claims me;
For my son, small as a crow,
Let me grapple and spill his enemy’s blood.”
To inflict the utmost torment,
And rescue divinely protected Külchoro
From the furious swine’s clutches,
She hastened onward.
What had been spoken early now seemed late;
Her two eyes strained in longing,
Her knees crackled as they bent,
Yet summoning her courage, she ran.
How could she hold herself back?
She leaned upon the sharp Albar sword
Given by Atash;
How could this grief-stricken old woman
Possibly spare herself
From the dragon’s affliction?
Following Aichürök,
The venerable white-haired Kanykey,
His mother, arrived.
With a protector, one’s belly is full;
Apart from one’s mother, there is no Mecca.
His mother had arrived,
And beside his protector
His beloved had arrived.
Külchoro saw his mother and sister-in-law,
He saw his dearest ones.
