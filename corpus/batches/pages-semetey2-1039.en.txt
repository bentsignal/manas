I shall seek their counsel.
If your claim proves just,
If my mother deems it fitting,
We shall agree to what you have said.
What deeds did
The bloodthirsty leopard Manas commit?
And what kind of man was this Köksö,
Who went to Beijing and fell to a bullet?
If you will not heed my words,
I shall return afterward.”
Thus spoke Külchoro:
“We ourselves are maternal cousins;
This conduct of yours will bring shame.
You are our maternal cousin.
Until I return, do not stir;
Remain right here.
Once I have received an answer,
You may then raise your great quarrel.
Until I gain the hero’s reply
And send you word,
If you are strong, if you are a lion,
If you are truly your father’s son,
Remain where you stand!”
So Külchoro
Entered Aichürök’s palace,
As evening was falling,
In the cool late afternoon,
And came riding in at a trot.
When the people’s games had ended
And the mares were being released,
Fiery warrior Külchoro
Gathered warrior Bakay and Semetey,
The two of them, together.
In the marshes of Choguldur,
All the great lords
Had assembled;
In the marshes of Üyrülmö
They had gathered together.
Then Külchoro—
The lion Choro aided by the sacred patron,
Immeasurably swift,
The resounding warrior Choro—
Spoke quietly but with grave weight,
And began his words
To lion Bakay and Semetey:
“White-bearded elder Bakay, my motherly guardian,
You, the elder, are here.
Semetey, my protector,
You, my elder, are here.
Have you seen what I have seen?
Do you know what I have learned?
If you have not seen what I have seen,
Man, if you do not know what I know,
Have you heard the news
Of the one who dwells in Sary-Arka,
Whom no enemy has defeated in battle,
Who yields his share to no man,
The fountainhead of the blue river,
The young champion of Khan Köksö?
He had ninety beasts slaughtered for his feast,
And amid the people’s uproar and the nation’s stir,
Was given the name Warrior Ümötöy.
Warrior Ümötöy has come.
Saying, ‘Pay my father’s blood-price,’
He has come to besiege the people.
He says he will massacre
Even the many venerable elders,
And everyone he sees.
He says he will plunder their goods,
He says he will sow utter ruin,
He says he will settle the score in full.
If his father’s blood-price is not paid,
He says he will raid and desolate the people.
Ümötöy of Khan Köksö—
If we quarrel and clash with him,
How can we ever come to terms?
