Your fortunes rose ever higher;
Your sheep shared the same fold,
Your feasts the same companions,
Your gains were amassed together,
And you were a peer of my brother the khan.
In the herd-rich Sary-Özön
You took so much land as your portion;
You took the prosperous Kyrgyz people,
Then went and conquered Afghanistan.
My children, wherever your hands reached,
How could Margalan remain unharmed?
You reached and took Afghanistan—
How could Jet-Kayt remain unharmed?
You went scouting together,
You faced death together,
And went among the people as khans.
Yet while among the Kalmyks, you yourself
Pissed into the common cauldron.
You are the black fiend himself,
The very eye of the troublemaker
Who devastates the land and corrupts the people.
Scorched and blazing,
You are the very face of such wickedness.
Yesterday, when I saw you as a child
And gave you my left breast,
Life nearly left my wretched body;
As you sucked at my breast,
Blood flowed from the left nipple.
My whole body turned cold,
My twelve limbs ached,
And my ribs seemed shattered.
As you suckled, my breast
Was split into six pieces.
The humiliation you would bring in the end,
You beastlike, gaunt old vulture,
Was already revealed then.
This child looked different in form
And bore an enemy’s malice within,
But my son would not heed my words.
My dull, blind-hearted son—
When Karakhan fled there,
He grew up as an orphan,
Somehow rejoined the people,
Came of age and became a man,
And stood equal with heroes’ sons.
When he stirred up the great conflict—
To tell what followed would raise a mighty clamor—
Külchoro, descended from Almambet,
And Kanchoro, descended from hero Chubak,
Were taken as spoils by the forty companions
When the forty warriors were slaughtered.
Because of what had been done in that slaughter,
After the carnage among the multitude,
Calling you “banners fixed upon the sword,”
He brought you two sons of bitches
Home as gifts.
He split the rich cuts of meat and fat
And fed them to you;
He made the whole Kyrgyz host and the many Argyn
Ask where the two boys were.
He planted and tended you like trees,
Built you a house and sheltered you,
And laid your mortal enemies low.
Into the great horde with its sharp-tipped roof
He admitted you both as twins.
He fondly mounted you
On steeds no lasso could catch.
He fed you delicacies you had never tasted,
And, saying, “You shall be inseparable brothers,”
I offered both my breasts, right and left,
And suckled the two of you.
He led you with him and made you warriors,
Making you equal to heroes’ sons.
He cherished and raised you both,
Made you full-grown lions,
And brought your fortunes to perfection.
Calling you two brothers from one nest,
Steeds that would not tire riding to Altai,
Iron-hoofed coursers,
He caressed you like white stallions.
