Alas, when the fatal guest, death,
Has come to pass,
When the eyes close and are sealed,
And its hour arrives,
When, alas, the head
Must enter the dark grave—
Here is the tale of your valiant father Bakai
At the hour of his death:
Raising their palms, wailing,
Lifting their hands, lamenting,
“Give your blessing before you die,” they said;
“Father Bakai, venerable elder,
Look upon your grandson,” they said.
When Bakai spread his palms:
“The people wept, every soul wept,
The whole vast nation wept,
All who wore turbans wept,
The learned mullahs wept,
Every white-bearded elder wept,
All the people, not one left out, wept.
All those who wept
Wept deeply from their sorrow,
For when every gray-maned hero,
Every mortal born,
Leaves descendants behind,
A man does not die,” they wept.
He was a soul grown exceedingly old;
Your gray-maned uncle Bakai
Had reached a great age.
Facing death,
He had grown too feeble even to crawl.
It was his destined hour;
Alas, your uncle Bakai
Had been overtaken by old age.
His raven-dark eyes were bloodshot,
His beard beneath his chin had whitened:
It was his hour to die!
No second one will come into the world;
Your forefather, old Bakai,
Will never return or be found again.
He was noble, he was a lion,
His fortune was complete;
The Creator will never again
Create a soul like Bakai.
The people wailed and the nation grieved,
They stood engulfed in clamor;
Unmatched among true men,
He stood amid the tumult.
If only the thoroughbred never tired,
If only frail man never aged,
If only the wide world never narrowed,
And each might live as he desired;
If only the mighty, boundless Creator
Would let all remain in its proper course,
I would have no regret—
If only it were so.
A man observes this and reflects,
Shaking his head and crying, “Alas!”
His body went slack as night descended,
And he turned his face toward death.
“Alas, he has fallen beyond recovery,” they said;
“This cruel, faithless, lying world!
Our mighty father Bakai
Has come to nothing,” they said.
“The teeth with which he ate his food
Have fallen out,” they said.
“The strength that kindled fire in both hands
Has left him,” they said.
“His feet that bore him
Down the slopes,
Alas, brave heart, have failed,” they said.
“The dreadful curse of affliction
Has wholly consumed him,” they said.
The season of courage and valor
Had passed away like this.
When old age came upon him,
The gray-maned hero Bakai
Had no flesh left, only bone,
Scarcely a spoonful of blood beneath his chin—
A creature reduced to crawling.
