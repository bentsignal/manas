Who could care for
All the Muslims standing here.
Even now Manas's
Strength is formidable.
You handful of Muslims,
Beware lest he deal with you all!
Do not remind Manas
And have him knock out your ancient madness.
Beware lest all the people standing here
He gather together,
And at Kakanchyn's Beijing
Have them slaughtered together.
He lives at ease in Talas;
If you remind him of Beijing,
You handful of Muslims,
He will bind and drive you in.
I know the story of Beijing.
In the time of the prophet Noah,
The Flood swept through here.
When the Flood surged and flowed,
When it covered the whole earth,
After that it became a land,
And the firmly rooted Chinese became a people.
When the holy prophet Muhammad
Came out to his palace,
Radiance poured upon Beijing.
His Holiness Ali the Lion—
Great Beijing has come down through the ages.
Beware of stirring Manas up,
Lest he gather you as a levy,
Drive you to Beijing,
And surely have you slaughtered.”
Though Koshoy gave counsel,
They did not understand his counsel.
The Muslims of the twelve khans
Would not heed his words at all.
Though Koshoy spoke,
Not one obeyed him.
With that, the Muslims
Did not hold themselves back.
Their finest spear fighters,
Did you see them lead them out?
Those skilled with the battle-axe,
Did you see them equip them?
Broad-jawed and broad-tongued,
They set their orators apart;
Those who shot without missing,
They set their marksmen apart.
Those skilled with the sword,
The finest who knew the enemy's ways,
Gathering all of these,
Did you see them lead them out?
A red banner with a golden crescent
They raised and carried at the front.
Twelve thousand marksmen
Set their faces toward Talas.
Riding their horses forward,
Swearing an oath unto death,
Chewing white wheat flour,
'Let whoever flees be a woman!' they said,
Swearing upon the holy Quran.
They headed for the bounds of broad Talas,
Making their promise firm.
The skilled among spear fighters,
The finest of them all,
Displaying his ample blue overshoes,
Making his thoroughbred hold its head high,
Kozubek, khan of Kokand—
Did you see the khan enter?
Surging along behind him,
Did you see them all enter?
Some of them rode
Karabayyr warhorses;
Some of them rode
Sieve-lunged, copper-limbed steeds.
The twelve Muslim khans,
Looking neither up nor down,
Not thinking they might die,
Their swords swaying,
Their spears quivering,
Rode down into Talas
To pick a fight with Manas.
Of Khan Manas's forty companions,
The finest of the forty,
Wearing a gray lynx-fur cap,
Playing his blue-mottled shield,
Sheltering his head in abundant gold,
Supporting his feet with a thousand silver pieces,
Who found an eye in darkness,
Found a word at an unexpected moment,
If he led the way, the road was blessed;
If he journeyed far, he was like countless troops.
Khan Bakay, the rich man's son,
Who rinsed his mouth with honey,
The sovereign of the companions,
A saint born of humankind—
Wolf-maned hero Bakay's
Wisdom was a broad river.
Wise Bakay, the saint,
When he saw this conduct of the twelve khans,
All the companions,
Did you see him gather them?
Syrgak of Khan Ulak,
Did you see him restrain him?
Calling out for consultation,
Hero Bakay then
Summoned Ajybay, Argyn's son,
His wise counselor:
“Your fathers' graves, forty companions!
We forty were warriors from forty places;
All of us alike were lions!
Some came without horses.
To the companion who came horseless,
An argali-footed, copper-limbed,
Swift horse he gave to ride.
To the companion who came without a coat,
Double mail with lark-eye rings,
A netted outer coat, a broad jacket,
Gold-collared and copper-sleeved,
He gave the finest coat to wear.
To the companion who came without a wife,
A thimble-mouthed, camel-eyed,
Broad-hipped woman with jeweled braid ornaments,
Mighty Manas gave as a wife.
To the companion who came without a faith,
Aykhojo in a mottled robe
Mighty Manas gave as a mullah.
To the companion who came homeless,
As usual, without hesitation,
Mighty Manas gave a royal dwelling.
Ajybay, son of Khan Argyn,
Knows sixty different tongues.
If this is an enemy from China,
Lest trouble befall us,
Find out the news of them.
If they are people of the Islamic faith,
Ascertain it and bring word.
To this Talas here,
No mortal has ridden in deliberately;
No person has come without ritual washing.
If we sit and consider,
A dug pit is coming toward us,
A great peril to our precious lives.”
The broad, screw-fitted telescope
He grasped by its edge and turned.
To ascertain this news,
Ajybay of Khan Argyn
He expressly sent.
Ajybay went to gather news.
Yraman's son Yrchy Uul,
With a tasselled drawstring of forty joints,
Old Kyrgyl, leader of the forty,
The mad elder who grew conceited at forty,
Shuutu the servant, his cap as big as a roof-felt,
Dark steel, broad-shouldered—
Have you heard of him?—
Chubak of Khan Baltay;
Golden-crested, with a fiery sash,
Almambet of Azizkan;
Baychoro of Khan Baabedin,
Skilled at fattening horses:
With Khan Manas among them,
Together they numbered forty.
The forty companions, born as lions,
Who never withdrew a thrusting spear,
Never fled downcast from the enemy,
Girded with warriors' belts—
