Toward Külchoro’s side
She took the narrow shortcut,
Saying, “Pale-gray falcon, so full of spirit,
Well-born morning star,
Black-browed goshawk,”
And when she brought into the house
The young hero she had borne amid death,
Seytek, son of brave Semetey,
Was the very image of the gray-maned lion;
More than the hero himself,
He resembled the dark pupil of his eye.
He was like a prince who ruled a nation,
As though one scalp
Had been fitted over another,
As though the gray-maned lion had been born anew
And clothed in this form.
His front teeth gleamed white,
Like a chisel forged to shape stone;
When the boy charged,
He was a calamity to enemy warriors.
He roused himself like a tiger;
None who crossed blades with him survived.
Upon the champion’s two shoulders,
Two men seemed able to sit at once;
His two cheeks were prodigious,
As though they had sated two giants.
If Kyyaz and Kanchoro attacked,
He looked able to swallow them both.
Before the sweep of the boy’s blade,
Countless troops seemed to scatter in panic.
When enraged, he seemed able to trample
A hundred souls together, letting none escape.
To enemies who studied him,
His eyes seemed as vast as lakes,
As though they could suck out one’s soul.
Any person who beheld his majesty
Would lose his senses and flee;
Every single man
Would panic, unable to find a stratagem.
When a person looked upon his stature,
The mind of the beholder
Was scattered in astonishment.
His father, the gray-maned Semetey,
Seemed to stand there in his very form.
He was the precious legacy, dearer than life,
Truly left behind
By the gray-maned Manas himself.
Taking long strides together,
She led the young hero of noble blood
To your grieving Choro
And brought him to his side.
When Choro looked as the boy was led in,
He was like the gray-maned lion’s dark eye,
The very image of Semetey!
His chest was like a ravine;
His dear gleaming teeth
Were like tempered steel.
The breath from his mouth
Was like a wind across the mountain pass.
If this boy were roused,
He looked ready to devour the whole world.
His strength was extraordinary,
His courage beyond measure:
Such was the nature of the boy.
His lashes were flame, his eyes were embers;
No mortal ever held such wonder.
If he were merely stirred,
He looked ready to devour whoever fought him.
At the formidable boy, one looked
And said, “He is Semetey’s son.”
Glowing embers flew from him,
Blazing fire burst forth;
Among all the sons of humankind,
No such fierce one had ever arisen.
The formidable boy’s courage
And lionhearted valor—
His bravery took after his father.
He was like a champion who terrified nations,
Like a sovereign who ruled a people;
Could anyone who wrestled him survive?
