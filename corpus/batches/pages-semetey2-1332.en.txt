They spoke weighty, masterful words,
Making their pledge firm,
Swearing an oath binding unto death:
May whoever breaks it die.
They caught upon the hillside
A gray mare like a white pavilion,
Held that mare for the oath,
And slaughtered it before the people.
As black blood flowed from the mare,
They cupped it in both hands and smeared themselves.
They made a solemn covenant,
Saying, “Let this befall whoever holds back.”
They licked the edge of white steel
And chewed grains of white wheat.
Kanchoro and valiant Kiyaz
Clasped hands as heroes,
Saying that whoever failed to do this
Was an infidel for seven generations.
Speaking quietly and resolving firmly,
Heroic Kiyaz began:
“You have been my friend, my Kanchoro.
As for the child in her womb,
I shall whip him and take his head;
When the child is born,
I shall put an end to him!
My wise khan, my Kanchoro,
I shall heed your words.
Sixty plots and eighty schemes,
Foul designs and twisted designs—
I shall ponder them all.
When her child is born,
I shall slaughter him while still an infant.
While the child is still in the caul,
If it is not female but male,
I shall put an end to him.
You spoke, my khan, and I listened.
You yourself spoke of what comes first,
My wise one, my Kanchoro,
And you yourself foresaw what follows.
My hero and kinsman on every side,
The affliction of these six years—
You who can now perceive it,
My open-eyed hero,
My watchful Kanchoro,
You are my black ridge hung with protection,
My lion-hearted kinsman!
You rekindle the extinguished fire
And bring the dead soul back to life;
My hero, fertile in wisdom,
My resourceful champion.”
So valiant Kiyaz spoke
And swore his oath to Kanchoro;
He carried the oath through to its end.
Then Kanchoro grew wrathful.
Once the matter had gone thus,
Once the words had found their place,
Once the speech had gone thus,
Once the speech had settled in its place,
The people rose clamoring, “We agree!”
When all the great black host of Jediger
Stood behind him without exception,
When the words Choro had spoken
Won the assent of all the people—
God forbid that one should see his face!
Such was Kanchoro's bearing:
Blood suffused his noble eyes,
Fire swept across his blood-red face.
He donned an arrow-proof coat
And mounted a horse no arrow could reach.
He took the pale banner in hand.
If you had seen Choro's form,
He stood built like a lofty mountain.
Choro's two cheeks looked
As though two Dangyt men had eaten their fill.
If anyone challenged Kanchoro,
He looked ready to slaughter the challenger;
If his wrath fell upon the Kyrgyz,
He looked able simply to wipe them out.
