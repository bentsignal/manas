She slaughtered seven thousand wether goats,
Fired bricks and built it strong:
“For seven centuries to come,
My perfect champion’s mausoleum
Must stand unbroken,” she said, and built.
She slaughtered six thousand wether goats,
Fired bricks and built it strong:
“For six centuries to come,
My generous one’s mausoleum
Must stand unbroken,” she said, and built.
Supkojo of broad Mecca—
She had Supkojo brought.
She had fanciful designs carved inside;
The stories left by Manas
She had shown in great pictures.
“When he grows, the orphan will come,” she thought.
“The courage of his father,
If he survives, the child will know.
‘My father was Manas,’
The child will know at last.
What his disgraced mother did,
My foal will know, unless he is a fool.”
At the entrance of the mausoleum,
Bakybai and Duulat, the two khans,
He portrayed them in a picture.
Khan Alooko and Azizkhan—
He portrayed them in a picture.
The haze that hung over Beijing,
The road he portrayed in a picture.
The great host that bore such hardship—
The host he portrayed in a picture.
He showed him mounted on Akkula,
Clothed in the white armored coat,
With Zulpuqor, descended from the sky,
Fastened snugly to his wrist,
Upon the swarming dark unbelievers
Charging and wreaking slaughter,
The unbelievers farther off
Felling with his spear.
The mottled gray pennant and red banner
He put in Khan Bakai’s hand,
Making the Chinese cry out,
Toward Khan Manas, the leopard,
He showed them driven in the picture.
Clothed in a coat of blue ribbed cloth,
Mounted on a horse like Kogala,
Tall in stature, broad of shoulder,
Akbaltai’s son Chubak,
Wreaking slaughter on the Chinese;
Joloy of the Kalmyks
He showed Khan Chubak spearing—
Him he portrayed in a picture.
On one side of that,
Mounted on a horse like Kokchebich,
Its long-haired tail tied in a tight knot,
Ruddy of complexion, radiant of face,
With a leather belt about his waist,
If seven myriads of dark troops came,
He would charge them with his coat thrown open,
Never turning back without drinking blood,
Never shrinking from death.
Yesterday, upon imperial Beijing,
When he advanced, besieging and driving them,
Making the Chinese cry aloud,
When he beat and drove them on,
Breaking ranks in spear combat,
At the age of twenty-one
His allotted death came, and he fell to a shot.
Khan Ulak’s son Syrgak,
Khan Manas’s impish champion—
Him he portrayed in a picture.
Mounted on dear Sarala,
Wearing a golden belt,
His moonlike face lit with radiance,
Wearing a silver belt,
His sunlike face lit with radiance;
Yesterday he had yellow willows planted,
His willows remained in Beijing.
Made of yellow gold,
His throne remained in Beijing.
He had stained his golden throne with blood:
“He will not enter the present faith,” he said,
And slew his father Azizkhan.
He had bloodied his silver throne:
“His sin is too great,” he said,
And slew his elder brother Kun Zhang-Zhung.
Purified of the dark faith,
He fled the unbelievers and became a stranger.
With a golden forelock and tasseled sash,
Azizkhan’s only son—
Him he portrayed in a picture.
Old Kyrgyl, leader of the forty,
Khan Shuutu of good disposition—
The men he portrayed in a picture.
Then that poor woman Kanykei,
Without a single soul knowing,
Without a single person seeing,
From the lands of Zhelpinish and Urum
Had seventy mighty men brought.
“My noble, clear-sighted leader,
They will open his grave and take him,” she thought.
“They will drag him upon the road
And have the dogs tear him apart.
The crooked-hearted enemies
Will take their revenge this way,” she thought.
At Echkilik’s black cliff,
That immense and towering mountain,
She supplied iron crowbars,
And sharp steel hoes;
She had the face of the cliff opened,
Had Manas’s resting place dug,
Had its entrance fashioned
Wide enough for a single person,
And the farther part hollowed out
To hold six thousand sheep.
“Lest its roof fall in,” she thought,
In eighty places around it
She set up gilded pillars.
“Let the inside lie bright,” she thought;
She had ruby stones split
And in eighty places
Had them set as lamps.
“Lest the inside rot,” she thought;
Your dear sister-in-law Kanykei,
Showing her lion’s courage,
Had a way broken through with iron crowbars,
