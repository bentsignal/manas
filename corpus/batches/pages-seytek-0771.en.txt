They grappled fiercely and hurled one another,
Beginning a grievous conflict;
Dust flew into the sky,
And the earth turned gray with haze.
The whole world was shaken;
Dust billowed over the hills,
And darkness filled the ravines.
The people surged in uproar,
And my ancient fury stirred.
The accursed world’s
Falseness was laid bare.
Before the frenzy of the infidel,
The monster’s son, no soul could stand;
The whole assembly despaired of life.
The furious conflict became a calamity,
And the multitude despaired of life.
The ravenous nature
Of the earth-crushing boar was revealed;
Blood gushed and was swallowed by the ground,
And the impenetrable mail
Was torn to shreds.
The towering black ribs
Were stripped apart layer by layer;
The monstrous nature
Of the swine, the monster’s son, was revealed.
Those with sense grieved,
While senseless men struck and stabbed blindly,
Crying, “None of us will survive now!”
And despairing of their precious lives.
Khans died in the collision,
And all who grappled died;
The place became one vast dug grave.
What an upright person witnessed
Was a seething mass of misery.
We clashed among the people,
Fighting beside the funeral feast;
I fought battle upon battle.
There among the multitude, the swine
Had sixty champions supporting him;
I slaughtered them where we fought.
We battled until our strength failed,
Struggling after the spears broke;
Without sleeping by day,
We fought for sixty days.
Innocent young men and khans died,
And blameless souls died.
People of many different nations died;
All who came to the feast died.
The war banner was raised,
And the sight became a grievous uproar.
I fought a fierce struggle
And began the slaughter in earnest.
This accursed but necessary life—
Though I was a maiden, for honor’s sake
I bound my precious, sugar-sweet soul
To a single hair and cast it aside.
I turned my heart toward death,
Girded my waist in the cause of honor,
And preserved my people.
My father was old, and I was a girl;
What other recourse was there against the swine?
Entangled with that monstrous brute,
At my khan father’s funeral feast,
Before the vast assembly,
He crushed the weak and forced a path,
Boasting himself a hero and a lion,
Claiming to be braver than every nation.
The rabid, fiery madman
Raged like a stallion
And lunged like a bull,
Taming a wild doe into a cow,
And displaying his bloodthirstiness.
Without cause or accusation,
He tormented the fly-like lives
Of the people who came to the feast,
Relentlessly heaping humiliation upon them.
When he began strife among the multitude
And reduced them to submission,
When he unleashed grievous conflict,
When the smiting God struck him,
