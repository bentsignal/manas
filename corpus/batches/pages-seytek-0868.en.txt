Spearing all who resisted.
Ah, the arrogant dog kept pressing on.
All the warriors burned with grief,
Each hero despaired of his life;
All the champions wailed,
For much of the army had been massacred.
Sarybai was their great commander,
And before Sarybai
His troops poured in with cries.
In Sarybai’s company
Was one who lived at his side,
Whose livestock grazed with his,
Who shared in gathering captured wealth,
A close neighbor and companion
Called Zhügürük the Eloquent.
That Zhügürük the Eloquent,
Zhügö the Eloquent, a master of speech,
Dark and squat as a griddle cake,
Spoke striking words,
And told of the disaster:
“My Sarybai, you are a hero,
Weighty in stature, stern in temper,
Quick to anger now, narrow of heart;
My blue-maned lion, arrogance dwells in you.
You are a warrior and a khan—
Can one like you truly be a hero?
A mountain stands without shelter;
Can it prosper if called a refuge?
Can one like you truly be a hero?
In the marshes of Zhelpinish,
Yesterday, while your people lay at ease,
You abruptly broke my sleep
And stripped me of my possessions.
You utterly embittered my heart
And plunged my people into turmoil.
Yesterday, while we dwelt at Zhelpinish,
You split the earth with uproar
And sent panic through my land;
‘I shall wage a mighty war,’ you said,
‘I shall encircle Talas,’ you said,
And made my homeland boil in misery.
Once, Sarybai,
You were a champion and a sovereign;
But now, Sarybai,
You have been leveled with the black earth.
Semetey’s hero Seytek
Has broken your strength;
His lion faced and slaughtered yours,
Folding and breaking your back,
Toppling your iron fortress,
Spilling your blood like water,
And massacring all your men.
When summertime came,
When rivers flooded and waters surged,
When the nightingale perched upon the branch,
You came after hearing much news from the road,
Intending to destroy
The offspring of the bloodthirsty lion.
We did not destroy them—we are destroyed.
Though we blustered like champions,
How can our fortunes now be mended?
We attacked a lion blessed by Kyzır,
And in the end became entangled
In unbearable, calamitous strife.
We attacked a lion wise as Lukman,
Started war and stirred up discord,
And thus were ensnared in shame.
Today we escaped alive—
From the calamity written upon our brows,
How did we escape without dying?
Thus have we fallen into death’s grasp.
Boasting ourselves heroes and lions,
Deeming ourselves braver than every nation,
We came in splendor;
Now, diminished, dead, and slaughtered,
How can we leave with banners flying?
Mountains may be leveled and passes remain;
The army is destroyed, and you alone remain.
How can we leave swaggering?
If we continue living without dying,
