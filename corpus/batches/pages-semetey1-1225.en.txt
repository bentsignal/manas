With blue iron.
Adorned and splendidly arrayed,
What did valiant Toltoi do?
He swayed as though struck from this side,
Roaring in his wrath;
Dear Toltoi thundered,
Growling in excitement,
And spoke to the Zhediger:
"Is there anything better than a dog's drink?
They say vodka is nourishment;
Let me drink that vodka
And ride shouting against the enemy.
If my death has come, let me die;
Whatever command Allah has laid down,
Let me meet it.
Black vodka in the Kalmak fashion,
One leather flask of vodka—
Saying, 'Pour out that dog-drink,'
They poured him the vodka.
He struck down four bowls of vodka
One after another.
Drinking vodka, he grew excited,
Not knowing that he himself would die;
He cursed without pause
Both the Zhediger and Kojo.
He girded on his unsheathed sword;
Like a cloud about to rain again,
Wrath spread across his face.
The sight arrested the heart;
Toltoi grew wrathful,
And anger poured from his mouth.
When your Toltoi showed his true nature,
He was like taut red iron;
When Khan Toltoi grew angry,
His bulk equaled half a mountain.
He took a blue-mottled spear in hand,
Slung a short blue gun,
And his war gear stood out;
He loomed like Opol-Too,
Overcoming the hearts of all who saw.
To raid the Afghan city,
To go as bridegroom
For Akun's daughter Aichürök,
Dear Toltoi then
Loomed like Opol-Too.
Roaring in his wrath
And gathering himself like a blue tiger,
As the sun was sinking low
And the people lay down,
Dear Toltoi mounted his horse.
Tölök and Tölöbai
Both shouted out
To remain and give their blessing.
They called to Toltoi of the Zhediger,
"Wait a while!"
When they called, Toltoi cursed them:
"Tölök and Tölöbai,
May you sink into the earth with your blessing!
I now go to meet
What fate has laid down;
Even if you give a thousand blessings,
I go to die.
If I go today, I shall not prosper;
Look closely—I shall not survive today.
Give your heart or withhold your heart;
Let me behold humiliation in full,
And die when death reaches me.
