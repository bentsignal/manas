Of anyone who goes there to fight,
Not a spoonful of blood remains, they say.
Of Kakanchin’s Beijing,
From the reports we have heard and learned,
On this side lie Dyngsha and the gray night;
For anyone who challenges
The Kakanchin Chinese, it becomes black night.
You would not heed
Your poor mother, my hero and only one;
You stopped me at the gate,
Cast your mother into desolation,
And made me lament the Kakanchin.
You would have me sing my lament;
Never heeding my words,
You would make your poor mother
Speak the truth within me!
The robed sheikhs and blue-robed attendants,
The chosen men of Kokon and Margalang,
With wound turbans upon their heads,
Pleading to the Almighty—
Let me tell of their way of life:
They have round-baked bread,
And houses built with four corners.
If you gather them as your support,
There are also the people of four khans.
If you muster them for Beijing, they are humble folk,
All a pack-camel-riding frontier people.
If you go to Beijing, gather an army;
Gather many as your support!
In renowned Beijing
There are the Black Kalmak people;
Among those people,
If you fight them, my champion,
There are giants of many kinds.
Among those giants
Are skilled men who level spears,
And masters able to cast the noose without missing
In the darkness of the moon,
Men perfected in their craft.
Against seething Beijing,
When you charge and level your spear,
Saying, “I shall avenge my khan father,”
And turn your whole body without restraint,
While guns fire and bows are drawn,
And weapons clash together,
Will the Chinese not truly reach you?
If you take no support beside you,
Will your life not be left without recourse?
They have jang-juns and Torguts;
They have Shibe and Solon.
The forty speak forty different tongues,
The forty tribes of distinguished Chinese.
I have said many words; heed my speech,
My only one blessed by Kydyr, understand!
When his mother spoke in this way,
The gray wolf surged and could not stand still,
Coiling like a dragon,
His eyes burning like embers.
The young champion, son of the bloodletter,
Had Kongurbai as the foe he would fight;
It was time for him to go to Kakan.
With broad teeth like a gate,
Such teeth as no people possessed,
Teeth unlike those of any other tribe,
He bared them white,
And, refusing to heed his mother,
Answered her with harsh words:
“Mother, who would listen to these words of yours?
Let some black night listen!
Tormenting the people to gather an army,
Making some of them weep,
Forcing some by violence,
Driving them up and down,
And bringing turmoil upon the people—
What desire would I attain?
Against the seething infidels,
Against hero Khan Kongurbai,
I shall go alone myself!
