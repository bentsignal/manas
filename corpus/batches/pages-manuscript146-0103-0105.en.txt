Thus spoke Jeljetpes,
Making it known to the heroes,
Bringing the boys to obey his words,
Providing sustenance for his people’s hearts,
Jeljetpes intended to ensnare them.
From Chyngteñir’s net,
From the hand of the wrathful day,
Jeljetpes was about to remain.
Before the sign of the sun arose,
Such a stern and imposing hero,
The accomplished champion Jeljetpes,
“If we leave together, you have seen it,” he said,
There was no movement and no quarrel,
“Simply take warning from me,” he said,
I came with the horse lathered and spent,
Jeljetpes prevailed over all of you,
“I will recover your due,” he said,
Without sitting and without ceasing to move,
“Remain where you understand,” he said,
Khan Bangyr said this,
He told of the dreadful hardship he had seen,
After the man departed again,
Jeljetpes entered upon the road.
The one he led was a heroic champion,
His breast was full of deep sorrow,
Toward the churned dust,
The ill-starred wanderer was to come.
Chyngteñir, who traveled with thousands,
From the snare laid over these people,
Had saved a man without entanglement.
Thus spoke Jeljetpes,
With a message carried in honor,
Sitting down, he put on his coat,
He set on his cap and mounted his horse,
He took the road toward the khan’s camp.
Where would a hero ever shrink back?
The gleaming Ala-Too,
The eye travels across the green plain,
Was it a land from which none would draw back?
Pitching such a firm encampment,
The camp of forty countless ranks,
Was this a land that would bear such a load?
Its crimson market-stalls,
Was this a land one would not visit?
Its masters were a broad and numerous people,
They were a blessed people,
Their nets were spread freely,
They were said to be a most prosperous people,
He bound them, intending to cut them down.
The hurried one dwelt in the open country,

Jeljetpes saw this,
There was no straight path where he roamed,
Jeljetpes of paradise-bright eyes,
If this wrathful boar attacks,
“What kind of brute is he?” I said,
The merciless boar,
At noon he fell into their hands,
Wondering what remedy there could be,
Cast many sheep upon the white ground,
His swelling grief was ruined,
He went once to see the livestock,
All saw the Kyrgyz people,
They would remain at the place of slaughter,
He went once after seeing their lives,
They were a people from the lesser side,
May God cover them abundantly like snow,
He spoke to his people as a mortal.
“Strike and drive them,” said this one,
A Chinese khan with no preparation or khanly order,
They were a merciless people,
Living until the fire was kindled,
Until wisdom closed its eyes,
Until then this people would live,
Wondering what would happen then,
Only a little of the plain remained,
Until he had settled this in his mind,
Until he had fully explained himself,
Until he sent word to the people,
May God’s command not show such a fate,
He appeared toward Ebegey,
What the wise among the people knew,
He had seen night here before,
He had seen the hardest trial of all.
