Scorching the face of the earth,
That dragon charged.
Grappling with it, leopard Seytek
Vanished from this world.
Unable to overcome the fierce one,
Only a spoonful of blood remained,
Only a bruised scrap of flesh remained.
The sword shattered at the hilt,
The heart burst from its sheath,
The fiery heart was stirred,
The Ala-Too where the people dwelt
Was reduced to dust and turmoil.
The fire they kindled was extinguished,
The countless Kyrgyz, vast as a cliff,
The ravenous brute sucked them dry
And drove them all away.
It mauled whoever it encountered,
And devoured whoever crossed its path,
Leaving this land ownerless,
Leaving many women widowed,
While its hostile people settled there.
Plunged into calamitous misery,
The ancient nation was massacred;
Everything that so much as stirred
Was swallowed from the east.
The crops they had sown
Were reaped raw before they ripened.
Whoever dared resist
Was swallowed like tribute and sucked dry.
The sword broke in the middle,
And vanished from sight;
Of that ill-fated disaster,
No one knew where it had gone,
It plunged daylight into night,
And whoever dared resist
Was cast into a pit with no return.
The Kyrgyz of this Ala-Too
Were trapped in a precious net
From which no struggle could free them.
“What could this mean?”
So then that
Ill-fated Perizada,
A peerless mistress of wisdom,
Recounted her many-colored dream,
Telling all its wonders;
Lady Perizada’s
Clear account was thus.
Sobbing bitterly, she began to speak,
And spoke in anguish:
Some parts were blissful, others grievous,
One was hell and one was paradise;
She told the whole dream she had seen.
When he had heard this dream,
Valiant leopard Seytek
Rose and stirred from sleep,
Alarmed for his beloved people,
Wondering what would follow,
And saddled Kulansur.
“For one whose mount prances proudly,
May his road be fortunate when he rides;
If it rears and strains against the rein,
May it not chafe his hands.”
A finely knotted, perfect halter,
Fitted superbly just so,
A halter made exactly for Kulansur.
A leather saddlecloth and blue saddle blanket,
A white bearskin saddle pad,
A fitting pad for a hero’s mount,
Its pommel of pure gold,
Its rear entirely of silver,
Iron plates along both sides,
Crafted with consummate skill;
While his princely father still lived,
Bölökbay, master of the forge,
Had made it as a final bequest.
Can one who fears death ever prosper?
In this fleeting mortal world,
Can anyone possess an iron soul?

Took the inherited white saddle, saying,
