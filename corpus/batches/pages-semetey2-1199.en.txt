My aged mother Chyiyrdy—
I have no other homeland than her.
Her blessing is renowned among the people—
She is the mother of my falcon-like father,
That venerable Chyiyrdy;
May she die without suffering disgrace!
Oh, saintly matriarch!
Damn you, treacherous world:
What is here today is gone tomorrow;
One day you show your face,
The next you show your back—
Such a vile thing is this world!
While I stood ready and strong,
My valiant mother did not die.
This soul of mine did not bury my mother.
I performed no arduous funeral service;
When Chyiyrdy died,
I was not there to bury her myself.
I did not have a spacious grave dug,
Nor, inside that grave,
Have a ruby lamp set;
I did not have her washed with kumiss,
Nor wrapped in cloth of gold;
I did not slaughter six thousand head,
Beginning with horses, in her honor,
Nor hold a feast for all the people;
I did not attend her funeral almsgiving,
Nor, saying, “My valiant mother has died,”
Perform her rites myself;
I did not stand at the head
Of my poor aged mother and watch over her.
When I myself am dead,
May they not call her a barren woman who devoured her child;
May hers not be a dog’s death,
May her grieving soul not be left forlorn,
May they not drag her along the road
And let the Dungans tear her apart!
Crying, “My valiant son is dead,”
May Chyiyrdy, when her own death comes,
Not claw both cheeks,
Swallowing blood, unable to recover herself,
Nor suffer grief like this.
May my valiant mother Chyiyrdy
Never meet with such affliction.
“She fed him rich rump meat and fat,
Yet her precious son has died,” they will say;
“Her cherished son has gone
To the true abode whence none return,” they will say;
“His allotted days are spent,” they will say;
“Her loving son has departed,” they will say.
When her own hour of death comes,
May she suffer no humiliation;
May my valiant mother Chyiyrdy
Endure only the grave’s brief trial,
For the grave is better than this life—
Let her endure only the earth’s brief trial!
May sorrow not impoverish her soul;
May the blessed old woman not linger behind.
May grief not impoverish her spirit;
May my poor mother not remain behind!
And my own dear mother Kanykey—
May she not turn her thoughts toward death;
Is he not her own descendant?
Let her take comfort in the child
Who remains after me!”
He murmured and rumbled,
And before the noble hero finished his last wishes,
Kanchoro, son of Chubak—
Alas, that troublemaking Choro—
Appeared ahead with a multitude.
Damn you, Kanchoro—
God surely exists, you traitor to your salt!
The earth churned beneath the teeming host;
He drove an army too vast for the land,
Driving all the troops together.
On the crest of a forked ridge,
Toward your prince standing gaunt,
Stripped of his steed,
