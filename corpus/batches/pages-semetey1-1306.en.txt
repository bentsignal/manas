Joined two against one to kill him;
On the day of the final reckoning,
Let no one say elder and younger brother united
And singled him out to kill him!
May the punishing God not strike us;
On Judgment Day,
May this swine not stand demanding his blood-price!
One day we too shall die;
When he demands his blood-price then,
If they say we went two against one,
What answer shall we give?”
Heeding the sultan's words,
Külchoro then
Released the khoja.
“Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Our road lies together through the passes;
Our roots share one nest.
Did I steal your Ala horse,
That you should shoot without mercy
And take an entrusted life?
Did I scatter your white silver in the corners?
Did I flee while driving off your kulan?
You raging fool, khoja,
Did I spill your goods from the saddlebags?
When I came to Talas,
Did I speak any such word?
Curse your father's grave, Chynkojo—
Did I seize and consume what was owed you?
If I bear black thoughts without a crime,
May the one Creator strike me!
You brought crisis while I went peacefully;
May calamity strike you, accursed khoja!”
Without swinging a hand at the khoja,
He seized him by the shins.
A black rock large as a house—
The ill-fated khoja,
He brought and hurled upon it.
When he thus settled the account,
When he smashed him against the black rock,
His valor now appeared;
Chynkojo split in two.
He slaughtered Chynkojo;
Black blood flowing from his breast,
He scooped it by the double handful and drank his fill.
Hardened from drinking blood,
By this deed your gray wolf
Became known as the black gray wolf.
Semetey, born after
His father, gray wolf Manas,
Shaking out his cartridge pouch and making bullets,
Destroyed the impetuous khoja
From this world.
The havoc he had seen in Afghanistan,
The wonder with which he then
Had sent the Jediger to Afghanistan—
Heading toward that place,
He took Külchoro with him
And rode far without sparing the horses.
Kanchoro, son of Chubak,
Had unleashed turmoil;
Standing opposed face-to-face,
Firing guns and bows,
Fighting without fleeing,
Jediger's Tölök—
He had reached and captured him.
Kanchoro proved a hero;
He settled his account exactly.
Having bound Tölök,
He hacked open his hamstring.
When the prince drew near,
He unleashed utter turmoil.
Dear valiant Tölök
He slaughtered as a sacrifice.
When they rode onward from there,
A gray-mottled standard and red banner,
Tumultuous cries rang rank by rank.
A white sacred standard and red banner,
Hungry shouts and uproar.
