“Just see how he lies,” she said,
And because of Kanchoro
She utterly shamed her.
Before all the people, his mother,
The foal of a surging river,
Was left whining like a howling dog.
Venom scattered across my face,
My mouth was sealed and could not open,
My honor scattered like ashes.
My heart burned and became embers,
My mouth faltered before speaking,
Then opened without the slightest movement,
And the fierce words leaving my mouth
Scattered like fire across the earth.
Koshoy and Bakai were there,
Men who had worn out horses and seen many lands,
With heroes of countless kinds,
And all the Kyrgyz people.
My soft-fledged pale falcon,
My morning star born of my own flesh.
You mangled my words,
And brought shame upon me;
You scattered my speech abroad,
And made me a public disgrace.
My wounded pride struck the earth;
The mouth that spoke was stopped.
I, the wretched, dishonored woman,
Breathed only through my back;
Through toil I won my people.
Thirty daughters-in-law and thirty maidens
I sent out to serve me.
I prepared a white, gleaming sword.
I raised an Argyn and made him a man.
While Kanchoro was still a child,
I thought of slaughtering him.
When the trembling sun was setting,
When the people’s herds had settled,
And the folk lay down in peace,
My thoughts departed, wretched as I was.
I opened the hearth and faced the heights—
Who is there to hear these words of yours?
Let the dark and gloomy night hear;
Who else but it can hear?
Your words are not fitting;
Mother, your words came like an enemy’s.
Clear your clouded brow,
And, suffering mother who raised me,
Close your mouth and nose.
Oh, this will bring you shame;
Remain where you are,
For what you contemplate is monstrous.
This deed will bring disgrace;
Stay among those distant people.
My chosen Külchoro,
The banner borne by my spear,
Companion of my khanly father,
The gift of Khan Almambet,
The remnant of my beloved,
The lock upon all I built,
The heart of the marketplace.
These boys are Bagdagül’s morning stars,
The support of my fleshly heart,
The mates of those I have left,
The finest of the beloved ones.
My father’s lion,
The Kyrgyz people’s eagle,
The Chubak of Khan Baltai.
On the rugged forked mountain pass,
With my father, the lion Manas,
He swore friendship for the next world;
He is the beloved of this entrusted soul.
The flower-filled hope of my heart,
The son of my foster-father Chubak—
This Kanchoro is that son.
See how this ill-fated mother of mine
Understands none of this!
She says the infant in the womb
Sucked her breast till she wailed,
And so became her mortal enemy—
My mother, darkened by misfortune.
