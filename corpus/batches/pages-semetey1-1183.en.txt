Countless camels came in rows;
Leading those countless camels,
A mass of people came on foot.
The people marveled
And asked many questions:
"Whose are all these camels?"
"Loaded for Kokotoy's memorial rites—
These are Kokotoy's camels.
Attending those camels
Is a countless throng of servants."
They came and undid the bindings;
To be staked as the grand prize,
Many splendid things had come.
With no mount left wanting, they brought goods,
Loaded gold coins upon the goods,
To serve at Kokotoy's celebration,
All equally as attendants,
They brought sixty beautiful girls.
Their trailing ornaments brushed the ground.
This vain wealth had reached its height:
Kokotoy's abundant riches,
Which flared brighter as they were spent,
A world of goods arrayed like grass;
The more was cut away, the broader it grew,
Abundant wealth that never diminished.
These were the things he had brought.
Now hear the condition
Of that boy Bokmurun.
He enjoyed honor among the people.
All this wealth and delight—
Dear noble Kokotoy
Had gathered it all together.
If Bokmurun left it untouched,
It could have lasted until Judgment Day.
Bokmurun, whose father had died,
Did not rein in his horse;
Infidel and Muslim alike,
He gathered every last one.
He opened the treasury
The mass of wealth and livestock
Before the assembled people,
He scattered lavishly.
Overseeing the feast
Was gray-maned lion Manas.
All whom ears had heard of and eyes had seen,
Every one among the people,
Attended Kokotoy's feast.
The thick-woven piebald cloth of Andijan,
Was staked as the grand prize,
Gathered like Ala-Too,
Made the hearts of onlookers waver.
The costly piebald silk of Bukhara
At Kokotoy's feast
Loomed like a misty mountain.
Gold and precious stones,
With rubies mixed among them—
Kokotoy's many prizes
Overwhelmed the multitude,
And troubled their poor heads.
Kokotoy's red banner with golden finial
Was raised sky-high above the feast.
Your gray-maned lion Manas
Had proclamations announced to the people,
Yrchyuul, son of Yraman—
He had Yrchyuul begin them.
When they heard his proclamation,
Willow cudgels were cut,
And every horse racer
Gathered without exception.
Karatai was made the starter;
When the time came to drive out the horses,
Infidel and Muslim
All assembled without exception.
All the heroes grew excited;
The simpletons surged forward.
The people gathered in a dense mass,
Dust flying into the sky;
They divided clan by clan,
And when they advanced, looked like a flood.
