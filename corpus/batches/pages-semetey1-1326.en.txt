Do not show me such a spectacle!
My tiger son, hear my words!
Take able Bakai, who knows the road;
Take the stout-lunged, roaring,
The mighty who do not retreat from death,
Who spare themselves nothing.
Gather seven sacred banners,
Gather an army that makes the earth sag,
Then go to Beijing!
Take an army from the Muslims,
Take no small force, but many,
Then go to Beijing.
Fools merely say “Beijing”;
Those who know call it “Great Beijing”—
Beijing is no small place but vast.
On its front side is Bakburchun,
The mightiest of all is Kakan-Chin.
Most numerous is Shang-Kai, the great Manchu;
Consider how many cities there are.
If you ponder, that Beijing
Also has the aspect of a holy place.
Against great, afflicted Beijing,
Only then begin your conflict!
I do not approve of this departure;
Guard your own life yourself!
My soul mate, heed me,
Take the whole Muslim army!
Raising the Muslim battle cry,
Send the army against this China!
Gathering a great dark host,
If you set out for Kakan-Chin,
Who will be left with this regret?
Turn back, enter, and wage enmity,
Then bring great conflict upon Kakan!
Saying, “I shall gird my waist firmly,”
You have grown impassioned,
Saying, “From the Kalcha in Beijing,
I shall exact my father's blood-price.”
Such is the nature of the journey;
Understand my words without making me lament!
May your journey be in the cool autumn;
May your enemy Kongurbai, whom you will spear,
Stand on a plain as small as a palm.
Of the giant you have longed for,
If I tell his qualities, let them be countless;
When you enter battle lamenting,
May the spirit of your lion father,
Your bloodthirsty hero, stand beside you!
Your father's sworn friend Almambet,
Akbaltai's Chubak,
Syrgak, born a lion—
In the midst of that battle,
May those soul-companions be your aid!
When you advance and extend your spear,
May all the heroes be present!
I am your mother Kanykei;
Receive the counsel of this unfortunate woman,
Take the Muslim army!
Against the seething unbelievers,
Against hero Khan Kongurbai,
Advance thus and begin the conflict!
If you gather them, you have many troops;
Against the seething unbelievers,
You have fine men who can clash.
Let me tell of the hero
Who dwells yonder in Sary-Arka;
No man has defeated him in combat either.
If you ask his father and lineage:
His lattice walls and roof poles are of wood,
His first ancestor was Alach;
Hero Kökchö, son of Aidarkan—
The heel of the blue boot,
Kökchö of the Kazakhs.
Broth rich with meat, fatty flesh,
His warriors are set apart,
Broad-chested and red-faced,
He overwhelmed every enemy he met,
And winnowed their millet clean,
