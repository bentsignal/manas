He has struck without cease
The ninety thousand horses in the mountains;
Your ruinous Kanchoro
Raids your migrating camp from Keng-Köl.
To speak plainly, no male child remains—
How will you take your revenge?”
So Aychürök spoke,
Placing the blame before
Your dear mother Kanykey.
Then your champion mother Kanykey
Spoke softly but firmly,
And began a speech of many kinds:
“Ah, my foal, my child Aychürök,
What you have said is true!
Yesterday, when the forty choros were slain,
When they were killed for what they had done,
They joined together as enemies,
Raised a wicked quarrel,
Drew bows and fought;
When the forty choros became bitter foes,
Kambar took Kanchoro’s mother.
He wholly forgot his father
And became the son
Of that dark-hearted infidel,
The choro Kambar.
Calling him the brave son left by Chubak,
A legacy left by a noble man,
My champion son Semetey
Took him in as a gift.
When he came trotting over the lowland,
When my son brought him to me,
I took him under my protection.
So his heart might grow rich,
I cut him fat from the rump and fed him;
So he would be a brother without suspicion,
I nursed him with milk from my breast.
When Kanchoro drew and sucked
At my left breast,
My entire body trembled,
My fly-sized soul screamed,
And every bone in me ached.
With every suck, my strength departed;
Black blood flowed from my breast,
And strength left wretched me.
I understood, thinking carefully,
That in the end he would become an enemy,
That he would slaughter the people
Without leaving a single one.
I knew he would cast down black night,
Raid the people when he grew,
Grapple with us and plunge us into misery,
Learn our secrets and reach the root;
Kin on the outside, foul within,
He would bring us to this end—
I knew it long ago.
From the very beginning
I knew that deep down he was an enemy;
I always thought he would become our foe.
I tormented my fly-sized soul.
Agybay is here, Bakay Khan is here,
And all the many men
Who have exhausted nations and seen the world;
There are saints and heroes,
Sages whose sight encompasses
A journey of six months coming
And seven months going,
Whose eyes see from afar.
If all the peoples beneath moon and sky raced,
He would emerge alone from the contest.
His speech is renowned:
The old man, father of the people,
Who finds words in sudden peril,
Who finds eyes in the darkest night;
When he leads an army, none can bind him,
And in darkness, if set upon a corsac’s tracks,
He will never lose the trail.
There are elders skilled in hearing and knowing;
All this multitude is here.
