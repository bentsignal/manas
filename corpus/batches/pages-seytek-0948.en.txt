“I shall roast him like wheat;
From this cur of a champion
I shall tear out the great artery of his head.
This wounded conqueror of foes—
I shall kill him and cut off his head,
And pierce his eyes, bright as Venus.
I shall unleash a mighty battle;
This boy is insolent,
So I shall settle his great account.”
He readied himself, gripping his axe,
While the other leveled his spear.
In less time than it takes to speak,
Urging on gray Kulansur,
Seytek reached him.
At the swine who came to face him,
He aimed his grooved steel ornamented spear
And drove it squarely toward the jaw.
Heedless when it struck,
The accursed Sarybay regarded
The thrust Seytek had delivered
As less than a touch upon his hem.
He neither swayed nor flinched;
The wrathful swine did not stir.
He did not die from that single thrust,
Nor did he reckon Seytek’s blow
Worthy even of a cough.
Pouring out his wrath, unconcerned,
Not counting the warrior his equal,
He deemed the fierce battle and great peril
Less than a touch upon his hem.
So Sarybay stood there,
While the noble, impassioned warrior
Gathered all his strength into his body.
Sarybay grew furious;
His menace was terrible, his venom strong—
Such was the villain.
The cunning boar
Was thick-armed and highly skilled,
Perfect in guile and fierce in venom,
Powerfully built and supreme,
One who had leveled the whole world.
At the sight of him, abandon hope;
Such was the accursed man.
All the powers in the world,
Countless conflicts, countless quarrels,
Countless perils, countless dark nights—
To all such things he was accustomed.
Wrathful Sarybay,
The many-muscled swine,
Bit hard upon his tongue
As his blood-drinking nature seized him.
His eyes blazed like a lion’s;
He bit his lips through,
And the swine would not cease until he had grappled.
Facing him in combat,
“I shall axe off his head,” he said,
“And reach the root of all he is.”
He roared menacingly,
Churning the blue heavens;
His beard and mustache bristled strand by strand,
And he thundered like an onrushing flood.
Anyone who watched him closely
Despaired of his borrowed mortal life.
Breaking through hills to make a road,
He attacked Seytek.
His black-jawed leather club
Deafened the ears when it struck.
The hide of a full-grown ox
Had been stretched over its core;
The hides of six wethers
Had been packed and braided around it.
Its grip was glassy,
Its wrist-loop stout as a jar;
Embers seemed set in its palm,
And blue thunderbolts lay within it.
Sarybay was adroit;
Such was his supreme courage.
He swung the leather club in a sweeping arc,
Then spurred the thoroughbred Tookyzyl,
Driving his heels into its flanks.
