Did you take him and let him go,
That mighty man who knew the enemy's devices?
You are a woeful Manas;
You would not heed my words.
When Algara was captured,
I ran especially to you;
When Algara was returned,
My lion, I despaired of you.
Your waist girded for battle,
Valiant gray-maned Manas,
Did you lay claim to Beijing?
You treated the Kalcha like a child;
Did you leave alive, without killing,
The calamity that befell your head?
He will not heed your pleading;
The Kalcha slave will find his chance.
If he does not die, Kalcha will knock
All the wind out of you.
You gave away for nothing, Manas,
A stallion like Algara;
Now, my hero, you have found
A kinsman like Kongurbay.
In the end Kongurbay will bring
The end of days upon your head.
Had you given me Algara,
Would I not have routed
The teeming Chinese on foot,
Slaughtered the Kalcha,
And given my poor head
Rule over the predators?
There are the Black Chinese people;
When enraged, they have a custom
Of drinking human blood.
They have waists girded for battle
And a place where they lie in wait.
They have the wise man from Beijing,
The valiant hero Kalcha.
A slave deft with a spear,
Kongurbay, who has long fought,
Is a mighty slave
Who knows the enemy's devices.
By returning the Kalcha's horse,
You have exhausted my every means."
Before Almambet, who spoke these words,
Had closed his mouth,
Kokotoy's thousand black camels apiece,
The great loads of gold,
The thick-woven piebald cloth of Andijan,
The costly black-patterned silk of Bukhara—
The prize staked for the joust—
The Muslim kinsmen
Now witnessed that spectacle.
According to their clans,
He distributed it among the khans.
Those who won the prize
Were content at heart,
Swaggering in their wealth,
And enjoyed the splendid spectacle.
They measured the jousting prize by the whip
And divided it;
Their strongest men grappled
And fell to whipping one another.
They made the people weary
Of all that wealth.
From the great loaded stores,
They let everyone who reached them
Plunder one kerchief apiece.
They enjoyed a mighty spectacle,
Measured Khan Manas's prize by the whip,
And divided it.
From the gold loaded on pack animals,
They poured heaps
For every kinsman.
The Muslims plundered gold
As their prize.
Let us leave this matter here;
Jamby shooting is another contest,
And let us begin its tale.
