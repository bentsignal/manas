He spilled my blood upon the field,
He crushed my soul,
He tormented me while I yet lived,
Severed my arm at the shoulder,
And made my blood stream like water.
He robbed me of my strength
And poured out my blood like water.
While my ill-fated uncle lived,
Wherever I faced became a crimson fire;
I was a lion surpassing all mortals.
After my hero passed away,
I became no lion, but mere black earth.
Parted from my princely lion,
I became a grave whose crown had caved in.
I fell prey to cruel wrath
And was trampled into the black earth.
I became Kyyaz’s spoil,
A herdsman’s slave.
For twelve years
I tanned raw hides.
I danced like the Kalmyks,
I endured nights of bitter wrath
And somehow lived on without dying.
My forefather was the Chinese Chylaba;
Ask me no more of this humiliation.
With the hero gone from sight,
Both my shoulder blades were gouged.
Your uncle Kül, the lion of the world,
Was flayed by every kind of ordeal.
Everywhere I stepped was a deep pit;
Our great master was Kyyaz.
Condemned and disgraced,
Külchoro, brother of the noble hero,
Your uncle suffered such degradation.
Thinking of the days ahead,
We burn until we lose our senses.
“He has been left an orphan by the noble one,” we say,
And take courage from that thought.
“If he is a lion who takes after his father,
He will wage many battles,” we say.
When they truly provoked our wrath,
We laid Adybay and Kölbay low.
We anointed the blue steel with blood.
When our true fury was aroused,
Those two villains Chyybyl and Kochkor—
We laid both villains low.
When they roused our dreadful fury,
We all broke free of them.
Not six villains, but forty choros—
We cut off their heads with our swords.
We surrounded Koton and took its citadel.
We attacked its principal people,
Besieged them in anguish and took their land,
Then raided Künjüt and conquered its people.
While my sultan uncle lived,
We chased white deer and made them cattle,
And made the whole world submit.
We chased blue deer and made them cattle,
And visibly brought the earth under our rule.
At the word “enemy,” our spirits soared;
We shattered Jet-Kait and ruled it.
We broke through the cliffs and seized the valleys,
Overthrowing and capturing many khans.
Then, grieving through youthful folly,
I lost Taibuurul,
The wing on which I wheeled and flew.
A fire burned from my very core,
And my own kinsman turned stranger.
We had kicked together like foals,
And quarreled while nursing at the same breast.
But Kanchoro the swine rose as my enemy,
Broke both my arms,
And kicked my side out.
Raging worse than any enemy,
My own kin dealt me such torment.
My kinsman became my enemy,
An unshifting mountain within me.
My wrath will never melt away:
That wretched Kanchoro
Is my sworn enemy.
