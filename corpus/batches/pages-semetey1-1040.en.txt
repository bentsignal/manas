To your Kyrgyz village in Talas,
Like men whose days are spent,
What shall we say when we go
To your ass-embracing wife?
Father, silver-eyed and sun-faced,
Thundering elder brother,
Could you not bear it
As a fault upon your neck?
You hunched over and quarreled
With the wife you have yet to take.
You swagger, alas for the day;
Can a husband behave like you?
By the custom of old,
While in her father's place,
A woman holds great power.
You quarreled before taking her—
Hero, how can your star be fortunate?!
Put aside your anger,
Adopt another manner,
Speak words of reconciliation,
Take your wife.
If you quarrel with such swagger,
This conduct of yours is rude.
Oh, my murderous leopard,
Restrain your anger!"
Your great-hearted man, scarcely contained,
Turned aside toward Chürök
And set his Buudan pacing;
Külchoro brought back Chürök,
Daughter of Akun.
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Was a tactful kinsman's son,
A servant beyond reckoning,
His appearance unlike other men's,
A fortunate-born champion.
He said this to Aychürök,
And spoke a scheme no one knew:
"There are gossips and old women here;
All these people are here.
Of the husband she had not yet met,
Will they not say Aychürök,
Daughter of Akun, became a bully?
With the husband she had not yet met,
Quarreling out on the steppe,
Will they not say Aychürök,
In her distress, became loose-tongued?
Begin with gracious conduct,
Put aside this anger,
Go to your gray-maned wolf,
Take him about the neck,
And indulge in a little affection.
Can a woman who quarreled as a maiden
Be like you?
If you keep matching yourself
Against the proud gray wolf, sister-in-law,
Will your affairs ever prosper?
Are there people like you two,
Who quarrel before even seeing each other?!"
Kanchoro and Külchoro
Had remained there together;
Each leading one of the pair,
Kanchoro now escorted
The swaggering prince back,
And Külchoro now escorted
The silent Chürök back.
Clouds drifted together in the sky;
The two wild rams made peace.
They met and clasped hands,
And these two, swaggering,
Kissed each other upon the throat.
They put aside angry words
And began words of play.
Like the Pleiades in the sky,
They were joined together now;
Aychürök and Semetey
Were united now.
