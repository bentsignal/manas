One who can restrain obstinate speech:
Your mother’s own sister,
My unfortunate mother Kanykey.
Let us go to my valiant, ill-fated mother,
To Kanykey,
And place the matter before her.
Let us gather every elder capable of counsel,
Everyone with ears willing to listen,
Leaving no one out.
Let us seek and find a solution,
And reason the matter through.
Without insulting or quarreling,
Let us remain on good terms.”
So Külchoro
Offered words of wisdom.
But Ümötöy grew furious—
Now God had struck the khan senseless.
Like a prince commanding a nation,
Like one about to overturn everything,
He stiffened in anger—
Now God had struck the hero senseless.
The only son of brave Köksö—
Now God had struck the champion senseless.
As though his father Köksö had died
Because magnanimous Manas
Had simply handed him over to the Chinese,
As though he were chasing down
The last straggling foal of the ruined Kazakhs,
The wretch swaggered and smirked,
As though scattering plunder from a saddlebag.
The black slave strutted about,
As though carrying off his rightful share.
The scoundrel was going mad,
As though scattering plunder of silver coins.
Stiff with fury,
Ümötöy no longer behaved like a khan.
Though noble Choro implored him,
He refused and blocked his way.
If left to Ümötöy’s will,
He would reap and gather them
And stuff them into his pocket.
He would grind Talas to dust,
Cut down and topple its trees,
Engulf it utterly with his army,
And push down all its houses.
If the Kyrgyz were left to his will,
He meant to reap and gather them,
Leaving not one of them behind,
And heap them on his threshing floor.
Slaughtering their fleeting lives,
Ümötöy would simply mow them down.
His hair bristling wildly,
He shouted in anger,
Calling his Kazakhs
To thrust their raiding spears.
Looking across Talas,
A crimson haze rose like a mountain;
Ümötöy and his Kazakhs
Had become foes worse than the Chinese.
A black cloud rose like a mountain;
With Ümötöy bent on enmity,
They had become foes worse than the Kalmyks.
If left unhindered,
He seemed ready to slaughter the people as he passed;
If left entirely unchecked,
He seemed ready to destroy the nation and depart.
He seemed ready to seize all their plunder;
Ümötöy was a troublemaker,
Ready to unleash all manner of ruin.
Simply carrying out a massacre,
He seemed ready to take heads with his sword.
Striking down the mighty,
He seemed ready to leave them split beside the road.
Such was this man’s violent purpose;
The Kazakhs of Sary-Arka
Called him the warrior Ümötöy.
Good heavens, he was like a black-headed billy goat,
A slave who only barked and blustered.
