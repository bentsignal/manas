Warrior, what will become of us in the end?
If we choose and mount swift horses,
Fire guns and draw bows,
And thunder forward in pursuit,
What will become of our blood?”
So Külchoro
Recounted the lay of the warrior,
The mighty infidel, Köksö’s son.
There he stood and sang it.
He truthfully told
What he had seen and learned.
He revealed the intentions
Of the Kazakh who had come to meet them.
When Külchoro had spoken thus,
His elder, lion Bakay—
Behold his surpassing wisdom—
Said, “That hero Ümötöy,
Born to Khan Köksö,
Has stirred turmoil across many lands.
You see what sort of man he is:
His banner is motley, his spear brass,
His horse swift and thunderous,
His great lungs pounding,
A sour-buttermilk, woolen-kurut,
Empty-chested braggart.
To that broad-chested Kazakh,
That swaggering scourge,
Make a gift of sixty horses,
With a stallion at their head.
Without dispute or quarrel,
Make true peace with him!
His father Köksö was a hero;
He had long been
A sworn comrade to Manas.
Every intention of his was pure;
The magnanimous Manas and warrior Köksö
Were true brothers and husbands of sisters.
When they rode to besiege the khan,
When they brought slaughter upon the Chinese,
It was Khan Köksö
Who turned his blue-dappled charger’s head.
Unflinching beneath the banner,
He stood firm as a fortress.
Strong in majesty and fierce in wrath,
Köksö, son of Aydarkan,
Was the mainstay of countless souls.
He was a khan who subdued
Chynmachyn and the Khagan’s realm.
When they entered Beijing, their wives were kin;
Köksö and Manas had matching names,
And such was his valor.
Five sets of nine mares,
For God’s sake, for my sake,
And for the sake of the lions Köksö and Manas—
Go and present them as a gift.
Heed the words I have spoken;
Give grim-hearted Ümötöy
Whatever he desires.
Go before him without hostility,
Saying, ‘Come and go freely among us.
True gray-maned warrior,
Recognize our kinship,’
And make him your kinsman.”
After Bakay had spoken these words,
Straining to have her say,
With wisdom inspired from the unseen,
Kanykey’s turn came.
Then Kanykey said:
“My princely sons, listen;
I have a remarkable tale to tell.
If I speak and lament at length,
The words themselves are precious before God.
In Karakhan’s household
There were exactly twelve of us girls.
The eldest of the twelve
Was Akerkech, whom Köksö married;
Burulkan, whom Buudaykan married;
