Saying it was Kökötöy's feast,
The place of the great gathering,
The mighty Khan Kongurbay came;
When his wrath seized him,
He became a deep-dug pit.
Before Khan Kongurbay,
Kökötöy's Bokmurun
Now presented
Sixty camel-loads of gold.
Giving many gifts of gold,
Kökötöy's Bokmurun
Barely pacified the Kalcha.
Eight thousand wethers and five thousand mares
He gave for slaughter;
The wood of eight thousand camels
He gave for burning;
Sugar large as a kneeling camel or cow
He gave them to crack apart.
Thus he won the favor
Of gray-wolf Khan Kongurbay,
Gathered the whole army,
And camped against the foothills.
There was yet another spectacle;
Let us leave this here
And begin with what came nearer.
Behind him,
Dust wheeled into the sky;
The heavens were clear but the earth was veiled,
And haze parted from the sky.
Their multitude seemed like a flood.
With piercing cries and shrill cheering,
When one looked carefully,
Ezkara's army appeared.
Ezkara, khan of the Manchus—
His multitude was like black worms;
If he attacked, he brought much grief,
For he himself was ill-starred, a calamity.
At that time, every mortal
Who saw Ezkara was afraid.
Ill-fated Ezkara
Had studied the wondrous arts.
His knowledge was considerable;
His sorcery was of many kinds,
A famed man who came from Beijing.
A peerless diviner and knowing servant,
He knew Bakburchun beyond,
Ulandy, Kengtung, and Düngshang.
Ezkara, having seen so much,
Knew that people's number,
And knew this people's temper.
He knew the might of that people;
Of this people visibly gathered
For Kökötöy's feast,
He knew the small number.
Ezkara of the Manchus
Was more skillful than any intriguer,
The finest deviser of means
Among black Chinese and Manchus.
He was the orator among the eloquent;
Ezkara, whose desire was fixed,
Was the champion of the many Chinese.
Straining and exerting himself,
The renowned hero Ezkara
Came straight toward this feast,
Swaying as though shoved this way.
He had many contentious words:
At the far edge of Altay,
He had lost plunder worth a thousand camels,
And had a thousand champions
Slaughtered by Manas.
The pale blue star in the sky was eclipsed;
He openly fled and escaped
Before Esenkan.
When he came to Esenkan,
He stamped the ground he trod
And reviled Khan Manas:
