They slit down the middle
And put into their trouser cuffs.
Upon the warrior-khan's treasury,
The Jediger feasted to their fill
And lay there in a frenzy.
They took the most skilled of the women,
Chose and took the maidens from among the brides,
Took up their shameful work,
And loaded gold coins onto the camels.
Kanchoro, who had become ruler—
God had struck down the khan—
The Kyrgyz of Talas—
The Jediger swarmed utterly over them
And subject them to bloody pillage.
They stripped the Kyrgyz bare,
Standing there and plundering them.
They took livestock as spoil,
Entered without restraint and took lives.
They seized the yellow rolling ridges,
And plundered the people till they turned pale.
Of the Kyrgyz left lamenting,
They raided their mares from the hills,
And drove some of them away.
When an owner went to his mares,
They beat his brains out.
The Jediger wrought true slaughter
And thus raised their strife.
The dear people who had lived at peace—
They rummaged even through their bundles.
They dug a well in the place of honor
And trampled them down from above.
They slaughtered the leaders
And left them like an extinguished fire.
Then the Jediger
Were sated without a care
With packhorses and thoroughbreds.
The Jediger of Jeti-Özön
All mounted up in high spirits.
They overran the villages
And slaughtered the wise heroes.
They made the bustling Kyrgyz
Like a fire put out.
The great people fled in panic
And met with ruinous calamity.
The great black host of Jediger
Rose as one with a thunder;
They left none at the root
And made them like a fire put out.
Now they brought another calamity;
They exacted together the vengeance
Not only of Toltoy but of Bagysh.
Not long after that—
A garden of plane trees all around,
A golden throne at its center—
When the mighty warrior died, Külchoro
Could no longer make himself known.
Our fortune had been bound fast;
A deep pit had been dug.
Both hands had been bound,
An iron net spread wide,
And vast misery set boiling.
Fortune fled and our backs were broken;
Courage fled and our spirits broke.
A dark calamity struck us:
Semetey was not dead, yet the people died;
All the great Kyrgyz nation died,
And the ancient joyous way of life died.
The wide world became narrow;
The open plain became a prison.
Our poor heads that never drank kumys
Longed for muddy water;
Our poor heads that never drank spirits
Longed for sour water.
Bereft of the towering lion,
The many Kyrgyz and Argyn in Talas—
The black multitude sobbed and wept,
All the other people wept together,
As they endured this butchery.
