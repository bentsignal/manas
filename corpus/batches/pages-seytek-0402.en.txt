Let it become a deed never forgotten.
Let his world collapse and be leveled;
Let his affairs burn and be set right.
For the many souls who will remain behind,
Let the destroyer of nations perish,
And let the people’s affairs prosper!”
Your beloved mother Kanykey,
Her wrath full against Kanchoro,
Spoke openly of the enemy,
Every word taking a different turn.
If you attend to every word,
Each word of your wise, fierce mother,
If you consider every word,
Was like a thrust of a spear.
Külchoro, son of Külüstan—
Against Külchoro himself
Her aspect was stern, her venom strong,
Able to humble anyone who opposed her;
So Kanykey stood urging them on.
Her grief and anguish broke open,
The turmoil within her burst forth,
Her eyes opened wide as bowls.
Without another word or sound,
Your dear mother fell silent,
And Kanykey abandoned her speech.
Then the noble-born Aichürök—
I, the wretched one, began to speak.
Taken as booty by the enemy,
Her heart scorched because of the foe,
The grief-stricken unfortunate woman
Cast forth her heavy sorrow.
Before the wrathful khan,
Aichürök began to speak:
“Without seeing Judgment Day
Or hell, you have returned, dear brother-in-law;
After spending many years in slavery,
Letting much of your life pass,
And enduring many torments,
From the true world whence none return,
You have returned without going there, dear brother-in-law.
Before all the people can safely depart,
Curse all that you have done!
The camel has fallen from the cliff;
Your deeds are finished, my son!
You brought it to ripeness before it was ripe;
Kanchoro, who deserved to burn when ripe,
You burned before his time.
He burned and blazed into embers;
His head was severed and blood flowed,
And my son vanished from this world.
Heap upon him the hell within your grasp;
Inflict humiliation upon him like a sheep.
Until my children begin to move,
Rip his belly open like a melon.
Cut off his head like a sheep’s;
From the enemy who inflicted this torment,
Turn away only when your eyes close.
Bind both his hands,
Gouge out both his eyes,
Stretch him out and beat him like a hide.
Return his own devilry to him,
Without spilling or omitting any of it!
Pierce his neck while he still lives;
Take a blunt knife that cannot cut felt,
And cut him with that knife.
Flay his skin while he is alive;
Kill him amid torment,
And for seven days keep the wretch wailing—
Neither killing nor releasing him,
Nor bringing his punishment to an end.
Spread the rack wide,
Grieve him and torture his soul fiercely,
And take your vengeance fully.
Ravage the wretch
And stretch him over the bare rock.
Return all his own deeds to him
Without spilling or omitting any.
If his soul is strong enough not to die,
Let the wrathful swine remain alive.
For a mortal bound for Judgment Day—
