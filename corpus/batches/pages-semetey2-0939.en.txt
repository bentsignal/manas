You two lions who have pulled apart!
Like a great river flowing through the valley,
Become a single spring;
Move toward friendship
And remain close companions.
My lion-uncle Kongurbay,
Consent to the words I have spoken,
And grant me the idol’s power.
I made their elders bow in reverence;
I converted them wholly to the faith—
All the Buruts.”
When Külchoro had spoken thus,
Alööke’s Kongurbay said:
“Ah, they call you a heroic warrior,
And what you have said is wise.
As for that great inscribed, hollow-bellied idol—
Oh, my child, take not merely its power but its head!
If it will bring prosperity to that bloodthirsty hero,
Take not merely its power but its head.
To Manas’s only son,
As the foremost of gifts,
Present it freely.
If that idol is too small a gift,
Let him take Ezkara’s great war drum
As a magnificent offering!
Let us unite and meet face-to-face,
Eating meat from a single dish;
Let us abandon our grudges and meet,
Like a pale star in the heavens,
Chest to chest as friends.
Let us begin our roaring conversation,
And, my son, cast our thunderous valor
Into the water and leave it there.
I shall agree to what he says.
For his father, the gray wolf Manas,
I shall pay the blood-price a full thousandfold.
Bring me your prince,
Your renowned bloodthirsty lord.
Lead here the infidel eager for battle—
The blood-drinking, bloodthirsty warrior.
As blood-price for his father Manas,
My gray horses with fetlocked legs—
All are peerless thoroughbreds.
From the unbroken herds
I shall drive out and give droves of every kind,
Without even unrolling
Such a tally, I shall give livestock.
One kind of horse is dun,
With a white blaze like an amulet;
All are peerless thoroughbreds.
I shall give a drove of kulans,
All evenly matched dappled thoroughbreds;
Of these I shall give a drove.
Black-and-white as a starling—
Of those too I shall give a drove.
If he comes within my reach,
I shall present him gifts made by craftsmen;
I shall present him maidens,
Immensely beautiful, with dainty feet.
There is no equal in Great Beijing
To what Prince Kong
Has in his possession.
From the splendid yellow horses
I shall count out and drive a gift,
And from the evenly matched black horses.
I shall send word to the Kakan:
“I shall befriend Semetey
And return safely to Beijing.
I shall agree to what he said
And pay the Magnanimous One’s blood-price.
I shall say, ‘This is how matters stand,’
And depart safely for Beijing.”
So spoke Kongurbai;
Kaldais and sentries closed around him,
And to the wrathful Kalcha
Wisdom came as if from the unseen.
As its heart pounded and it pranced,
His matchless thoroughbred Black Horse—
