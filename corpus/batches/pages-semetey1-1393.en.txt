With their ears pricked upright,
It was a land where wolves roamed;
With their tails trailing behind,
It was a land where foxes roamed.
Crawling over the sands by day,
Its young were as large as yearling colts,
Galloping proudly like young horses,
They played over the open plain!
Its wonders dazzled the eye;
No living man could tire of them.
Its cold allowed no mosquitoes!
In the fiercest forty days of winter,
Kites, falcons, and saker falcons—
These birds made the place their home.
It curved through hillock after hillock;
Deer poured out from the hummocks.
The trail descended toward the saddlebags,
Where the grass on the hummocks grew sparse.
Wild asses snorted on the level ground;
What a wondrous place this Jyldyz was—
Snakes hissed in the gullies!
Beyond Kichi-Jyldyz,
On this side of Chon-Jyldyz,
The projecting headland of Kara-Kyr—
The land Khan Almam made his home,
This is the sacred place!
At Kara-Kyr stand twin tombs,
Called the Chymam tombs;
The place where true elder Ala camped
Was a sacred place.
All around it the air whistled,
With wind blowing in a circle.
It was a homeland
Of the mighty heroes of old.
Its surroundings were secure;
They called it the Chymam tomb.
It had firewood and water;
For the boys left horseless and on foot,
It was a place to remain with the baggage train!
It had a ridge from which to keep watch.
The wary lion Bakai
Had seen it, the mighty one.
When he climbed to the lookout and gazed,
Seeking safe ground,
When he turned his face to the right,
The gray haze divided;
When he looked this way,
A dim desert appeared.
Your falcon, uncle Bakai,
Unwilling to remain in that place,
Then felt his heart torn!
He marked down the day, they say,
And reckoned the count of days.
He marked down the month,
And reckoned the count of months.
Clasping hands and speaking together,
Saying, “Farewell, remain safe!”
They made their promise firm.
His white beard gleaming,
Cackling like a migrating goose,
Speaking softly but firmly,
Their uncle Bakai began:
“Springs flowing through the valley,
My champions, precious as children seen late in life,
Unless what I tell you is false,
Unless my God himself knows otherwise,
That is the place called Chet-Beijing;
Those are the Chinese people with their army.
When their wrath is aroused,
They are a flood that overwhelms!
While my bloodthirsty lord lived,
I am lion Bakai, who fought them
For months and years!
Beyond the gray-hazed mountain neck,
That is the pass beyond Beijing.
With eyes reddened like a raven's,
My pale beard trailing,
Parted from my companions,
