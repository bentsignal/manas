“Let me shed my blood for the people.
For my people and my nation,
Let me spill my precious blood;
Let me grapple with him and see.
Ever since I became Külchoro,
Ever since Külchoro mounted a horse,
I have seen giants of countless kinds
And endured the horrors of many battles,
Yet fate has never brought my death.
Never have I seen so fierce a champion,
So venomously strong as this infidel.
Long ago, God above,
My father Almambet’s pursuit,
His birth from Aruuke,
That poor soul’s tottering steps,
And all the hardships he endured;
How both my mother and father died,
Leaving me an orphan;
How I grew up an orphan,
And somehow joined my people;
How I matured and became a man,
And stood equal with heroes’ sons;
How I climbed awkwardly onto a horse,
And lived among men.
My doom has not come, and I have not died;
In all my life, never have I seen
Such a terrible calamity.
First, God created me;
You gave me life and made me a hero,
Made me a champion throughout the world.
Yet, O one God, you have made what remains of me
Equal to the black earth.
You did not hear my prayer or my words;
He was about to break my back.
He ruined my stronghold
And cast my people into turmoil.
He dug my grave deep
And showed us a marvel.
The end of time has now begun;
Nowhere in the world have I seen
Such anguish and grief—
A calamity fallen upon the people,” he said,
As the nation wailed in sorrow.
“He truly is a hero and a champion;
Sarybay, the ogre’s son,
Who brought the whole world under his sway,
Is the mightiest man on earth.
He tears apart all who encounter him;
He is like a raging boar.
Finding no mountain to strike,
And no enemy to fight,
This brute has come here.
His monstrous cruelty is plain;
The warriors despair of their lives,
And the people stand wailing:
“What inheritance could I gain
By making these people wail?
For this land protected by Kydyr,
I shall attack the raging brute
And go to my sacrifice.
His valor is as vast as a mountain;
Let no one say that Külchoro
Gave his people and nation up as plunder.
Let the khan I grapple with die;
If my own dear eyes close,
Let all the people of this great realm die with me.
Against the infidel hero Sarybay,
Now I shall thrust my spear.
For my poor, beloved people,
I shall sacrifice this life!
While this devoted warrior still stands,
How could I surrender my people?
Sarybay has run wild;
I shall match this fellow’s prancing
And see what comes of it!
If angered, I shall take him;
Or against this emboldened infidel,
I shall die this very moment.
Thus, filled with fury,
I shall grapple with him and see.”
