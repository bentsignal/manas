Elder, here I am if you would spill my blood!”
When his companion said this,
His prince stood and said:
“For a boy like you, small as a crow,
My dear loyal companion,
I would sacrifice my dark life!
His deeds have much left unfinished;
At twelve, a boy is still a child.
He has not yet turned a thoroughbred's head
Or shouted and leveled a spear.
Your words sweeter than sugar—
Almighty God will show them
To your own eyes for certain!
He ordained that I would not be diminished;
The Almighty Himself made me.
I was God's beloved servant;
I was poor Manas's son.
Among horses fit to catch and ride,
There is no mighty mount but Buurul;
Before God, there is no kinsman
For whom I would bow but you!
My bright star in the sky,
My dear Kulchoro,
The precious sable at my collar.
When laid before me, my white road;
My cherished shade,
When following behind me, my countless host.
Seeing you today, my heart is full.
My gift left to me
By my blue-maned foster father—
I have no kinsman but you!
To cast shade as we travel,
My mighty, leafy poplar,
My great support, vast as a mountain,
My great hammer made of gold!
My irrigation channel bearing a river,
The one beneath whose protection I sit,
Kulchoro, my light.
Foot-mist rising from a hollow—
Toltoy, who took the spear wound,
My dear Kulchoro,
Will not escape you tomorrow!
If you did not spear him today,
You will spear him tomorrow.
If you did not bring him hell today,
You will bring him hell tomorrow!”
Comforting Choro with these words,
To the treasured camp,
To the delight of the people,
He led the boy inside,
Into Aychürök's palace.
Aychürök's white palace—
When they reached the white palace,
For a boy such as he,
They had nine black calves
Cut down,
And offered them in sacrifice.
Saying Kulchoro had returned,
With a celebration no one could devise,
All Khan Akun's people—
Those who slaughtered mares slaughtered mares;
Those who slaughtered camels slaughtered camels.
They held a great public feast.
For Khan Akun's daughter,
They had the treasury opened,
And countless red-gold coins
Scattered in largesse.
Every widow who reached them received some;
Every orphan and young child
Received red-gold treasure.
They sold it to shopkeepers,
And the walnuts they bought,
The orphan children then
Threw about the street.
They pledged a sheep to Koroson;
