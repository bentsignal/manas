With his hair bristling wildly,
With his mane like a famished boar’s,
Thundering like hell itself,
Making mountains crumble beneath his surging force,
The infidel began his roaring,
And the deep black earth below
Seemed ready to break apart.
When he raged in fury,
His pine-like spear
And the roar he gave
Seemed his only stratagem.
Such was Sarybai’s nature:
Where a torrent ran, no water could pass;
Where flame swept, no ember could pass;
When a musket fired, no bullet could pierce him.
An end-time terror, mighty in venom,
His fury swelled whenever he roared;
Like a fire-engulfed flood,
He advanced alone,
A sovereign power of might,
For life is fashioned in every kind.
Whoever faced him with leveled spear,
Stripped five times over by his dread renown,
Turned back without confronting him.
Not only speaking men, but beasts
Panicked beneath his gaze.
Before the infidel’s dread presence,
Not even five thousand troops could stand;
Against the infidel’s terrible force,
No age of men could endure.
No man could answer the infidel
And meet him in combat;
No man could harry him with strife,
Draw near, and overcome him.
No brave man could withstand
His formidable courage.
Though all the peoples of the world surrounded him,
No man could defeat the villain.
Of towering courage and stony heart,
Great indeed was Sarybai;
Thus he raised a mighty tumult.
What regret is left to those who saw?
A warrior indeed was Sarybai;
Thus he raised a mighty conflict.
His fame brought devastation;
He raged and wrought havoc,
And the dog’s violence only grew.
When he strode onto the field and roared,
The forest fell and the willows shattered,
Mountains broke and eyes flashed;
No man could advance against him,
Courage fled and eyes stared wide.
Sarybai stood there raging,
Armed with all his weapons,
Upon the broad plain of Kosh-Töbö.
The people clashed amid a great outcry
Upon the field of battle;
Those who boasted of strength and valor
Lost heart and bowed,
While my champions and lions
Despaired of their wretched lives.
“Such is Sarybai;
He has waged the battle relentlessly.
Whenever the madman moves,
He slaughters multitudes of us.
Against this infidel Sarybai,
Can any man’s strength prevail?
Can even a lion’s fang pierce him?
For us to regain peace,
When will this swine ever die?
He has emptied the land and slaughtered the people;
On what blessed day will dawn break,
And when will God deliver us?”
Pining away in anguish,
The people despaired of life;
The savagery of the boar
Who devastated the nation was revealed.
Wildly exulting, he roared,
“Who will come out to single combat?”
And called forth in fury.
