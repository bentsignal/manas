Toward devil-ridden Kozho,
He now stood surging,
And spoke to Tümönbay:
"You are a hero, Tümönbay;
Your heroism has become clear.
Like a wolf among sheep,
Your ferocity has become clear.
Among your many devils,
Your greatness has become clear.
Like iron placed in fire,
Your hardness has become clear.
You have surged too far, Tümönbay;
Have you brought on my rage?
Accursed Afghan,
Shall I cut one of you down!"
Chynkozho stood surging,
And put on armor no bullet pierced;
If you had seen Chynkozho,
He strained like a blue bull.
He made ready to take up
His golden-edged battle-axe;
He roared in anger,
Shouting the cry, "Alaman!"
Tölök struggled there,
Quarreling with Chynkozho:
"Fool struck from above!"
When pure God was about to grant
The rattling Afghans to you,
When the Afghans, drawn by the spectacle,
Were now about to come before you,
Why did God strike you
So you reel in frenzy?
If you speak evil words again,
Will a living soul not move in anger?
After Aychürök is given in tribute,
Will poor Tümönbay not burn with shame?
When matters were about to enter agreement,
When they were about to make an offering before you,
When they were about to give their daughter,
You prove a foolish man;
Why did God strike you?"
Speaking such words,
He spoke many words that humbled
Kozho, son of Shygay.
Raging and making him strain,
What could dear Tölök do?
He held Kozho, son of Shygay,
And kept him struggling.
Clinging without releasing him,
Grappling with Tümönbay,
They stopped between the sides,
And bristled at one another.
Stopping in the space between,
They cursed one another aloud;
Unable to reach each other,
They fought with their mouths.
Displaying his superiority,
Tölök brought Kozho, son of Shygay,
To heed his words.
Displaying his eloquence,
Azhybay barely brought
Tümönbay to heed his words.
Deceiving him all the while, Azhybay
Had Tümönbay mounted
On his black-blazed thoroughbred.
Tümönbay came to a halt.
These two among the Zhediger
Made an entertaining tale of it;
Tümönbay, son of Tülkü,
Was left with a bad name.
Trampling the ground he walked,
They spoke ill of Tümönbay.
Azhybay, son of Azhy,
Was a swift-tongued eloquent servant,
A son who found sound arguments;
When he acted with wisdom,
For the entire world
He was a servant beyond price.
