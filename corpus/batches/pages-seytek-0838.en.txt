Among that hollow-chested horde,
In the absence of the daughter of Kaiypchal,
They took the lead in the battle.
Külchoro and brave Seytek,
Together with lionhearted Semetey,
Fought until the grass ripened and swayed,
Until the meadow flowers scattered;
Until every ravine and gorge
Was filled with bristling white snow;
Until the wind whistled down every valley
And drove the dust before it;
Until the birches shed their ashen leaves,
And the rowans lost their buds;
Until the flowering trees withered,
Autumn waned and winter came,
The feather grass dried and reeds grew old,
Until seven full months had passed.
No single warrior remained without a wounded horse,
No brow remained unbroken,
No forehead remained unscarred.
They stood face-to-face,
With the lionlike champion,
With the warrior named Semetey.
Divine Shay upheld Külchoro,
And the dashing warrior Seytek;
For seven full months they fought.
Banners fell and khans died;
Every soul who clashed in battle died.
Through seventy days of fighting,
The seasoned gray-wolf warrior,
Wounded in seven places,
Stooped with his back bent,
His eyes bulging and blazing,
Gathered all his remaining strength.
Though they fought, they could not prevail;
They could not defeat that mighty brute.
All the people were slaughtered.
Külchoro mustered his courage,
Semetey joined the clash,
And hero Seytek shattered the ranks.
Champion of every living soul,
Wisdom of all who lived,
Strongman of all mankind,
The morning star of Bagdagül.
Many heroes fell to bullets;
Such was the conflict and turmoil.
When they clashed, none could prevail against them—
Descendants of the honored lion,
Offshoots of pure steel,
Who destroyed all they encountered,
Descendants of the cherished hero.
Their first forefather was Tügöl Khan;
That gray wolf of ancient roots
Had made eighty-four charges,
And every charge he made was fire.
Enemies who came against him met destruction;
He was a born lion.
He was the fortress of the white-capped Kyrgyz;
Never, it was said, would another such soul be born.
Just behold how the son
Surpassed his father!
No mortal had ever faced him;
No enemy, however fiercely resolved,
Had defeated him in battle.
Descendant of the commanding lion,
He would not yield even to ten men.
God had prospered his deeds.
He was nothing like Sarybai,
Who ravaged nations and slaughtered peoples.
His arms filled with strength;
Those he grappled fell and lay level with the earth.
Struck by an unseen power,
The cherished warrior gained strength,
And all the men who grappled with him
He scattered, spilling their blood.
He had grown fierce from destroying foes;
The son, surpassing his father,
Came to be called a lion.
He fought with Sarybai,
Standing against him on the battlefield.
