I shall now show my weight;
My name is Manas, the champion.
Grasping the black-jawed whip
And swinging it around,
He struck a hero like Ezkara
Across the crown of his head.
His fur cap flew whistling;
The jeweled plume scattered.
Unable to withstand his wrath,
Six thousand Chinese and a thousand kalday
Fled away with a roar.
The handful of Muslims
Prayed, "May God preserve Manas!"
Crying bitterly.
Unable to withstand his courage,
The Manchu people cried out.
The courageous hero Manas
Then fell upon the Kalmyks
And brought the end-time upon their heads.
Whipping them in fury,
Hero Manas drove them together.
Making blows crack,
The black Kalmyks and Manchus—
Hero Manas began beating them.
When Manas raised his hand,
Everyone raised a hand;
The tiger, elder Bakay,
Raised his aged hand.
Dust flew and haze descended;
Turmoil arose.
Khan Manas's forty companions
Clashed and fought.
There was Almambet of Azizkan,
And Chubak, son of Akbaltay;
A champion equal to Manas,
Syrgak, son of Ulak Khan,
Moved among them sowing turmoil.
Throwing all into confusion,
Your lions then
Brought battle upon the Chinese.
Good heavens, hero Manas
Carved through and beat their center;
All around them, hero Bakay
Slaughtered and beat them.
Their two flanks, hero Almang
Drove and scattered;
All around them, hero Chubak
Drove them evenly.
The seething Chinese—
Tiger Manas shouted
And beat them with fierce delight.
At the edges, the forty companions
Clashed and fought.
When one looked, the eye caught nothing;
Among the seething multitude,
None could tell which was which.
Dust covered them and clouds flew,
The dust wheeling into the sky;
The might of the black Chinese
Was now restrained.
There the tiger, bloodthirsty Manas,
Unleashed havoc!
May God not turn his face against us;
When we said we would beat and drive them,
He slaughtered and drove them away.
Some black Kalmyks
Ran fleeing in disorder,
Hiding among their firewood.
Saying they had brought excessive trouble
Upon the unyielding Burut,
Many bitterly regretted it.
Heads split and eyes came out;
Many roared in pain.
Unable to find their bearings,
Many rushed about in panic,
Sheltering beside their own horses;
Many rushed about in panic.
