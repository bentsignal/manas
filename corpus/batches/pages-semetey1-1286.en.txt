Scowling, he girded his waist;
From Beijing beyond Solomon's reach,
He exacted judgment and took the throne.
Upon Beijing beyond the reach of Noah's flood,
He unleashed turmoil and took its blood.
From Beijing beyond Alexander's reach,
His need arose and he took its craftsmen.
He took beautiful maidens as plunder.
His valor became known among the people;
He was called bloodthirsty Manas
And inscribed in the register
Of Kakan-Chyn's Beijing.
Semetey, left as a gift
By the valiant gray wolf Manas,
Rode out facing battle
And stood amid calamity.
His father had left him as a gift;
Khan Akun was his father-in-law,
And Aychürök the wife he would take.
The hero supported by the spirits—
Saying they would seize his wife,
The empty-heads whose doom had come,
Chynkojo and Toltoy, had brought disgrace.
Argyn and Kyrgyz were kin;
Chynkojo and Semetey
Were born from equal branches.
He was fire sprung from the core;
Khoja himself was doomed to die.
Of separate minds but sharing life,
With the foolish dog Khoja,
They tended their mingled livestock together.
Their battle cry and encouragement were one;
Were it not for his foolishness,
Their wishes and sacrificial rites were one.
They gathered the treasury together,
Children of one father,
Seeds of one grain,
Descendants of one man.
His own kinsman became his enemy;
Within hero Semetey
Walked a hostile foe.
The mount beneath him
Was a charger fit for six months' riding;
The hero he would fight
Was his foolish kinsman Khoja,
Who had escaped from the village.
A mighty tumult beyond a spear's reach—
That was Chynkojo's doing.
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
The khoja whose deeds were bold.
Khoja's fortune was complete;
Were it not for his devilry,
He was a hero supported by the spirits.
He had a black hat large as a cauldron;
Dear Khoja was a mighty one.
Across the whole world,
Whatever he wished, he had power to do.
His courage was precious, his venom strong;
Lion Khoja was a mighty one.
Beneath him he had thoroughbred stock;
Across the whole world,
Dear lion Khoja
Had strength to strike it down like grain.
Had he not traveled with Manas
And grown beside him?
Had he not seen
Calamitous great tumults?
Was he not wise among spearmen,
A resourceful khoja?
Did he not know the enemy's stratagems well,
A mighty khoja?
In his youth, among good men,
Had Khoja not grown?
From lion, gray wolf Manas,
Receiving so much instruction,
Had Khoja not become seasoned?
Armor no bullet could pierce—
Had Khoja not worn it beneath his clothes?
