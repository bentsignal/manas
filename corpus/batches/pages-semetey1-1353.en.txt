To raid every one of the countless livestock
Kongurbay gathered as tribute,
Leaving none behind,
The prince stood prepared.
His butcher uncle Bakay
Put on a blue lynx-fur cap,
Mounted a horse like a blue courser,
And on the plain of Üch-Koshoy,
Your elder, old Bakay,
Beside broad Talas,
When the predawn hour sounded
And dawn grew bright,
His saintly uncle Bakay
Woke with a start from sleep,
Turned onto his right side,
Dipped his hands in water,
Performed his ablutions,
Prayed to the one God,
Quickly recited the namaz,
And saddled the blue courser.
He girded on his curved sword,
Paused deep in thought,
And remembered his former concern:
To come and take counsel
With the young son of Oyrong.
Straining like a lion,
Your lion uncle Bakay
Received wisdom from the unseen;
Bakay made ready and reached
The house of Jetigen.
Bakay brought
All the weapons,
And Bakay entered
Semetey's palace.
The old man faced a hard journey;
While alive his eyes were open;
Death is shared, and speech is plain.
He found words in sudden need
And found sight in darkness.
When he spoke harshly, none recognized him;
In moonless darkness,
He never lost the corsac's trail.
While exhausted Manas lived,
When armies moved glittering by tens of thousands,
Marching in their full multitude,
Lion butcher Bakay
Was leader of the moving host.
The old man who bore the banner
Was the saint who served
As butcher Oyrong Manas's standard.
Hearing that the hero's brave son
Would gird his waist firmly
And pursue the hero's blood price
From the Kalmyks in Beijing,
Poor Khan Bakay, your uncle,
Put on his battle clothes.
He hung the golden-jawed battle-axe
From his wrist,
Put on his warrior's garments,
And placed upon his head
A helmet of blue steel.
As the sun began to shimmer upward,
On the plain of broad Talas,
Facing Üch-Koshoy,
At the edge where the Talas flows,
Below Tört-Kül,
In the celebrated garden
First planted by Manas,
At the black eye of the spring,
Beside the tall plane tree,
Within the gated fortress,
At Kanykey's palace,
Were five guest palaces,
Built exceedingly strong;
Its guards were beyond number,
And still more were its sentries.
When an enemy appeared,
They closed it in an instant,
