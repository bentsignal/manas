His body turned cold and trembled,
And his wretched life, merely held in trust,
Started from his crown and shrieked.
Finding no place to make a stand,
He despaired of his fly-like life;
Not a trace of life remained in him.
His voice deserted his throat,
His last owl-like feather fell,
And he stood disheveled in anguish,
Thus despairing of life.
His whole body shuddered
When he saw Külchoro and Kanykey
Together with Aichürök;
The wretch found no recourse,
And his tiny soul screamed.
He flew from the throne
And crashed upon the ground.
His head struck the earth,
And split open
A full span and two fingers wide.
The oath-breaker was struck by his oath,
The accursed was struck by a demon,
Struck by the dust of the white horses,
Struck by the glory of his father Manas,
Struck by the strength of Semetey,
Struck by Kanykey’s milk.
The warrior lay there, struck by his oath.
The accursed was struck by a demon,
Struck by the bloom of the white fruit,
Struck by the days of kumis and nights of maidens,
Struck by the mighty age of the Kyrgyz.
As Kanchoro stood there thus,
Kanykey and Aichürök
Rose together in equal fury
And addressed Kanchoro.
Speaking softly but striking hard,
Kanykey entered and began:
“Yesterday, when my son Semetey
Was left fatherless,
His mother carried him away in flight.
Deprived of his champions,
That son of a bitch, that swine,
That hero who troubled the world,
That infidel who spread seductive turmoil,
Was deserted by the retinue at his side
And left sitting thus.
His guards fled and his strength departed,
The watchmen beside him were gone;
He had no champions and no khan.
All the fierce men who would swarm
At a single command were gone.
However loudly he shouted and cried,
He had neither support nor strength,
No stronghold from which to shoot,
No khan to serve as protector,
And none of the courageous heroes.
He burned fiercely in grief,
And was parted from the wife
Who had been his protector,
Leaving him alone in the horde.
The two women and Külchoro
Prepared to punish the villain who ruined the people,
The wild-headed instigator of strife,
The great lord known throughout the age,
The fiercely wrathful destroyer,
The slayer of so many heroes—
They would make the staggering lord,
The great Kanchoro,
Pay the penalty.
Kanykey and Chürök burned with grief;
Wretched Kanchoro
Despaired of his precious life.
Courage left his heart,
Strength left his wrists,
Tears fell from his lashes;
Wayward Kanchoro
Lost all his power.
His heart burning and his insides boiling,
Like a wolf caught in their hands,
He stared wildly, his two eyes glaring.
