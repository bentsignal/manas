You steeped its writing in blood,
Why would my heart not fear?
The garment that came into your hand,
I struck down the fierce ones you saw,
For God and for the people,
I will bind your head to misery.
I am not dead; I am alive,
If hostile death reaches me, let me die.
The khan’s command is precious,
Let me tell the truth.
Of the brave white-kalpak Kyrgyz people,
Let me tell their secret.
If this head bows, will it not die?
Can warmed-over humiliation protect anyone?
For its elders to remain whole,
How much hardship falls to the hero,
Before the black eye of the scourge,
To the hell created for him,
Speaking my verse with a cry,
Telling my truth as faithfully as belief,
Of the brave white-kalpak Kyrgyz people,
Who destroyed six generations of this one’s fathers,
From this father came the lionhearted people’s spirit,
Let me tell their secret.
For true speech and for the secret,
For the quarrel between the two peoples,
Let me die at the hands of the wrathful one.
Speaking heatedly, Zhelzhetpes,
The accomplished Kyrgyz spoke out.
Below lay the people within China,
He was one of the true brave men,
Of all things and of unity,
Waging war with his weapons,
Tormenting with death,
The bad and the good,
Of heat and cold.
He was a man who understood what was plain to see.
A great people guarded by a mighty fortress,
Clear-eyed in this world,
Even if he died, his eyes would remain open,
This Zhelzhetpes was a heroic warrior,
They would call him a true saint.
He sharpened his eloquent tongue,
He paused and sharpened it once again,
Then Zhelzhetpes spoke out.
At thirty, he himself was an eloquent hero,
The Kenens who guarded every land,
A sage eloquent in speech,
Coming out ahead in the contest,
The land of China is no lesser,
Among the people there is an impartial judge,
Who addressed the forty tribes,
From the common Kyrgyz people,
His words and his tongue were golden,
Do not rage or pour out your wrath,
On this side lies their blue-gray expanse,
They possess freedom like a towering blue mountain,
Among the Kyrgyz there is Khan Kenen.
