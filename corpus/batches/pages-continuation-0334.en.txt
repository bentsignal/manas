The vast herds, the people, and all their wealth
Lay helpless, finding no way out.
“My son, what can this mean?
When brave Kogoi dreamed at night,
Fire swept in from the sunrise,
Engulfing not only the Kyrgyz age,
Not merely an age, but multitudes,
All eighteen thousand worlds,
Burning everything in its path.
Though he took a coarse robe in hand
And beat at it for many days,
It did not die down or turn to ash.
The blaze burned Ebegei and Kogoi,
Brave Kenen who was their refuge,
The hero, khan, and sultan—
It burned them all;
Its red embers reached the sky!
It had taken Kertulpar and Kermekash
As spoils of war.
What can this mean?
Listen, my twin lions,
And heed these words of mine!
This blessed Ala-Too
Is no mountain destined to become ash;
This dark dream cannot bode well!
For the Ala-Too to suffer so,
For your uncle to fall into torment,
It means this poor head has shouldered
The calamity of the world!
Go home, my two lions.
If hostile death comes, we shall die;
Whatever joy or torment is written
Upon our brows, we shall see.”
After Ebegei and Kogoi said this,
They somehow sent
Their twin lion sons on their way.
That night the two heroes lay down,
Resting their heads upon their elbows,
Taking a brief and peaceful sleep.
Their outstretched legs would not draw in,
Their closed eyes would not open;
They sank into a deep sleep,
Knowing nothing of the signs
That came from this world.
Sunlight encircled the mountain,
And a cuckoo called from the plane tree.
When that hour arrived,
They awoke with a start,
Saddled their pair of coursers,
Readied all their arms,
Alarmed as though an enemy had come,
Leapt into their saddles,
And rode patrol, surveying the land,
Until they halted on a rise.
Beyond the gray mountain spur,
This side of the gray headland,
Past the swelling mountain ridge,
In haze beyond the reach of sight,
From the nose of the gray hill
An immense cloud of dust surged up.
His ribs trembling,
Wondering what strange disaster this might be,
Brave Kogoi stood and said,
Beginning these words to Ebegei:
“Ebegei, my twin,
My equal, a pillar of the world,
My companion in dust when we scout,
My companion through life,
My sheltered vale rich in livestock,
My keen-bladed steel
That splits a rock into forty pieces—
Did you see what I saw?
Did you sense what I sensed?
The drifting dust divides;
It seems an envoy is approaching,
A mediator among the people.
If he is a brave man and an envoy,
Let us make him a sworn friend
And draw out his secret.
If he is an enemy, a foe,
Let us lie down with his head for a pillow.
Let us not reveal the lower road,
Nor let the Kyrgyz, blessed by Kydyr,
Yield even a sliver of their secret.
Let us face his wind head-on
And learn how much cunning he has.”
So the two lions,
Grinding their teeth, unable to stay still,
Girded themselves, trusting in fate,
And stood watching carefully.
Riding a strangely fashioned mount,
The coarse tail of the mule beneath him
Knotted with a sharp twist,
Facing dust with dust,
Gauging their strength and watching their actions,
Zhelzhetpes came bearing news.
