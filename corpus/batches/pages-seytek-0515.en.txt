He appeared wherever he could be seen,
And in unseen ravines
He streamed like blue smoke.
His eyes shone red,
His stride lengthened like an argali’s.
When he came to a bend,
The creature proved a true courser,
His eyes reddening like a stag’s.
When he came to winding ground,
He stretched his stride and raised tumult,
His fleet pace growing longer.
His hooves thundered on the earth;
The black-steel bit
Clattered in his throat.
He flung his forelock to the sky,
And drove his hooves into the ground.
The black-steel bit
Beat against his gullet;
His willow-silk tail
Lashed his spreading legs.
He laid back his ears like a kulan
And swept the earth like a whirlwind.
At the sound of clamor he surged,
And when he ran, the black earth
Split layer upon layer—
Such was the nature of that tulpar.
Spear in hand, breaking formations,
Külchoro hastened onward.
He rode across the rise called Horse-Cannot-Pass,
Pressing steadily forward.
Through the open desert of People-Cannot-Cross,
He went to settle the matter.
Toward the infidel, the monster’s son,
He went to fight to the death.
At the Sheer Cliff of Soul-Cannot-Pass,
He went to carve a road.
Against the raging infidel,
Against brave Sarybai,
He went to launch his assault.
Against the master of land and water,
The rabid, frenzied Sarybai,
He went to capture him if his strength sufficed,
To unleash terrible slaughter,
To throw all into turmoil,
And search every pouch
Of that champion-posing swine.
He went to cast hell upon his ranks,
To charge in and fight without stopping,
And thrust that infidel Sarybai
Headlong to the ground.
Thus matters came to pass,
And Külchoro resolved
To meet him face-to-face.
They would clash and test their strength;
If fortune favored the valiant man,
He would spill Sarybai’s blood,
And if not, he would die.
Gripping his spear, steeling himself,
He bit hard upon his lip.
He scowled in fury,
And buckled on his gray girdle.
He arranged and fastened his weapons;
When his rage made him bristle,
Wrath transformed his face.
He surveyed the broad plain,
And braced his spirit, saying, “I trust in fate.”
He seized his black-handled whip,
Swung it around,
And Surkaiyk, a steed like none but a spirit—
Support of his life held in trust,
Pearl among all flowing-tailed horses,
Foal of the spirit-gray mare,
Duldul among the horses of Karanda—
That dark-bay Surkaiyk,
He circled the whip over him
And struck it against his flank.
He made the rugged ground quake,
Galloping onward upon Surkaiyk.
Külchoro rode out to single combat
Upon the fiery Surkaiyk.
