If seven hundred thousand black-clad troops came,
He could plunge among them alone.
If an enemy came with spear made ready,
He could enter and range among them alone;
Before anyone could utter a word,
He seemed able to strike the whole world.
The gold-edged battle-axe
Seemed ready now to enter the foe,
Hanging at his wrist.
Zulpukor, inherited from his father Manas,
Was fastened upon his forearm.
The coat of mail made by Kanykei
Was fitted firmly upon his chest.
Akkelte, inherited from Manas,
Hung across his broad back.
Akolpok, inherited from Manas,
Was worn by Kulchoro.
When he drew near the maidens,
Kulchoro was like a vast sea;
At the sight of him, minds were overwhelmed.
He appeared like a prince,
Glancing to either side;
Your gray-maned one, like a hungry tiger,
Licked his lips.
He wore a golden belt;
Sunlight struck the moonlike face
Of that surpassingly handsome young man.
At the sight of him, minds were overwhelmed.
His figure and his majesty,
His bearing mounted on the horse,
Appeared like Ala-Too.
The maidens who saw him
Lost all command of their senses.
When they saw the young man's form,
He seemed capable of carrying them all away,
Of taking them together to his home.
He came ambling in a stately line
Toward Aichurok's white pavilion;
He went and took shelter there.
There they whispered together,
Teasing one another and laughing;
Some fled at a run,
Despairing of their precious lives.
They stood as though harboring guilty thoughts,
Their mouths muttering;
They looked at one another,
Moved their lips without being heard,
And whispered among themselves:
"Will he fondle us after manhandling us?
Will he take us all together to his home?
Will the offspring of that coarse khan
Seize us as plunder and carry us
Together to Talas?
May God strike the brute!
Will he make us captive wives?"
Thinking this, the maidens and brides
Cried loudly enough for God to hear;
They pressed close against one another,
And, as though slaughter had struck them,
Collapsed and fell together.
Kulchoro was twelve years old.
Upon the red horse Buurul,
The rascal came moving sleekly,
Calm and ringing with vigor.
Thirty brides and thirty maidens,
All those souls swarming there,
He did not value as much as his boot-tip.
Though still a boy, the courage
That Almighty Allah had given him
Was beyond all measure.
Upon the red horse Buurul,
He rode at an angle,
Throwing the maidens and brides into panic.
To test and learn his courage,
Beside a steep ravine with a flowing spring,
A wretched woman, burning with passion, said:
"If he has courage, he will ride on
And trample straight across," she said,
