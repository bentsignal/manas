The people heeded Semetey’s words;
The countless Kyrgyz and many Argyn
Grieved with him, crying aloud.
Khan Bakai raised his hands,
While elsewhere men sobbed and wailed.
The multitude of Kyrgyz wept,
And all the khan’s giants wept.
“Whenever battle calls, this lone hero
Enters against whatever foe he chooses,
With no one at all to support him.
Whenever the hour comes, this gray wolf
Goes in all alone.
If my destined end comes, I shall die;
But for this dear Choro of mine,
Someday, at some hour,
What can I, poor father’s only son,
Possibly do?”
The fierce lion grieved,
Then angrily set about his task,
His heart aflame with embers.
The splendid, noble hero
Would slaughter the Kaldai and settle the blood-price;
Wondering what would become of his Choro,
The valiant man’s heart smoked with anguish.
Father, noble lion, proud as a cock,
Your warrior stands close to greatness;
He is truly a gray-wolf hero.
Your lion-born hero by descent,
Your lion Choro, Almambet’s son,
Your openhearted warrior Choro,
Slung on his jezail,
And, his heart burning, the noble hero
Pleaded with his uncle:
“Do not grieve every day
Over your doomed son, my khan and uncle.
Between infidel and Muslim,
Such is the way of holy war;
Who could be pleased by it?
Father, do not keep grieving, khan and uncle,
Over your luckless servant.
If I remain alive and do not die,
I am a son worth a sheepskin;
If I fall and lie stretched dead,
I am a slave-son worth a dogskin.
You have firmly girded your waist,
But why should you grieve so bitterly,
As though this valiant man were born beside you,
Or sprang from the very loins
Of moonlike Manas,
Or tore his way from the womb
Of my dark-faced mother Kanykei?
Why stand there mourning?
My sorrowing warrior-uncle,
Do not grieve and torment yourself:
Where was I ever Manas’s son?
Warrior, silver-eyed and sun-faced,
My thundering lion of an uncle,
Who brought the whole world under his sway,
Father, my beloved heroic uncle,
Kongurbay is the one I truly contend with.
I have thoroughly deceived the swine;
If the chance comes, I shall cut him down.
Ezkara is the one I stalk;
If the Kalmyk gives me an opening,
I shall overturn and fling him aside.
I shall beat the calculated fury from him.
Kongurbay is the enemy I stalk;
I have deceived your dog
And shall somehow find a way.
Father, my beloved uncle,
If my appointed death comes, I shall die.
If death has not come and my days are not done,
If my skill suffices, I shall make
A wondrous spectacle of the Kalcha.
You were both guileless and lionhearted;
You had courage but no caution,
A fierce hero who would heed no counsel.
Understand what I tell you;
