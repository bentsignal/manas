“Charge, Seytek, charge!”
The guardian saints who took him by the hand
Clamored from every side
And leveled their spears in line.
Companions appeared from the unseen world
And served as protectors
To the noble lion’s son.
A lion strode before him
As though ready to rend its prey,
Prancing in terrible majesty.
One of the holy guardians, a gray tiger,
Crawled ahead of him.
A mighty-limbed leopard,
A black-mottled prince,
Bore the terror of a thousand men.
Striding fiercely at his flank,
He kept ill-fated Seytek
Within the line and guided him forward.
Another, clad in iron armor and shield,
Led him by the hand:
“If you act, act like this;
If you slaughter the foe, slaughter them so.”
Another surged ahead,
Calling, “Seize that enemy!”
On every side rose a mighty din,
As his supporters spoke.
Where the hero above them
Heaved forward in a wave,
No mountain could have withstood him;
If he poured out his rage in one place,
No Chinese force could match him.
Long-bodied and broad-shouldered,
That noble, strong-boned lion,
Who made every confronting foe submit,
That noble son of a valiant hero,
Was like a towering plane tree in a tangled forest,
Clearing a road as he advanced.
Ninety-four armed enemies stood together,
Led by the fortunate Chongbilgich.
He attacked Chongbilgich,
Throwing all ninety-four into confusion.
He urged on the gray horse, huge as a mountain,
Making it stride proudly
And gallop with bounding steps.
Kulansur loomed like a mountain
As the champion waging holy war
Plunged once more into the fray.
He struck Kulansur once upon the thigh
And cried aloud, “Akjoltoy!”
His extended spear was a burning ember;
Fury boiled and his soul blazed,
And he valued his life no more than a hair.
Does not a hero die for honor?
Does not this borrowed, fleeting soul behold
What Almighty God has ordained?
If He demands this life,
Must it not be given, whenever the hour?
When fate claims this head,
Where is a night like the sacred Night of Power?
Against the infidels who came by choice,
Against the warriors who fixed their hatred on me,
Let me test myself in battle.
Let a man be seen by his enemy;
For the land where his cord was cut,
For the people who openly sustained him,
Let the champion’s blood be spilled.
Better than every worldly blessing,
Best and noblest of all,
Is for a hero to die by an enemy’s hand.
Shall they plunder and scatter
The wealth of the teeming people,
And let the enemy trample the land
Where our cords were cut and our swaddling washed?
Rather than lose our champions and break our strength,
Rather than let the people be pillaged,
Let me face the frenzied foe.
To my doomed elder Joltoy,
To the sheltering figure who protected us—
When I see him, my heart is shocked,
Yet at the sight of him my heart finds peace.
