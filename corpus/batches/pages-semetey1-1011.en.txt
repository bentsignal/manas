Let him turn back
From this flowing river."
Pondering every kind of scheme,
To test his heroism
And take her vengeance
Still unspent upon Chynkojo and Toltoy,
She read words in Arabic
And cast a spell upon the weather.
All the glaciers broke apart,
And springs overflowed and streamed;
Hail fell across all the uplands,
And rain across the riverlands;
Every slope ran red,
And pale water flowing from the heights
Joined from every direction.
It flowed black and filthy,
Filling the gorge as one body.
Because of Churok,
It ran muddy and stinking of dung,
Entering a flood no one could withstand.
Those who saw it invoked God;
Its foam heaved and divided,
And to the companion watching
It appeared like a devouring beast.
Kulchoro, who had never seen such water,
Grieved at the sight of it.
When he saw Great Urgench,
The poor hero stood in terror,
Despairing of his borrowed life:
"Does one who crosses this Urgench
Not die in terrible distress,
And behold the face of Judgment Day?
Does one who crosses ever turn back?
Does one who meets it return alive?
It is water like a devouring beast.
I shall return to my elder Semetey
And tell my elder
That its waters are too fierce."
Kulchoro wheeled about
And now turned Buurul's head;
He turned and stood examining
The land and water of this Urgench.
Ah, that creature, Great Urgench:
Its leaves were broad as leather,
Its black beetles thick as thumbs,
Its lizards like snakes,
And its snakes like ropes seven fathoms long.
Almighty God in his power
Creates living souls of every kind.
Its saplings were like plane trees,
Its small birds like snowcocks,
And its hogweed stalks thick as forearms;
When you looked at its red deer,
They were like pale, white-faced cows.
It was a broad and tranquil land,
Its apples ripening until they became compost,
A place of wonders of every kind.
Kulchoro stood astonished;
When he looked toward the farther bank,
Thirty daughters-in-law and thirty maidens
Were there beginning their games.
In their midst Aychurok
Flashed like a poppy.
The fitted coat and fine dress
Of the poor, formidable beauty
Showed their hems billowing.
Aychurok, daughter of Akun,
Wore a skullcap made of gold;
She appeared worth the whole world,
That poor daughter of Akun.
Of moon-beautiful Churok,
Which enchantment shall we tell?
When snow falls upon black earth,
Look at the snow, and you see her flesh;
