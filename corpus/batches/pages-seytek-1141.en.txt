Let me witness upon the open steppe
The ruin of all ninety-four.
If God ordains it and opens the way,
For my elder who has suffered torment,
For my forebear who knows my worth,
Let me become a shield.
There is an ancient saying:
For solid iron there is a hammer,
For the forlorn there is God;
Where there are eyes, grass will grow,
And the earth will swallow the tyrant.
They attacked my people living freely,
And tirelessly raided my herds.
Accursed sons who have renounced their mothers,
They tore my sleep open like scattered entrails,
Scattered my possessions over the hills,
And drove my people to distraction.
The wretched Chinese have made
My elder Joltoy, my lake of strength, waste away.”
His battle cry split heads.
When Akjoltoy looked across,
The figure of Seytek
Appeared before his eyes.
When he saw brave Seytek,
His blood surged and raced,
And his beloved protector came into view.
Strength entered him; grinding his teeth,
The doomed man said:
“My protector has come—so be it!
If I now die at the hands
Of a warrior mighty in terror, my heart is content.
My valiant one has appeared before my eyes;
If I die now, I have no regret,
Not the smallest regret remains in me.
If I should die suddenly,
If by chance my time has come,
There is one who will seize and fold me at the waist,
One I trust as my support,
Who will load me for the journey to Ala-Too.
He will not let the beasts devour me,
Nor let it be said that Joltoy died
At the hands of the merciless.
He will not harbor dreadful thoughts,
Nor let wolves tear flesh from my haunches,
Nor allow the fanged beasts
To dishonor my soul in the end.
‘Dig and turn the earth with a mattock—
Seytek has come, bury me,’” he said.
Placing his hope in the young man,
He was wholly freed from anxiety;
No longer fearing the Chinese calamity,
His spirits lifted and his heart grew calm.
“My protector has visibly come;
As I depart this world,
I have seen my beloved one,” he thought.
Taking heart, he braced himself.
Seeing the gray wolf, Akjoltoy
Counted that lone hero as a host;
His anguish subsided,
And hope in his fleeting life returned.
His sorrowful heart relaxed,
And Joltoy stood watching.
He saw a broad-chested figure,
Seeming like a bridge across a mighty river,
Guarding all the people.
That falcon born a champion,
Whose fathers and forefathers
Had never retreated before an enemy,
Tightened and buckled his war belt,
Then attacked the ninety-four.
A roar of shouting rose before him;
The valor of the young champion blazed
As he invoked his ancestors in his battle cry:
“In this holy war,
Support me, my own heroic father!
My forefather Manas the lion,
Make straight the path before me now!”
Upon his mountain-like gray horse,
In his billowing golden coat,
He poured out his wrath, grim-faced.
