Parted from her hero,
Was thus brought to disgrace;
She yearned for just one glimpse
Of the mighty slayer whose life had passed.
All the Jediger
Gathered their strongest wrestlers;
Their most capable and formidable warriors
Surrounded your poor, sea-hearted sister-in-law
On every side.
They bound her hands and feet,
And the Jediger tied up
The moon-faced lady Chürök.
They mounted her on a dapple-gray pacer
And dressed her in ash-colored silk,
Saying, “Here is the perfect bride,
The mate ordained by God.”
Many warriors called her an infidel,
And said she was a widow to be taken.
They divided gold by the bowlful
And gave radiant Chürök,
The noble-born bringer of turmoil,
To brave Kyyaz.
Unable to break free, however she struggled,
Aichürök fell into their snare;
Brave Kyyaz, lord of the Jediger,
Took Aichürök away by force.
As Chürök sobbed and wept,
There was no swift steed for her to mount,
And no kinsman to rescue her.
Her waist tightly bound,
The legacy left by the slain hero
Was parted from the land of Talas
And separated from the Kyrgyz people.
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Took red-tailed elephants,
Loaded the furs and gold,
Placed Aichürök at their head,
And took a thousand of the fairest women.
He destroyed the royal camp and seized its spoils,
And, when counted, took this multitude of lives.
Every capable Jediger
Took six thousand horses,
While even the poorest fool
Took some six hundred horses.
There was so much gold and silver
That they could not load it all;
So many noble brides and lovely girls
That they could not carry them all away.
Their hearts were content;
The Jediger filled the Seven Valleys,
Each gaining all he desired.
Brave Kyyaz, son of Agysh,
Became khan of the Jediger,
Ruling the people and governing the realm;
He now enjoyed every pleasure.
All of Semetey’s wealth
He measured out with a whip and divided.
His tent covering gleamed with fat;
The triumphant Kyyaz became khan,
And his heroic heart was at ease.
He made the fortified palace his dwelling,
And made Semetey, Bakay, and Külchoro—
Those three—the foundation of his fortune.
In Talas he made Kanchoro
Khan to govern the people.
The nation clamored and the people seethed,
While for that hero Kanchoro
They devised honors beyond imagining.
They held a feast for many days,
Placed a golden crown upon him,
And seated him on a golden throne,
Setting him above
The Argyn and the Kyrgyz.
That hero Kanchoro
Had Semetey’s name erased
And Kanchoro’s name proclaimed.
The towering Karakyr
Was called the domain of Khan Kanchoro;
