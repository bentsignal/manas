Will any man remain alive?
O black-bearded men, this is a distant road;
Will I die without returning?
Farewell, my dear people!
God knows whether one dies;
God knows whether your Toltoi
Will return to you alive!"
Hero Toltoi spoke of
A fatal calamity,
Pouring himself recklessly into drink;
Hero Toltoi raised the uproar.
He raced upon his gray horse;
Sultan Toltoi spurred forward,
The earth splitting in a mirage,
And Toltoi drew near
The city of broad Afghanistan.
Was he not capable Toltoi,
Who knew much of the whole world?
Was he not renowned Toltoi,
Who knew perfectly what was and was not?
Was Toltoi not exceedingly
Skillful among spearmen?
When his company stood complete,
Was Toltoi not the foremost champion
At breaking enemy ranks?
He reconnoitered the capable road
Leading to Kashgar;
Finding no track there,
He turned back again.
He reconnoitered the direct road
Leading to Turpan;
Unable to find a track there,
He turned back again.
He reconnoitered the safe road
Leading to Beijing;
Finding no track there,
He turned back again.
The winding road of Toguz-Zhol,
The trail of wolf and sheep,
The side-road leading to Andizhan—
He reconnoitered that road;
Finding no track there,
He turned back again.
The woeful road entering Koton,
The seven roads sinking into the earth—
He reached and reconnoitered them;
Finding no track there,
He turned back again.
Echoing Zhar-Kyya,
Where a single horse-track wound on—
That was the road to Talas.
Those who knew would know it;
All who did not know
Would follow the former road.
When he came to that road,
A whirlwind spun;
The black road lay under a curse.
Nothing caught the eye,
And one could not tell which road it was.
Toltoi's sight was bound:
Aichürök, daughter of the hero,
Recited words in Arabic
And cast a ground fog;
Thus that road
Vanished entirely from sight.
Clouds formed in the hollows,
And ground fog descended and closed in;
The accursed woman Aichürök's
Weather-working power appeared.
Fine rain separated through the air,
And cloud covered the moon;
Toltoi of the Zhediger
Lost his wits.
Then Toltoi grew excited
And spoke to himself:
