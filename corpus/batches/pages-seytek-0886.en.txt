They swiftly cried, “Baabedin!”
And those who knew the invocations
Called on Aituyak,
Quickly uttering every prayer:
“His strength is strange, his fury great—
God, cast the infidel beneath him!
Let the cherished one, black as a crow,
Let God set the boy above him!”
Brave Seytek, bearer of the sacred standard,
Was the lord of the people, a lion;
He was the stronghold of their inheritance.
Set him in the vanguard and he brought good fortune;
Let him lead, and he was like a myriad troops.
From a ridge he saw keenly;
He was the heir bound to the people’s back.
While this boy lived,
He was a black, snow-covered crag,
And the people were his cherished own.
The people reeled in anguish,
Praying to the figure
Of the gray-maned lion dear as a crow.
The young champion who faced him
Fought Sarybay—
Such was their grappling.
As the people prayed in distress,
Sarybay poured out his wrath
And prepared to attack brave Seytek.
Beneath him was Tookyzyl,
The red mountain that flew like a cloud,
Both swift and steady;
He tugged and trailed the reins,
His doe-like eyes turning red,
His argali stride stretching long.
Set upon stone, he never slipped;
He never faltered on sandy ground,
Never stumbled or tired,
His parrot-like tail streaming.
A fully grown giant demon had come
And asked for Tookyzyl;
When he asked, the horse was not given,
Nor had any cold-hearted stranger ridden him.
He pulled free and twisted the reins,
And leapt whenever he saw water or ravine.
He never panicked at a battle cry;
Once his hooves strode forward,
He never stepped twice in the same track.
When he burst away, he left all others behind;
When he ran, the black earth
Split apart layer by layer.
Ill-fated Tookyzyl
Revealed a supernatural form.
Set upon stone, he never slipped;
Chasing wild asses, he caught them,
And never tired of a full gallop.
He never slid from the heights,
Nor did his speed diminish
Against the mountain’s wild asses and deer.
When relentless Tookyzyl gave chase,
No wild ass escaped him.
With his pale, low-lying mane,
When sparkling springs and lakes appeared,
When some mighty creature opposed him,
When a giant came intending to seize him,
He flung his forelock to the sky,
Drove his hooves into the earth,
And planted all four feet,
Until whoever faced him wearied.
When that rabid Sarybay
Was struck by Seytek’s spear,
He raged in injured pride,
Swelled with menace and constriction,
Thrashing over the blood-slick earth.
The accursed one remained there,
Wild enough to scorch the mountains.
He launched a dreadful frenzy,
Ravaging valley and mountain,
His blood burning from fury,
Men losing their lives at the mere news of him,
Blood streaming from both his eyes,
Raving and running mad.
