When battling an enemy,
When fighting face-to-face,
They wondered, ‘Could an arrow pierce it?’
And said, ‘It would be folly not to find out.’
Alas, it was seized from me;
When Kyyaz took my precious,
Costly bulletproof armor,
Alas, I had it torn apart.
I brought what remained in trust,
Thinking, ‘Let my sorrow-dispelling son wear it.’
I give it to you as a gift—wear it and prosper!
Arm yourself and strike the foe!
My cherished son, wear
The bulletproof armor I had specially made,
And charge to attack the enemy!
Strike the Jediger of Jeti-Özön
And drive off their mares from the hillsides;
Draw out all the ancient rage
Stored within the slaves.
Bring ruin upon their khan!
Exact your long-awaited vengeance!”
So saying, Aichürök
Set the half-dead, wretched slave’s
Soul ablaze,
Clothed him in the bulletproof coat,
Transformed his appearance completely,
And made him cast away
The felt coat he had worn in slavery.
Thinking the boastful warrior Kyyaz
Might come shouting upon them,
Or, if Choro rashly lost control,
That an axe might strike
The heroic Choro on the head,
And death might needlessly overtake
The weakened Choro—
Thinking it folly to overlook this,
His sister-in-law Aichürök
Showed her wisdom
And placed upon Külchoro’s head
A helmet of blue steel,
Pulling it firmly down.
There was no gap in the armor he wore;
Unless God had ordained death
For the orphaned Choro,
No mortal man could kill him.
From his sister-in-law, Külchoro
Received a fine bulletproof robe,
And, considering it, found the garment fitted well.
Then Aichürök
Dressed him in the bulletproof armor.
His precious sister-in-law Aichürök
Gave him honey when he thirsted,
Served him food at leisure,
And began entertaining conversation.
She had a fattened lamb and a savory head
Boiled until tender,
Heaped sugar white as snow,
Cut off chunks of yellow butter,
And filled his belly with tea.
His grief departed and joy arose;
Clad in bulletproof armor,
His painful ailments subsided,
His dimmed eyes opened,
His pent-up sorrow eased,
What had been broken was joined,
And what had scattered was gathered.
On seeing his sister-in-law, Külchoro
Became a full-grown lion once more;
He thrilled as strength filled his blood.
His head rose free from slavery,
His thirst was quenched and blood filled his veins—
Such was Külchoro’s condition.
