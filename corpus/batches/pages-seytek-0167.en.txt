To unleash slaughter without looking back.
If his old vigor returned to his limbs,
He would avenge the gray wolf’s death.
They told Choro, “Shoot through
The ninety mattocks in a row,”
And thus put his courage to the test.
To shoot through those mattocks,
Külchoro, favored by the spirits,
Would measure what strength he had.
If powerless, he would die of shame
Before death itself could come;
If he had the strength, he meant
To unleash some great calamity;
If this poor man had no strength,
He would surrender his fly-like soul.
He took the steel bow in his hand,
His heart aflame and his eyes blazing,
Biting his lip and grinding his teeth;
The poor defender of the people
Stood with both eyes blazing.
He readied himself like a hawk,
And found wisdom in his courage;
The troubled poor hero
Found keen strength within his body
And strung the mighty bow.
He gathered his strength in his mind,
Mustered his courage throughout his body,
Prepared to draw the bow,
And prayed to the Creator.
Every watcher’s attention was seized;
His lion-like aspect appeared.
His form was like a massive boulder,
And your rugged eagle seemed
Ready to devour anyone who crossed him.
If the gray-wolf servant surveyed the world,
He seemed capable of conquering it;
For ages beyond the Jediger,
His cry seemed fit to unleash slaughter.
Whoever came within his reach
He seemed able to cleave and cast down.
Such was his wrestling might.
Wide-mouthed and cliff-browed—
Behold and ponder his gray-wolf courage.
He broke through passes and opened roads,
And, heedless of his own destruction,
Attacked an army six hundred thousand strong.
The hero Choro, pillar of the people,
Was fed carved horse-rib meat and fat,
And given plentiful food.
The orphan-raised hero Seytek
Had special delicacies prepared:
Fat lambs and choice heads,
And thick white broth boiled;
He had him crunch sugar white as snow
And gulp liquor from a bowl.
“I have tended my uncle well,” he said,
“For I know he is a lion.
If my uncle remains alive,
He will yet prove useful.”
So the people had said
Of my dear gray-wolf uncle:
“There is none like him in the world,”
As those who had seen him from afar knew,
Amid sky-splitting turmoil
And in single combat.
If he truly was a pillar,
The chieftain’s surviving legacy,
If he truly was a lion,
His courage must now have sharpened and filled him;
The time must have come for him
To clash with Kyyaz, son of Bagysh.
To put his strength to the test—
If his vigor had matured against the Jediger
Who filled the Seven Valleys—
To take revenge upon Kyyaz,
To make a glorious slaughter,
Pour blood into the dark waters,
Set the riverlands ablaze,
Begin carnage on the open plain,
And raise a feud to destroy the foe,
