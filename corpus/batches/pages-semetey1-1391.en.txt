It was a land where people could travel.
The tops of its willows bent low;
The surface of its sands lay finely traced.
Whoever saw it marveled,
And any brave man rejoiced to see it!
When you descend there, my boys,
If you grow weary, your longing is eased:
There are creatures of countless kinds!
When a hero descends and enters,
There lie Mangkan and Kordoi,
And many choice lands.
Honor the words
Your able uncle Bakai has spoken!
While my dear one was alive,
What did I not see,
And what did I not learn!
In wonders, festivities, and many games,
How often I plunged and reveled!
While my beloved Manas lived,
From sunrise to sunset,
I reconnoitered it all!
While my noble Manas lived,
I wore the flanks of my horse lean;
Through the four corners of the world,
I roamed and traveled!
While my able Manas lived,
Over mountains, rocks, and waters,
Here am I, Bakai, who crossed them!
To the end of the earth, to Jelpinish,
And Beijing in the east—
Here am I, Bakai, who saw them!
Before the eyes of your uncle who saw them,
Before your grandfather who saw so much,
Was there anywhere beneath the sky
That, while my able one lived,
His gray tulpar did not tread?
Was there any whole people
Untouched by the point of his blue spear!
Was there any wild place
The pale falcon did not strike?
While my companion Manas lived,
Was there any whole people
Untouched by Bakai's hand!
Through the four corners of the world,
Was it not Bakai who swept,
Across the whole universe,
Roaming far and wide—
Was it not Bakai who traveled?
Leaving no mountain, large or small,
Leaving not half a spring or stream,
While radiant Manas lived,
Was it not Bakai who traveled them?
He saw peoples in many lands,
And lands in difficult places;
He commanded the countless host.
This wretched world—
Your auspicious uncle Bakai
Surveyed it and left it behind!
Scattering golden words from his mouth,
Bakai goes onward;
Along the familiar road he himself had seen,
Bakai travels.
A great valley no man sees
Bakai leaves behind;
Through rugged mountain necks,
Bakai leads the way.
All that your noble uncle Bakai
Has said is true.
When he reaches the open plain,
Its tortoises are as large as pitchers;
Your uncle, elder Bakai,
Mingles with the beasts,
As though the two were born the same day.
It is a sheath fitted to a sword—
This accursed, barren world;
What precious Bakai has said
Is no lie, but truth.
If told again, it is another tale;
Can we tell it once again,
Just as it was before?
