When word of his heroism was heard,
The khan of Beijing was stirred to action.
“He never stood watching when battle began;
He is calamity itself,”
And the people of Chinmachin wept.
Told to take a cap, he took a head;
Told to spill blood, he spilled souls;
Told to drink water, he drank poison.
That dread calamity, Külchoro,
Was my lion’s confidant,
Your magnanimous father’s companion;
Their sheep shared one fold,
Their feasts shared one fellowship,
And all they found and gathered they held in common.
With your lionhearted father
He shared death and the same faith.
Yesterday at Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
When the massed Kyrgyz descended to the plain
And grappled in battle,
When dawn whitened over the mountains
And day was breaking,
Among the renowned Kyrgyz of Talas—
While he stood as their khan—
These words are not spoken in vain:
He was your lion father’s retainer,
The iron-covered gate of his enclosure,
The comrade of your leopard father,
The dashing warrior on the blaze-faced steed,
The mighty and renowned falcon.
Those who challenged him he set straight,
Those he speared numbered a thousand.
He guarded all that was precious,
Seized the crooked and made it straight.
He was that famed strength, that very bastion,
That lion surpassing mortal men,
The champion who humbled the arrogant.
His laughter ushered in the dawn;
He was the lion who humbled the arrogant.
A solid hammer of cave-forged steel
Is this Külchoro standing here.
If your gray-maned warrior father
Sent him ahead, the road opened fair;
If he led them, hosts followed behind.
His stature towers like a lofty mountain;
He is the thunderbolt that shattered the khagan.
Even death cannot disperse
Your uncle Külchoro’s ardor.
He is the strength with which you gird your waist,
The stronghold in which you lie secure;
He breaks through dead ends and opens roads.
This dark, sharp blade with the mottled hilt
Is the refuge beneath which the people dwell,
The sheltering, cliff-like mountain pass,
The lion who guards the nation.
He is an inheritance left by your father,
A peerless one proven before the people;
Among all the peoples of the world,
He is a peerless kinsman, tried and true.
Your elder brother was born of a khan
And brought turmoil upon the enemy.
He became a fortress built for Talas,
And the enemy who opposed him found the grave.
He drove the khagan’s people to the frontier.
When the Chinese armored themselves in stone,
Saying, “We shall seize Talas,”
Their finest warriors burned.
He hacked down all who encountered him,
Devoured all whom he met,
Cut down whoever crossed his path,
And ruined whoever faced him.
Such is the story of Külchoro.
Against the infidels swarming like black beetles,
He began a savage battle
And slew with his own hand
Their khan and elder, Kongurbay.
While your warrior father and khan still lived,
His prowess and lionhearted strength,
His valor surpassing multitudes—
Whenever he faced a challenging foe,
He surged like a lion.
