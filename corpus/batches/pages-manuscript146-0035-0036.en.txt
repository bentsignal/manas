Grieving, he would close his eyes,
Those who pursued him pressed onward,
Softening that people,
They say he found it as he had hoped,
Coming thoughtfully to the barren shore,
They say he found what lay at the root of his thought.
Like Manas, over Afghanistan,
He crushed its turmoil,
He scattered its four kinds of livestock,
Binding its claim through hunger,
Entering from Karak toward its mountain,
Tethering his mares on its pasture,
He bore down upon them from above,
He crushed their nobles,
Taking the booty and that people he found,
He subdued the four who had found shelter,
He scattered their four kinds of livestock,
The Afghan domain he had set his mind upon,
Without turning the reins toward the seat of honor,
There, standing exposed, their spirit broke,
He turned back the Afghan herds.
At that time, that people,
Saying the gray wolf had moved them away,
Turned every one of their necks.
A small number of Muslims,
Saying, “I shall make it a shrine,”
Having once been born into the world,
Saying he had come upon the face of the earth,
Making his court in Kokand,
Making that one into a shrine,
He set it close to paradise,
Losing sight of the white yurts,
Taking Afghanistan and Konguz,
Making that one a saint,
He laid out his paradise wide.
Gathering around a corrupt man,
Whatever ears had heard and eyes had seen,
Of distant Khodynch,
The finest of the Khakanians was its khan,
Among the Chinese people of forty khans,
There were a thousand watchful eyes,
There was a gray-maned hero to rule the people,
To all of Khodynch,
Sending word by a written notice,
The nobles Ebei and Shygai,
He set the Kazakhs marching against the Khakan,
Every living soul gathered,
The Chinese who had drawn their swords,
The Chinese people of forty khans,
Gathered in Beijing,
In this Beijing upon which radiance poured,
In this inexhaustible land,
There then stood the Old Khan,
Before the eyes of the Old Khan,
The one who became khan over ten khans,
When two thousand years had been fulfilled,
The Old Khan lived long,
When he was among the people,
Born in Altai and grown mighty,
His people gazed their fill at him, moonlike,
They saw no defect in his stature,
He became a match for foreign peoples,
His spoken word brought contentment,
The forty tribes of China, to the Khakan,
Paid neither tribute nor tax,
And did not heed the word he spoke.
