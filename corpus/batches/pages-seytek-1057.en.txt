Desire is not dispelled by seeing it,
With its towering plane trees,
Its mingled birches and poplars,
And all the abundance filling it.
There are apricots, dates, and cherries,
And how much else we have never seen!
Female apes and their young
Are breeding there in multitudes.
Bull deer and camel deer—
Every kind of living creature is there.
All the animals upon the earth
Seem to have been created in abundance.
Such is radiant Keng-Özön,
A valley blessed with perfect fortune;
Ripe walnuts fill its ravines,
And fallen apples rot into manure.
Such a paradise of a land it was.
Is it not the finest of lands,
Containing everything upon the earth,
Where those who dwell never have their fill?
No soul can tire of its delights;
Those who love it never wish to leave.
No living mortal could exhaust
Its amusements and diversions.
A brave man devoted to the hunt
Would never wish to go elsewhere.
There are many things no soul has seen,
Including unclean scorpions
As large as a horse’s head.
On seeing this, Akjoltoy
Pulled Kulunsur’s head around.
Amid the rugged foothills,
He stood utterly astonished.
Calling out as he surveyed the land,
He saw many fleeing game beasts,
And all manner of wondrous creatures;
He gazed until his eyes ached,
Amazed by all these marvels.
It was a black ridge no beast could cross,
No pass that man could traverse.
This radiant Keng-Özön
Was a land that the ancestors,
Through seven generations, had never seen.
Seeing it cannot dispel one’s longing;
The delights of Keng-Özön
Will not fade until death,
Nor will desire for this valley.
The shining sun never passes away,
Nor does the dark night pass.
O faithless, fleeting world—
Who does not pass from this false life?
After gazing fully upon all this,
He yearned for it intensely.
His heart surged with joy
As he looked across the valley:
“If I made this our lifelong home
And brought my own people here,
Could this be a land too vast
For all our games and revelry?
Could it be a land where we drank kumiss,
Grew merry, and never lay down?
Could it be a land where, shouldering our Almabash guns,
We hunted its bears
And its tigers?
Could it be a land where a golden hero
Never failed to find his match?
Could it be a land where villages camped
On both sides of the valley?
Could it be a land that failed to fill
A brave young warrior’s heart?
Could it be a land too vast
For young men’s merrymaking?
Could it be a land where we did not fire our guns
And shoot its deer?
Could it be a land where we tethered racehorses
In the valley and never raced them?
Could it be a land where cheerful marrow-loving hunters
Did not feast?
Could it be a land where every day
No one cried, ‘Bravo, young warrior!’?
