Cease working yourself into a fury.
At heart we are brothers of one blood;
Abide by the words that have been spoken.
Heed what has been said.
Towering like a blue cliff,
My lord, wherever you appear,
Do not quarrel with the lion,
With the heroic, fierce-wolf warrior.
A prince becomes a true prince
Through the multitude of his people.
If you make peace, Ümötöy,
Semetey will say, ‘My cousin, my soul’s companion, has come,’
And, sparing nothing,
Will give you countless dark-maned mares,
And camels numbered in their thousands.”
So saying, Külchoro
Stood before Ümötöy
And delivered these words.
Growing furious and obstinate,
That wretched Ümötöy
Was now struck by God.
His eyes blazed with rage;
Good heavens, his bloodlust seized him fiercely.
Turning sideways and working himself up,
He shouted in madness:
“The fly-like life within my breast,
And my spoonful of black blood,
I shall sacrifice to battle.
Look what this damned, wretched slave
Has dared to do!
Spouting smooth, broad words,
Talking of a time when life will be sweet—
Look at the words uttered
By this fool whose life has fled him!
You two renowned, fierce lions,
Who have girded your belts,
Do not appear, companion,
Before my eyes again!
Become night without first growing dark,
And, miserable slave, bathe not merely in blood—
Become one with your blood!
If I do not meet you in single combat,
If I alone do not destroy
Your army covering the earth,
May I die no longer Ümötöy,
May I sink away as though never born!
Unless he pays the blood-price for my father,
Unless he yields to my grievance,
Unless he loads up six measures of gold
And draws the high-stepping Buurul
Beneath me here and now—
See what words this ill-starred wretch
Has just spoken!
One of you is Khan Semetey,
Who sits there as khan;
Ümötöy’s wrath has risen,
And now he will beat the frenzy from you.
I came demanding my khan-father’s blood-price;
It was for this purpose I came.
Who told you to make anyone khan?
Look at the words uttered
By this wretch who deserves a shameful death!
Look at what this damned companion
Has dared to do!
Tell me of your tawny, rolling hills;
Do not prattle on,
But tell me how you mean to fight.
The matter, in brief, is this:
I will not prosper by befriending Semetey
And riding at his side.
Nor can I accept Külchoro and aged Bakay
Even if they are given to me as slaves.
I shall rage as your neighbor,
Destroy your camp and swallow it whole.
I shall bring doom upon your people,
And slaughter your nation to the last.
I shall raze your ancestral fortress;
If I do not bring utter ruin upon you all,
