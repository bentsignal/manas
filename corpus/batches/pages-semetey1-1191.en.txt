Kongurbay, who has long borne a grudge,
Has come here himself;
The time has come for me
To contend with that dog.
Raise your palms and give your blessing;
At the swaggering Kalmyk
I shall charge and thrust my spear,
With that grim-faced Kalmyk
I shall fight to the finish.
Before all the people we shall clash,
And I shall settle the quarrel once for all;
Before the nation we shall grapple,
And I shall ease my pent-up grief.
At that Kalmyk huge as Opol-Too
I shall level and stretch out my spear;
For this wretched life, so dearly troubled,
I shall find some course at last.
Against the blustering Kalmyk
Let me meet him face to face.
If my death has come, here is my life;
If fate has arrived, let me die.
The decree that God has written—
With this man, this mortal soul of mine
Will close in combat and see.
Before the people we shall joust,
And I shall firmly prove my valor;
From the infidel, for our ancient feud,
I shall take revenge this very moment.
You handful of Muslims,
Raise your palms and give your blessing;
I shall grip the yellow spear and spur myself on.
You handful of Muslims,
Thus I ask you to guard my honor;
If fortune favors me, the infidel
I shall drive all the way to Kakan.
I speak my farewell while I am safe;
With this dog standing here
I shall return after trading battle cries.
There will be spear-play, a true fight—
Thus I tell you my heart's longing.
At the pig who is the monks' khan
I shall stretch and thrust my spear.
Open your palms wide and bless me,
You handful of Muslims;
From the pig who is the monks' khan
I shall now take my revenge.
Spear in hand, breaking armor,
If my death has come, I shall fall to a bullet;
Against the grim black Chinese
I shall fight to the finish."
Ala-Mongku spoke these words,
Openly making his plea.
He readied himself to charge,
As wisdom came to him from the unseen.
His face looked like oiled wheat,
His eyes like the twilight mist.
The blood-drinker Manas, the panther,
The saint whom the patron spirit upheld,
Broad-chested, with a generous breast,
With great, imposing black eyes,
The brindled black panther,
Tensed along his flanks,
The stumpy-maned gray lion,
As though about to tear open a wound,
Gathered himself at the loins.
Your gray wolf of lofty spirit,
The panther blessed with matchless fortune—
When one looked upon his front,
He bore the majesty of six thousand men;
When he turned his back,
He had the aspect of a dragon.
No challenger had ever come against him,
No enemy he faced had gone unbeaten;
Hardened by a life of drinking blood,
He was famed as the black gray wolf.
Born in Altai, he grew mighty,
And his renown grew great.
