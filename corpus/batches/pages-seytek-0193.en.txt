I became a winged steed.
In the waters of Jeti-Özön, you of the Jediger,
You were the hero who dwelt there,
But today, Kyyaz,
You are laid level with the black earth.
You have courage but no sense,
You witless Kyyaz,
Cease being Kyyaz and die,
Drown before you were ever born!
You let a woman take your horse—
Die, black-faced Kyyaz!
The golden-collared coat of mail
You shall never shake out and wear;
The renowned racehorse Tootoru
You wretched Kyyaz, shall never mount.
Be a tomb on Black Cliff,
You who let a woman take your horse—
Alas, Kyyaz, farewell!
I thought you were Kyyaz the hero,
But you are Kyyaz the woman!
I thought you were Bagysh’s son,
But you are a woman’s slave!
When witless Kyyaz dies,
The true abode from which none return
Is what you shall see, Kyyaz.
At a woman’s word, into your sheath
You are one who shrinks, Kyyaz;
From the crown of your head, the one God
Is sure to strike you, Kyyaz!
Letting a woman take your horse
Is how you live, Kyyaz;
Headfirst into the earth
You are sure to go, Kyyaz;
Chasing after women, great affliction
Is what you shall meet, Kyyaz.
You ranged among nations and ruled the people,
You were mighty Kyyaz,
But enslaved to a single woman
Is what you have become, Kyyaz!
You have courage but no sense;
He fitted an arrow to the bow.
He seized the specially fashioned whip
And swung it round,
Making the poor beast cry out
As he lashed it on the rump,
Driving it at a rolling gallop,
Making it stride like a maral,
Its eyes reddening like a wild ram’s.
From before brave Kyyaz
She led it away with skill,
And when she wheeled it aside,
The day waned and night passed,
And Tootoru gained the power of speech.
Tootoru plunged into the tumult;
For those who understand, strife is a great affliction.
Tootoru entered the uproar,
When reined aside, he seemed to fly;
Tootoru climbed the ridge.
To foolish, empty-headed Kyyaz
Tootoru began confiding his secret.
Folding back his ears like a kulan,
Sweeping the earth like a whirlwind,
Making the ground crumble under his tread,
Drawing in his belly beneath Kyyaz,
Rearing and pitching forward,
The great bay horse swayed and plunged,
Straining as though about to fly,
Neighing and bucking,
The beast spoke words to Kyyaz—
To Kyyaz of the Jediger
He spoke as follows:
“Hero Kyyaz, valiant one,
I was your swift steed Tootoru.
We were joined when you were no bigger than a louse,
We came together when you were the size of a thumb,
We met when you were tiny as a tick.
You were a child at play,
And I a foal just beginning to graze.
I became a noble warhorse,
And anyone seeking me was a stranger.
