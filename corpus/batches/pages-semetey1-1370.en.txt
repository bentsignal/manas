Driving them all swiftly without looking back,
Having received the prince's* blessing,
Asking gracious Allah to grant a road,
With a spare courser made ready,
Your valiant uncle Bakai
Took the champion hero with him,
And Külchoro and Kanchoro,
The two matched lions.
"I shall gird my waist firmly;
For Kongurbai in Beijing
I shall pursue the blood-price
Of my valiant father Khan Manas,
And lions Chubak and Almambet.
How could a nobleman's* son
Go on without taking revenge?!
My valiant uncle, where is the road?!
The Kakanchin are those I shall fight,
Bakburchun those I shall confront;
There is no remedy against fate.
At the house built in Kerüü,
Lion uncle, we have made ready
Against the mighty khan of the monks!
Let us set our faces and level our spears
Toward the mighty city of Beijing!
If death reaches us, let us die;
Against the infidel son of the kalday,
Let us set out on holy war
Against hero Khan Kongurbai!"
Committing himself to fate, he spoke;
Old Bakai, mountainlike, said:
"The road to Beijing divides five ways;
We shall choose one and see what we meet!
Wherever we arrive, we shall press on;
If we grapple with the infidel,
Hero Khan Kongurbai,
We shall spill his blood!
Entering Beijing is a toilsome task;
There are countless black cattle gathered as tribute,
And tens of thousands of treasury livestock;
At the very least, we shall raid
The infidel's horses and escape!
It has a kalday and a khan,
And every kind of foe to fight.
There lies the land of Chin-Machin;
If Bakai speaks the truth,
His true account is inexhaustible!
The road to Beijing divides five ways;
Fix this firmly in mind, my son!
When Bakai cries out his warning,
He knows horrors no mortal has heard!
The road to Beijing has five branches;
There is no mortal who has traveled all five—
So formidable are they, splendid hero!
On one road to Beijing,
Whoever goes does not return—a calamitous road.
There is one road to Beijing
Where whoever meets it never comes back—a dark road!
There is one road to Beijing
Where the mortal who sets out upon it
Wanders and dies—a dark road!
There is one road to Beijing
With a pass that has been sealed shut;
On one road to Beijing
There are guards and a khan;
On one road to Beijing
Are every kind of devourer!
It has its obstacles and its lore;
When we go upon that road,
Do not grieve me, son—heed my words!
Its people have giants;
What does accursed Beijing
Not possess within it!
