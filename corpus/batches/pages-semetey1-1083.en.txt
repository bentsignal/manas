In a tumultuous land she has a hero.
Alas, Kozho, hear my words,
You pass on rumbling and raging.
Raging onward, one day
You will reach my very end with me.
If you rage with brute force,
You will dig a deep grave,
You will make my sorrow boil.
Before my eyes you will show
A marvel no man has seen.
You will have us wait on the yellow steppe,
And you will make us contend
With the son Manas left behind.
Why do you covet, Chynkozho,
Akun's daughter Churek?
My khan Kozho, you mean to meddle
In turmoil from which there is no recovery.
You had the goats for provisions slaughtered,
Withered Kozho, may you come to no good,
You are about to make us suffer punishment.
You have brought Afghanistan's city down upon us,
You are about to lay upon my head
A calamity no man would cause.
You have thrown the exhausted people into alarm,
You mean to lodge the guest overnight,
Ruined Kozho, thundering on,
You are making me tell this tale.
You are a wretched Kozho,
Does she know what you have said?
Akun's daughter Aychurek—
If we implore her, will she marry us?
Will God consent to force?
She is to be Manas's daughter-in-law—
Will Akun Khan give her to us?
Akun Khan's daughter Aychurek
Strokes the flanks of her horse,
The luckless one, rich in magic,
Throughout the whole wide world
She flies and travels.
Hero Kozho, hear my words!
Sleeping in the night, I dreamed a dream,
I saw a dreadful, grievous deed.
I will not take your Aychurek,
I will not fall into her torment.
There is a flaw in these words of yours:
Will they give us the wife
When the son Manas left behind still lives?
When I slept at night and dreamed,
I saw an evil deed in my dream.
He rode a red-bay horse,
Its horsehair tail tied with a snap.
He had buckled on a silver belt,
A dark mark lay across his sun-bright face.
Thick-wristed and stone-hearted,
A fierce young hero filled with courage.
His sword, for my ravenous destroyer,
Was bound bare to his body without a sheath.
Like a cloud about to rain again,
He wheeled back and forth.
At the sight, my heart was torn apart,
Wrath poured from his face.
Of middle height and broad shoulders,
He was clad entirely in iron.
He roared in fury;
If seven successive hosts came,
He faced them alone and scattered them.
A demon-like boy,
He shouted the battle cry, “Manas!”
Leveling his spear,
In this manner he brought to an end
Every stratagem of your Toltoy.
Manas led countless men.
Azhybay, Bakay, and Almambet,
Valiant Chubak, and a thousand men
Charged in along my flank,
Each of them thrust a spear,
And struck me as they closed around.
