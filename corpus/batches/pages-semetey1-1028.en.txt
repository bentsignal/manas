The fourteen horses, mere animals though they were,
Flattened their right ears;
In terror, the fourteen horses
Flung the crust of the black earth
Up toward the sky.
They traveled in haste and agitation,
Covering great distances without sparing the horses.
Many meadows and many grasses
Were erased beneath their hooves;
Behind your lord,
Wolves and foxes fed their fill.
When he came to certain places,
He appeared like a cuckoo;
In wrath and astonishment,
He berated the fourteen horses.
Under the gray-maned hero's anger,
No person could stand and look upon him.
His aspect was grim and his venom strong;
When frenzy seized his spirit,
He leveled whoever opposed him.
The more he was exhausted, the more he smoldered;
His power mounted from its very roots.
Wherever he passed,
A thriving market seemed to arise;
Khizr had supported him from the beginning.
For seven generations his fathers were mighty khans;
He was the very image of a man-eating demon,
Blessed seven times by Khizr.
From father to forefather,
Openhearted audacity was their custom;
Their nature was to cast disorder
Across the whole wide world.
Tell him to drink water, and he drinks poison;
Tell him to take a hat, and he cuts off a head.
Tell him to spill blood, and he spills souls;
The madman destroys whoever opposes him,
And what engulfs him is red fire.
His fathers and forefathers
Filled their quivers with arrows
And annihilated whoever challenged them.
Driving fourteen horses from the Kyrgyz lands,
The bloodthirsty man marched into Afghanistan;
If an enemy came forth in hostility,
It was his custom to drink their blood.
Gray-maned Semetey raged,
As though someone had carried away
Churok, the wife he was to take.
God forbid one should behold him—
The imperious son of Manas,
Who had killed his own father.
Semetey, born greater
Than his gray-maned father Manas,
Appeared upon the plain of Ak-Kyya
With fourteen horses.
Your gray-maned hero reached
The bank of the Great Urgench.
In a place with high, sheer cliffs,
Aichurok, the daughter of Akun,
That wondrous wretched woman,
Knew that the lord would come.
Thinking, "How will he attempt the crossing?
If the enraged lion enters the Urgench
Without heed,
He may die there for nothing,"
She uttered a cold incantation
And worked magic upon the weather.
