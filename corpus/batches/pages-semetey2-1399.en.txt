The people bustled, holding a feast,
And while the multitude stood thus,
Hero Kiyaz, son of Bagysh,
Who ruled the people and sustained the realm—
Was this not Khan Kiyaz?
He was a lord of highest rank;
Among the Zhediger of Jeti-Ozon,
The accomplished Kiyaz became khan.
Such was Kiyaz's standing.
Wrapped in the blue haze of celebration,
Such was Kiyaz when you saw him.
He strained like a gray tiger,
His weapons set in order;
Such was hero Kiyaz's bearing.
A bare sword hung at his side,
Like a cloud returning with rain,
Wrath spread across his face.
Thick-wristed and stone-hearted—
If you ask what Kiyaz was like,
He was powerfully built and fierce.
His lashes were flame, his eyes embers;
Among men there was none like him.
Looming over the front of his saddle,
Broad as a tinsmith's bellows,
His bristling mustache
Was like the handle of a great whip.
Above his eyes, his black brows
Were like fists turned downward.
This formidable Kiyaz—
Such was his appearance.
He towered, huge-limbed,
With anger as vast as a mountain.
When this Kiyaz grew enraged,
He seemed ready to swallow his opponent.
Then hero Kiyaz,
On the plain of Jeti-Ozon,
Having carried Churok off,
Was holding the feast for taking her.
"All the precious four kinds of livestock
I mean to scatter lavishly,"
Was Kiyaz's intention.
Dust flew into the sky;
The army seethed in a dusty throng.
Hero Kiyaz, son of Bagysh,
Had brought relentless turmoil,
The people said.
Moonlike Lady Churok,
Taken as spoil from Talas,
Akun's daughter Churok,
He had brought away, they said.
Her gold and silver
He had loaded and carried off, they said.
Moonlike Lady Churok—
After ravaging and destroying her camp,
He had seized her, they said.
"The lands of old Talas and great Keng-Kol
I have won and brought home;
For the fallen and extinguished Zhediger,
I have taken vengeance," he said.
"Upon the steed none would give when asked,
I cast the cruel catching-pole;
Churok, who would not consent in her right mind,
I have taken and led away as spoil.
Today I mean to scatter all four kinds of livestock
At the bride Churok's wedding.
My people, now that you have come, I shall spare nothing.
Zhediger of Jeti-Ozon,
Your khan stands here before you.
All you multitudes born into this world,
Every one of you stands here.
Say, 'This is Khan Kiyaz's design;
This is Aichurok's wedding.'
Let cries and clamor abound;
Let the din split the sky.
Let dust trail upward from the earth;
Let all these accomplished people rejoice.
I shall scatter livestock by the tens of thousands;
The treasury left by my father Bagysh—
