His cavernous mouth gaped wide,
And the fire pouring from it
Shot upward toward the sky.
His flesh quivered and his eyes blazed;
His color fled, his arms drew taut.
Parting away in fury,
Terrible in bearing and potent in venom,
He took on the aspect of a dragon.
Wild with frenzy, he sprang,
Preparing to attack brave Seytek,
Raising his pinewood spear,
And riding straight against him.
His staring eyes opened wide,
And black venom from both corners of his mouth
Spattered toward his ears.
His face bristled with coarse hair,
Sparks flew from his mace,
Fire flashed from both eyes,
And dust rose from his heart.
He unleashed a terrible wrath;
The people clamored and tumult broke out,
And many, overcome with terror,
Lost their senses and fled in disarray.
“The moon is dark, the night close at hand—
What will this night bring?
When the offspring of that fearsome lion
Meets such a day of upheaval,
What will daylight bring?”
Sarybay was the one who clashed with him,
Malicious in thought and narrow of heart.
He assailed the wrathful lion,
And the hero became entangled in disaster;
He had challenged a champion unlike any other.
“O lion who departed for the other world,
Gray-maned one, cast down your gaze!
Watch over the child sheltered by your shade,
The figure left behind you,
The falcon you reared,
The courser you suckled,
The young hero left as your gift,
The lamp burning upon the ridge.
Spirit of gray-maned Manas,
Come yourself and sustain him!
Bind fast the path
Of this wrathful warrior-lion!
Entangle and bar
The way of that infidel Sarybay!
True Almambet and brave Chubak,
Protect the cherished boy Seytek;
From the calamity of doom and death,
Come to Seytek’s aid!”
All the champions joined together,
And the people cried out in tumult,
Praying sincerely
To the Creator, the spirits, and God:
“O host of holy masters who protect us,
Come and lend your aid;
Forty holy guardians, Khizr and Ilyas,
Come and shield the boy!
From death’s bullet of steel,
From the enemy’s iron snare,
From the wrathful one’s hand,
From the pit dug deep,
From the bitterness boiled for him,
From the fiery embers of hell,
From the merciless, grim-faced
True enemy’s hand—
God, preserve the boy!”
The people streamed about, crying aloud,
Raising a roar as though the earth had split.
The poor folk lost their senses,
Their thoughts scattered and divided.
Of monstrous strength and venom,
Sarybay looked dreadful.
The vast host watching him
Were dumbfounded and distraught.
The people lamented in anguish:
“What will become of the boy?”
The watching multitude
Despaired of their tiny lives.
