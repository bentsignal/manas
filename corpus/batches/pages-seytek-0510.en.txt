They grasped the stout shafts
Of their true-steel spears,
While war banners rippled in their hands
And black-steel Zulfikar swords
Flashed in every hand.
Traveling by night and not by day,
They raised war cries and a mighty clamor,
Preparing the army and leading the host,
Gathering all their forces together.
The earth shook beneath their streaming banners,
And the uproar deafened every ear,
Throwing the whole world into alarm.
He made Buurul beneath him
Stride forward proudly.
A warrior’s courage split stones,
And his battle cry split heads.
His great banner rippled,
As the earth cracked and mountains fell,
And the battle standards billowed,
While the courageous thundered onward.
The marksmen whose shots never missed,
He drove forward against the enemy’s flank,
Along with the deftest spearmen
And the strongest battle-axe wielders.
He sent onto the road
Warriors who were wolves in battle
And champions who knew the enemy’s stratagems.
The fleeing multitudes surged and scattered,
And the solitary figure of one man
Appeared as great as a thousand.
They donned bulletproof coats
And chose to mount
Stallions too swift for bullets to reach.
They selected and sharpened their spears,
And the valiant warriors bound
Their entrusted lives to a thread.
They leveled their muskets and fired,
And the muskets’ reports rolled and boomed.
The host that rode out was ten thousand strong,
Shouting, “Khan Manas!”
Their champions led the way,
The brass pipes blaring,
And the trumpets sounding sharply.
No bird could fly through the broad valley
As shrill notes rang from the brass horns.
The kettledrums deafened every ear,
And courage poured from the champions,
While their warriors and lions
Spread out across the ridges.
They were a countless throng.
Beneath the banner rode Semetey,
With Külchoro beside him,
While dust covered the sky.
Brave Seytek led the host,
Immovable as dark-blue steel.
Upon his head he wore
A Kyrgyz cap with layered sable trim.
Surveying all, he led the army,
Drawing the converging troops together.
Brave Seytek, commander of the host,
Bore a steel-edged, ornamented spear,
Marching against the warrior who made the earth split,
The infidel Sarybay’s son.
He went to wage a fierce campaign
And avenge the Black Giant.
Dust rose high enough to split the sky,
As gray-wolf Seytek took the lead,
Raising a wondrous tumult to behold
And filling the earth with clamor.
Steel-tipped spears crossed,
And a mighty battle began.
For the glory of the fray, precious lives
Were bound and cast upon a thread.
They raised a wondrous tumult and strife,
Making it a trial for the gathered people.
They pledged their lives for the nation,
To unleash a dreadful battle,
And lead the valiant Kyrgyz and Argyn onward.
