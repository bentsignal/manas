My valiant prince was lost,
My two knees buckled,
My suffering soul was overcome,
My tiny life cried out,
My whole body quaked
When I was caught in a net
That no leap could break.
My two hands were bound,
My two eyes stared wide,
When I fell captive.
When the seething Jediger host
Closed in and reached me,
They were thousands, and I alone,
When I fell into their hands.
They stripped off my robe as plunder
And slashed my neck ten times.
They tormented me, demanding blood-price,
And took as spoil my kangadai,
My trousers that no bullet could pierce.
They showed me many marvels,
Crippled both my hands,
And stripped my robe as booty,
Making my eyes behold
Things no man should ever see.”
Thus Külchoro spoke,
And before he had closed his mouth,
Aichürök brought the blue saddlebag,
The great green-hued camel saddlebag,
A saddlebag filled with treasures,
No ordinary pack for a gelded camel—
That saddlebag held things beyond price;
Within this saddlebag,
What was there, and what was not!
Kalyipa entered and took out a letter,
She who had been branded a dark-faced shrew:
“The kangadai spoken of before
Was meant for my son and brother-in-law to wear,
So he might strike the foe without tiring,
So he might charge whenever the enemy fled.
If I fail to understand this,
I will surrender my tiny life.
If my destined death has not arrived,
Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
I shall make a spectacle of you;
I shall seize whoever attacks me
And destroy whoever assails me.
I shall hunt down whoever meets me,
And if Providence brings me face-to-face
With my adversary Kyyaz,
I shall drink his blood.
I have gathered what was scattered,
My faith is all I desire.
Brave Kyyaz is the one I shall grapple with,
The enemy on whom I have fixed my gaze.
If I cannot cross the dark ridge,
What mark of manhood is there
If I cannot wrestle and take revenge,
Cutting off heads and spilling blood,
Thrusting spears and venting fury,
Meeting face-to-face,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
And crashing together in battle?
With their fangs laid bare,
Before dead horses lie in heaps,
With their fine moustaches stroked,
Before many brave men are slain,
Can there be any glorious deed?
Can a brave man who fears slaughter
Truly be called a man?
If foes confront each other
Yet the hidden, razor-sharp Zulfiqar
Is not driven into the very heart,
Can there be valor in a hero?
In this world that lasts but two days,
Does it become a hero to fear?
Brave Kyyaz is the one I shall fight—
My sister-in-law, lovely as the moon,
Is there armor I may wear into battle?
My valiant mother Kanykey—
How many robes did she give as gifts?
