Riding a gray pacer in splendor—
If you make trouble, my child,
How will you depart,
Your felt coat hanging loosely,
Bouncing upon a brown filly?
Understand, that is a great disgrace.
Bouncing upon a gray filly,
How will you wander away,
Your hide coat flapping?
Do heed me, my child.
All the people blessed by Kydyr are here,
The precious elder Bakai is here,
Sarykan with bowl-like eyes,
The khan of sacred power is here,
Hero Jamgyrchy of the Esheteks is here too.'
When the senior wife said this,
The foolish woman Chachykei
Now rose, seething,
To the khans of the Esheteks
Speaking in a most peculiar way:
'Women who have come to mediate,
Leave at once, you infidels!
I shall dig your pits deep,
I shall boil up your misery.
You have come relying on
That lanky great man of yours, Bakai.
I know every one of you well,
Your old Bakai with his dried-out beard,
[UNRESOLVED]
The one you have made into a saint,
Your bald man without a patch of hair,
I know your business,
The one you chose and brought,
That old fool born foolish,
Your man called Jamgyrchy,
Your elder with a swan-white beard—
May God strike you all!'
That Bakai was a ruler,
The foolish woman did not know.
Beginning with Khan Bakai,
She abused every last one.
With the three senior wives at their head,
They hurriedly fled outside,
Their necks stretched like geese,
Both cheeks flushed,
Finding no remedy:
'She has abused the khan!'
They all pinched their cheeks.
Her terrible abuse,
Old Bakai heard.
All that he heard
Brought distress upon Bakai.
The three khans sat dejected,
Hunched like camels stripped of their loads,
Unable to die of shame,
Unable to die and enter the grave,
That loudmouthed daughter-in-law,
They could not understand her nature.
Ever since Bakai had been Bakai,
Ever since he had borne the name Bakai,
Grown to maturity and become a man,
Become the equal of other men's sons,
Ever since he had borne the name Bakai,
Become a pillar of the people,
His appointed death had not yet come.
Let alone from a woman, hero Bakai
Had never suffered this from a man.
To experience such a dreadful ordeal,
To have his beard and head reviled,
Was the death of Khan Bakai.
Leopard Bakai, the khan's son,
Who had gathered an army and attacked the kaldai,
Your elder Bakai, the khan's son,
Burst into tears, sobbing:
'When our good fortune was intact,
When leopard Manas was alive,
