I, Bakay, it seems,
Have eaten my fill to the very snout.
Rather than lay this grief upon my head,
God, why did you not take me before I grew old?
Rather than lay this torment upon my head,
Why not leave me lying swollen in this earth?
Kanchoro, who has blinded my eyes—
Why did I not gouge out yours?
Why did I not slaughter you
And leave you no life
When you were still an infant?
The saintly princess Kanykey
Had spoken prophetic words:
If he survived, she said,
He would prove an enemy at heart.
When he sucked the breast, blood flowed;
His appearance was strange,
For at heart he was an enemy.
A clamor rose from his heart;
The words spoken by the wretched princess
Have proven true.
I, Bakay, failed to take care,
And swung this way and that;
My white beard has been shorn—
I let myself be caught unaware.
I panicked before his affliction;
My saintly gift fled from my head,
And I became a wretch.
Unable to see afar, I, Bakay,
Had become blind.
Struggling on for many years without dying,
Parted from my precious one,
In old age I, Bakay,
Was torn apart as a kökbörü carcass.
My ribs have been stripped away;
My twelve limbs and eighty bones,
Now my final journey is near,
Have been utterly torn apart.
I was a great man, I was a giant;
While my honor alone remained,
I ruled a far-ranging people.
Since my one great lord departed,
I have gone about crushed,
Become a slave, burning and blazing;
My uplifted head has died.
Burning to ashes,
I have suffered this much torment.
While my dear gray wolf lived,
While my sacred power was whole,
I was a steel fortress on the ridge.
My sacred power was at its height;
I was a prophet-like man.
My sacred power vanished;
I mounted a captive’s horse as an old man.
Living long without dying,
I have fallen into disgrace.
Yesterday, while Bakay was khan,
While wisdom remained in my breast,
I slaughtered every foe I encountered
And stood beneath the war standard;
I was called a truly mighty khan,
Called leopard Bakay,
A man of stern renown.
I was the river of the ravine;
When I struck, I severed heads,
I was the cutting edge of steel.
My steel is gone and turned to brass;
Parted from my only khan,
I have encountered Kanchoro Khan.
Herding camels and tending cows,
I am destined to die under beatings.
My white beard torn out,
My white coat stripped away,
I am destined to die from violence.
Mauled by wrestlers,
My hands and feet torn off,
I am destined to die from humiliation.
When death comes, I will not die simply,
