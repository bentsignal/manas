And forced the massed black Jediger
To join our side.
They hailed the Jediger’s hero Kyyaz
As comrade and champion,
And swore, “Let any man who disobeys
Have an infidel for a father!”
They took a death-bound oath
And swiftly made their pledge.
The massed black Jediger
All raised an uproar,
And took the black-hearted Kanchoro
As their commander.
They raised a moon-crested banner
And gathered an army whose roar split the sky,
Not a small host, but a multitude.
They chose the finest from among thousands,
Men skilled and ready with the spear,
To search together and find
The wounded, suffering hero
Külchoro,
To cast him into a dungeon forty ropes deep
If he fell into their hands,
And take his head with the sword.
Nursing his hatred, Kanchoro set out
To find Külchoro,
Gouge out both his eyes,
And, without sparing his fly-like soul,
Flay him while he screamed.
The pale-mottled standards thronged,
And a wailing clamor arose.
They girded on unsheathed swords
And made their weapons ready.
With shafts of steel and sharpened points,
Black tempered blades fitted to their tips,
Double-edged and four-sided,
They bore countless steel spears.
Now they searched for the warrior.
Wearing great boots with horseshoe heels,
Külchoro had crossed
The rugged hills and ridges;
Külchoro had passed beyond them.
Through Kara-Buura and Chatkal
Külchoro had ridden like the wind.
Plainly he had crossed
Kara-Üngkür of the soothsayer
And arrived there.
Once Kanchoro and Külchoro
Ate the Kyrgyz people’s yellow butter,
Ate fat young mares that had never foaled,
Rode colts and flew hunting birds,
Played shabiyet and made merry,
Laughing until the sun went down
And feasting until dawn.
When bored, they shot roe deer;
When not bored, they spent their time
Playing freely.
They had grown up together tending horses,
But alas, Kanchoro was ill-fated.
They had crossed open country and seen many peoples,
Gathered horses and seen many lands.
Out there in Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
He must have been unable to flee farther.
Kara-Üngkür of the soothsayer,
The true cave where people hid—
The warrior must have fled there
And crouched concealed within.
With hero Kyyaz in his train,
Kanchoro led the host.
They followed poor Külchoro,
Placing grass upon his fading tracks,
And pursued him until they grew pale;
They flung dust upon his fresh trail
And chased him in a frenzy.
Three times around
The pass of Üyrülmö
They pursued him without tiring.
Twilight fell and the sun went down,
And the whole host lay silent.
