The great host of Argyn and Kyrgyz
Stood gathered in a throng.
In the marsh of Chogulma,
Chinese and Kyrgyz seethed,
Swarming like black worms,
And held a common assembly.
They stood there gathered;
Külchoro moved between them,
The seasoned lion Choro,
A warrior who knew the ways
Of both Chinese and Kyrgyz.
The sight overwhelmed the mind:
Gray-wolf Semetey had encamped upon a ridge.
He looked like the blue mountain crag,
Like the cool shade
Of a cloud above the peaks,
Like the gaping throat
Of a sheer ravine.
The mountain crest seemed but a ridge beside him,
And he seemed as vast as half the valley;
He looked ready to devour whoever approached,
That broad and fearsome firebrand.
His bullets were like stones in a ravine,
And the smoke from his nostrils
Like ridges across the broad blue steppe;
Such was his awesome depth to behold.
When Semetey encamped there,
His boundless pavilion and court
Seemed spacious enough for all mankind to rest.
Twelve thousand men lay utterly still,
As though horses and all had vanished inside it.
From every side
It seemed they were playing ordo;
Such was the prince’s encampment.
Forty patterned silk carpets
Were spread ridge upon ridge.
The red banner inherited
From precious Khan Manas
Was raised sky-high.
It became a golden-crescent banner,
Amid clamorous battle cries;
It became a sun-tasseled standard,
Amid deep, resounding tumult.
Making the whole world rejoice,
They boiled barren young mares
In the great communal cauldrons.
The people descended in a mass;
Brave Semetey and Khan Bakai
Set about their preparations.
The golden-crescent patterned spear
Was lifted high into the heavens,
Beginning to make
The whole wide world bloom.
Leave that matter there;
Now begin the tidings of Kongurbay
And the forty tribes of China.
Their scout sent to reconnoiter,
Their envoy passing between the hosts,
Was a man of many guises.
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Became the mediator,
And the gray-wolf lion moved between them.
The infidel son of Kaldai,
The warrior Khan Kongurbay,
Ezkara and their khans—
The time had come when he deceived them,
While the fortunate Choro went between the hosts
And drew alongside them.
He came before Kongurbay,
And began delivering
The weighty speech the hero intended.
His knee bent to the earth,
And the blazing firebrand
Did homage to the Kalcha,
Bowing like a bride.
