He held sway over Zheti-Özön.
He began the struggle fiercely;
Whenever he met a sworn enemy,
That hulking, brutish slave
Tore him limb from limb.
All who saw his figure marveled.
His spear was wound with brass;
Whoever fought him collapsed.
Many wrestlers, encountering Küzhürmön,
Had crawled away.
From among the people Küzhürmön
Whipped on his Badam horse,
Taking a pine-like spear;
Enraged and fully prepared,
Onto the marked bank and stony field
He burst into the contest,
Making his accursed champion steed
Rear and surge beneath him.
If you ask about Karadöö,
He was still somewhat childish and awkward,
Still unseasoned in every speech.
Leaning against the gray mountain ridge,
Wrapped in gray-mottled dust,
He had never before competed for a prize,
Never speared a man face to face,
And had not yet won fame among the people.
The hardy warhorse,
With sieve-like lungs and copper forelegs,
The gray five-year-old fit for battle,
Had been trained from birth
And kept for Karadöö,
For it was a true champion steed.
Its parrot-like tail streamed behind;
The brass-beaked spirit woman
Had tested and recognized it
As an unfailing racer.
Many judges and skilled experts
Had it raised unseen by the people.
At its birth Khan Koshoy,
The aged father of the people,
The saint who outlived generations,
Saw and approved it.
Bilerik came from afar
And repeatedly asked for it.
The gray-maned sultan Semetey
Would not give it when asked:
“Let this gray horse of Kambar-Ata
Run among the herds;
If hardship comes upon us,
If a precious life is hard pressed,
Unknown Karadöö,
My falcon, shall ride it.
He will be my heir;
Karadöö will mourn and weep for me,
And grieve when I am gone.
It is a falcon no man should see,
A steed surpassing many others.
Its back is high and its belly low;
Wherever one rides it, it never tires.
Holding its reins never wearies the hand.
If a stallion charges into battle,
It will not surrender its rider to the foe.
It must be kin to the kulan-chasing
Karager of old Kaiyp,
Zhoruger of the raider,
And Er Töshtük's Chalkuiruk.
It must be kin to all of those.
It is a perfect, peerless steed;
Those four princely horses are one kin,
Children of the wind-spirit.”
Encountered in the unseen,
White-bearded old Kydyr,
From old Kydyr's hands,
From beyond Arabanty and Buura,
