The distant road takes six months;
Until I return after six months,
Farewell, my grieving people!
I will travel outward for three months,
And return for three months;
I will come in the sixth month.
If I do not come in the sixth month,
Kakan-Chyn is the one I fight,
And I will have perished and died!
I am going to see
What the Creator has ordained;
Fighting without sparing my life
Against the teeming black Chinese,
I am going forth to die.
Girding my waist firmly,
I am going forth to subdue them.
I am going to pursue the great blood price
Of my hero Syrgak.
I am going to enter
The teeming khan's domain,
And drive this way all the countless livestock
Gathered as tribute,
Taking them in a raid.
I am going to see
What the Creator has ordained;
For the sake of my hero father,
Rather than remain alive in the world,
I am going forth to die.
The land I face is Beijing;
I will wage fierce war,
Trample the black Kalmyks,
And break apart their nest;
I will tread upon the head
Of lordly Kongurbay,
And scatter the four kinds of livestock
Of the chief who struck you from above.
I took courage and made ready;
For black-Chinese Beijing,
I have longed to fight.
If I travel toward the khan,
And travel far, not a little,
Why should I trouble
My small Kyrgyz people and drive them as an army,
Bringing them calamity,
Making every one of them weep,
Compelling them with cries
That I am Manas's son?
What inheritance would I attain?
Against the teeming infidel,
Against warrior Khan Kongurbay,
I will go with four men.
To ride in the hot desert,
And breach and enter
The fortress of the fortified black Chinese,
I will take fourteen horses from Ogoy
And receive the old saint's blessing.
The warrior known to the people,
Left by my royal elder, the lion,
Is far-seeing Bakay.
I have my uncle Bakay,
Sated with campaigns and roads,
Who brought a calamity
Upon Beijing.
My heroic uncle Bakay
Is the waist I gird upon my own!
My support, like a vast army to my soul,
Master of roads and knower of lands,
Is he not skillful Bakay,
And in Beijing where battles were waged,
Is he not renowned Bakay?
My uncle Bakay who knows the road,
My grandfather like a prophet,
My uncle Bakay like a father,
Who sits giving counsel,
I will lead onto the road to Beijing,
Whence no traveler returns—
My tiger uncle Bakay.
