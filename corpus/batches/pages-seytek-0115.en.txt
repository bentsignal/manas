Deprived of my swift steed,
I came back a clumsy wretch.
Deprived of flowing mane and tail,
I came back dull and broken.
My two eyes gouged out,
My two shoulders flayed,
Captured and grieving,
Parted from my beloved,
With every spirit beaten from me,
I came back meek as a mullah.
Captured and disgraced,
My days passing in humiliation,
I came back reduced to a pacing nag.
My hands bound, my eyes staring,
I came back trapped in a net.
From a place no man could escape,
I came back as from a fortress.
To the infidel in his full strength,
To mighty hero Kyyaz,
I went as plunder.
I went after breaking my own pride,
I went and surrendered myself as spoil.
In her old age, Kanykei,
And when Chyyrdy had grown aged—
Killing my lord day after day,
He dragged away from Talas
My two venerable mothers,
He plundered my people,
He broke my back,
And raided my village in Keng-Kol.
Kanchoro took away
Akbala, daughter of Jamgyrchy—
My wife, the fair Akbala.
Those swine killed
My giant warrior Er Sarykan,
They shattered my brain
And brought me endless grief.
When I recall the old days,
These ribs of mine
Part layer from layer.
After that, I went about
Wishing to die and leave this world.
Then I saw your orphaned eyes,
Young falcon left by the hero,
And longed to behold you.
Eager to see you, I hurried,
Staggering beneath firewood,
Beaten with clubs by the herdsmen,
Ravaged and thrashed,
With cudgels raining on my back.
“Master, show me mercy,” I begged,
Pleading even with lowly slaves.
Your uncle went about flayed raw,
With no strength left to fight,
His shoulders deeply gouged.
Your uncle, once like a mighty stallion,
Became pliant as leather,
And all I saw was turmoil.
Instead of food, I drank
Nine bucketfuls of water,
Stretched out like a hide,
While a horse’s knobby ankle bone
Was given me in place of food.
Your uncle suffered pillage,
His precious body endured beatings.
Clubs struck my back,
Spikes pierced my rump.
I set out to gather firewood,
And three times every day
I was wrapped in thorns.
I was mauled by strongmen,
Beaten by the herdsmen.
Long did I carry firewood,
Hoping, if I did not die, to see
The orphan left as our legacy.
May God never inflict upon another
The humiliations I endured,
My son, nor the torment that befell me.
My horns broken, left forlorn,
I came back shorn of all power.
