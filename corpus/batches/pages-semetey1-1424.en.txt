I have described his measure in this way;
That is the look of Karagul!
How much have I told you of Karagul,
Swarming like ants—
That is the multitude of horses!
His geldings outnumber his stallions;
His herd contains every kind:
Mottled roans and blaze-faced horses;
That swine’s herd
Mixes argamaks and thoroughbreds.
It has bracelet-marked gray horses,
And untouched horses that have never been ridden.
Among the livestock are wondrous coursers;
They are among those horses,
And even Kambar-Ata is there.
That swine Karagul will not leave his horses;
While Karagul remains alive,
Your undertaking will not prosper!
Wondrous creatures, the finest of livestock—
Kongurbai’s countless horses
Are all consummate coursers.
There are horses with tails an armful thick;
He gathered the coursers with a clamor,
And the mighty one seized them all.
I have said much—understand;
My mighty orphan, heed my words!
I will tell you the road to take:
Sometimes visible, sometimes not,
The cursed place lies
Where the blue haze parts—
That is the desert where no living soul travels.
Drawing this way from there,
That is the lake of It-Ichpes.
Where the gray haze can be seen
Is the edge of Uzun-Too.
The Kara-Chinese and the many Manchus
Dig gold in that mine.
When you reach that place
And turn to your left,
There is a gaping gorge.
Left behind by Manas, I, Bakai,
Tell many of my sorrows.
Yesterday, when the gray wolves went scouting,
My champion Almam, called Saiypburush,
Went forth,
There was a pass called Tuman-Kyr;
Looking directly across from it,
There was a place called Madangshong.
The thoroughbred Aisarala—
That is where my noble gray wolf Alma
Pulled its mouth and turned it;
Of the infidel like a black worm,
Of hero Khan Kongurbai,
That is where my dear elder brother took his measure
And kept watch upon his sentry post!
When you reach that place,
My formidable one left by the hero,
Ride on along the road
And raise your spyglass to your eye!
Who pastures at Chok-Tabylgy,
Who tethers his great black horse,
The infidel son of Kaldai,
Hero Khan Kongurbai—
Get sound intelligence of him!
This year the mighty swine, khan of the Kechil—
The impetuous great Kongur—
Has taken command of the Manchu army
And is gathering it, I hear.
“Of the orphan son of Manas,
How great has his valor become?”
Of everyone who comes and goes,
He goes about asking, I hear.
Beyond those horse herds,
On this side of the Sary-Suu,
Where seven rivers unite at their head,
There lies Kaldai’s Kara-Suu.
Honored orphan, heed my words:
There lies this dark peril!
