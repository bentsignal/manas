The red-steel Zulpukor
Seems destined to take up.
He seems destined to rule the people
And govern the whole world.
His bearing is that of a wrestler;
He seems born to lead the people and rule the realm,
To stir turmoil throughout the world
And win resounding fame.
He himself is a fierce and wrathful lion;
If only this hero were in our Khakan’s realm!
Strong at the hilt and sharp at the point,
The man-killing Zulpukor
Is a blade this son will draw;
He is a son destined to unleash
A great upheaval across the world.
Yesterday—beads, coral, and precious stones,
And the blood of Lungtun’s khan are mingled in him.
However closely we look, no such pillar can be found;
We have seen many men,
But no hero like this has arisen from China.”
Thus the Chinese cried out,
As horns and trumpets sounded,
And powder and shot were loaded onto the rhinos.
A pale-gray banner became their standard,
Their tumult split the sky,
And the uproar swelled ever louder.
Beside the army of heroic Kongurbay,
That mightiest of infidels,
The people of Kendeme stood
With Keng-Tung.
As for the Kalcha Kongurbay,
His spear was beyond all price,
And in that Kalcha’s host
There was nothing they did not possess.
Within his seething army
Were wrestlers of a thousand kinds;
Kongurbay’s warfare
Surpassed all the ages.
Among his champions
Were snarling infidels,
Heroes versed in the ways of the world;
His host was filled with mighty men,
With seers and giants,
The lords of Great Beijing.
There was black-bearded Dyngsha,
Who had studied occult arts,
And many formidable diviners;
There was an iron-rope net,
And Tegiz-Kanjarkolcho,
Whose every charge struck home;
Foot soldiers whom no swine could sate,
Who tore the life from men,
As though they would devour every giant they met,
As though they would finish everything at once,
And, if they bellowed, leave the world
In utter ruin.
What accursed creatures they were,
Riding mules and bearing spears!
Anyone who saw them recoiled;
The infidel’s champions,
His champions, were unlike any others.
Anyone who saw them recoiled;
Those rabid warriors
Had studied occult arts.
His seers and lions
Foretold the Altai-born torment
That was now to come.
He drove his seer forward,
And the seer examined
The valiant gray-wolf Choro.
Before Prince Kong, huge as a furnace—
The mighty prince known throughout the world—
Beside that great lord
Stood the Manchu khan Ezkara.
While all were standing in their places,
The seer came before them.
Bowing with frenzied reverence
And treading carefully where he stepped,
