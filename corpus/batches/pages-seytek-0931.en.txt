Gathering clouds of dust behind him.
Ask no more of Sarybay’s state:
Bull-necked and camel-thighed,
He ruined all who grappled with him,
Subjugated all whom he confronted,
And stirred conflict throughout the world.
His looming shadow spread across the earth;
At the words, “Sarybay is coming,”
Even speechless infants
Lying in their cradles fell silent.
His wrath was known among the people;
Any mortal who went against him
Was known to despair of life.
He hewed down all who encountered him
And devoured all who opposed him.
Such was that knave, brave Sarybay:
No man could advance against him,
And he valued no one’s worth.
Mighty indeed was Sarybay;
Behold what a dreadful one he was.
Like a boar that devastated nations,
When Kuyaly thrust her spear at him,
He cried, “I have taken a spear-blow from a girl!
Blood-soaked bitch, you!”
His face darkened and his aspect changed;
Wrath choked him.
He looked before and behind,
Licking his lips like a hungry wolf.
His lashes burned like embers,
And he poised himself like a darting snake.
Unable to contain his fury,
Sarybay unleashed his wrath.
Under the force of his menace,
When the blackguard grew angry, the earth shook,
And seemed about to collapse.
His shriek rang piercingly,
Like a bolt of lightning;
The breath from his mouth
Was like the embers of burning saxaul.
His fury was unmatched, his venom strong;
Such was the sight of him.
If he charged in wrath,
His assault seemed bound to end all.
His dangling beard
And bushy mustache
Were such that pied crows and magpies
Could fearlessly roost in them.
At nightfall they might lodge there till dawn;
Whoever encountered him would spill blood.
Such was Sarybay’s nature.
He turned Tor-Kyzyl’s head;
Surely God had struck
That hellish, wrathful swine.
Before one could cry out,
He wheeled Tor-Kyzyl around.
With his nine-knobbed steel spear,
Before the nimble woman could pull it round,
And hold it twisted at the ready,
Before Zhelzhetpes could find his bearing
And come straight onto the path,
He thrust his spear without pause…
A great calamity began.
Atop Zhelzhetpes,
The consummate warrior, the lioness,
Was a girl whose strength boiled over,
A girl who had overcome the whole world
Through wrestling and combat.
She neither swayed nor flinched,
And cared nothing if she died.
Sarybay’s thrust,
And all the havoc he wrought,
She did not count worth her boot-tip.
Her heart did not falter,
And the cowardly knave’s spear
She deemed no harder than tumbleweed.
When Sarybay drove in his spear
And it crashed against the warrior maiden,
Yet she did not so much as stir,
Sarybay flew into a rage
And shouted to his vast army:
