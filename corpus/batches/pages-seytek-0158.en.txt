From the renowned, magnanimous gray-maned hero,
My Morning Star left to me as a gift,
The support for which I yearned,
The prop bound about my waist.
From the noble one gone to the next world,
My precious little one left behind.
In the marshes of Kara-Kyya,
When the massed enemy fired their guns
And surrounded the magnanimous hero,
My uncle had told me:
“If the orphan survives,
Make him your support.
Make the wooded Keng-Kol and broad Talas
His shelter,” he had said.
“Take the boy Seytek who remains
And make him your own child.”
When my uncle spoke and departed,
I blazed and burned with grief.
For the uncle who had gone before,
For brave Seytek left as his legacy—
I burned with longing for you.
Yesterday, when the panther vanished,
I wailed and lamented in grief,
Hoping my refuge Seytek would come.
My river valley and spring,
My champion beheld when death was near,
I have longed for you.
Amid nothing but humiliation and misery,
I have burned for twelve years,
Thinking you would come alone.
“If my orphan should arrive,
He will overcome all whom he faces;
He will loosen one shackle
From this wretch who suffered grievously.
He will make the cruel flee;
He will cut off the enemy’s head,
Make splendid havoc in the slaughter,
And crush the cruel, formidable foe
To take his vengeance.”
So I have waited for you.
My poor head, which drank no kumis,
Thirsted even for muddy water.
When the magnanimous hero’s eyes closed,
Everything ended, and I became like snow.
When the noble gray-maned hero lived,
This head, which drank no liquor,
Thirsted for sour drink.
When my gray-maned uncle lived,
He bestowed good fortune.
Into my mouth, where no millet grain could fit,
A yearling calf’s head fitted with ease.
My panther uncle, the thoroughbred,
Sacked Bukhara and bestowed good fortune.
After my thoroughbred uncle passed away,
Into my mouth, where no grain of wheat could fit,
Even a bull’s head fitted with ease.
Every beating I saw became poison;
Everything I ate became a knucklebone.
My vigor failed and my strength departed;
From your laughing uncle, the mighty hero,
My power ebbed and my blood drained away.
What befell me was an iron cage;
What I endured was toil and misery;
What befell me was a deep pit.
Now that my brave orphan has come,
My ancient pain has stirred again.
Now that my extinguished fire has kindled,
Now that God has granted my destiny,
Now that what I prayed for has arrived,
Let me open the darkness to the light,
Let me calm my fevered heart,
And open wide my dimmed eyes.
My hero, cut down by calamity,
My champion left by the precious one,
My majesty left by the noble one,
My courage that marches before me,
My spring flowing close at hand,
Quenching my thirst when I am parched,
My champion beneath the planted banner,
My sturdy boy turning gray and white,
