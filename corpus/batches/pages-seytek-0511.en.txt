To rescue him,
To die if death should come,
To grapple with
The fearsome champion Sarybai.
To defeat the cruel foe,
To shatter his stone city—
Seytek’s mind is set
On rescuing Kara Döö,
If he is still alive.
Before the army, brave Seytek
Leveled his white gun and fired,
Raised a clamor like Khan Manas,
With a hungry shout that split stone,
With fierce courage that broke heads;
Whoever saw him stood amazed:
He loomed like a blue mountain.
His lashes were burning embers,
His wrath became an arrow;
When he saw the foe he must fight,
How could he remain still?
Tumult rose before the army,
And a lion’s roar rang out.
Could dear young Seytek,
True son of the bloodthirsty khan,
Ever flee from battle?
Behold how the spirits
Of his fathers upheld him!
A black-mottled leopard
Crouched at his side,
As if about to tear flesh;
A black-maned lion
Waited close beside him,
As if ready to spring and strike.
A gray tiger went before him—
No living creature was its equal.
Looming like a blue mountain,
The giant Zymyrik black eagle
Seemed ready to swoop,
Stretching out its talons
As though about to seize him,
Carry the world’s hero away,
And cross the rocky peaks.
It thrust its talons wide:
One of the seven black eagles
Truly stood protecting him.
Forty tigers lined the ridge—
No creatures like them had long been seen.
What he faced became flame,
His spirit became a glowing coal;
Once he saw the fearsome foe,
How could the valiant son
Of his father remain still?
The dappled bay with swaying ornaments,
The heavy, hostile dappled bay;
His foe in combat was Sarybai—
His splendor was of another kind.
His fury was mountain-like,
As though he might devour
The whole wide world at once.
He sprang like a tiger;
None survived his blows.
The army roared, filling the fortress,
Surging and blazing like fire.
They stirred to close in combat;
The troops clamored, covering the earth,
Crossing their steel-tipped spears,
Binding their lone fly-like lives
To a single hair,
They crossed the mountain pass
And descended onward
Into the plain of El-Jürbös.
The army settled amid a roar,
Set out food and slaughtered colts,
And had all the coursing steeds
Tethered along the lines.
From barren, well-fed young mares
They boiled their meal.
They finely dressed and slaughtered colts,
Then calmly laid out the feast.
