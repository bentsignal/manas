Its haft was gold, its edge sharp.
When Turks came from Bukhara,
Surpassing all after taking food,
He was a friend of David—
The master smith Bölökbai,
Had made the poor thing.
Stars shifted in the sky;
David and Bolokbay
Became true friends.
Their charcoal swelled and melted;
The two joined together.
Unable to withstand their charcoal,
The forested mountainside was hollowed;
Unable to withstand their bellows,
A levy fell upon the Kyrgyz
And many young bulls were slaughtered.
They joined and formed its point;
"Let whoever it touches die in pain,"
They tempered it six times
In a dragon's venom.
Its edge was red, its mouth fire;
Whomever it struck died at once.
Whoever faced it found bitterness;
One whose death had come met it.
Golden-cheeked battle-axe,
Gold-adorned side pouch,
Its excellence visible to the people—
No one dared face it.
Your valiant-born sultan,
Terrible in bearing and strong in venom,
The true son of your generous lord,
Was deeply troubled.
When one looked before him,
The generous lord's young son
Had the might of six thousand men;
When one looked behind him,
He bore the form of a dragon.
Towering high along the shortcut,
The mountain-like Buurul—
Dear Kanchoro led him.
Upon that Roan's head
Was a white halter knotted in loops,
A fitted halter worthy
Of the mountain-like stallion.
Without haste or confusion, the heroes
Now saddled and prepared
The mountain-like Roan.
Semetey's Akkangky,
A Kangky fitted to the dapple stallion,
Its pommel pure gold,
Its cantle solid silver,
Both bows broad iron—
Akkangky for riding downhill.
To go to the Jediger,
To arrive and begin slaughter,
Displaying his gray-maned courage,
From before the golden throne,
The mountain-like Buurul
They brought and mounted Semetey upon him.
They readied his weapons
And prepared every piece of armor.
Your lord came out apart,
Looking like a solitary mountain.
Your lord was alone,
Your sultan was unsurpassed.
Beside that lord
There was no forest to flee into,
No Kyrgyz to make a host.
He had no waist girded beside him,
No place of ambush.
He had no fortune founded at Keng-Kol,
No time to stride proudly.
The gray-maned son of Manas
Had courage beyond all measure.
The Argyn who would have ridden as support
Was not there.
He had no khan to find counsel;
The Jediger were set to shoot,
