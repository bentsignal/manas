“He would become a support at my waist.
You took me when I was a fledgling,
Saying, ‘My legacy left by the dear departed,
My child left by my khan and elder brother.’
You fondled and indulged me,
Never gainsaying my word.
You made your mother divide
The milk of her breast and nurse me.
Kicking together like colt and yearling,
Competing as we suckled her breast,
You made my cause one with yours.
Just when we were about to take
The two reins and single lead in hand,
And rule the entire world,
When, upon the earth the Creator made,
We were to live as sovereigns,
You dug my grave deep
And brought my sorrow thus to the boil.
You had my free hands bound,
Had my dark liver crushed,
And my hide of leather flayed.
Alas, because of you, bloodthirsty warrior,
Why was I beaten down
And plunged into so many sorrows?
Whatever I grasped became embers,
My lungs became leaden shot;
What I had not seen became blindness,
And I burned until I became black earth.
My horns broke, though my head remained;
I was ruined like a hornless beast.
Deprived of mane and tail,
Tormented, burning, and grieving,
I was ruined like a clay vessel.
Deprived of both hands,
For a time I was utterly wretched;
Deprived of both eyes,
For a time I was blind.
My turn passed and I was diminished,
I became the equal of a cripple,
A shriveled blind man.
From the beginning, this gray-maned lion
Was called the young gray-maned lion.
He was a hero and a lion;
Those who opposed him became black earth.
The stranger’s affairs prospered,
And the man who fought him vanished.
Such was Külchoro’s nature.
Unable to endure his fury,
He confronted his bloodthirsty lord;
The poor soul could not contain himself,
His heart burned and became flame.
Coiling like a venomous snake,
The warrior Choro, the gray-maned lion, grew fierce;
His heart burned into embers—
How could he possibly stand still?
Burning and blazing, he turned sideways,
And the noble Choro began to speak:
“You were a heroic, gray-maned lord,
The mighty protector on whom I relied.
No foe could overcome your snowy heights,
No enemy could advance against you.
You were a towering black mountain pass,
And I was the one who took shelter in you.
Hero, you were orphaned of your father,
You grew to manhood as an orphan,
And barely rejoined your people.
When you reached twelve years of age,
You became a blazing ember,
A leaping red flame.
You shattered royal camps and took their spoils,
And attacked mighty nations.
Sated with blood, you grew fierce,
And were named the bloodthirsty gray-maned lion.
You enriched the people of the valleys;
When those you hated fell into your hands,
You laid Khan Jakyp low.
You sent Köbösh into the grave
And revealed your gray-maned valor.
You conquered the Sary-Kaikan pass,
And seized every land your heart desired.
