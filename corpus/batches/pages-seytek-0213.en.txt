“If my strength fails and proves too little,
If I falter and retreat from the clash,
May I endure misery unknown among men;
And if I do not die
At the hands of Kyyaz of the Jediger,
May I early behold at the infidel’s hands
The narrow road from which none return.
But if my strength rises and proves enough,
May I throw mighty Kyyaz
Flat upon his back,
Drag him along the road,
And proclaim it to the Dangyt people;
May I scoop up and drink my fill
Of the black blood within his heart,
And only then cast aside
The anguished misery of my humiliation.
When I fight Kyyaz,
May I lay him flat upon his back.
He loudly seized booty,
And slaughtered my strongest men;
He butchered all our heroes,
And cut down so many
Of our tender young warriors.
He crushed our gray wolves,
And spilled the blood of many people;
He slew my falcon outright,
And took every southern woman,
Old and young, as plunder.
He plundered my migrating people,
Binding us together with ropes,
Thrusting spears with deadly aim,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
And fighting in a roaring tumult;
He scattered my caravan across the open plain.
From dragon-like Kyyaz,
By the power of Father God,
I mean to take my revenge.
Ah, accursed Kyyaz,
Once I have traced your lineage,
From that vicious dog himself—
At the hollow of Kashatchy,
On Khan Kyyaz’s road,
Beside the new irrigation ditch,
By the echoing cliff,
At the narrow pass for a single horse,
At the junction of nine roads,
Where wolves tear the carcasses of sheep,
On the shoulder where the mountains end,
At the place where Kyyaz would come,
Külchoro, guarded by the spirits,
Lay with a white stone for his pillow,
His head turned toward the qibla,
Wrapped in his bulletproof armor,
His weapons prepared.
His outstretched leg did not draw back,
His closed eyes did not open,
His snoring did not cease;
He slept soundly, utterly unconcerned,
Regarding mighty Kyyaz’s coming
As worth no more than the hem of his robe.
Courage and lionhearted valor
Had been granted beyond measure
To Choro, guarded by the spirits.
Though lion-born brave Kyyaz
Was riding down upon him,
He valued him less than the hem of his robe.
Courage and proud daring
The Almighty Lord
Had bestowed without measure
Upon the lion and gray wolf Choro.
He did not think Kyyaz of the Jediger
Was any mighty lion;
He gave no thought to death,
Counted true peril as nothing,
And did not spare his tiny life:
“May I bring utter grief
Upon brave Kyyaz;
Though I have been plundered and crushed,
May I fight tirelessly for six months
And somehow avenge my wrongs.
