Dust flew into the sky;
The army spread in a dusty glitter.
They advanced with battle-axes in hand,
Swords striking from their sheaths.
Their divisions split and opened,
Their multitude plain to the people.
They were many tens of thousands,
An army by no means small.
The people bustled and dust swirled;
They seemed to fill the face of the world.
The layered black earth below
Smoked thickly
Beneath Kanchoro's wrath.
The earth turned gray amid clamor;
A din arose that split the sky.
Haze spread and darkened it.
The multitude of Zhediger
Flooded over the land
Until they seemed one with the earth.
The cunning and the mighty,
Those who broke ranks with spears,
Met the men of the gate;
Those whose death had come fell to bullets.
Shouting the cry, "Kanchoro Khan!"
The furious myriad army
Raised the battle cry, "Kiyaz!"
They flew in like flies,
Surrounded them, writhing like worms,
And streamed like ants.
The gatekeepers and Zhediger
Stood facing one another,
Firing cannon and bows,
Quarreling and fighting.
Kanchoro had taken a wife
From among all the Zhediger,
From their accomplished Khan Kiyaz,
Their lion of noble custom,
Their truly courageous hero.
Gathering the Zhediger,
Kanchoro gave counsel
And roused them to fury.
He had sharpened his sword keenly;
Trampling some men,
Gnawing, cutting, and berating others,
Kanchoro drove them onward.
He surrounded the gate in an uproar,
To reach and plunder
The widowed, orphaned woman,
And bring calamity upon her.
With his cruel design, Kanchoro
Drove the countless troops without respite
And gathered them together.
He beat down many without pause
And slaughtered them.
Kanchoro's intention
Was to plunder the palace.
Displaying all his cunning—
Such was the dog-taken villain,
Hiding his claws like a snow leopard,
Displaying his valor against the foe.
Ah, accursed one whose road will fail,
Hiding his claws like a tiger!
Long ago, when the leopard entered battle
And charged straight at the enemy,
While the valiant gray wolf still lived,
There was the famous great war drum.
Its hide was from a wild bull;
Such was the drum's design.
When you hear its voice, flee far away.
Facing toward the enemy,
Raising the fortunate spear,
The hero beat it as he entered.
Ordinary foes
Simply lost their courage.
That drum left by the hero
Thundered again and again,
And the army lost its wits.
The army lost all sense.
