If all the eighteen thousand worlds
Were gathered together entire,
There would be no youth like him;
He was a flaming red arrow.
She marveled as she looked:
Almighty God creates
People in every different way.
The horse he rode was bay;
Unless Semetey's fortune was greater,
He was a man a full fathom tall.
Kanchoro, son of Chubak,
Was a black-hearted man;
And Tölöbay, son of Tölök,
Was a man who could make
A band of Jediger cry out in distress.
That I flew away without testing them
Was a God-stricken affair.”
Unable to find her bearings,
Your sister-in-law was sorely troubled.
She could neither follow behind him,
Nor go openly and marry him;
Your unfortunate Chürök was a marvel,
The poor woman kept changing form.
Semetey, son of the gray wolf,
Circled around Kötörmö;
He had seven bustards and six geese
Taken and bound together.
He made Buurul surge beneath him,
Made him strain as though his master had died;
He loomed like Opol-Too.
Each time Chürök saw him,
Her heart was painfully divided,
And she stood watching from concealment.
From the fortress with its gates,
From Chachykey's encampment,
The pampered wife Chachykey,
Wrapped in a lynx-fur coat,
Prepared to speak fondly
As she came before Semetey:
“You say you will fly the falcon each day,
And leave me burning with worry;
You linger long upon the steppe
And bring me this suffering.
Though I am here, you say you will fly the falcon
And cast me into distress;
You go far out upon the steppe,
Where there are many pale, broken mountains,
And many enemies ready to grapple with you.
There are Chinese along the frontier;
You have many deeds to perform.
A gun is before you,
And many enemies await a contest.
The Kyrgyz migrate through Talas;
Many people there bear grudges against you.
The Kyrgyz migrate through Keng-Kol;
Everyone you meet bears a grudge against you.
Month after month, saying you will fly your falcon,
You go away into the steppe,
Leaving me staring like a fool
And placing me in misery.”
Chachykey spoke fondly in this way
And walked directly toward him;
She became exceedingly affectionate.
Semey the hero, son of Manas,
That learned man who had studied scripture, said:
“We flew the falcon and surveyed the land;
It thoroughly inspected the watchposts.
We had seven bustards and six geese
Taken for us,
“Senior wife, we have brought them as a gift.”
So he said,
Then he put his hands behind his back,
That dear, immovable hero.
That dear, immovable lion,
When alone with his wife,
Was utterly overcome.
That dear lion, tender toward women,
Climbed down from his horse with a ringing sound.
