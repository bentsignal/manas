I shall take my vengeance at last.
Dragging him along the road,
I shall expose him to the Dangyt.
Making my gray-wolf nature known,
Sending the arrogant one into the grave,
I shall slaughter much of his people,
And that accursed fugitive,
Together with his slaves, all at once,
I shall destroy, and only then rest.
I shall plunder all he owns,
Thus shall I wreak the mischief.
The bloodthirsty one who came raging,
I shall utterly finish.”
Turning back and crying “Chu!”
Chynkojo departed in wrath;
Abandoning the struggle then,
Chynkojo went to his city.
To take abundant ammunition,
Chynkojo went away.
To bring calamity upon your mighty one,
Chynkojo went away.
Let us leave him there,
And begin the news of
The lion prince Semetey.
The Ak-Olpok upon him,
The coat your fierce heroic mother
Had made firm, could be seen.
Whenever the lion moved,
From atop Ak-Olpok
Bullets poured like hail.
When he saw Kojo's bullets,
The prince's heart was shaken;
Jezail bullets and bow-shafts,
Whenever he moved then,
Spilled to the ground like rain.
“If Chynkojo turns and comes back,
I despair of my precious life;
The end of days will appear.”
God's sun in heaven was overcast,
And upon the lone lion of his father
Sixty worries came at once.
The bullet's blow had struck hard,
Striking deep into his breastbone;
When he cried, “Oh!” from his mouth
Blood gushed out in spurts.
His strength left him, his insides burned,
And power departed from your lion.
If the moon is darkened by the sun,
That is what the night is like, he thought;
When its light comes,
That is what the day is like, he thought.
In anger his words came to an end,
He despaired of his precious life,
And grief smoldered deep within him.
There was no steed to ride into battle,
No kinsman to hear his last testament.
“What will the day bring?” he thought;
There was no forest into which to flee,
No Nogai to serve as his support.
My being a hero is beyond compare,
But when I bowed, the one who found counsel,
Dear Bakai, was not here.
Before the mountain-like Kojo,
It seems I shall come to grief.
I shall die an unclean death,
A feast for crow and raven.
With no man and no people,
It seems I shall remain on the mountain pass.
Clutching Buurul and grieving,
It seems I shall remain in the desert.
Strength left the Buurul horse,
And from the mouth of lion prince Semey
Black blood flowed.
The bullet's blow had struck hard,
His whole body grew numb,
He stood looking dazed;
The forlorn Ak-Kelte
Had no bullet for a single shot,
