Why not take brave Toltoy's Surkoen
As the chief horse?
Why not appoint Chynkozho's Black Horse
As the ceremonial mount,
And take those two tulpars
From the mountain-like heroes?
Why not agree to what I say?
Saying, 'A maiden's shame is her sale,'
Only after that should you give me."
After Chürök had spoken thus,
Her two elder brothers bustled about,
Prancing as they spoke persuasive words:
"Agreed, it shall be agreed," they said;
"May the seven forefathers be infidels
Of anyone who does not take
Those two tulpars as the ceremonial gift."
After they had spoken so:
"My dear two elder brothers,
Walking between the two sides,
You have grown thin;
Enduring much suffering and violence,
You have grown worn and old.
I have slaughtered a barren yearling mare,
One turned loose for recreation,
A sight that satisfies longing,
So my elder brothers may eat;
I have laid out food and drink.
Grow stout and refreshed, accept my hospitality;
Afterward, hand me over.
My father is old and I am a girl;
Have I any remedy against Toltoy?
My mother is old and I am a girl;
Have I any will of my own against Toltoy?
If you tell me to go, I shall go;
Without causing slaughter for my sake,
I shall preserve alive
The great multitude of Afghans.
Take off your boots and stretch your feet,
Drink spirits and eat food;
Grow stout and refreshed.
After that, elder brothers,
Stand firm without fear or alarm,
And lay claim to the two horses!"
Having said this, Aychürök
Took the inner shortcut
And led Azhybay and Tümönbay
To the palace
She herself had built.
Aychürök's white palace
Was magnificent beyond measure.
At the entrance to its door
Stood eighty warriors like Bakay;
She bit her lip, narrowed her eyes,
And now flicked her brow.
It was the signal meaning, "Open it."
Her warriors gathered
And pulled open the gate.
Leading her two elder brothers,
That clever dark-faced kinswoman
Brought them inside
The splendid palace.
Aychürök had forty young attendants,
All beautiful, skilled young women;
When this Chürök placed them beside her,
All forty young women
Bent their knees to the ground,
And ran swift as arrows.
On this side, all forty women
Ran and now raised
The curtain together.
If you looked to the far side,
Fourteen tulpars stood tethered.
When they saw these two men,
All fourteen tulpars strained;
They no longer appeared as horses,
But seemed true tigers.
At the sight of them,
The two men's hearts were torn with desire.
