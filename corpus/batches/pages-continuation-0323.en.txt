Kökbörü of the people of Rum,
The lion-maiden Aikanysh,
Having heard she was a peerless beauty,
Had come to the lands of Talas,
To wed Kenen;
From the one Creator God,
This was what dear Seytek the gray wolf
Had prayed for.
Akjoltoy and Seytek the lion
Had gone there as intermediaries.
The people raised and covered a yurt,
And they had gone there to stay.
The hero’s heir went to see
The foremost of maidens,
And they had gone to give
A great herd as bride-price.
They had come to the land of Rum;
This was what they desired.
Hero Bolot stood upon the hill,
While a thousand troops stood below;
Aikanysh was a warrior among maidens,
Ready to raise a quarrel at the slightest stir.
The two heroes, Akjoltoy and Seytek,
Caused neither dispute nor strife;
Saying they had come to arrange a marriage,
The encircling people waited upon them.
For the six thousand headed by horses,
We have now tied on cotton tufts;
Everything we gathered and acquired,
We dedicated to Aikanysh.
We are neither warriors nor lions;
Should six thousand head be too poor a gift,
Take Akjoltoy’s Kulunsur,
Who tears up the ground beneath his hooves,
Who gnaws upon his bit,
Whose parrot-like tail streams behind,
Who, faced with a ninety-day desert,
Races onward without restraint;
They declared him a perfect thoroughbred,
And twelve foremost judges
Among the sharp-eyed old women
Tested the brass-muzzled steed.
Take my thoroughbred Kulunsur;
I will spare nothing for Kenen.
If this wealth of mine proves too little,
Raise a great tumult and take my life.
Take jewels, gold, abundant silver,
Take my vast treasury and livestock!
To this corner of the world
We have come to become neighbors.
Before the descendants of noble khans,
We bow in homage and offer wealth;
We have come to make peace.
We shall settle securely beside your throne,
And gather round as in-laws;
Driving livestock for the marriage pact,
We shall meet one another with honor—
May our souls feast upon your celebration.
Our forefathers joined us first in friendship;
Hero Bolot, you yourself
Seal the marriage bond and add to our joy.
Having said this, hero Seytek
Closed his heroic lips.
Then the imposing and incomparable
Hero Bolot began to speak:
—The sign of those who share one root
Is that they intermarry and join their lives,
Embrace and become friends,
Devise wonders no man could conceive,
And hold a feast that feeds the people.
They cut the ceremonial sapling together
And plan a celebration that stirs the nation;
They fulfill every hope
And fill the whole treasury
With goods of yellow gold.
The brave man’s daughter Aikanysh
Was herself a valiant, ill-fated soul.
She bound up hair long as meadow grass
And wore garments of lofty splendor.
Would the warrior-maiden Aikanysh
Be withheld from you by the mighty khan?
We heard word from the caravans,
Heard that Kenen, son of hero Seytek,
Had arisen as a hero;
Surging and roaring like a lake,
We heard that a champion had arisen;
One who could fell forty in a short day,
We heard that a mighty warrior had arisen;
One who would not retreat before a lion,
We heard that a valiant man had arisen.
We wished to open our eyes and behold him,
We wished to give him Aikanysh,
We wished to pitch our village at Talas,
To unite and become one,
And dwell upon a single hill;
If a vast enemy horde should come,
If a foe that shatters ribs should come,
We wished to answer together,
And camp in one settlement.
