Let us scatter their blood like seed,
Let us strike at their people,
[Uncertain: They have made that land their camp,]
Saying, ‘If you wish, a detachment of a thousand,’
Let us overrun Tobochokon,
[Uncertain: Let us set the rolling host aflame,]
We shall deal with their people as we see fit,
We shall plunder without leaving a soul and then rest.
[Uncertain: Let us despoil those who are broken,]
If the accursed enemy breaks apart,
Let us make it one realm,
Among the people who raised their royal camp,
After taking counsel, we shall appoint a champion,
Of the Burut who poses as a hero,
One who would plunder everything,
Making a khan from among the Kalmyks,
Laying low whoever opposes us,
Let us make their land a fortress,
Rather than leave even one among the Burut,
[Uncertain: Let us take the land and learn their intent.]
Learn the disposition they have,
Learn their hidden ways from their backs.
Their brave men and their marksmen,
Learn them again and again.
Putting their very lives into it,
Without revealing their inner secret,
They learn one another’s intent beforehand.
How many roads there are, how many cliffs,
The bounds of their barriers,
The mountain passes girded with barriers,
The places they have bound fast,
Their great lords and their khans,
By name—
[Uncertain: Distinguish what people they are called,]
Of the souls accustomed to that land,
Observe them carefully from afar.
Taking measure of how long the road is,
Come and range across this ground.
Go out scouting and survey the land,
Keep watch and reconnoiter the country,
Let us see which way there is movement.
Take the measure of the army,
Stand ready again, comrades,
Heroes ready for the hunt,
Enter the battle without fleeing,
Fulfil the duty owed to the people,
Go today for the people of Beijing,
For the people and lives of the forty khans,
For all of this,
For the hero Khan Chyngteñir,
Who would die as a sacrifice,
If you go, do not stay behind—truth or falsehood,
The mortal who was born has seen it.
Behind him he has a splendid swift one,
He has a champion complete in every way.
He stayed watching this closely,
From him came my seven forefathers,
The common Kyrgyz move in an uproar,
They saw the full extent of it all.
Having seen it, make this known,
Its sovereign was wrathful,
If so, break the roadward side,
He goes on, supporting and driving them,
He had remained after seeing many things.
They are numerous, but have no sense,
They are a thriving Chinese people,
If you sit and look upon their multitude,
They are black vermin beyond counting,
Unable to master even this small people,
Your driving them has brought disgrace.
Short of wit and without honor,
With no brave man to inspire awe,
With little pride and without honor,
Their heads are dark, their antlers forked,
These seem to be a hostile people to me.
Without courage and without honor,
What sort of great creature has emerged?
Kakan-chyn, khan of forty in battle,
This Beijing upon which light has fallen,
Owes a debt of words to our father,
Striking Aidarot against the people,
There is our father's grievance in it.
My Kaldais, my commanders,
A pearl-stone fit to wear as a necklace,
My warriors who stand dressed for battle,
My marksman reared for the letter's summons,
My hero who was left childless,
I joined what had not been joined,
Having seen much, both great and evil,
I asked the noblewomen.
After asking, I myself,
Burned and baked until I became an ember,
I mourned what had come for its sake.
I shall stand towering like a giant,
I waited and asked for their chief,
Neither eating food nor taking a meal,
I am wounded as though struck by a bullet,
My age has been overturned,
Thinking, I longed for many things.
In the fortress-gardens of Beijing,
In the time of my forefather, the radiant man,
Many wondrous decrees are written,
When I look upon the many decrees,
Dwelling in Alabotu,
A man who yielded to no one in combat!
