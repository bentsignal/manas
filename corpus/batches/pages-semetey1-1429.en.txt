Loping with a bounding gait,
Gathering itself in rolling surges,
Sweeping on as though pitching headlong,
He flew along upon Buurul.
Delighting in its pace,
Behind him the dust
Trailed like a mountain.
His sword buckled on in eagerness,
Your gray wolf, blessed by Kydyr,
Rounded Kaspan’s Kara-Too,
Crossing rugged mountain country,
And when he came to certain places,
Crossing shimmering desert wastes.
The noble hero journeyed without stint;
The horse covered a vast distance.
How could your valiant hero spare himself?
He drew near the herds,
Your young champion, the noble lord’s son,
Approaching Kyr-Kaying.
When he reached Kyr-Kaying,
There was a gaping gorge;
When he looked below,
There stretched a boundless forest,
And when he looked this way,
There was a black ridge curved like a sickle.
“This must be the place
My formidable uncle Bakai foretold.
Let me take the measure of the land,
And learn how many warriors there are;
Let me reconnoiter their route,
And count how many heroes they have.
Let me count them one by one
And learn the worth of their courage;
Let me set all the accursed man’s horses moving
And raid every one of them;
Let me learn the number
Of the horses doomed to be left masterless!”
So saying, Semetey then
Made his way up that mountain,
To its jutting black ridge,
To the place his formidable uncle Bakai
Had marked out for him.
When he reached the ridge,
Do not ask how the valiant hero fared:
In the hollow of Kara-Kyr,
Along the Kara-Archa,
Your bloodletter now dismounted.
The splendid hero, son of Manas,
Was ready to draw the line,
To raid the khan’s horses
And make a spectacle of the deed.
Tethering him where he could not be seen,
The great and towering Buurul,
The gray-wolf warrior lay down in wait.
Let us leave him there for now;
Karagul, a man of fierce courage,
Was the khan of the herdsmen—
Let us begin the tale of him.
