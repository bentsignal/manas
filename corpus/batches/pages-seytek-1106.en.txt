Whoever unexpectedly beheld their appearance
Despaired of his wretched life,
And the reason for that despair
Was the lord’s bluntly spoken word,
For the affair had become truly grievous,
And every thoughtful soul despaired.
This faithless mortal world is narrow,
Its falseness has been revealed,
Many among us babbled out their last wishes
And went about lamenting.
“We have fallen into a bitter ordeal,” they said,
And many among us hurried in alarm.
When the khan’s decree arrived,
The gathered people rose in an uproar,
Streaming onto the road like sheep.
The ill-fated Chongbilgich
Tested those favored by fortune.
Their exceptional and consummate courage
Became known to the sorcerers.
From among the countless multitude, one man—
“This one will do,” they said yesterday—
Was inscribed in the register
Of ill-omened Bilgich.
About a hundred men destined to go
Were entered upon his roll,
And thus the accursed Kite
Was recognized as khan.
They dressed them in the finest coats,
So stout that embers could not burn through them,
And mounted them on fleet horses
That no bird could overtake on a journey.
Whoever offered resistance
They seized and put to rest.
The sorcerers chose one man
And made Chongbilgich khan,
Raising an uproar with their idol,
And in seething Beijing
They made a tumult never to be forgotten.
The treacherous Chinese settled down,
And over the Chinese people of forty khans
The precious Kite became khan.
Both reins and the single bridle
The hero Kite took in hand,
And in Khan Esenkhan’s place
He remained as sovereign.
He gathered an army to take revenge,
And assembled a host of champions.
He draped armor over his back,
And the frenzied wretch
Gave him a hundred sorcerers as companions
And sent Bilgich on his way.
On broad-rumped steeds with spade-like hooves,
Thoroughbreds bred upon the open steppe,
They rode handsome horses,
Knotting their horsehair tails tight,
And making all their gear ready.
Each man strapped on
Six copper cauldrons of provisions.
They circled the black mountains of Kaspang,
Showering hail upon the foothills,
And drawing black mist across the level plain.
From the summits of mountain after mountain
The layered fog broke apart,
So that in especially suspicious places
No person might detect them,
He poured six streams of glacial snow from the sky.
The sorcerous swine’s
Weather-working power was revealed,
Everything shimmered and shook evenly,
And the grasses of the earth parted.
A blinding ground mist
Poured close over the earth,
And from every side, bitterly whistling,
A cold wind broke forth.
Wherever one looked across the hills,
Water surged and flooded,
While all the deserts and plains
Were covered in an impenetrable vapor.
His ferocity became known
When Chongbilgich turned the clouds,
