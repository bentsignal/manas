I, your mother Kanykei,
Sent men through many cities.
Bölökbai’s forty Kalchas
I sent roaming over many lands;
I had every place searched.
I, ill-fated and disgraced,
Had them bring the keenest iron.
When it came to making charcoal,
A whole bay of green forest was cleared;
At the forge no living soul could endure.
Alman dismounted from the argymak,
And a blue bullock was slaughtered;
Hammering with sledge and mallet,
The forty Kalchas wore hollows in their hands.
The master smith Bölökbai,
After eating, set to work;
He was a friend of the prophet David—
That poor man fashioned it.
He stretched gold along its hilt,
And filled its edge with sharpness;
Bölökbai, the accomplished smith,
Barely finished it in seven years.
This wondrous treasure of mine—
That single dagger
Is worth forty-five camels.
When you wreak slaughter among the Chinese,
When you confront the giants
And close with them rein to rein,
Draw it from its sheath,
And plunge it between their ribs.
Its hilt fits within the fist,
And to carry it against the body
It has a special case.
I give four treasures to you four;
Fasten them at your waists.
Accept them as gifts, saying,
“Our valiant mother gave them.”
Your destination is the Kakanchin;
Face them and wreak slaughter!
I learned this from the transmitters,
And asked those who knew.
They say Beijing is a hot land,
Whose heat gives no respite,
Whose heat boils like fire;
Whoever meets that heat
Is left without strength or power.
They say its desert is forty days across,
A land of surpassing severity.
None has brought it under my sway;
No one has endured the journey there.
Its land is distant and its people many;
If my champions set out upon the road,
Shame on me if I did not foresee
That their strength would fail.
Far Egypt and broad Jerusalem—
I had those cities searched,
And the travel rations found there
I bought for their weight in worked gold,
Barely obtaining them at death’s door.
I wrapped the outside in rawhide,
And destined them for the clash
With Kakanchin’s Beijing.
How much this poor head has learned!
Food for the tulpar and food for the hero,
With nourishing herbs all mixed within.
For each of you four, a separate portion—
I prepared them long ago.
As the prince’s faithful wife,
I entrust this treasure to you.
Fasten it behind your saddle,
And in that forty-day desert
Pour it straight upon your palate!
So that in crushing battle with the Chinese
You may unleash havoc,
Fight six months without tiring,
And avenge the noble hero,
I prepared these travel rations,
For the valiant heroes themselves
