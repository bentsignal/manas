"I know you, wretch;
I know the waist you girded,
I know, Aychürök,
Your Chinese husband in Beijing!
I shall break your back,
I shall cool your breath,
I shall drive out of you
All your far-flung bluster—
The husbands you sought and chose,
Chynkozho and Toltoy!
I shall show the strength of my arm,
And begin many a tumult!
I shall bring the end of the age,
And take warrior Toltoy!
Luckless woman who went seeking men,
I shall take you and make you pay.
Come here without delay;
Give me the falcon and I shall leave!"
Your murderer grew furious
And now stood hunched over.
From father back to forefather,
Descendant of black-hearted men—
"He will leave without taking me," she thought,
And God now struck Chürök.
Your dear sister-in-law wailed,
Offended by her murderer.
Weeping hard and sobbing,
She reeled, her knees giving way,
Despairing of her destroyer.
In crimson gown, with a moonlike waist,
Your graceful sister-in-law,
Her white breast laid bare,
Her white throat exposed,
Reeled toward the tent,
And her breath now went cold.
Kanchoro flew like an arrow
And went to Külchoro.
The two companions met,
And after endless counsel,
They began with these words:
"Has our hunched-over elder brother
Now come to grief?
Quarreling before he has even seen her,
Has God taken away
Our rather arrogant prince like this?!
If he leaves without taking Chürök,
And we set out for Afghanistan,
We shall face merciless turmoil,
We shall see the end of the age.
How could we bow and plead
With that ass-embracing woman,
That infidel of furious temper?
Would she not taunt and devour us:
'The wife who went willingly
He returned without taking,'
Would she not devour us like this!
My dear Külchoro,
You were the more resourceful one,
A ruddy, handsome-faced
Lad skilled with maidens.
Find a way, Külchoro;
Go to your sister-in-law,
Speak to her with tactful words,
And bring your sister-in-law back!"
Külchoro flew like an arrow
And ran to his sister-in-law;
Kanchoro, sparing no strength,
Ran to his elder brother.
When he reached the prince,
Kanchoro stood and said this:
"Father, my dear elder brother,
Where is your good sense?!
When we were in Talas,
We used to carve and eat the horse's haunch;
We said we would subdue the governor
Without brawling with a woman.
If we turn and go back,
