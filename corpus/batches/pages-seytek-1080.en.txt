“God has granted your heart’s desire;
The man you wished for has arrived.
God has fulfilled your prayer;
The man you longed for has come.”
Thus Külayim
Joyfully brought her the news.
Then your dear sister Bermet
Spoke softly yet with firm resolve,
Beginning all manner of words,
Thrilled and overcome with haste.
In red and green silk,
She dressed in garments precious as life,
And hung upon her breast
A solid-gold amulet,
Then prayed to the Creator:
“You were my companion, Külayim!
My lady, precious as the Night of Power,
You have rekindled my extinguished fire,
Restored a dead soul to life,
And joined what had been severed.
While Akjoltoy traveled the road without rest,
I plainly withered in misery.
Wearing sores upon every steed,
And grievously tormenting my heart,
I suffered the torments
Of this world and wasted away.
If these words of yours are true,
Then today, Külayim, I rejoice.
My burning heart has been soothed,
And my surging spirit has opened.
The plane tree planted for me has come;
The joyful time for play has come.
A new prosperity has arrived,
And a day of radiant sunlight has dawned.
For tireless play and laughter,
A dark night has fallen upon misfortune,
And news beyond all finding has come.
You tireless beauties,
Every one of you, make haste!
You forlorn maidens who sat in silence,
Put on the garments of the fairies!
You poor dears who walk beside me,
You beauties lovely as paradise,
Whose like cannot be found in this false world,
Whom I cherish more dearly than my life,
It seems to be lion Seytek himself;
All of you, heed my words.”
When the maidens heard this,
In red gowns, with moon-slender waists,
Those graceful darlings,
Upon hearing Bermet’s words,
Could hardly remain without preparing.
When she gave her stately command,
How could the maidens hold back?
The one whose speech was sweet,
The lovely one with gazelle eyes,
The most gifted and graceful of all—
With graceful Külayim at their head—
Put on silk gowns
Fastened with diamond buttons.
By thoughtful observation she could discern
The secrets of the human heart.
Her beauty was perfect,
Setting hearts aflutter anew;
The Creator had made such a mortal.
Everyone who saw her lost his heart;
Her words were sugar and her tongue was honey.
Having taken so fair a maiden,
So kind-hearted a companion,
The ill-fated woman had made ready.
Through the many wonders she displayed,
And the way her beauty stole every beholder’s heart,
Her manner delighted the soul,
And she served her faithfully.
Her beauty was renowned among the people.
Peacock feathers
Were set directly above her crown
In ninety different patterns.
Everyone who looked upon her
Marveled and lost his heart.
