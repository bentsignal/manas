Shayymbet, man of Jamgyrchy,
Could meet them as an equal,
And if we suddenly joined battle,
If we unexpectedly fell upon each other,
And I charged in with a roar,
Their hearts would fail and they would die;
That wretched, feeble people
Would behold the face of Judgment Day.
They were an exceedingly weak people,
And their name, he learned, was Afghan.
They had houses built with four corners,
And bread made round;
Displaying countless different forms,
Almighty God in his power
Had created them wondrously.
Your lord grew weary of the city.
Holding the spyglass at a slant,
He filled its field of view;
Holding it somewhat lower,
He satisfied his heart.
He turned the right-hand wheel
And directed it toward Opol.
Your lord settled himself,
And from that distant forked place,
He coiled like a dragon
And gathered himself like a white tiger.
That poor, proud-standing hero
Was seized by agitation.
Suddenly growing wrathful,
Your tiger entrusted the spyglass
To his own dear eye,
Toward a spur of broad Opol,
Toward the nose of the mountain there,
Toward the meeting of nine roads,
Toward the expanse of Boru-Koy,
Toward the hollow of Ok-Kyya,
Toward the banks of Great Urgench,
He now trained the spyglass.
Beside the flowing water,
The poor daughter of Akun
Your lord now saw in full.
Your gray wolf, with a fierce appetite
For liquor and women,
Now fixed his eyes intently.
Amid thirty daughters-in-law and thirty maidens,
He now saw
Churok herself.
He began an uproar;
Your lord of the dark day faltered,
And when he saw Aychurok,
He nearly dropped dead:
"Good heavens, I had thought,
'I took a wife from Bukhara,'
And had gone on believing it;
But instead of taking a wife, good heavens,
I have been living as a bachelor."
Thirty daughters-in-law and thirty maidens
Moved lightly in their games;
In their midst Aychurok
Glittered like a poppy.
She wore a fitted dress and a sable coat,
With its hem showing below.
Your lord's heart grew tender,
And his mind was deeply stirred;
Each time he looked, Aychurok
Appeared like a precious jewel.
Green-patterned ornaments, powder, and rouge
Of that sister-in-law Churok of yours
Showed upon her bosom;
Each time he looked he gasped,
And your lord's heart opened.
Adorned and resolute,
She swayed as though moving toward him.
Upon her head was a skullcap made of gold;
Her splendor surpassed every living soul,
That poor beauty, daughter of Akun.
