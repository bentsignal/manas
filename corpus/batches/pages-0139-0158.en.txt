He mounted in wrath;
Against the lion himself
A doomed man would come.
If you saw the hero's form,
He was like a fortress built of iron.
Not only Kochku—if he attacked,
Even Esenkan could not survive.
High-browed, fierce in menace,
He would lay flat whoever challenged him.
Wide-mouthed, cliff-browed,
His face like oiled wheat,
His eyes like dusk and mist,
Rich Jakyp's young Manas
Girded his full-grown waist,
Struck Toruchaar once on the thigh,
Took his lacquered spear in hand
And roared in wrath.
Behind him, forty children
Cried the battle cry ‘Manas!’
Making a road through roadless land,
Valiant Manas the leopard
Then attacked Kochku
And the seven hundred under him.
Their very center, mighty Manas,
Who was twelve at that time,
Came and tore through it.
The forty children came to their very middle
And closed in upon it.
Through their center your mighty one
Went tearing his way out.
Toruchaar beneath him,
When he heard shouting, surged forth.
When the uproar rose, the noble beast
Showed the bearing of a tulpar.
Unflustered when he heard shouting,
In the darkness of the moon,
He never misplaced a single step.
Surging forth when he heard uproar,
Toruchaar beneath him
Showed the bearing of a tulpar.
When he galloped, the black earth
Split apart layer by layer.
He did not slip on the heights;
He was in no way inferior
To a wild goat treading stone.
Ridden into combat, he did not falter;
Ridden into battle, he did not tire;
Ridden with the baggage train, he did not flag.
Hoe-hoofed, broad-rumped,
The tulpar of spacious lands,
A hardy, well-bred horse,
Sieve-lunged, copper-limbed,
Khan Manas's Toruchaar,
Growing wild and straining,
When he entered combat,
He bucked as if his rider had died.
The stones he trod shattered;
Clods as large as a traveling hut,
Above wolf-maned Manas the tiger,
Whistled overhead.
His flesh stood out like a sheep's;
His back straightened like a hare's.
His mouth opened like a chasm;
White foam mixed with blood
Splashed upon his chest.
Making him rear and strain,
The hero Manas entered combat,
Surging forward with Toruchaar.
The socketed lacquered spear
He raised high, looming above.
Before the enemy striking at him,
He did not open his guard in fear.
The men who struck at him,
He stamped into the ground like hares.
He moistened his spear with blood.
The champions who came to shoot
He pressed to the ground like hares.
When they had raided the horses,
When the first shout rose,
There had been seven hundred champions.
When he attacked with a shout,
Nothing of them remained.
When Manas lunged to attack,
Twelve champions fell.
Their mail-clad champions
Lay dead, stretched out.
Their jacket-wearing champions
Lay dead, sprawled out.
The renowned died, people died;
Their champions numbered seven hundred.
All whom he met died;
All the havoc was stilled;
All the horses were gathered.
Manas the mighty leopard,
When he lunged again and again,
Twelve champions lay stretched out.
When the mighty one attacked,
All who faced him
Fell from their horses, stretched out.
Before one could say this or that,
Before an eye could blink,
He began the turmoil.
Their champions numbered seven hundred;
Your accomplished Bloodthirsty Manas
Reached them and slaughtered them all.
Their champion hero Kochku,
To raise a struggle,
To spear young Manas,
To stir up trouble,
That bastard turned his thoughts
Toward Esenkan's presence,
To take Manas there bound.
Fire burned throughout his innards.
Taking his true spear in hand,
Striking his pure chestnut once,
Roaring in wrath,
Crying the battle cry ‘Möndü!’
His blue-gray coat of armor showed.
Those who saw him were troubled;
When your unbeliever raged,
He appeared like Ala-Too.
That Kochku who came
Had a head like a great cauldron,
