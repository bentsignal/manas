Until each passing day sank into night,
I bore firewood upon my back.
Whenever Kyyaz came out shouting,
At the memory of it I would faint away.
The earth was too hard to enter,
Yet life was too sweet to die.
Pierced and divided by sorrow,
My tears poured down,
Sixty hells and a thousand afflictions
Appeared close above my head.
While my heroic uncle lived,
He towered over all the people.
Yet in the hands of brave Kyyaz,
I suffered disgrace for many years.
The vile, cunning enemy exhausted me,
And my days passed in slavery.
I withered under suffering,
Wasted pale with yellow sickness.
My collarbones were rubbed raw,
And the flesh of my sides was torn.
It became clear that this darkened,
Treacherous world had left me behind.
There is no forest for the blind,
Alive today and gone tomorrow,
And I had no strength left to gird my waist.
When I escaped my suffering,
My valiant uncle was no longer there.
When I lamented, my tongue poured forth,
While my two eyes blazed.
I could do nothing by my own will,
For my two hands were bound.
Brave Kyyaz’s herd of horses,
His surging, gleaming herd!
It thundered down from above,
And tending horses was hell.
For the herdsman himself,
I hurriedly carried firewood.
Unable to face the day of death,
As the day grew late and the sun set,
That herdsman clubbed me
Until my skin was tanned raw.
When dawn broke bright,
I danced like a Kalmyk.
When the sun reached midmorning,
I slung a milk pail around my neck.
The hell of this world—
This is how I endured it.
I carried birch firewood,
While the herdsman drank his fill within.
He made me gnaw clean and swallow
The knucklebones of two horses.
I was imprisoned by a hostile slave,
And beaten by the herdsman.
Saying, “The slave has grown insolent,”
They plundered what little I possessed.
The garden-city of our fortune was bound,
And our destiny was broken then.
Disgraced, my eyes still blazed;
Thus did my misery boil.
I suffered torment from the enemy,
And cruel humiliation crushed my soul.
Only a spoonful of blood remained in me,
My flesh was but a blue, raw scrap.
At that time I thought,
“The death of this wretched head
Has arrived without my dying.”
I thought, “The strength and vigor
Of my steel-boned body are gone.”
Lioness maiden, hear my words:
Many burdens cling to a man at death;
Surging strength and courage
Disappear one day,
In the blink of an eye.
While the vigor of youth remains,
While fortune is in its season,
A man destroys those he pursues,
And when he holds dominion,
He subdues the whole world
And rules over it.
That dear cap of blue lynx fur—
