For the hero who would uphold the nation,
For the many Kyrgyz people in Talas,
For the champion who would be their support.
When mighty Kyyaz demanded him,
Aichürök burned and trembled;
She could not say, “I will not give him.”
Before the renowned hero Kyyaz’s
Overwhelming authority, Chürök’s
Life nearly left her then;
Her tiny soul shrieked,
And every bone in her body seemed to shatter.
What began in the morning lasted until evening.
If only I could return and see
Reedy Keng-Kol and Talas,
And take the young hero who remains of his khan
As my solace and support;
If only, in his old age, I could show Bakai,
And my mother in her declining years,
This token left by the hero,
Saying, “The young hero has come boldly home,”
If only I could let them see him once.
If only I could take my long-held vengeance
Upon the black-hearted choro,
And make the child my support—
How many dreadful thoughts she pondered.
Kyyaz has ten thousand, while we are few;
Will he cast him into a pit?
Will he rob the newborn infant
Of his sight in this world
And send him to his death?
The courageous woman Aichürök thought,
Rather than walk brightly, poor wretch,
Only to stand weeping in anguish,
I shall summon a hero’s courage.
Displaying the bearing of a warrior,
I shall save alive
My only refuge and excuse.
Though cast into fire, I shall not become ash;
Though plunged into misery, I shall not become a slave.
I shall make my words more forceful.
He means to tear me from my darling
And blind my eyes with grief;
May the doomed man be struck by God!
By tearing me from my only one,
He means to extinguish me like a fire.
God’s sun in the heavens grew dark,
And in poor Chürök’s mind
Sixty sorrows came at once.
Her heart was pierced and torn apart,
And Kyyaz, demanding, “Give me the child,”
Appeared in the guise of a dragon.
Seeing him, Chürök
Shed tears from her eyes.
The madman hero Kyyaz stood there,
Preparing to take the child;
If you had seen hero Kyyaz,
He raged like a blue lion.
Aichürök stood shrieking,
Tormented and distraught,
Yet betraying no suspicion to Kyyaz.
Her heart wandered and broke apart;
Unable to collect herself,
She shed tears from her eyes.
She could not wipe away her tears
Or draw back the mucus from her nose.
The dear bird called the kazgaldak
Flies over the plains without returning;
How much Aichürök had endured,
All for the child left by her khan.
The dear bird called the bezbeldek
Flutters away seeking a willow;
Your poor woman endured toil and misery,
For the child left by her lord.
Over the plump, pale-gray falcon,
The ruined woman stood lamenting,
Over Cholpon, born of her own body.
Grief-stricken over them,
Aichürök wailed,
The ruined woman, for what remained of her khan,
For the black-blazed young falcon.
