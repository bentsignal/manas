What can we possibly do?
Can anyone prosper who shoots at
The hero who has gone to the other world?
Can any two men prevail in combat
Against the bloody-handed hero who protected us?
You have a wife beneath your roof,
And, having traveled among many peoples,
You bear the name Sary Khan.
Find us a land to which we may go;
Hero, find a man who will care for us.
Hero, do not call us the Jediger army
And stiffen in fury;
Do not devour these innocent worms.
The one you must fight is Kanchoro,
Your own close kinsman.
If you mean to wage war, wage it in earnest;
First strike your kinsman,
And only afterward decide
Whether the great Black Jediger host
Is guilty or innocent.
And that hero Kyyaz, son of Bagysh—
He too is your own kinsman.
First contend among yourselves
And learn who is your equal.
If you are a khan, heed our words;
If your claim to be khan is false,
And you still stiffen in fury,
Then here stands the great Black Jediger host—
Go ahead and slaughter us!”
When Sary Khan heard this,
His whole body went limp,
And his flesh and sinews stretched.
Regretting the words he had spoken,
He realized Kanchoro and the hero Kyyaz
Were the true enemies,
And his two eyes strained eagerly
For a clash with them.
Sary Khan’s Joykuma mace
Had a haft with four ridges and a thousand-edged head,
An iron-headed weapon reserved for battle.
When it was swung, blood flowed;
Before it was drawn back, life fled.
That iron mace in his bare forearm
Looked fearsome and was venomous;
If you saw its shape, you fled far away!
Its wrist-loop was intricately braided,
The handiwork of the smith Bölökbay.
With the iron mace in his hand, he said,
“They were the hidden enemy,
And I very nearly failed to see it.
I nearly attacked this multitude,
And since death comes one day out of seven,
I nearly became needlessly entangled in disaster
And met my death.
By assailing the innocent,
I would have become a fool;
This precious head, famed in many tales,
Would have been cut short.”
Sary Khan did not falter;
Saying, “Let my anger break upon stone,”
He seized a black boulder
As massive as Opol-Too and swung it around.
Making the rock ring aloud,
Sary Khan brought his mace down
And struck it with all his force.
Sparks flew from the mace;
The boulder split cleanly and burst apart,
And fire sprang from the black stone.
Lives scattered from beneath it,
And, swaying upon Tootoru,
The mountain-like Kyyaz emerged.
When Sary Khan saw the hero Kyyaz,
He at once leveled
His ornamented spear with its steel shaft,
His ornamented spear with its steel point,*
And thrust it forward with a roar.
