His heart smoked fiercely with grief,
And he mourned the days gone by.
As though held fast in place,
Semetey, behind the herd,
Stopped before Khan Bakai.
Poor Bakai wept long:
“My gray-maned Manas, companion of my youth,
If only you had not died!” he wept.
“Manas, my companion, sharer of my soul,
Parted from you, my soul’s companion,
You are nowhere among the living,
Yet nowhere in the grave if called dead.
With a wail that splits stone,
Your Bakai wanders distraught.
When that Manas was alive,
Why did I not then behold
The ground below, like Judgment Day?
When my lion, my gray-maned wolf, was alive,
Why did I not die then?
O beard of one who felled the foe,
Parted from my tiger,
Is this your time to lie upon the ridge?!
When my black-steel Zulfiqar
Hung smoking at my side,
When I drove back
All who attacked,
When I kept my sharp, lamb-ribbed spear
Bound ready at my side;
When I drove back many peoples,
To say nothing of the Chinese;
When my world was wide, not narrowed,
In the time of fortunate Manas—
Why did I not die then?
When, if bored, we hunted deer,
And if not, in Talas
We passed our days in freedom;
When I wore my paired sable cap
And set it jauntily,
Lamenting and singing with the hero,
With my dear lion Manas,
When I lived in joy—
Why did I not die here then?
My long-suffering Manas, if only you had not died,
My sheep sharing one fold,
My food sharing one circle of companions.
My valiant Manas, if only you had not died,
So Bakai need not whiten a beard like a swan,
Nor redden eyes like a raven’s,
Nor live to see this torment.
Who did not cock the sable cap upon his head
And wear it with a flourish?
When strength remained in his arm,
Who did not walk the earth roaring?
Before that noble soul’s venom,
Did the Kakan’s people not tremble?
Even distant Beijing, beyond a stallion’s reach,
We fought until we made it submit;
It was an army that trampled
The swaggering Kalcha.
We scattered the army he had gathered,
And when we cast Beijing into turmoil,
We took the daughters of Beijing’s lords
And carried them away as prizes.
Why did I not die then?
Great Beijing, that city with its court—
We broke Beijing and took its realm,
Loaded gold pieces upon camels,
And took a thousand dark-eyed maidens.
My name, Bakai, split the stones;
When I was mighty and strong,
Why did I not die then?
When the pitch-black city’s ruler
I held surrounded,
When I beat the black Chinese and many Manchus,
Roasting their people like wheat,
And scourged them;
When, in this accursed false world,
My wing, Manas, was still beside me,
