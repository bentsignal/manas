Quickly promised a white-gray mare.
Saying, “God, set my grave task right;
My valiant father Almambet,
May your spirit support me now.
This one too was a lion,
Chubak, son of Akbaltai;
Girded at the solitary pass,
If there were an army of seven divisions,
He entered lightly clothed and attacked.
Syrgak, son of Ulak Khan,
May God set my task right;
My lone life has come under strain—
Spirits, support me now!
His heart torn apart,
Wondering, “What recourse have we?”
Tears poured from his eyes.
Grieving, weeping, and distraught,
Pleading with the one God,
Saying, “My Creator and Lord, protect me,”
Külchoro lay there.
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Khojo, resolute in his deeds,
Rounded Mount Kasaba,
His arms and armor fastened on.
The earth split with his thunder,
And monster Khojo roared;
Looming like Mount Opol,
He had fastened on enough powder and shot
For six months.
His courage cruel, his strength immense,
Leopard Khojo then
Leveled anyone who challenged him.
Leopard Khojo, the destroyer,
Had the custom of drinking the blood
Of every enemy who opposed him.
When his rage came boiling,
Dark clouds could not obscure him;
While his black stallion remained,
No mortal could escape alive.
Foul mist and low mist
He spread over the earth;
Khojo, marking Buurul,
Came storming up.
His eyes blazed like a boar’s;
His great teeth, broad as a gate,
Dear Khojo then
Gnashed row upon row.
Chynkojo took
The wide-mouthed secret bow
And shot a hundred arrows with it.
He had a golden bow beside him;
And leopard Khojo
Shot a hundred arrows with that too.
An eleven-charge ochogor—
Chynkojo was no easy foe;
Taking aim, he fired a hundred shots.
The white gun at his shoulder
He took and fired a hundred shots.
That leopard Khojo
Drew his bow like pouring rain
And fired bullets like hail.
Khan Khojo grew furious;
As though black doom had struck his wife,
Khojo lashed his horse.
His bullets streamed like hail;
Külchoro then lay
Sheltered behind Taibuurul.
As though ready to swallow him,
Khojo came on in distress;
Külchoro lay there
Pleading with the one God.
From the sky, pale clouds split apart;
With no prince visible upon him,
Taibuurul stood in view.
When Khojo saw Taibuurul,
He exulted wildly,
And now raged in frenzy.
