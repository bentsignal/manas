With both hands upon his breast,
He gave Manas his greeting;
Ah, warrior, Ajybai came pleading:
'Here stands a city with its sovereign;
“Ah, warrior, here I am—take my head!
I offer before you
A horse such as Kartkurong here;
Since my eyes were overcome,
Ah, warrior, here is blood to pour by the flaskful!”
Then Manas stirred his great frame,
Your warrior heartened, speaking boldly:
“Ajybai, do not shrink from this hard task;
Pull the spear out of my waist.
Ajybai, let no one see you!
Over the spear within my waist,
Let the great host not grieve;
Let the Muslims not grow sorrowful,
And those left living among the dead,
Warrior, let them not lose heart!”
Ajybai hurried up to help;
The belt around his waist
Clung to the warrior’s spear.
Told to pull straight, he pulled it slack,
And when he twisted the spear,
The steel point gave a crack
And broke off within the warrior’s waist.
Then his many sorrows overflowed his breast;
Bereft of his fierce wolves,
Then his tears streamed in ten directions:
“The crow that flies with croaking cries
Will gouge the flanks of noble men,” he thought.
“The martyrs fallen on campaign
Will turn to the shit of beasts,” he thought.
Searching through the hills, he saw
The brim of the black felt cap—
Syrgak, the leopard’s champion.
The warrior had gone straight to Mecca.
The warrior had gone straight to Mecca.
Then, fallen in the flower of his days,
Ah, warrior, struck down in his youth!
The giant Kutan and giant Mangkush,
The Lama and enormous Joloy—
He had killed all four alike,
Offering them in sacrifice,
Cutting off their heads and tying them on,
Taking them as gifts;
When Syrgak wreaked havoc,
Of ninety myriads of Black Chinese,
When yesterday he broke apart their ranks,
Beginning with their giant Kutan,
When he slaughtered ninety giants,
Never turning back, he dealt your Chinese people
A crushing blow that broke their backbone.
He missed the foe he should have met
And met the one he should not meet;
Whirling his spear and breaking ranks,
He encountered Kojojash,
And the warrior fell a martyr to a shot.
Having first wrought slaughter,
My Syrgak died a martyr—
Ah, warrior, at twenty-one!
Noble Syrgak, the leopard,
Fallen in the flower of his days,
With the holy Quran for his pillow,
Ah, warrior, his head toward the qibla.
Where Syrgak’s soul departed,
Its back broken upon the ridge,
His horse Kokchebich died beside him.
In the ravine of It-Kyya,
Muzburchak and Khan Kokcho died.
At the farthest corner of the mountains,
The warrior Bokmurun had died as well.
Then thirteen souls he found upon the plain;
When he saw the mighty fallen,
Your mighty Manas, the leopard,
There, in the forest’s green recess,
Caught and slaughtered a thousand steeds;
With Khan Syrgak at their head,
He laid them there in entrusted burial!
And thus that green recess,
From that day down to this,
Became a great and sacred burial place.
Circling in anguish, unable to leave,
The dear lion circled, searching,
For Almambet and Chubak—
Their bodies he could not find at all.
In wrath he gathered his host;
The golden-cheeked battle-axe
He braced against his flank,
And your warrior stood weeping aloud:
“From these vast ranks of unbelievers,
My people, how can I go away alive?
There is no trace of Almambet or Chubak;
How can I leave without seeing them?
How can I part from my twin champions?
To these Chinese bastards—
My noble Alake had said—
‘Do not abandon my body,’ he said.
‘Take me by the waist and fold me,’
My champion had told me.
‘Load up my body for Mecca,’
My noble one had said—
Ah, warrior!
‘Catch Sarala and slaughter him;
Let my body not lie unburied’—
Ah, warrior!
‘Lay me in a known place,’
My Alake had said.
‘Saddle your horse properly,
And if you can, Khan Manas,
Recite the Quran with loud lament.’
While I go on living, spared from death,
My people, what legacy can I attain?
If Almambet’s body is not found,
Let me die here in this place!
Into dark, brooding Beijing
I will go straight with leveled spear.
That grim-faced Beijing—
I will wipe them out, destroy them all!
I will bring distress upon their heads
And demand Almambet back.
If the great Chinese host turns back,
Thus I will unleash my wrath.
The teeming people of vast Beijing—
I have no desire for their city;
I will demand Almambet back!
