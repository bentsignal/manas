Unable to find such a renowned man,
Unable to find such a lion among mortals,
The dear hair grown down my back
I had merely been braiding;
Unable to test and take my destined man,
I have only been living toward death.
His fortunes were complete;
He was a lion supported by the spirits.
My wandering to Temirkhan's city
Was evidently fitting.
A golden skullcap upon her head,
Unable to find her bearings,
The wretched daughter of Akun!
Her white throat gleamed,
Her fair face shone;
Sixty clasps and forty pearl buttons
Rattled upon her breast.
Look at the sweetness of your sister-in-law
Who went in search of a man; hear her words,
Look at her camel-calf eyes,
Her waist a handful wide, her face like the moon—
Your famous sister-in-law, the wretched one.
Deeply distressed, unable to rest,
She stood looking at the hero.
Unable to approach him openly,
Chürök's mind was divided:
If she seized him with entreaties,
Her ill-mannered kinsman, alas,
Seemed likely to use force.
If he caught her on the steppe, leaving her shamed,
Saying, “My wife came to me herself,”
And refused to go, provoking a quarrel,
Would Toltoy of the Jediger
Raid my father Akun
And destroy us all together?
If I went and married him myself,
I would mount the horse of disgrace
And earn a bad name.
In anguish she gave a harsh cry;
A great golden earring
Rattled upon her breast.
The famed beauty, her face like the moon,
Could make desire descend upon anyone who looked;
The radiant wretched woman
Had, above her lips,
A slightly protruding feature,
That Chürök who sought a husband.
Where was any defect
In your exhausted, unfortunate sister-in-law?
There are two versions of her fate;
Of noble, unfortunate Chürök
There are two different accounts.
Some people say,
“Chürök is Akun's daughter,”
And some people say,
“Chürök is the hero's daughter;
Without marrying until she grew old,
She brought turmoil upon her people.”
Thus Aychürök,
Finding no man to marry,
Finding no place to settle,
Searched the whole world,
Roamed through all the universe,
Flying about in her search,
And saw him while still captivated.
Let us leave this matter here
And begin from another side.
Two companions were beside him:
One was his tiger worth a thousand men,
The spring flowing down from the ridge,
The lamp left as a gift
By the hero Almambet—
Almambet's son Külchoro.
There was no such youth in the world;
This filthy world creates
Its servants in every kind of form.
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Was a blazing red ember.
