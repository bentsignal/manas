My son Semetey,
Born of his father, generous Manas,
Was destined to surpass him.
For that son of mine,
Upon my child’s brow,
Three marriages were written.
Let me tell of the first two:
When cruel disaster struck,
My dear son, only six months old,
Fled to the city of the Kara Khan
And wandered there in exile,
Until my noble child grew up.
Among the sights of Bukhara,
He matured as an orphan,
Joining in the affairs of the land,
And when he reached twelve years,
He married Shaatemir’s daughter Chachykey,
Pouring out treasures for her raven hair
And holding a feast for the gathered people.
Willful, foolish,
Swaggering infidel,
Restless madcap of surly temper,
In the end you did as you pleased.
The moon waned and the days passed;
Afterward he met
Aichürök, Akun’s daughter.
She stole away his falcon
And wore down his racehorse;
She became his precious wife,
A cultivator of wisdom whose speech was honey—
No other human could equal her!
Let us speak of the third:
Kuyaly, daughter of Kayypchal,
Was his third wife.
The twenty-year-old warrior maiden—
I, unfortunate woman, had hoped
She might come into my hands.
I camped beside the river,
And loved, not my son,
But my daughter-in-law herself.
The moon rose and the days passed,
And many days went by.
Then Ajybay of the Argyn khans
Brought me certain news.
When Ajybay finally came,
Sixty ladies and eighty maidens
Went out to meet him.
Speaking softly yet firmly,
Ajybay of the Argyn khans
Then began his account:
“Through the world’s four corners,
O senior wife who bore a prince,
I traveled four full circuits.
Through Medina’s five quarters
I traveled five full circuits.
I saw lands of countless kinds,
I witnessed many astonishing things,
And saw peoples of surpassing wonder.
Where the women were human and the men were dogs—
Such marvels have I seen.
Every creature that was born
I saw matched with its heart’s desire.
From savage waters to wondrous steeds,
Every strange and ill-starred being,
I saw them all without exception.
The warrior maiden you seek,
A maiden who brings strife among the people,
Is fiercely venomous and full of quarrels,
And lays flat whoever grapples with her.
No one has dared approach
That forsaken, ill-starred woman.
Many giants went to claim her,
But not one remained alive.
That ill-omened maiden
Is called a girl, yet bears a lion’s fame;
No man like her exists in all the world.
The matchmaker sent to her has died,
And the shield-bearer died in peace.
Those who sought to grapple with her died;
All who journeyed there died.
