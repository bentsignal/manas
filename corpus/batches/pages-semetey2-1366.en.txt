I took the woman as spoil;
I avenged the old grievance,
The ancient rancor of brave Toltoy,’
If you cannot boast of that,
What sort of khan are you among the Kazakhs?
Where is the spirit in you
That sustains a people and keeps a nation?
You smashed her gate with axes,
And took her gold into your sacks;
You made the red-robed, slender-waisted
Maiden your spoil.
Yet when you met her face to face,
You grew terrified of Aichürök
And started a quarrel, wretched Kyyaz.
You took the renowned land of Talas,
Took the Argyn and Kyrgyz peoples,
Took Külchoro and giant Bakay,
And took countless thoroughbred herds.
The many champions who entered combat,
Every one of them renowned,
You took so many lions among men;
Yet now you shrink back,
Turning your neck as if to flee.
When truly challenged, she is your widow;
If you master yourself, she is your own wife,
Yet you stand afraid of a woman.
You babble, Kyyaz—
Whom do you think you are mocking?
You whimper, saying,
‘A woman slaughtered the wrestlers;
This is what a woman does when enraged,’
And you, warrior, turn soft.
If a woman frightens you so,
What will you do if bloodthirsty Semetey
Should suddenly return?
Can an enemy ever be spared?
If one of the tigers turns upon you,
Kyyaz, will begging save your life?
My friend, can these words be yours?
You have no spirit, Kyyaz;
You utter worthless talk
And take fright at a woman
As though the bloodthirsty khan had come.
At your side, Kyyaz,
Stands your champion, famed in battle,
Your bloodthirsty hero Kanchoro.
While your hero stands in fury,
What can happen to my hero Kyyaz?
Wretched Kyyaz, listen to my words:
Your Kanchoro is a black cliff
No goose could circle;
Your Kanchoro is a mountain
The lamenting people cannot cross.
I was a ravine and narrow pass
No one could advance against.
If blazing red embers come,
If fire comes upon you, brave Kyyaz,
If a deep-dug pit appears,
If danger overwhelms your fly-like soul,
From cruel slaughter and calamity,
From suffering sent by a khan,
I was the warrior who would turn it back.
Is it right, my friend brave Kyyaz,
For me to turn to the enemy?
Having delivered you to a madman,
Would I then split myself in two?
You speak on and on, Kyyaz,
And arrive at witless counsel,
Planting the worst thoughts—
Ah, you are a foolish Kyyaz.
You pretend to be wise.
Once Aichürök is taken as spoil,
For a month or two
She will rage and storm;
After many months have passed,
Once that woman has tasted meat,
She will make peace with you.
