If you do not watch carefully, my son, the affair is dire.
One road to Beijing
Has a pit dug across it;
Another road to Beijing
Has brine in which one sinks.
It has a Kaldai and a khan;
That swine doomed to burn
Has even beasts standing guard.
It has a firmly fortified pass;
The Chinese are no idle people,
And have places that bring disaster.
A khan stands among its guards;
That swine doomed to burn
Has stationed sorcerers,
And every kind of watchman.
Beyond the sentry post
Is a giant who guards the road;
It is that infidel's custom
To cut down whoever approaches.
At the infidel's crossing
Are Kerik and Bil;
My contrary one, let me appeal to you—
Listen, my only one, and heed me!
Such is the nature of conflict;
I tell it, my son—understand!
One road to Beijing
Lets no one encountered escape alive;
One road to Beijing
Lets no one who meets it return alive.
An army awaits from Kaldai;
The Black Chinese of Beijing
Have a tale without end.
One road to Beijing
Has a desert forty days across;
Accursed Beijing
Has many such tales.
If you ask the nature of the desert:
Gray haze and burning dust;
Many a soul, mouth hanging open,
Upon reaching that desert,
Died there gasping.
You have rolled up your sleeves,
Saying you will enter straight
And fight costly Beijing!
Your uncle Bakai has grown old—
Would you throw him to the khagan!
If you grapple with Beijing,
You need provisions and coats;
Gather them from across the world,
And you need at least ten thousand troops.
When you reach that desert,
You need bull camels to haul the loads;
The one you grapple is Kakan-Chyn—
You need many things such as these!
When you reach that desert,
There is no grass to pinch and eat,
No water to sip even a mouthful.
When a sandgrouse flies it is exhausted;
When a black vulture flies,
Its wings burn and tire.
A hero's eyes grow dim.
When crossing that desert,
Your horse staggers in exhaustion;
When you are losing consciousness,
The accursed Chinese
Have yet another wonder:
They have a teeming people,
And among that people
Are giants of many kinds.
When they see an enemy, they strain;
One begins the war song,
And when he charges in wrath,
There are giants among the infidels.
There is a fleet horseman
Who can strike a camel flat with one blow.
Beijing is no cheap place;
When told, it holds such turmoil.
It has peoples of many kinds,
