“If I do not slaughter all I meet,
If in the end I do not sate myself
Upon that dog Sarybai’s blood!”
Hero Külchoro spoke these words;
Your renowned uncle rose there,
Your uncle Külchoro spoke thus,
And his eyes glittered like flame.
When he heard of Sarybai,
Fearsome rage seized him.
His precious, clenched teeth
He ground again and again.
Hell showed upon his face;
His lion-hearted valor
Surpassed the host and set him apart.
He gathered himself like a blue tiger,
Clad himself in armor no bullet pierced,
Set all his weapons in order,
And girded on his gleaming sword.
Hearts sank and parted,
As he parted from the vast army,
The tasseled standard swaying in his hand.
And so Külchoro,
Galloping upon Surkiyik,
Made the earth’s navel quake.
Tall and broad-shouldered,
Both champion and lion,
He was a seasoned hero who knew the enemy’s ways.
Toward the infidel of mighty arms,
Toward the hero of abundant courage,
Toward the infidel Sarybai,
He hastened without pausing for breath,
To clash fiercely in combat,
To fight to the death,
To wreak great slaughter,
And meet him face-to-face.
What he said at dawn was fulfilled by dusk,
And his eyes strained with expectation.
Gripping his spear and grinding his teeth,
Truly exultant, his eyes ablaze,
He went forth to single combat.
Do valiant champions
Not die against the foe they meet?
When need demands, do they not give
This life to whatever cause they choose?
You lost your senses,
Rushed about and raised a clamor,
Then fled without finding a way.
Here are your heroes and warriors;
When you begin to stir up words,
You have weaklings who grow afraid,
And fools who panic without counsel.
You have men who throw vast armies into chaos,
Break their strength, and flee.
You have cowards and warriors,
And weaklings who spread disorder.
Babbling praise of Sarybai—
Damn you, wretched people,
God has surely cursed you!
Is Sarybai such a peerless lion?
Is that infidel, the monster’s son,
A greater warrior than I?
I shall go to the great battlefield,
And grapple with Sarybai.
I shall fire my gun and draw my bow,
And wage a relentless fight.
Broad Talas is no cheap land;
Külchoro, born of the renowned,
Is no common warrior.
Sarybai is the one I shall fight,
That infidel whose shape is like a mountain,
The warrior Khan Sarybai,
The brute who scorches the earth,
The champion who destroys nations.
I shall rain many shots upon him,
And slaughter his pampered people.
If I do not seize his spoils,
If I do not unleash a shattering battle,
Standing face-to-face with him
And fighting until this dear life
Vanishes before all eyes,
