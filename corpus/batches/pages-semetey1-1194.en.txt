How could I ever live at ease?" she said.
She had twisted its warp with care.
At Kokotoy's memorial feast,
Before the vast assembly,
"He will be killed if they joust," she thought,
And knew it from the very beginning.
She repaired every weak place
And prepared it for the joust
At Kokotoy's great spectacle.
Before all the people, Kanykei
Showed what a wife she was;
She brought Khan Manas's Akolpok
And placed it upon him.
The capable wife Kanykei
Showed the skill of her hands.
Its ties numbered nine;
On the prince's inner side
She had them fastened crosswise.
Its buttons numbered nine;
She closed all its buttons,
Fitting them across the breast
Of the prince who loomed like a mountain.
Her voice fell mournfully
As, over the noble steed,
The gray sash that fell from the sky,
Over the Akolpok armor,
She buckled around Khan Manas:
"Recognize this as my service;
Go against the enemy without fear.
Though I may be a proud, haughty woman,
Know the worth I bear.
Many souls face deep regret;
If you wear the Akolpok,
It will be of great use in war.
If you raise a mighty clash with the Chinese,
When infidel and Muslim
Come together in bitter struggle,
May my panther prince wear it,
And understand my worth.
At night I went without sleep,
By day I had no rest;
I crafted its precious lining
And worked it with my teeth for many years.
Gnawing at the dark metal,
I wore my shovel-like teeth away;
Staring as I worked the dark metal,
I dimmed my black eyes.
I twisted its warp eighty times,
Thinking of every place
My lion prince and panther might go.
Thinking it was the tree planted for Talas,
Thinking it was the hour
My lion prince and sultan would clash,
I went on making the Akolpok.
Kongurbay, son of Alooke,
Would bring torment to this feast—
I knew that they would fight."
Telling her grief, Kanykei
Moved all the watching people to sorrow;
Upon the lion hero
She placed the Akolpok armor,
And Kanykei remained watching.
"She is truly the khan's wife," they said,
And the people's lament was this:
"This woman Kanykei—
That astonishing woman of perfect wisdom—
Beneath her skirt has ten heads,
And thimbles seem to move there.
Had any man but Khan Manas lived,
He would never have taken a wife,
But would have remained a bachelor.
Whenever it was, Khan Manas
Was carried away by his valor;
The panther met Kanykei
When she was fourteen years old.
She is truly a holy handmaiden;
Her meeting with Khan Manas
Was one of the nights of sacred destiny.
