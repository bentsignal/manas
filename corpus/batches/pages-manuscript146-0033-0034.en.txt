From the great branch that would remain with Kerimche,
I heard that no offspring remained,
Among peoples of many different ranks,
One named Kenen was born,
I heard that a descendant remained.
Making Ala-Too his homeland,
I heard that Kongur remained,
When he went to Karakuman,
When Kaltai attacked from his side,
Fleeing, he saved the khan alive,
Laying a claim against Kaltai,
I heard that he took his blood in vengeance.
Advancing three times from the right flank,
They say he waged a boundless battle,
Firing the great jazayil and the bow,
They say the wretched Kyrgyz attacked.
The Örötkül came and moved around them,
The Chinese who went unpunished,
Ambushing them in a hidden place and striking them down,
Scattering all their possessions,
The Örötkül then turned back.
Turning back the edge of that host,
Their hero was Badykan,
That glaring Chinese warrior,
With no soul left to answer back,
That ill-fated eagle,
Casting them down as though slaughtered,
Without looking any man in the face,
Black-headed and fork-footed,
Unable to reckon a man worth even coarse cloth,
Having every passerby bound,
Bringing down the leopard-like khan.
From the man who went to meet them,
He has wrung the news from him.
His heroism and princely bearing,
He is a warrior better than them all,
A man who traveled there has seen him,
To every eye that beholds him, he is wondrous,
He roamed among peoples and reconnoitered lands,
The riders who went to gather news
Lost their wits,
Saddling their horses and making ready,
The hero, a moon-bright eagle,
Set out at that very time.
Making camp at a lodging place,
Taking the spoils that lay spread out,
Yielding no ground when surrounded,
Submitting to no man of courage,
They settled in Talas as their land.
If we make a nest for the shamed,
If we ask among the hills,
He had drawn close to the Chinese,
Red gold and white silver coins,
Saying he would seize it from the land,
Flaming up like a white gale,
