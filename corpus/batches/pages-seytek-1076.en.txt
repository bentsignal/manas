Lion-hearted Seytek grew impassioned;
Whatever your elder, hero Akjoltoy,
Told him early already seemed too late.
To behold the radiant poor maiden,
To gaze upon her face,
His two eyes became four with longing.
His precious soul cried out,
His heart grew hot and his body melted,
Longing to plunge into pleasure,
To lie upon a narrow bed,
To eat meat from a single bowl,
And meet her face-to-face in tenderness,
To play until his desire was sated,
And surrender himself to passion.
His heart surged and his eyes shone,
As though he might never reach her;
He boiled with impatient haste.
Yet he could not leave Joltoy behind,
Nor could he abandon the poor, troubled man
And pass him by upon the road.
The noble warrior, mindful of honor,
That noble man like a towering mountain,
With eyes reddening like fire,
Grew gaunt in frantic haste,
And sank into sorrow and regret;
His heart turned toward his beloved,
And he withdrew alone.
Now Joltoy understood his impatience.
His elder kinsman Akjoltoy
Stepped into the golden-soled stirrup
And sprang astride his horse.
Leading his companion Seytek,
He raced across the open country;
Kulansur and Kulunsur
Rode with their stirrups clashing.
To the lowlands of the Op River,
An immense and mighty stream,
Along the banks of Keng-Özön,
The grey wolves came at last.
They were travelers upon a campaign,
Their souls blazing and burning.
Full of cunning and fierce in wrath,
They stood apart from other men;
They would yield to all the world for none,
Illustrious heroes mighty in spirit.
Those who saw them thought them Mount Kap,
And those who grappled with them met ruin.
Grey wolves of singular splendor,
True lion-hearted heroes,
Their majesty mountainous, their wrath severe—
Those who fought them were laid low.
Wearing out their horses as they scouted the land,
They took on a thousand fearsome airs.
Seeing the maidens, they grew excited;
The champions who had never seen such girls
Lost all composure,
And rode forward with great bounding strides,
As though they might trample everything down,
Striking panic into every living soul.
Those who stood and watched them
Were left bewildered and distracted;
Their majesty was like a tiger’s menace,
And they looked ready to seize their prey.
The unfortunate fairy maiden,
Of wondrous and cutting discernment,
Experienced from seeing many things,
Was exceedingly astute and capable;
She had reckoned the world a thousand ways,
And had seen the shrewdest maidens.
Resourceful and richly endowed with wisdom,
Eloquent in speech and mistress of words,
She was accomplished and outstanding.
She had set sentries upon the heights,
Stationing them there by magic,
And guards stood at her gate.
The unfortunate sovereign lady
Was ringed about by an iron fence,
With only one road by which to enter;
So vast was her wealth.
A grove of plane trees encircled it.
