Summoned his champions:
“Drive the captured horses
By night and rest them by day;
Scout the land without losing hope.
Slaughter a colt for your provisions,
Make camp and post sentries.
Let us fortify our lives,
And after seizing all their horses,
Let us earn the praise
Of the renowned Khan Kite.
When summer comes, let us ask
For the rank of governor.
Let every heart be satisfied,
And every deed be brought to light;
Let it be written in the record
Of the great ones of your nation:
‘We avenged our fathers
And slaughtered the Buruts.’
There is no infidel like Akjoltoy,
And no wisdom in an empty breast.
Do not spoil the horses’ condition;
Keep driving them onward.
In six or seven days
There will be time to tether sheep—
Mark well these words of mine.
As for your ruler Bilgich,
When eight full days have passed,
Then you will know his fate.”
He said this to his warriors,
Telling grim horrors to those who understood,
Telling it to the bloodthirsty
Among his living men,
Telling it to those bloodstained warriors
Who crushed all they confronted.
To the heroes who did not flinch
When the fierce gun cracked,
To the champions who never fled from birth
When the black musket thundered,
He spread his hands and strode about,
Entrusting every detail
Of the turmoil that would follow.
He taunted some,
Confirmed the words of others,
And they pledged themselves.
For the Kakanchin realm was mighty,
And thus they stood together,
Speaking as though they guarded the world,
Binding the whole fleeting world
Firmly within their words.
Before Bilgich had closed his mouth,
Before he finished ordering the champions
To drive the horses onward,
Before he rebuked the foolish,
Encouraged the wise,
And finished speaking,
A shout arose from the qibla side.
When they heeded that cry,
They saw a lone man appear,
One who dwelt in the Ala-Too,
Whom no man could defeat in combat,
Who would not surrender his comrade’s life.
“God forbid we see his face!”
He raised the cry of “Kyrgyz!”
And thrust forth his spear,
His skill surpassing all measure.
When he saw that sign,
He truly perceived the worth and stature
Of Joltoy, who boasted of valor;
Now, damn it,
Chonbilgich’s confidence deserted him.
“God forbid we see his face!
What will the meaning of this be?
His figure and noble bearing,
His heroic appearance—
He will strike down anyone who faces him.
He will suck them in like Black Mardai
And devour countless lives.”
Chonbilgich was keen-sighted;
He truly was a great knower,
Even in death his words were clear,
