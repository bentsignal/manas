Your gray-maned wolf, fraught with peril,
Would never turn back from an enemy.
His protectors numbered nine;
That poor man riding with a swagger—
It was then they came to sustain him.
His forty holy companions cried out
And gave their full protection
To the gray wolf of surpassing fortune.
Each possessed the awe of a thousand men;
The forty holy companions and Khydyr Ilyas
Surrounded the hero on every side.
Much of it is false and much is true;
No person has gone and seen it.
Half is invention and half is true;
Unless the Creator himself knows,
No person has returned from his presence.
The lions who departed long ago
We made into tales for our diversion,
So that we might relate the deeds
Of heroes who have vanished.
Your champion, son of the Bloodthirsty Manas,
Gladdened the heart that looked upon him.
He held the strength of a thousand men
And appeared like a gray hill.
He had the racehorse the Creator gave him,
His beast Buurul beneath him;
He had strength to slaughter alone
The swarming Chinese and Manchus.
Aquiline-nosed and stern of face,
Here was one worth an entire age.
If you observed the young lord, that gray wolf,
You could only marvel.
Ask nothing of the hero's nature:
He could throw an age into turmoil.
The pure Creator's name is great;
He creates every kind of living soul.
With fourteen swift horses in his train,
Having received an old saint's blessing,
Semetey rode eagerly,
Setting out toward Afghanistan.
His soul-companion Kulchoro
Resembled a glowing red ember.
The Creator makes souls of every kind;
No one like him seemed to exist in the world.
Slightly stooped and broad across the shoulders,
Of that gray wolf with a hero's frame,
Which quality shall we describe?
Kanchoro, the legacy left
By the gray-maned hero Chubak,
Was also broad across the right shoulder;
He was a hero who could rout the Oyrat.
His fortune stood complete,
And he too was a hero worth the world.
Yet he had one small flaw:
There was a black-hearted place within him.
Seized by restless ardor, they traveled on,
Covering a prodigious distance.
They raised toward the sky
The red banner left by Manas,
And heard reports from every side:
"Churok, daughter of Akun Khan,
Carried the falcon away
And took it with her," they heard.
They set their road toward Afghanistan,
Crossing ridge after ridge,
And land of rounded hills,
Fording winding streams,
And traversing curving mountains,
Passing rough and rolling sands,
And crossing countless lands.
When the sun sank dimly,
At beautiful Kary-Kyya
The hero turned aside to camp.
The poor lion, proud as a rooster,
Folded his arms behind his back;
Sighing as though weary,
Your gray wolf fell deep in thought,
