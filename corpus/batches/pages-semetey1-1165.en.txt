When someone provokes his anger,
May he lay that man's father beside him!
May he grease his blue steel;
When someone crosses him,
May he lay low the forty companions.
May this Semetey become a hero,
May he become a keen-eared lion.
On a downward slope, may he spear a hero;
May he spear many in a single row.
May his fortunes be complete.
Descendant of ocean-like Manas,
May he be a lion like his father!
May he be a fire blazing on the ridge;
May no enemy withstand him!
Amen!” he said, spreading his hands,
And Koshoi gave the blessing.
Infidels and Muslims,
Saying, “Such is his sacred rite,”
Joined in the blessing.
The earth resounded.
Khan Manas received his blessing.
As for your dear elder Koshoi—
People, look on in wonder!
Burning and raging,
He put on the kandagai.
He prayed to the Almighty;
Your poor elder, the giant Koshoi,
Made ready like a starving tiger.
If death came, he was ready to die;
If strength came to your elder,
He was ready to meet
The Kalmyks' giant Joloy in combat.
Khan Bakai of surpassing strength,
Your elder, old Koshoi,
His white beard flowing,
Barefoot and bareheaded,
Advanced toward the wrestler.
Your ancient elder Koshoi's
Muscles were like a bull's back,
As though he could devour anyone who grappled him;
Like a tiger about to pounce—
Such was Koshoi's fury.
It seemed no man could wrestle him.
Thinking of honor and shame,
Your elder went out to the wrestler.
Your elder, old Koshoi,
Who left no one he seized unharmed,
Was going to contend
With the Kalmyks' Joloy;
He was going to fight to the death.
It was Kökötöy's feast,
Before the great assembly.
Dust flew to the sky;
People seethed in the dust.
They swarmed like black worms;
The people gathered, buzzing.
Saying it was Kökötöy's feast,
The gathering of many leaders,
Infidels and Muslims
Said wrestling would be held for sport,
That whoever threw his opponent would win the prize,
And whoever fell would be left with nothing.
The prize for all the wrestlers
Was a thousand black Bactrian camels
And a thousand black, blaze-faced mares.
Bringing them to war and having them guarded,
Having anyone who came near them beaten,
Among infidels and Muslims,
Hero Manas had set them aside.
When Manas unleashed his wrath,
The people gathered in a throng.
“The plundering Chinese and Manchus
Lined up as though they had drunk poison.
The peoples chose not to flee;
They stayed to watch the spectacle.
When Koshoi advanced, ready,
Let us leave this matter here
With the wrestler of the Kalmyks.
