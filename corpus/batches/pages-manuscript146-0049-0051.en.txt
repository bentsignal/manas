Without beginning the quarrel first,
Burulka hastened to speak,
Without bringing suspicion upon his people.
If he is base, if he lies low,
If he falls short of one praised by the people,
There is none among them whose courage is lacking,
If there is a man who would give his word,
You spoke words that set them at odds,
Do not provoke their champion,
When your intentions come into words,
Hold your pride within yourself,
Saying that what you did was granted,
Draw back this way again.
The command from Kyzyrdanak,
Learn, Kenen, who has drawn near,
Of the Chinese people’s quarrel.
Find how great their number is, then return,
Observe how many champions they have,
Though it be six months away, go to that land and return,
Learn their condition and return,
Observe their prowess and return,
Follow and learn their valor, then return,
Go and see what the valley folk are like,
See whether he truly is a courageous warrior,
Whether he can withstand a single clash,
Learn the measure between the two and return,
Go safely and return unharmed,
Return safe and sound,
Do not fail—take heed and return,
So spoke Kyz Tokur,
There is a single word you spoke,
Through a six-month hardship,
Guard your honor, and set out,
Go to learn his inner secret,
Without tiring, without faltering,
Do not cast away your honor in shame,
Go to learn the warrior’s secret,
Go to the one who came beneath the standard,
One man fit to face a thousand,
He has a wrestler’s strength and a lion’s courage,
When he meets a foe, he makes the soul recoil,
My dark lord, you possess it all,
A swift horse unsurpassed in horsemanship,
Your name is known throughout the world,
Behold your formidable fury,
When many enemies stood all around,
You have a champion plainly in sight.
You have the force to stir turmoil to its roots,
Even your wisest one has a flaw,
When he turns to disorder among the Burut,
You have one who levels a spear like wheat,
When battle joins, you have one who bathes them in blood,
Without losing the way in darkness,
You have one who ties them at the watch-post and shoots,
You have one who plunges into blood and fury,
You have one who turns back those who flee.
From the burden laid upon Ala-Too,
Putting out the fire they kindled,
Driving their lives from their bodies,
Without letting his back touch the ground,
Without mounting him on some poor nag,
Without letting his people pasture in the mountains,
Without letting them sow any crops,
Without letting them tether even two mares,
Let us give them no time to gather their senses,
Let us give them no time to rest.
Without letting them live by their own will,
Without letting them linger even two days,
Without letting a single one settle by himself,
Let us bind their bands of thirty,
Let us lay their bands of thirty low,
Their heroes and their lions,
Let us spill the blood of their chiefs,
Their unbridled ones who know no master,
Let us quench their breath in the water.
This Kurman seems to be Külchoro,
Let us dig out the roots of the slaves.
If strife rises from his ashes.
