I have parted, and from my city.
My heart has burned in flames,
And my arms have withered like dry reeds.
When I close my eyes and dream,
I lie with him and satisfy my longing;
While asleep, I walk beside him,
But when I wake, he is not there,
And I burn in this affliction,
Weeping and grieving until I turn pale.
Whenever I remember the leopard,
I am robbed of sleep at night.
My friend Külayım, hear my words:
When I sleep at night, I dream,
And in my dream I see a wondrous thing.
This is the sign within the dream:
Hungry lions and leopards
Have surrounded me.
What can this mean?
Interpret this dream for me.
The moon rose from my right breast,
The sun rose from my left breast,
And between my two breasts,
At the very core of my heart,
They descended and joined together.
I held the sun in my right hand,
And the moon in my left;
I was immersed in this wonder,
Clutching them tightly and refusing to let go.
‘Until my beloved returns from his journey,
I need you,’ I said, holding them,
And lay wrestling playfully with them.
What can this mean?
Interpret this dream for me.
Then I saw another wonder,
A marvelous thing whenever I recall it.
From an unknown place there came to me
Twelve keen swords of tempered steel;
They came to me as an inheritance.
One splendid, fitting blade
Fell into my right hand.
When I seized and swung it,
The black rock before me split apart.
The twelve steel blades were strong,
And wherever they struck became flame.
What can this mean?
Interpret this dream for me.
Wherever they struck became flame;
And I have another wonder to tell,
So listen closely, companion of my soul.
From out of nowhere there came
A wheat-colored falcon that would alight on no hand,
And its jesses wound around my waist.
That wheat-colored falcon of mine
Had a broad and sweeping flight,
Leaving torn prey on every side.
Its talons were black steel,
And blood rained wherever they seized.
I perched it upon my hand,
Mounted a chestnut horse,
And donned a precious silken coat;
Then I climbed a height to survey the land
And removed its hood.
It attacked a host of fanged beasts
And threw them into turmoil.
It seized lions and bears
And ripped open their bellies.
All the fanged beasts,
Helpless, shrieked aloud;
They all fled and vanished,
Scattered and shattered to pieces.
It crushed whatever it chased
And defeated whatever it fought.
Those that escaped its grasp
Died with their hearts broken by terror.
‘This is a peerless hunting bird,’ they said,
‘Blessings upon your falcon,’ they said.
A white-bearded holy man sobbed,
And all the people cried out together,
Bestowing their blessing upon me.
