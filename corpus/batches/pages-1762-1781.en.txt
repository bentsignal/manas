The Chinese people's red gold,
Brave Koshoi had loaded and taken.
When Aykojo's Bilerik
Fell into Chinese hands,
Bringing slaughter upon the Chinese,
Brave Koshoi had rescued him.
His wide eyes were like the morning star;
His great ears were like shields.
His blessing enriched the people,
Brought light to those he regarded.
He had saddled a leopard,
Studied in Mecca for five years,
Received an old saint's blessing,
And was called old Koshoi.
In Egypt and Baghdad,
He had studied five years there.
He had received Khojosan's blessing,
And was called leopard Koshoi.
Across the face of the earth,
He had sent the tidings all together.
Khan Koshoi of the Katagan,
When he had grown old—
‘Your khan has fallen gravely ill,’
Your spirited Koyonaaly said.
Khan Koshoi of the Katagan
He brought along in grief.
Aykojo of Andijan, in his mottled coat—
‘For my family father Manas,
Stand at the funeral prayer,’ he said,
And brought him along now.
Beyond him were forty holy men;
He went to the forty holy men.
To all forty holy men,
He brought news of the mighty one's plight.
Urgently speaking courteous words,
He went to the forty holy men:
“For my family father Manas,
Stand in honor of him.
