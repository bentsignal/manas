He was a man reeking of blood,
His valor was beyond compare,
A hellish figure when enraged,
His prowess was immense,
A hero worthy of girding at the waist,
Not merely the white-capped Kyrgyz people,
But the whole wide world he would subdue,
And bring to ruin, they said.
He was that lord, that hero,
When he waged war against the khagan,
That lion wielded a steel mace.
Against the infidel son of Ai-Zhangzhung,
Seytek, that mountain-like hero,
Before one could cry out,
Before a sentence could be finished,
Before one could pinch one’s nose,
Before a closed eye could open,
The socketed spear Syrnaiza,
Which would not recoil even from striking stone,
Whenever it found its mark,
Never returned without shattering it.
Upon the heroic son of Ai-Zhangzhung
He came from the flank,
Thinking, “Here is the point of the elbow,
Here in a living man are the lungs,
And here is the very windpipe.
If the Creator grants me honor,
Now is the moment to overturn him.”
That ever-ready hero
Measured and fixed upon the spot,
With matchless swiftness,
And drove the splendid steel Syrnaiza
Into the chosen place.
When he struck, they collided,
The iron-black charger
And Seytek’s Kulansur
Sank knee-deep into the sand,
The spear broke apart with a crash,
White splinters as large as a nomad’s hut
Flew rattling into the sky,
And the mighty son of Zhangzhung’s
Supporting stirrup strap
Was torn clean through.
His deep-blue iron helmet
Flew off and struck the ground.
There was a battle-axe with a steel head
In Seytek’s hand,
Its haft entwined with gold,
Its lamb-liver-shaped blade was keen steel,
Forged and honed by gathered masters.
So no place it struck would remain unharmed,
But would fester and grow inflamed,
So that the death of one it gashed
Would come within a mare’s milking,
His gall would burst and his soul burn,
And he would depart this world,
They had whetted its edge,
In the venom of a dragon
And the deadly vapor of a ravenous demon
They had tempered it sixty times,
And any mortal whom it touched
It instantly robbed of life;
Such was the Oikuma in his hand.
The most nimble and skillful champion,
A wise man versed in every stratagem,
Your finest gray wolf of war,
Grasped it by the haft,
Drew Oikuma from its sheath,
Skillfully swung the blade,
And struck the heroic son of Ai-Zhangzhung
Upon the head;
His head split in two,
The champion’s blood poured out,
And like a collapsing mountain
He toppled from his horse.
When the champion fell from his mount,
When he came crashing to the ground,
Er Bilgich, son of Kojozhash,
A destroyer surpassing all mortals,
A sorcerous archer,
