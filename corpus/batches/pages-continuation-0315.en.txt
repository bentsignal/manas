KENEN
THE BIRTH OF KENEN
The moon waned and days passed,
Many more days passed,
Nine months were fulfilled,
And after nine more days had passed,
When her time was complete,
The unfortunate noblewoman
Went into labor.
Her waist ached above,
Where death might strike, it ached,
Both her dark thighs ached,
Every bone, limb, and sinew,
Without exception, ached,
Her frail soul screamed,
Unable to endure, she sobbed,
Her ribs seemed torn apart,
As though it would take her life now,
The poor lady was utterly spent,
As though bowing low, she would surrender her life,
So grievous were her labor pains,
Her life hung by a single hair,
Her thoughts scattered in a thousand directions,
Her frail soul was blown away,
She strained and writhed,
Her bewildered mind was divided,
That accursed, merciless death
Now appeared before her eyes,
Worn down by torment,
Seeing no hope, she despaired of life,
When the fiercest labor came,
For life is sweet until death,
She kept writhing in agony.
At midnight,
When her labor came to term,
When the appointed hour was fulfilled,
Sixty old matrons
Waited in a circle around her,
What a wondrous sight it was,
A figure blessed with sacred grace,
The wonder of it was this:
The moon rose upon his brow,
The sun stood above his crown,
His perfect fortune set forth,
The fierce black-maned leopard—
She gave birth to brave Kenen.
The child dropped forth crying,
Shouting every battle cry,
He landed upright on his soles,
His kin rejoiced,
But the hateful enemy who begrudged him
Felt his breast split apart.
He bore every heroic sign,
And when they looked at his back,
He had a black mane a span long,
All the old women scurried,
And all the young wives,
Saying, “What an extraordinary horror!
This is no child but a calamity,
He is something wholly different,
He has a roaring voice,
Hair grows upon his back,
Fur covers his body,
He is one who will leave none unharmed,
The black eye of disaster!”
Losing their senses,
As though he might seize and swallow a young child,
They panicked and trembled,
Many fled in terror,
Many muttered incantations,
And many among the people grumbled.
