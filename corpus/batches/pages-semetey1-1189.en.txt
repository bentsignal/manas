Youngest son of Khan Alooke,
He was a fortress set against the foe.
At that time Kongurbay
Was the mightiest khan of the Kechils.
He bore a grudge,
Saying, "They slaughtered my father!"
Terrible in bearing, powerful in venom,
Kongurbay, son of Alooke,
Laid low whoever challenged him.
The black horse beneath him—
That black horse
Had wings upon its sides.
Kongurbay, son of Alooke,
Habitually threshed challenging foes
Like grain.
His waist was bound with cotton;
He was born in broad Andijan.
At six years old
He caused an uproar in Beijing.
At seven years old
The Manchus and Chinese wearied of him.
When he reached eight,
He scourged Beijing of Kakanchyn,
Making its people cry out.
At nine he stopped;
When he reached ten,
He devastated and ravaged
Great walled Beijing.
When he reached eleven,
He besieged great Beijing.
Kongurbay threshed
The finest stone-bedecked Chinese
Like grain.
When he reached twelve,
He was entered in the chronicle
Of Beijing in Kakanchyn,
And became known to the great men
Of the seething Chinese host.
The frightened Chinese
Made him a fortress against the foe
And the mightiest khan of the Kechils.
Kongurbay, son of Alooke,
Laid low everyone he met.
Kongurbay was that gray-maned lion;
At Khan Kokotoy's feast,
This was where Khan Manas and Kongurbay
Were destined to clash.
He mounted Algara
And put on a golden coat.
Taking the white spear in hand,
He waved a bay-piebald pennant.
A great breastplate shaped like a leather flask
Flashed beside his horse;
Bowl-sized gold and coral stones
Shone from the crown of his head.
His spectacles gleamed.
Cotton boots and hempen waist,
The swaggering great Kalcha;
Leather boots and belted waist,
The raging great Kalcha;
Girt with latticework iron,
The hulking great Kalcha.
His eyes were like the whole of a lake,
As though they would swallow whatever they saw.
The black brows above his eyes
Were like a folded cloak.
He bore the head of a crouching tiger
And the brows of a molting tiger.
He narrowed his slanting eyes
And stroked his hair-thin mustache.
If you looked at his mustaches,
They were like the haft of a great axe.
If you looked at his nostrils,
They were like the pouch of a selebe.
The dirty-nosed Kongurbay
Of the Chinese,
With a separately made golden bowl,
And tobacco six spans long,
