The people gathered,
Made a claim against my poor head,
And laid the penalty upon my neck,
O gray-maned leopard lord.
You left without lying in my embrace;
In the fortress at Bukhara,
Because of this hero,
I was left an unmarried woman in Altai.
"Rather than be left unmarried as a maiden,
I will die from this sight;
I will see the face of Judgment Day," I said.
When Manas returned to Talas,
My poor soul sought vengeance
To catch him from behind,
To stalk the bloodletter
And offer him as a sacrifice,
And thus take revenge
For being left unmarried.
My poor hair, thick as sedge,
I tied tightly atop my head,
And put on a man's clothing.
A golden-cheeked battle-axe
I fastened firmly to my arm;
The double-edged Zulpukor
I strapped on loosely.
When I pursued
Generous Lord Manas,
Firing a gun and drawing a bow,
Thinking to stalk and slaughter him,
To put the broad-chested lord
To death,
I advanced in terrible rage,
Saying I had been left unmarried as a girl—
A grievous wrong before all the world;
Among the Kyrgyz there is no such deed.
When I stalked the leopard,
He had camped
On a spur of the accursed mountain,
Playing chatyrash and making merry;
So he was passing his time.
They were slaughtering
Forty barren foals.
Without a care for worldly goods,
He was living in pleasure.
All forty companions
Surrounded the hero
In a circle.
The hero of the forty companions,
Saintly hero Bakay,
The valiant one among them all,
Had readied his bow, poor soul.
As I meant to shoot Manas,
Grease issued from my fingers
And blood from beneath my nails.
When I raised the bow to my face
And drew near the valiant man,
Nine guardian spirits
Stood at the armpit
Of the hero favored by Khojos;
Forty chiltens supported him
And fixed their precious gaze upon him.
All forty lions
Had then surrounded
The hero.
A black-striped leopard
Strained at his side.
Unable to endure the sight,
When I saw his guardians,
I thought, "Alas, lion and leopard,
Let him leave me unmarried if he wishes;
Let his heart make me a concubine if it wishes.
If he will not take me from Bukhara,
I will gather girls from forty households,
Go stay in a white yurt,
And ingratiate myself as his wife."
I formed this plan,
Turned back my black-blazed courser,
And galloped away,
