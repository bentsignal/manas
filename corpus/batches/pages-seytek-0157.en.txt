I have been waiting for you.
I listened carefully to your words,
Calling you the legacy left by the noble one,
I made you my hope.
I gladly listened to your words,
Calling you the legacy left by the fierce warrior,
I made you my protector.
I watched you closely,
Thinking to bind the sole heir of the mighty lion
As a support around my waist,
And set out to avenge
My valiant gray-maned uncle,
To trample upon the head
Of Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
To scatter all his livestock,
And dig a well in his place of honor,
To unleash a great fusillade,
To hurl him down and, from his blood,
Drink by the double handful,
And gouge out both eyes
Of Kanchoro, the foe I never forget;
Though once we shared the same ancestry,
I would feast upon his flesh.
I would drain his blood by the spoonful
And end the anguish in my heart;
To unleash a terrible conflict—
Waking or sleeping, my desire
Is to avenge my gray-maned uncle.
Though countless herds fill the plains,
Whenever my falcon-like uncle Semetey
Comes suddenly to mind,
I flare and burn like fire,
And my life cannot be contained in my grasp.
When the towering gray-maned hero lived,
I was a glowing crimson ember;
There was none like me in the world,
I was a bullet ready to be fired,
Subduing the whole wide world,
A daredevil surpassing all mankind.
Without my hero, I became like snow;
I slaughtered those before me and laid them low,
I made the Manchus cry out,
And crushed the strongest
Of the coat-clad Chinese.
The khan upon the throne was slain,
All the finest of them were slain.
I asked the hero for his renowned swift gray horse
And mounted it,
Subduing all who faced me,
And drove them into Beijing.
Hear of your father, the hero who passed away;
Listen, my child Seytek:
By following him, I became a hero
And stood equal with heroes’ sons.
Orphaned, I came of age;
With difficulty I rejoined my people.
Left tiny as a louse, I grew mighty,
And among all creation became Choro the Great.
Such is my story, if you will hear it:
My wings were broken,
And I lost my fierce warrior,
The panther huge as a black mountain.
My lungs are rising into my mouth;
I stand here unable even to cough.
Bullets pierced and bound my chest—
Twelve charges and eighty bullets—
And I can no longer bear them.
I have no strength left to gird my waist,
No sheltered place in which to lie,
No mountain looming as my refuge,
No mighty warrior uncle
To sit as my protector.
I lack my two hands, dear as life,
Which shattered rocks in service.
Your uncle grieved in servitude,
For the fierce warrior who had sheltered him
Was lost to him.
I burned with grief at that loss,
But, my child, bold Seytek,
I knew the noble one had left an heir—
