It seemed to be running.
Having died, he seemed
To come alive again.
The treasure-filled forty rooms—
He opened their mouths wide as sacks,
For the sake of the spectacle,
He squandered and scattered it.
He had horns and trumpets sounded
And word proclaimed by the herald:
"Infidel and Muslim alike,
Your peoples have come here.
Many seething great lords,
All of you have come here.
My father Kökötöy has died;
The journey from which none return—
My khan-father has undergone it.
Let one champion from the infidels
And one champion from the Muslims
Wrestle in the center;
Let them seize each other's trouser cuffs.
Let the champions step forward
And begin the spectacle.
For these excited people,
Let them provide a spectacle.
At Kökötöy's feast,
Let them say such was the custom;
Infidel and Muslim alike,
Let them tell their peoples.
As the wrestling prize,
I stake a thousand black camels
And a thousand black-blazed mares.
From red gold coins,
Filling one saddlebag,
I stake it with all its contents;
I stake a bondwoman with her child.
Of all that ears have heard and eyes have seen,
Who has staked prizes like me?
Dust flies into the sky;
Infidel and Muslim alike,
You have gathered in a mass.
It is Khan Kökötöy's feast,
The place of the great gathering.
There is the swift horse Maaniker.
Do not think my wealth exhausted;
There are six rooms full of possessions.
I migrated here from Tashken;
There is a line already drawn.
For infidel and Muslim alike,
There is a spectacle to display.
I have a song to sing;
For infidel and Muslim alike,
I have a contest to begin.
Even in forty years it would not end—
I have livestock left by my father.
Why should I tell of the rest?
For carrying red gold,
I have a myriad red camels,
And livestock worth a great deal.
Infidel and Muslim alike,
Divide Kökötöy's prizes
Fairly among yourselves."
After Bokmurun issued the decree,
The common people heard.
From the black Kalmyk people,
Saying, "I shall go out,"
Khan Zholoy stepped forward.
Do not ask of Zholoy's station:
He was as large as a mountain,
As though he would eat whoever appeared.
Khan Zholoy of the black Kalmyks
