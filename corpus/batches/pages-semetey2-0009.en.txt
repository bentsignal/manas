Carrying her child upon her back,
She fled toward Karakhan, they say.
Into the city of Khan Temir
She wandered and disappeared, they say;
Into the city of Karakhan
She fled and disappeared, they say.
Thus bereaved Kanykei departed,
And Semetey fled away.
Living as an orphan, he grew to manhood,
Joined the people of the land,
Grew broad and vigorous, and became a hero,
The equal of any hero’s son.
He came and settled in Talas, they say,
And became manifestly a gray wolf
Far surpassing his father, they say.
I heard he settled in Talas,
At shrub-filled Keng-Kol.
I heard the Bloodthirsty One’s son Semetey
Sucked dry
Whoever offered resistance.
He emerged a greater hero than his father,
A lion who convulsed the world;
His name is heard scorching the earth.
I received it from the transmitters of tradition
And asked those who knew.
I heard that orphan did as he pleased
To every one
Who challenged him.
I heard he cut down
Anyone who crossed his path.
I fear that accursed one,
For they say that orphan is coming;
They say he will gird his waist firmly
And pursue Manas’s blood-price
From us who dwell in Beijing.
They say the misbegotten son is coming,
Gathering troops and assembling an army.
They say he will bring distress upon Beijing.
Whether alive or dead,
That splendid man and I
Will face one another, they say.
I hear reports from messengers;
I hear that champion, son of the Bloodthirsty One,
Will not refrain from challenging us.
They say he is a valiant man who will clash;
If his strength suffices, they say,
He will battle even the Kakan.
I heard he surpasses
His father Manas, the gray wolf.
I heard he does whatever he pleases
In the land where he rules.
When his wrath was roused,
I heard he slaughtered them all,
Beginning with his own father, Khan Zhakyp.
With no soul left to protest,
I heard a hero had arisen—
An infidel fiercer
Than Manas, I heard.
He is no son who will stand and watch,
No slave who will refrain from grappling.
You were a hero, Karagul;
Be wary of this side!
When we fought in those days,
There was one called Bakai;
He was the finest man of all.
He was highly skilled among spearmen,
And was foremost at reading
The stratagems of the enemy.
He was a lion quick with a sword,
A hero who was a marksman with a gun.
Girded at his narrow waist,
He attacked Beijing alone,
That city too vast for the earth to bear.
His eyes blazed like a lion’s;
He regarded death as less than a hair
And carried Manas’s golden banner.
If a hero was speared, who speared him?
As I recall, hero Bakai speared him—
