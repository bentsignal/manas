Ask for Akshunkar and bring it back!
If you abandon it, there will be disgrace;
From your poor, fortunate sister-in-law
I entrust you to ask for the white falcon!"
While Semetey stood speaking,
The young man shot away without stopping;
From beside Semetey,
Before one could say this or that,
He vanished from sight.
He urged Buurul in surging strides;
Without turning back, Kulchoro
Drove him as wildly as one bereaved.
Before one could say this or that,
In the time it took to open and close one's eyes,
Hero Choro, sustained by Saint Shay,
Had crossed over
The pass of Agynek at speed.
Like a practiced horseman,
He covered Buurul beneath him
With a broadcloth horse-cover.
Behold the wonder of the young man:
He rode red Buurul.
Distinctive was Buurul's nature;
He tied the horsehair tail in a firm knot.
Semetey's tall Buurul
Had grown fat upon tumbleweed grass;
His sleekness was like a lance,
And that creature Buurul
Jumped like a flea.
The sight made the viewer's soul burn;
His splendid, abundant forelock
Seemed tied precisely level with his eyes.
His splayed hind legs were like a ravine;
The white blaze upon his forehead
Was like steel tempered and set.
His great hooves were broad as basins,
And the croup behind him
Was like an open, unbounded plain.
No mother could say, "I bore him here,"
And no father could say, "I sired him."
He was a child of the wind spirit,
A racer alongside the wild kulan,
Born of Tulpar
And Maid Saykal's Algara.
He did not start when he heard an uproar;
Even amid shots that deafened the ear,
He never misplaced a single step.
Raised apart among the spirits,
This creature, the horse Buurul,
Grew upon rocks where deer could not walk.
He skimmed across mountain summits,
Leading ninety swift horses,
And roamed across countless lands.
He summered with the spirits
And grappled with wild kulan.
Ask nothing of every limb
Of Buurul standing in the line.
Ridden for six months, he would not tire;
Driven over hills, he would not wear down.
Until he reached sixty years of age,
His molars would not loosen with age.
Saying, "He may flee the saddle,"
They made him drag a rope from his neck;
Saying, "His limbs will become bronze,"
They trained him over rolling ground.
When Buurul was a yearling,
They had an amulet fixed around his neck;
The horse-judges tested and approved him.
Fondling him, hero Manas
Had him fed from the treasury's stores.
Long ago an owl feather was fastened to his head.
When Buurul belonged to Manas,
He curved his neck like a deer;
If game a target's height appeared,
He leaped without hesitation.
Once his quality as a courser was known,
Offering weighed gold and fine cloth,
The khan of Bukhara asked for him.
