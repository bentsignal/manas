He enslaved in Talas
My mother, noble Kanykei.
The accursed man seized as plunder
My sister-in-law Chachykei.
He drove away and seized my livestock
And drained my blood till I wasted away.
He made me taste bitter salt
And let my daughter be despoiled.
Those witless dogs grew insolent
And turned savagely upon their master.
The strutting villains gathered
And raided my caravan from Keng-Kol.
Grieving and sorely disgraced,
I returned without taking revenge.
They plundered my old people.
Against those swaggering dogs,
Because I stood alone,
I returned unable to ease my pain.
To gird my waist firmly,
To avenge my heroic lord and brother—
My tongue speaks, but I have no strength;
Though I glare at them, I am helpless.
To clash with them and raise a battle,
I no longer have the thick wrists,
The stony heart, or the days I once had.
My two hands can no longer move;
My six limbs are no longer whole.
I no longer have blood surging beneath my skin,
Nor strength to spur against
Those dogs accursed by Heaven.
The thoroughbred once pranced among the herd,
But your uncle’s former valor has departed.
Unable to die though wishing for death,
I languish here like this.
The bright flowers have vanished from the pasture;
Your uncle, once a lion of the world,
Now languishes like this.
If I fought them as an equal,
I no longer have my towering stature.
If I met them face-to-face in battle—
“He will become your enemy,”
My mother had told me.
“That Choro who will turn foe—
Remove him from your sight.
My precious mother Kanykei
Said to cut off his head with a sword.
“His mischief will harm many;
Finish him once and for all.
Set matters right and end our grief;
Let the sorrow people speak of disappear.”
But my foolish uncle, the hopeless man,
Would not hear of it.
“If this lone boy proves a kinsman,
What blessings might he not bring?
And if this boy proves an enemy,
What evil might he do?
If I grow angry, he will die.”
When this boy fixed his hostile gaze
Upon my foster father Chubak,
Upon the young falcon Chubak left behind,
My heedless, misguided uncle
Still would not listen.
He steeped poison in my cup;
Kanchoro brought me to ruin.
He slaughtered and ate the fostered foal,
Yet whenever enemies surrounded us,
He claimed he would be a true brother.
In the end, Kanchoro
Drank spirits and ran mad;
Greedy for blood, he slaughtered my brother.
He drank kumis and ran mad,
And struck down my people in his frenzy.
No pleading moved him;
Though I begged, he would not spare my life.
No entreaty moved him.
He drove away my livestock,
Made my blood flow like water,
And utterly destroyed my honor.
He gave my banner up as spoil
And hacked down my pride with an axe.
