I am wretched Bakai!
While the lions lived,
This is where we shot the Alengir bow;
Against the teeming infidels,
Waging battle there,
This is where we seized their khanate!
This is where we slaughtered many of their people,
Plundered their daughters, and rested!
I know the road to Beijing well;
My valiant boys, wait without haste.
It is ill-fated that you were left on foot!
This is darkness, this is night;
If a brave man is parted from his horse,
This is the day of the end of time!
I have begun my journey to Beijing;
Champions left behind by the hero,
I have left you with the baggage train!
The outward journey is three months,
The return journey another three months;
Your noble uncle Bakai—
Look for him in the sixth month!
If I fail to return by the sixth month,
Shoot an argali and eat it,
And depart saying, ‘Our uncle,
Elder Bakai, has died.’
Cross through the water of that distant caravan route;
Lamenting in grief,
Saying, ‘Both our uncles died together,’
Give up hope of us and go!
The cannon the Creator ordained—
We go to see it;
If our doom comes from the khagan,
We go there to die!
To Beijing, where even a prophet would not tread,
To unleash a wondrous slaughter,
And in the khagan's Beijing
To avenge the mighty hero!”
So saying and exhorting them, the gray wolves
Rode out and took the road,
Leaving the boys with the baggage train.
Your champion, the bloodthirsty man's son,
Born especially
Of his father, noble Manas,
The lone hero, son of the gray wolf,
Mounted Buurul and made him surge,
The splendid hero rode out,
Making him leap and strain.
“I shall bind my belt tightly,” he said;
“I myself shall face them,
And from the Kalcha in Beijing
Seek vengeance for the valiant man.
I shall spend upon Beijing
All the strength gathered in my hands;
I ask the Creator
For the single faith within my breast!
The one I shall grapple is the khagan of China;
The one I oppose is Bakburchun.
I go to grapple with its khan,
Marching on a holy campaign
To Khan Kongurbai's city,
For my father, lion Manas,
For the hero gone to the next world!
Against that hero Kongurbai,
I go beginning a holy campaign,
Against the infidel full of wrath.”
Such was his word of counsel.
Leaving the boys with the baggage train,
They repaired and donned their ak-olpok armor;
They buckled around their waists
The gray cartridge pouch left by Manas.
The boys wailed and wept,
Entreating the Almighty!
His anguish smoking fiercely,
Külchoro stood sobbing;
Beside him was steadfast Kanchoro,
Known as the son of Chubak,
His dear eyes like a saker falcon's,
Unblinking, filled with tears,
