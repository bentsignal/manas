Speech was his custom.
He was a renowned steed,
And of ordeals six months away
Tootoru would speak like a man
And give warning.
If there was a net none could escape,
A calamity no living soul could cross,
An army encircling the land
From which no winged creature could flee,
Whenever he was mounted for battle,
Tootoru, my winged steed,
Was accustomed to speak of it.
The reason he could speak was this:
This very horse Tootoru
Had appeared by providence
From the desert of Mediyan,
From the pass of It-Kyyan,
Where no cawing crow was found,
No croaking raven flew,
There was no grass for beasts to chew,
No water for man to swallow—
From the desert no dog could cross,
From the shore where no living thing roamed.
He was encountered as a two-year-old
And found as a three-year-old.
He was raised in a dark place,
With an amulet fastened to his head;
The blood-drinking brass-beaked crone
Tested him there and approved him.
No other soul could ride him,
And he could not refrain from speaking.
“Hold and turn my horse’s mouth;
What will Tootoru say now?
Wait, woman Chürök, stand still!
No human speaks such words—
What is my beast, my bay, saying?”
Thus spoke brave Kyyaz,
Now raging, now calming,
A sheathless sword strapped to him;
Blood-stained Kyyaz raged.
When the key struck his forehead,
Tootoru, being a swift steed,
Staggered in circles,
Swaying like one dazed,
Yet did not topple and fall;
He shied away, then recovered.
The bay horse that had been speaking
Lost the use of his tongue.
His forelock fell over his eyes;
The hair tassel split his forehead
A full span wide.
Subdued by the blow,
The poor bay horse
Had his speaking tongue bound.
Alas, that woman Aichürök
Raised her stately head like a banner,
Turned a woman’s head into a warrior’s,
And struck the speaking steed
Down level with the black earth;
She deafened his hearing ears
And bound his tongue.
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Was that day in Jeti-Özön,
In the pleasure garden of the fortress,
Seated there
Upon his throne, ruling the people.
Such was Kyyaz when you saw him,
His bluish fist as large as a knob;
Such was his appearance.
He raged like a blue-maned lion,
As insight came to him from the unseen;
He roared in anger,
Calling out to Aichürök,
His bellow splitting heads,
His shout splitting stones:
“Accursed Chürök, wait there!
If he speaks, that is my learning;
Tootoru is my wing.

When a black day weighs upon his soul,
