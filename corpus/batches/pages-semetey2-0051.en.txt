The sun sank, shimmering,
And the foremost of the people lay down.
From the encampment with gates of steel,
From the fortress of Great Beijing,
Jerking the reins of gold
And urging Algara onward,
The Kalcha’s state was such:
The flesh of his forearms creased as he strained,
“What is wrong with the dog?” he said,
Quarreling with Algara.
When Algara opened his stride—
That unsurpassed great black horse—
If he stepped upon black stone,
It burst and crumbled into powder;
The madman’s black horse
Flew rumbling like a cloud.
Around the hour of midnight,
The Kalcha rose in agitation;
Before midnight had even come,
He reached the roaring Sary-Suu.
Coming upon the Sary-Suu,
Algara stretched out his stride.
Was Algara not a thoroughbred?
At the great roaring river,
Algara did not draw back,
But leapt wherever he met it.
To the pasture where the horses had lain,
To the pass of Kyr-Kaiyng,
To the shore of the salty Kürpüldök,
To the horses’ grazing ground,
Hero Kalcha came riding.
When he looked toward Zholum’s camp,
Hoping his people were safe,
The herdsman at Zholum’s camp
Lay stretched out dead,
His body remaining, his head gone,
Plainly and utterly slain.
Dust had been churned in every direction;
As though a giant beast had trampled it,
All of Zholum’s camp
Lay smashed and ground to bits.
“It is true that Karagul
Saw what happened in this place.
And it is true that all the others
Have been killed.”
Then Kongurbai,
Unable to stand still in his fury—
With his great moon-crested golden pipe,
That Kalcha huge as a yurt—
Into its bowl he thrust
A great handful of tobacco,
And now took the pipe in hand.
He struck the Chinese flintstone,
Set an ember upon it,
And the keen-minded hero Kalcha
Drew upon his pipe;
It rasped aloud in the usual way,
And when he released the smoke,
It billowed like a sleeve.
The hair on his crown stood on end:
“They have slaughtered my herdsmen,” he said,
“And carried away my horses;
They have worked another outrage,” he said.
Unable to collect his thoughts,
He turned Algara’s head.
As though he had endured great grief
For many days and nights,
He stood there on that spot.
Kongurbai pursued behind them;
Let us leave him there,
And begin the news of Semetey,
Who was driving the horses:
Upon a towering mountain ridge,
On a jutting spur,
Lay Zhangy-Zher, found by Almambet.
There in Zhangy-Zher itself,
At the mine where gold was dug—
Upon the heights above that mine,
