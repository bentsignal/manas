I was weak; now I am a hero.
Submit to what we demand
And give us Akkelte!”
Against Kerke, khan of the Russians,
They brought overwhelming force;
Saying, “Give us Akkelte,”
Lion Bakai the leopard
Pressed the siege hard.
Wise Khan Kerke
Was struck by mighty God;
Akkelte, which they came to ask for—
He devised a way not to give it.
Speaking drawn-out words,
Offering evasive excuses,
He delayed them.
Saying, “Take it after six months,”
He set the time far off.
Sixty Russian surveyors
Had gone on patrol for six months.
“Count the days,
Record the days in writing;
Return after six months,
And then receive your answer!”
Hearing such words from Kerke,
Hearing, “After six months,
Come and take Akkelte,”
They attacked the Russians,
Shattered their palace city,
And raided it there.
In fury they made ready,
All the bloodthirsty heroes devised a plan
To enter the original city directly
And raid it completely.
From a jutting promontory,
From a pine-covered spur,
Valiant Manas and Khan Bakai
Came up onto the pass.
Dragging their wagon,
Sixty Russian surveyors—
They saw and captured them on the plain.
Turning red haze into night,
Bakai brought the sun to a halt.
Keen Bakai and Khan Manas
Did what had to be done.
They captured the surveyors;
With the piebald stallion at their head,
They seized Akkelte.
When they had seized Akkelte,
Kydyr blessed
Keen gray-wolf Manas.
The twelve joined hands as one;
Your keen gray-wolf Manas
Became renowned as mighty Manas.
Akkelte, taken as spoil
From the Russians’ palace city,
Did not deviate when fired afar;
Akkelte became renowned,
The sharp weapon that struck at distance,
The treasure inherited
From his father, gray-wolf Manas.
Its keenness was proven among the people,
An inheritance remaining in his hand
From Manas of matchless fortune.
When enemies approached, he slung it on;
The gray wolf cast it across his back.
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
And Semetey with a mighty host
Were to meet in battle.
With Akkelte slung on,
The sight stirred the heart;
In your champion’s hand,
The beauty of Syrnaiza appeared.
Seeing Syrnaiza,
The Afghans’ hearts were stirred, saying,
“Your champion will spare no foe.”
The Syrnaiza in his hand
Was made from birch of Orolmo-Too,
Cut straight from the tree,
