Their time to come had now arrived.
At Küdür in Ükürchü,
He had the cattle driven out to graze.
Below Aygyr-Zhal,
He had a great tethering line stretched.
Along Üch-Kapkak,
For Kökötöy's cooking pits,
He had the hearth pits dug.
To spacious Küdür,
He had horses brought in.
On the far side of those horses,
He had the heroes slaughter them.
For Kökötöy's feast,
This was suitable food.
For the Shibee and the Kalmyks,
So they might eat it when they came,
He slaughtered mighty-horned oxen and cows.
For the Manchus and Black Chinese,
So they might eat these when they came,
He slaughtered camels suited to them.
For the Kyrgyz and Oyguts,
He made merry and slaughtered mares.
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Made it splendid and spacious,
Devoting all his wealth equally.
He marked the midpoint
Between the Black Kalmyks and Kyrgyz,
And made this feast of Kökötöy
Unforgettable to the people
Until the end of time,
Making meat as high as Ala-Too.
Leading the people,
Your elder uncle Koshoy
Raised the standard with its golden finial
And sent it soaring into the sky.
The turmoil Bokmurun
Brought upon the people!
Your uncle came riding
His swift Kerkabylan.
All his weapons made ready,
He fitted the golden-cheeked battle-ax
To his wrist.
His brow was high and his face imposing;
Those who looked at him marveled.
Tall and broad-shouldered,
Kökbörü, son of Urumkhan,
Came from among those I had seen.
If you ask who came after him,
Wearing a coat called chayingi,
Riding a horse like Chalkuyruk,
Carrying a spear without a tassel,
With only a sword at his side,
The youngest of nine sons,
Eleman's cherished son,
A servant of the one God.
It had been seven years
Since he descended beneath the earth;
It had been only seven days
Since he emerged upon the earth.
When the seven-headed witch
Worked her perfect spell,
She frightened his father Eleman.
That old woman imprisoned Eleman
For seven days,
Tore out and flung away his beard,
Yanked out and flung away his mustache,
And beat him for seven days.
Softened like a worked hide,
The wretched old man was exhausted.
She took brave Töshtük's knucklebone
From his daughter-in-law.
Begging for his life, Eleman
Went before the old woman.
Because his knucklebone had been left behind,
Eleman's brave Töshtük
Came the following day.
Beside that knucklebone,
The seven-headed old woman saw him.
