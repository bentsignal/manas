Bearing enmity toward us.
Semetey, son of Manas,
That mighty hero, had come;
He had brought splendid slaughter.
Beginning with you, Abygyi,
He cut off heads with his sword!
What can this mean?
Interpret this dream for me!
He shattered the people among the rocks
And brought splendid slaughter;
Beating them into a spectacle,
He slaughtered upon the road
All who challenged him
And divided them among the Dangyts!
What can this mean?
Interpret this dream for me!”
His anger flared and bristled;
When Karagul heard this,
He rose in towering rage.
The hair on his crown stood on end;
Karagul, who descended upon you,
Had hair bristling at his nape.
He raged against the boy,
Had him confined in the broad house,
And blocked his way, Karagul.
For saying he had dreamed, he had him butchered,
Clubbed and beaten,
His hands and feet bound,
And beaten from head to foot.
He had him driven about with blows,
Had all his clothing torn,
And made him swallow nine bowls of water
One after another.
Giving him an ankle bone, he tortured him;
For saying he had dreamed,
They all tormented the boy together,
Dragged him by the ears,
Had the boy clubbed,
And had the herdsmen beat him.
Bozbala, who thought himself clever,
The youngest of the herdsmen,
Was made to learn his reckoning thus:
When he bent over,
The prince had a whip laid across his backside.
“Your dream is fox dung,” he said,
“And you yourself are my dung.
I am the one who deceived
His father, bloodthirsty Manas, six times!
What profit is there in turning back?
While I, Abygyi, stand here,
How could the Burut come!
Look how brazen he is,
Saying, ‘In my dream I saw
The orphan son of Manas’!
Look how worthless he is,
Saying, ‘I saw Oen's son’!
Look how useless
Accursed Bozbala is,
Saying, ‘I saw the son of Manas’!
He beat the dreamer like a dog;
Now no one remained who would dream.
The two-eyed, clairvoyant boy—
Was he not a bakshy,
Saying, ‘I saw the son of Manas,’
And, ‘I learned all that is and is not’?
Was he not a good boy!
The accursed Burut
Has no power to come to China.
All the heroes who would bring
Splendid slaughter are gone!
He has no pass on which to set his resolve;
That orphan who wandered away
To the city of Temir Khan
Has no reason to come here!”
Because Bozbala said he had dreamed,
Karagul saw it as an evil burden.
His shouting deafened the ears;
Karagul lay there that day.
