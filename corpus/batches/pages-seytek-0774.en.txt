I enslaved all his encamped household,
And his thoughts became black as night.
His violence became known among the people;
He could not find a handspan of ground,
Nor a grave to enter,
Nor a hollow in which to shelter,
Nor river water to plunge into,
Nor a village where he might encamp,
Nor any land to flee to.
I waged countless battles.
Strewing grass upon his yellow trail,
I pursued the infidel, the monster’s son,
Until he turned pale and wan.
Strewing grass upon his fleeing tracks,
I hounded him until his face went white.
Upon the swaggering swine
I brought a mighty conflict.
I tore the villain away
From the lord in whom he trusted
And from his secure stronghold.
Forcing the villain into submission,
I severed him from his people.
I slaughtered his strongest men;
Straddling his bravest,
I annihilated them utterly.
I trod upon their heads
And hacked their people apart alive.
I began a thousand conflicts
And slaughtered every last one
Of the men who showed courage.
The hillsides, bluffs, and valleys
Became the road I myself had cleared.
I had taken an army with me
To seize the villain, the monster’s son.
Unable to find the monster’s son,
I wore the backs of my horses raw.
I roamed all four corners
Of the world, circling them four times.
I opened my white charger to full speed,
Driving the villainous slave before me;
I couched my steel spear
And roamed over the earth,
Searching without rest for seven years.
His oppression has not abated;
He has besieged Zhylgyn, Keng-Kol, and Talas,
And his tyranny still lies upon them.
That bright pass with its lofty bluffs—
That is the road I cleared.
This is the army that fought
The infidel, the monster’s son;
This is the mouth that traded words with him.
I am the maiden Kuyaly, the champion—
I shall unleash a great conflict for the ages.
If I meet him face-to-face in combat,
Could Sarybay ever stand against me?
This was all I ever asked
Of almighty God.
Having blunted my steel
And destroyed Sarybay,
At the encampment pitched here since yesterday,
Where Kökmonchok, daughter of Beri,
Was holding her games,
I hobbled Zhelzhetpes beneath me
And tied him to graze.
I removed the saddle
From my courser and left him bareback;
Beneath the plane tree below,
I tethered him on a long lead to graze,
Quenched his thirst beyond the ridge,
And let him crop the lush grass.
I shod Zhan-Baspas,
And beneath me the charger Saykashka,
With shoes of gold.
Finding no mountain left to tread,
No enemy left to fight,
We hunted with clamor, raising dust,
Played on the swings, making merry,
Held maidens’ games to great acclaim,
And played bold games amid clouds of dust;
When bored, we hunted roe deer.
