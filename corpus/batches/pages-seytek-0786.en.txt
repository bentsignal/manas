My heart pounding like a horse,
For I had received tidings from the unseen.
My dear heart rejoiced,
And I wondered where the famed champion Kuyaly
Had been born,
And in what land her mother
Had given birth to that champion.
He could traverse a horse’s whole course;
Across the entire wide world,
He could roam in twelve days.
He knew seventy different tongues
And traveled freely over the earth.
So to Agybai, son of the Argyn khan,
The interpreter of that gray wolf,
I gave six swift horses
And a letter small as my palm,
Dressed him in a fine gray robe,
And mounted him on the swift camel.
To discover whether she was indeed
The destined bride of the noble lion
And return with certain knowledge,
I mounted him on the swift camel
And sent him journeying across the earth,
Quenching his thirst with sunshine,
Feeding him provisions rich as külaзык,
And clothing him in flowered silk,
While the mount grew stronger with each day’s ride.
Mounting him on the swift camel,
I sent Agybai on his way.
Without letting a single soul know,
Without giving any person an inkling,
Without the gossipers learning of it,
Without even the dust-bearing wind knowing,
I spread out abundant wealth and countless livestock—
Pure gold coins and precious stones—
Before Agybai,
Saying, “Take these as gifts.”
Displaying all my riches,
I made poor Agybai,
From that time until this,
Grasp the holy Quran.
I had him pierce a steel bullet,
Chew a loaf of gray-wheat bread,
And swear ninety varied and solemn oaths
That he would reveal nothing.
I made him lick the sword’s edge
And swear upon immediate death.
“My dear Agybai,
When you mount Endik, rein in his mouth;
Among witless people, restrain your tongue.
The sorrowful words I have spoken
Have a name heard, but no visible form.
Kuyaly, daughter of Kaiypchal,
Is destined to be my daughter-in-law,
My daughter-in-law for the world hereafter,
The dark eye of prosperity.
My son Semetey,
A lion surpassing all heroes,
Is the one she shall marry.
She is the Night of Destiny itself.
My dear Agybai,
Learn where the champion Kuyaly
Sows her grain;
Learn her lineage, worthy of a father,
And the land where her clan took root.
If you return knowing this, Agybai,
We shall arrange the match while we live:
Setting aside many thousands of mares,
Separating double-humped camels,
And allotting abundant wealth and livestock,
With a white kerchief bound upon the brow,
We shall drive all the livestock
To seal the marriage pact.
Let us place a halter around her neck
And fetters upon her feet;
Let us hold a feast beneath the open sky
And take that proud one
To be our daughter-in-law.
Bringing her into the white palace,
We shall serve abundant kinds of sweet food.
