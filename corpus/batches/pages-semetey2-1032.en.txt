When the people clamored and spoke,
The man who said, ‘I alone am myself,’
Was driven on after they slaughtered and ate him,
Driven toward a deep-dug pit,
Driven toward a net in which he would be caught,
Driven toward torment where he would boil to death;
My noble father Köksö
Was led captive, tethered to a black horse.
His peerless steed was tied,
And all the giant champions
Were handed over to the forty warriors.
By force, there in Beijing,
He laid twelve khans to rest.
How could you have done this?
Heed what I am saying;
Without starting strife or raising turmoil,
Pay the blood-price for Köksö.
Lean, gray-maned Semetey,
If you are the hero Külchoro,
Then I am brave Ümötöy of Sary-Arka,
Whom no one has defeated in combat.
That is who I am.
If I bind tassels to my pinewood spear,
And demand the blood-price for Khan Köksö,
Accept the terms I have spoken.
Saying, ‘This is the price for your father Köksö;
This is your share of the blood-price,’
Give his son your answer.
Load gold coins and treasure
Upon Arbuurul.
Saying, ‘This is your father’s price,’
Bring before me the red roan with the sparse mane,
The fierce, slant-eyed steed,
Without suffering carnage or seeing strife;
Saying, ‘This is your father’s price,’
Lead it here before me.
With the roan horse at their head,
Quickly gather chosen horses from among the racers.
Without killing one another,
Gather them swiftly, causing no hardship.
Put your dark eyes up for sale;
Offer me Khan Bakay’s head as a gift!
Fold your hands and submit!
If you still know sin from merit,
Say, ‘We submit ourselves to you!’
My own cousin and dear friend,
Say, ‘We stand wholly at your command!’
This is all I now demand:
Pay my father’s blood-price now!
If I stiffen in anger
And my terrible rage takes hold,
Name the ridge beyond which you will cross;
There is no avoiding our confrontation—
Name the place where you will face me.
Name your pasture by twin Kara-Kyr.
If you will not heed my words,
If you will not pay my father’s price,
Name the place where we shall clash!
If you Kyrgyz say, ‘We will pay no blood-price,’
Do not smirk—speak the truth.
If you would meet us spear to spear,
If you would die exchanging gunfire,
If you would stand face-to-face,
Thrusting spears and firing bullets—
If you Kyrgyz are strong,
And would fight in bloody struggle,
I have no livestock I fear to leave strewn in the valley;
If you would fight to the death, Kyrgyz,
I have no life I wish to spare from death!
I have mustered an army for battle,
A vast host, leaving no man behind.
If I cannot take my father’s price,
Why should I remain at peace?
Valuing this life not even as a hair,
I shall fight to mutual destruction!
Through Achmaral, Merki, and broad Talas
I have come to carve a road;
