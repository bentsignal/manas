Hide your poor one within your breast today
And be my guardian, Kara-Üngkür.
Would that you opened
Your white-mottled canopy, Kara-Üngkür.
Why not press your wretched one
To your bosom today, Kara-Üngkür?
Dear Kara-Üngkür,
Nurse this poor soul.
Today all strength and vigor have left this forlorn man;
Dear Kara-Üngkür,
Be a mother and protect me.
Your wretched one has come seeking refuge;
Be fruitful and sustaining, Kara-Üngkür.
Let down milk and open your heart;
Be a mother, Kara-Üngkür.
Oh, beloved Almighty,
You made this the place where infidels were routed;
You poured forth your sacred power,
Yet brought death before my eyes.
Why have you reduced me to such misery?
I was a lion who strode forth proudly,
But you made my wide world narrow.”
Grieving, Külchoro
Took the cave for shelter,
And there your lionhearted hero lay.
With withered grass and feather grass,
With dark sedge and pale reeds,
He sealed the mouth of the cave,
Drawing a screen of dry stalks across it.
His strength gone, growing weaker,
The warrior lay bleeding.
At times he could speak;
At times he could not.
At times his eyes could see;
At times they could not,
So grievously had his strength failed.
Let us leave him there
And turn now to brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
And Kanchoro, son of Chubak;
Let us begin the tale of them.
In the marshes of Üyrülmö
They had gathered in a swirling throng;
In the marshes of Chogulma
They had assembled together.
Matching wits and comparing their gains,
Chasing and fleeing after one another,
Driving and testing each other,
Like creatures stricken with rabies,
They bickered, quarreled,
And plunged into a tumult.
They had entered an immense commotion
That could not be calmed;
They had entered a contest
Beyond human comprehension.
They had entered a mighty quarrel
That would not subside;
They had entered a trial
No man could face and pass.
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Was a wise and discerning man,
One who had plainly known
Both suffering and joy.
Hotly disputing their words,
They embarked upon their course;
They had entered a struggle
No man could hope to resolve.
Devising an extraordinary plan,
He found a cunning stratagem.
Bringing the whole world into submission,
What else could noble Kyyaz do?
He committed himself to a proposal.
Kanchoro and brave Kyyaz
Stood bristling at each other,
Wrangling angrily.
With shouts that split the sky,
They plunged into an uproar,
Into a tumult
From which no man could turn away.
