Broad-rumped, with long hair ornaments,
The young married women were beautiful;
In red dresses, with reed-slender waists,
The girls were exceedingly beautiful.
The pampered wife Chachykey
Prepared herself with eager resolve;
Such was her stately appearance,
Swaying as though struck by a hero.
Twenty were on her right,
Her companions sharing one life,
Coming out while supporting her;
Twenty were on her left,
Her companions who would share her suffering,
And they too came supporting her.
Wrapped entirely in silk,
Holding a white silk kerchief,
Turning without stepping firmly,
Composing herself like a noblewoman,
Showing a flank like an argali,
Displaying her white breast,
When your mother came before her,
Aychürök, daughter of Akun,
Turned obliquely like a parrot
And now began speaking
To the pampered, ill-tempered wife.
Speaking softly but striking hard,
Each of her words bit deeply:
“Elder sister, listen to my circumstances!
I fell in love with your husband and suffer;
I plead with you, calling you elder sister.
This dear hair grown down my back—
Setting aside my pride and wandering,
I have come before you, poor soul.
I shall make you a gift of livestock;
Take my red-tailed elephant,
My precious elder sister, heed my words.
Bowing before your face,
I bent my knee and lowered my head.
If you are human, you will understand
And consent to these words of mine.
My words are gold; understand them,
My caravan, my sister, heed my words!
The people are a treasury, a great river;
Though poured out by the bucket, they do not end.
Your gray wolf is a deep-rooted treasure.
Red silk and fine fabric,
Precious garments and mail—
What if we both wore them, sister?
What if we became elder and younger sisters
And married the same man, sister?
What if we joined in celebration and kept a home
And lived together, sister?
If he lies with you for six days,
Let him lie with me on the remaining day;
Let him lie with you seven days,
And with me on the day that does not fit.
I shall hear your stern words;
I shall enter with your fire
And leave with your ashes.
I shall bow my neck to you
And perform your service.
I have spoken wise words; understand them;
Do not make me wail—heed me, sister!
If you heed my words, elder sister,
I am the fortune granted to you;
If you do not heed them, elder sister,
I am the wrinkle upon your brow!
I am the blessed night that met you;
If my conduct pleases you, sister,
I shall be one of your servants.
Searching the whole world,
I have come here deliberately
After roaming among all its peoples.
I have come to braid
The side-locks beside my cheeks, sister;
I have come to serve you
And surrender myself before you, sister!
Understand these words of mine;
