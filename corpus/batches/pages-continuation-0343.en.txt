Thus he cast his spell,
Blocking the road
Of the surging infidels,
Turning aside the arrow
Of the overwhelming, fatal calamity,
And barring the very heart
Of the fiercely raging disaster.
Looking through the mingled dust,
Longing for his people to be safe,
His fly-like life cried out;
Every bone cracked.
His whole body was covered in blood;
The two hands that had served him
Would not rise and were paralyzed.
His precious head, dear in life,
Was a red bucket of blood.
The broad world narrowed;
Even saliva, life's sustenance,
Was too hard for him to swallow.
His unbending back bent,
And he despaired of his precious life.
The cold lasso, death's bullet,
Was loaded and poised above his head.
His fly-like soul cried out;
All his flesh and bones
Cracked and crumbled to powder.
Ebegey fought on,
For many days and many nights,
Standing there in fury.
In Yengkor's sight,
Ebegey's ribs
Appeared faintly through the haze.
An ember as large as a yurt glowed at the match;
There was no other infidel like him in the world.
He brought the gold-trimmed sight
Up to his glass-like eye,
Then pulled and fired.
The gun boomed and cracked;
“Lie there, wretched Burut!” he said.
From the edge of the ribs,
From the very front of the lung,
Blood gushed and gushed.
His fine mustache drooped,
And his ruddy face grew pale;
His breast filled with blood.
Ebegey, father of the people,
Was sacrificed for his nation.
Blood-handed Yengkor declared,
“I have shattered the mountain ridge
Of the steadfast Burut,
And taken control of the road;
I have brought down a peak like Kap-Too
And cast the world into black night.”
He went to bear glad tidings
To crowned Chyntengir,
Great khan of the Kakan.
Now he saw that hero Yengkor
Was truly a man,
And recognized his marksmanship.
Without openly rejoicing, he smiled,
His mustache barely twitching,
And laughed almost imperceptibly:
“He was a khan named Ebegey;
You have taken blood for blood.
He was a match for our Er Odu.
You have broken the enemy's back
And set Chyntengir's heart at rest!
I have heard news from those who came:
They say Er Kenen on Kertulpar
Still stands fighting.
Strong men in coats,
Shaven warriors girded with stones,
Thick-armed heroes,
Men deft and skilled with the spear,
Fully armed Kaldays,
Strong men swift in wrestling—
They say these fell in turmoil.
They say he destroys without pause,
And keeps inflicting hell.
They say he crushes the strong
And beats the rage
Out of courageous Kaldays.
May fire burst from your bones!
An enemy has risen from among you:
Jeljetpes has gone over to the Kyrgyz
And become a rebel.
He has brought a day of dire disaster
Upon the people of China's forty khans,
And reached their very foundations.
Thus that black demon
Has become an enemy.
That accursed Jeljetpes
Has ruined my undertaking.
He has broken my back,
And openly destroyed
Er Odukan and Choyun Alp,
Heroes such as these.”
