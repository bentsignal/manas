Your people flee in terror,
Your voice is heard far and wide;
When I charge in fury,
Horses tumble over one another.
They have no strength to attack,
No power to meet me in combat;
When I shout, none among them has courage.
There is no one to rouse the people and stir the nation—
No Sary Khan or Bakay Khan.
These people will not even move;
They are as countless as slaughtered dogs,
Yet have no mettle to attack,
No strength to wrestle,
No might to grapple.
When they hear my roar,
They abandon the foe and amble away;
When I grow angry, these people
Take shelter in their homes.
When I roar in wrath,
Their hearts utterly fail,
And they begin to quake;
They readily forsake
Not only their livestock, but their lives.
Listen, my friend Kyyaz,
I have a grievance to tell my lord.
Yesterday, on the plains of Zhylgyn and Keng-Kol,
Along broad Talas,
At Kara-Buura and Batkal,
When the Kyrgyz lay spread across the land,
Camping on the hills and settling the country,
When Argyn and Kyrgyz tethered their mares,
When they had made camp,
And there was merriment by day and games by night,
In those days,
When the bored went hunting deer,
And those who were not bored
Sported freely among the people,
Growing merry on kumis,
Wiping sweat from their brows,
After drinking their liquor,
We could find no mountain to assail,
No enemy to fight;
In Talas we stood raging,
Eager for any foe who ventured near,
Breaking through rock to make roads,
Laying waste to many lands,
While we held them besieged—
Had word of this reached you?—
We had girded our waists firmly.
From the Chinese khan of Beijing
We pursued the blood-price of mighty Manas.
From the banks of Kara-Suu,
From the plain of Kaspan-Too,
From the edge of Keng-Dyngsha,
From the face of Keng-Norko,
From the plain of snowy Ala-Too,
From the banks of Karkyra,
From the plain of Ükürchü,
From the fork of Üch-Kapkak,
From beneath Aygyr-Zhal,
Where he pastured at Chok-Tabylgy
And tethered his great black horse,
The infidel son of Kalday,
The youngest of sixty nobles,
That infidel son of Kalday,
The warrior Khan Kongurbay—
Spurring on Telküröng,
I reached the Manchu khan;
Ezkara, khan of the Manchus,
And Kongurbay, khan of the shaven-headed,
I buried them and rode on.
I cut off his head
And laid it before the foolish hero
As an offering.
I raised a mighty commotion.

With dimpled mouth and camel-calf eyes,
