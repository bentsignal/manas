Without treading the road that was laid out,
He would leave them a land of pursuit,
Without looking to the one who drove them,
He would set the people fighting behind him,
He set fighter against fighter in combat,
The age was thrown into disorder and strife,
To the Khakan with a horse-length host,
Keep laying siege, he said,
I shall encircle them all, one by one,
A soul lamenting over what had happened,
Wherever authority had arisen,
A mottled khan arose from the people,
He showed the enemy in the distance,
A man came forth bearing news.
Manas, lion of the people, had arisen,
Do you call only that one Manas?
A hero called Semetey arose,
Azhybai and Seytek arose as his equals,
However many fenced clans split apart,
All of them struck out straight from China,
We issued the command: go there,
What hero Seytek had seen,
Khan Zholtoi had gone at their head,
The great son-in-law of rich Kongur,
A fortunate son-in-law in all he did,
What a mighty son-in-law he was,
From the tidings of the dark warrior,
A wakeful soul was here,
The hero recognized the burning words,
This was Beijing’s backbone,
This lion was its own true son,
This was his surpassing place and station,
No word could touch him even for a month,
Black Zholtoi was undone,
This was the one who had seen so many wonders.
Looking at him, he closed his eyes,
Indeed, this is the enemy himself,
On earth he encountered a spirit,
To the Burut whom the black earth swallowed,
I have gone—yet the soul endures,
To this accursed people,
Having asked, it became a robe for him,
He did not cease from asking,
When he took the lance, it became an arrow,
If he drove them in the darkness,
There was an enemy like one who had fallen upon them,
Yours became eighty-four,
What he struck against became a seal,
What he made march was swift,
What he observed became renowned,
Of the accursed Burut,
Your heart that is bent on destruction,
He himself goes upon your tongue,
Always bearing the enemy’s rancor,
The broad-valleyed Ala-Too,
It is no land to sweep across like an open waste,
The white-kalpak Kyrgyz people,
In the end, against our Chinese realm,
They are no people whose settling goes unspoken,
Three young camels stand ready at the grave,
A grieving land of the Bugu,
Bearing enmity toward the Chinese,
Those two were valiant men,
Will they lead the standard into battle?
Saying, “I will become Kenen’s companion,”
