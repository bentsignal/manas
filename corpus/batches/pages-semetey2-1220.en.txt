Damn you, Jediger,
Strike tirelessly and take revenge!
This year your turn has come
To gouge out eyes black as currants,
To gorge yourselves upon rich broth
And the fat flesh of royal feasts;
This year your turn has come
To slaughter the gray wolf, the bloodthirsty man’s son!
Why do you not strike down
The bloodthirsty man looming there
And lay him dead upon his back, Jediger?
Why do you not take the vast wealth
Left by his father Manas,
Divide it as spoils, and carry it away?
Do you not see, Jediger,
How boundless his riches are?
Why do you not divide, Jediger,
His countless treasures and myriad goods?
Why do you not behold, Jediger,
Wonders no man has ever seen?
Why do you not launch, Jediger,
A mighty campaign and a great war?
And has not moon-faced Aichürök
Been left proud and unprotected?
Why do you not take her too,
And claim her as your prize, Jediger?
Why do you not finish the bloodthirsty man
And depart, Jediger?
On the hills are piebald herds,
With thoroughbreds and swift coursers mingled;
Why do you not take your share
Of the finest steeds and leave, Jediger?
You stirred and fled, Jediger,
Though your numbers swelled into a nation,
Though you surged like a mighty people!
You have already crossed
Yonder Kara-Buura and Besh-Tash;
You miserable people scattered in fear—
You have fled away!
From his magnanimous father Manas
He inherited and still holds
The red-tailed gun called Bili.
You vast host of Jediger,
Stop snarling and heed my words;
I have spoken precious counsel—understand it!
Seize his herds from the mountain slope;
Ah, slaughter-stricken Jediger,
Sooner or later
You will flee snarling again—
Take your vengeance while you can!
Understand what I have told you;
Ah, Jediger, heed my words!
Here stands Kanchoro, sharp-eyed as a khan,
Who will devise the way to victory.
You fleeing Jediger,
When Külchoro pursued you in fury,
What did you have to say then?
You found the chance to level your spears
And thrust them with proper aim,
Yet by fleeing the warrior, wretched people,
You have broken my back!
You vast host of Jediger,
If you ignore my ceaseless words,
Then sink into the earth, unworthy to be a people!
You have left me helpless.
From along the Seven Valleys
You came surging in great numbers;
You came saying you would overwhelm
The heroic son of Manas.
You vast host of Jediger,
You came with champions and giants aplenty;
Ah, you babbling paupers,
Why did you come, not knowing you would flee?”
He descended upon the hill, roaring;
Kanchoro bellowed
Until the apricot treetops bent:
“You beastly Jediger,
The man you came to fight is Semetey—
