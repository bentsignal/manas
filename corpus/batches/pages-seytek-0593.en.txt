When he came over the rise
And saw Sarybai’s form—
If you looked upon Sarybai,
He was like a lion clawing the earth;
At the infidel’s very silhouette,
Every mortal human being
Fled far away, daring not approach.
His brow and head sagged heavily;
The wrathful infidel’s brow
Stretched like a vast wasteland,
As though his gaze would swallow a soul.
He carried a whip like a pine tree,
His bloodless face ashen gray;
He drove back all who fought him,
And if a living soul fell into his hands,
The madman sank his five claws
Deep into their ribs.
He grappled with the soul-taking angel,
And for seven years without cease the brazen-beaked one
Had wrestled with death itself.
Women and men alike, the dog,
He had bound even the great vulture;
He raided nearby and seized booty,
The bloodthirsty, accursed wretch,
And captured strongholds from fortified places.
Every living creature
He attacked without sparing one.
Hardened by drinking his fill of blood,
The earth-greasing incendiary
Was famed as the monster’s son.
Wherever he went, dust arose,
And all he scattered was red blood;
Whoever fell firmly into his hands
Was smashed to pieces and ground like grain.
He roamed devouring all who fought him,
Believing himself the only one
Ever born upon the earth.
The madman brooded,
Longing to swallow the whole universe;
His eyes were merciless, his nature mean.
He raised his steel spear,
Rejoicing at the thought of an enemy,
And donned armor of blue steel.
He roused himself like a blue lion,
While companions from the unseen
Came to the precious lion.
The reason allies appeared
Was that his fortune was full;
He was a lion unlike any in the world.
Among all mortal sons ever born,
None like him would soon arise.
When roused, his strength overflowed;
His fathers and forefathers
Were heroes who conquered the world.
Was he not the bloodthirsty warrior
Who settled along the river and ruled the people?
Was he not the bloodthirsty warrior
Who subdued and drained the whole world?
For the white-capped Kyrgyz
He was an iron enclosure, a fortress of steel—
Such was the bloodthirsty warrior.
Against the infidel whose shadow loomed like a mountain,
Against brave Sarybai,
Who fixed his hatred on the people and slaughtered them,
He rode out alone,
To slay his foe if he could,
To wage a furious battle,
And deliver the multitude
From disaster.
Across peaks where mountains vanished,
Across bare projecting crags,
He made the whole universe tremble
As he thundered onward.
The land grew hazy with dust;
Külchoro, upheld by providence,
Was brave lion Seytek’s
Second in combat.
“There is no resisting fate,” he said,
And came to stand as his support,
Saying, “Your descendants remain behind you.”
