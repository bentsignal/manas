Aktelki has forded many flowing streams;
The poor beast's hooves are worn blunt.
If he fords a fiercely flowing river,
May this gray wolf of yours not die.
Let the stain of being called a slave be mended;
May the enemy before my face
Flee without meeting me and vanish.
Ah, my gray-wolf elder,
May my misfortune flee and be made right!
If you give me your fine-cloth robe, I shall wear it;
Give me Buurul and let me ride him.
I shall learn who belongs to the caravan
Where thirty daughters-in-law and thirty maidens
Are playing.
Radiant like the younger son of Manas,
Let me shine
And walk among them as a man."
When he asked for Buurul,
The tiger did not grow sorrowful.
Unable to say, "I will not give him,"
He did not turn back his hero's heart;
Unable to say, "Stop, young man,"
He did not reject the first desire
Of the poor man born alone.
The tiger born to surpass
His father, gray-wolf Manas, said:
"If you would wear it, put it on,"
And took off and gave him the fine-cloth robe.
"If you would ride him, mount him,"
And dismounted and gave him Buurul.
Once the words "I give him" left his mouth,
Would your heroic companion stand still?
He sprang up and ran,
And seized the bridle
Of Buurul, standing in the line.
Tall, lean Buurul had come into condition;
The poor beast stood there restlessly.
His elder had traveled this way,
And sweated out the horse's bitter sweat.
He had removed the saddle and left him bare,
And placed him in the yellow breeze;
Through this the sleek horse
Had returned to fighting condition.
The bow-shaped wretch's
Mane and tail had grown thin;
He bristled as he came into condition,
And assumed an altered form.
Gazing ceaselessly at the sky,
Every joint and bone
Drew smilingly into his frame.
He raced with flying birds
And wrestled whoever held him.
The belly flesh from which kazy is taken
Had remained drawn tightly inward,
Clinging against his ribs.
Snorting and darting about,
The sleek horse played,
And with his muzzle tossed the young man
Like a skullcap through the air.
Semetey, the hero none could stir,
The hero born to surpass his father, said:
"Hey, hey, what is the swine doing?
His vigor has returned to his flesh;
This is the hour his ardor has risen."
Your young lord strode forward
And approached the courser.
Standing tall, he caught him himself;
He held the courser firmly,
Not allowing him to move.
Kulchoro came and saddled the horse;
With remarkable quickness
He saddled him in a flash.
A leather saddlecloth and blue pad
He laid straight upon his back;
The Akkangky made by Almambet,
A saddle fitted for Semetey,
Its front bow of white gold,
And its rear bow entirely of silver,
