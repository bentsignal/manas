I shall take the road to Chardak,
Test how much strength he has,
Learn how great his power is,
And enter into his vast wealth.
I shall rain shots upon him.
My sorrowful uncle Bakai,
My grandsire left by my father,
My khan whose back is like a mountain,
My elder, like a prophet,
He who wears a blue lynx cap,
The handsomest among all men,
My sacred old man,
My khan and uncle Bakai—
If I do not cross the hills to seek him
And set him free;
If I do not rescue old Bakai, my heirloom,
Whole and unharmed;
If I do not go to Khan Kanchoro
And unleash cold havoc upon the world
For what he will not yield when asked;
If he will not surrender the possessions through counsel,
If I do not seize them without another word!”
So the hero Seytek spoke,
Preparing himself in fury,
Raging like a lion.
His heart surged with many feelings,
And he looked like a lofty blue crag
As he stood before them.
Külchoro, sustained by the patron spirit,
Spoke slowly and firmly,
Beginning his counsel to brave Seytek:
“Ah, you are my beloved, my mainstay,
My lion left by the bloodthirsty hero.
You are the banner fixed upon my spear,
The support of this entrusted life,
A cherished gift cradled in song.
You are the joy before my eyes,
My child Seytek, my good fortune.
When I behold you, my belly is full;
Without you, my cherished one, I have no life.
I shall strike the swaggering, wrathful enemy
Across the nape of his neck;
Even if he has sixty lives,
I shall slaughter him and leave none!
I shall charge in and test
His courage and valor;
I shall divide all his wealth.
With that fiercely wrathful swine
I shall grapple without cease.
If Kanchoro, whom I fight,
Is a warrior and a hero,
A lion who has shaken the world,
Then, if fate decrees, let me die
And let my youthful head behold
The true realm from which none return.
How could I surrender my honor
To that strutting false kinsman?
Boastful Kanchoro,
If your death comes suddenly,
If disaster strikes by chance,
May my perfect mother Aichürök,
Saying, ‘My son died in battle,’
Forgive me through her grief.
For the milk of her white breast,
May my lion mother forgive me.
How could I endure alive
The humiliation he has inflicted?
If I do not repay shame with shame
And relentlessly take my vengeance,
May I sink away as though never born.
He boasts of valor and lionhood,
That consummate, depraved lecher,
Posing as righteous and heroic.
If I do not somehow settle accounts
With cunning Kanchoro,
Going tirelessly upon the road;
If I do not beat the circling madness
Out of that white-deviled swine,
May I die without human honor,
May I sink away as though never born.
