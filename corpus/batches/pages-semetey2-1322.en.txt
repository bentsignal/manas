Her strength left Kanykei;
She lost consciousness,
Her face turned ashen,
And unable to stand, she fell,
Lying there insensible.
When Kanykei's voice was heard,
The birch grieved and the willow wept,
And all the gathered people wept.
Every nose steamed with breath,
The heroes raised a great clamor,
All the people were in uproar;
The earth shook and stones wept,
The chiefs of all the Kyrgyz wept,
The deep earth beneath them wept,
And the warriors, led by Akjoltoi, wept.
Have you heard the tale from long ago
Of the creature found by providence
In the desert of Media
And brought to Khan Manas—
Kumaiyk, born of royal stock?
Opening his mouth, he howled,
And bayed in mourning;
Parted from Semetey,
The white sighthound met disaster.
Casting off his collar,
The white hound vanished.
Black-eyed, with blue-gray legs,
He tucked his wings and never stooped;
His talons were iron, his jesses brass,
He let no goose rise from a lake.
His wings spanned two fathoms,
And nothing he struck escaped alive;
He ate flesh upon his master's hand.
His steel pinions flashed pale;
When he coursed at game,
He fulfilled every true desire.
With silken leash and golden anklet,
The peerless white falcon Akshumkar
Beat his wings at any living prey;
When his gray-maned master flew him,
He carried nine bustards at a time.
A saker falcon thirsted for his head,
And brought the bird to Semetey;
He was raised in the royal mews.
“This must be a portent,”
Khan Bakai judged and approved him.
He had gold set on his collar;
When urged, he danced upon the hand,
And when this eagle soared,
He left no creature alive.
When he drove straight at prey,
His eyes saw it from afar.
He wheeled upon anything flying true,
Never settling straight, but sweeping wide;
The one who flew him rejoiced.
When the falcon drove head-on,
No living thing remained unfallen.
Such was the white falcon you would see—
A bird to cast loose,
Semetey's own treasured possession.
Mourning, the white falcon
Twisted and cried harshly,
Clawed and tore at his own legs,
And struck against his hood.
When a hand was raised, he refused food;
Deep in mourning,
He tore the feathers from his breast—
So the white falcon mangled himself.
Then he vanished across the steppe;
The white falcon fled away.
No eye could catch sight of him,
And no one knew where he had gone.
Vanishing, the white falcon
Shredded his golden leash;
Thus the noble creature tore it apart.
Seen by no person,
Known by no living soul,
The white falcon departed.
