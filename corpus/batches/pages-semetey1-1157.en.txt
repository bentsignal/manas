He seized Koshoy of the Katagan
And led him out to the champion:
If he won, he would take honor;
If he fell, they would stand watching.
As the tale of the honorable people tells,
Your uncle went out to the champion.
"Go out yourself, Manas," said Koshoy,
Pleading as he stood.
These were Manas's words:
"When my father Zhakyp pursued,
When my mother Chyiyrdy gave birth,
I grew to maturity and became a man, Koshoy,
And became equal to a hero's son.
Since Manas became Manas,
Since fortune settled upon my head,
Since I mounted a horse astride
And donned the coat Akolpok,
Many gathered together;
I had no ax for a man on foot.
Once mounted on a horse,
I was more skillful than an intriguer,
Mightier than a brave man.
Send me against a claimant in debate;
Send me against a spear-bearing enemy.
Send me against a foe who closes knee to knee;
Send me against an enemy who draws rein to rein.
If I do not level my spear and thrust,
Then cast my eyes
To the flank where whips are traded;
Cast me into the black-burning fire."
Then Koshoy heeded his words
And stepped out to the champion.
Speaking many words in a ringing voice,
Your uncle entered into grief:
"Eighty-five is an age,
The humiliation of a living man.
Rather than live on like a mountain,
It is better for a man to lie in a mound.
Had I met the Kalmyk at sixty,
Would I not have taken him?
Had I met him at seventy,
Would I not have thrown him?
When I speak, I have sorrow and grief;
Magnanimous Manas, hear me.
I have trousers made
From the hides of forty ibex.
When jostling in combat
With a dog who boasts of strength,
If that enraged pig seizes them,
My trousers will split,
And your uncle's shame will show openly.
His splendor will spill like ashes.
In your uncle's old age,
His face will change from shame;
Because of the sprawling black Kalmyk,
Your uncle will fall."
