Hero Toltoy of the Jediger,
When the spear struck him,
Cried, "Ah, alas, my back!"
His eyes darted,
His legs dangled loose.
Surkoen ran uncontrollably;
Toltoy of the Jediger
Despaired of his entrusted life.
His senses failed and he reeled,
Despairing of his poor life.
"I have seen the place of Judgment Day;
My accursed wretched soul is dead.
I was a mighty man, poor soul,
And guarded myself for so long.
I have been caught by chance
By this mighty-born stripling.
He is a boy of twelve—
Must I suffer at his hands?
My devices fail me here;
Will I die before my destined death?"
Hero Toltoy, wracked with pain,
His temples tightened,
And he quarreled with Surkoen:
"Damn you, Surkoen!
You were a swift stallion,
In the prime of your flesh,
At the height of your speed.
Did the enemy slaughter and plunder you?
Damn you, may your road be blessed—
Has Satan hobbled you?"
Fortunate hero Toltoy
Spoke of his calamity.
At twelve, Kulchoro,
The stripling who crossed his road,
Had devoured hero Toltoy.
His backbone throbbed with pain,
And the fly-like soul
Of the lord who came for a wife shrieked.
The pain became severe;
His spine ached and tightened.
Hero Toltoy rode raging,
Quarreling with Chynkojo:
"I was returning thoughtfully to the camp,
But this exile lay in wait for me;
This fugitive has reached me,
Driving me to my end.
Never heeding words,
The accursed fugitive slave
Has dug my grave,
Saying, 'Seize his wife,'
And confronting me on a dark day,
Has performed a fine deed."
Toltoy despaired of life.
Arbuudan flew like an arrow
And ran past Toltoy.
When gray-maned Toltoy saw it,
He plainly despaired of life.
"This boy is a fool;
If he recovers, he will not act rightly.
If he turns back recovered,
He will not leave my poor life whole.
I will cut off with my sword
The head of the boy who comes before me.
Before the accursed boy
Can glance back and turn,
I will make a spectacle of him!"
Hero Toltoy of the Jediger
Was one of the accomplished heroes.
Before the boy could turn back,
He drew from its sheath
His sword that cut through honor.
When his opportunity came,
Dear spirited hero Toltoy
Struck off his head with the sword.
Before the boy could wheel around,
When he looked at Kulchoro,
The wretched young lord—
At one glance he was one man,
