Lofty and hard as blood-dark iron—
By God, such a gray-maned hero
No woman will ever bear again.
He would never let the Khagan’s realm destroy us;
He was a dark, gray-maned hero,
A warrior who guarded
Many mighty cities and peoples.
Yet however much we drank from his bounty, we were never sated,
And never abandoned our greed;
Unable ourselves to strike down a wolf,
We could not bear to see a son who did—
Ah, we sly people have not prospered!
From time immemorial until now,
We have never ceased this mischief.
This Sultan Semetey
Was the mountain we relied upon,
The land where our backs found support.
He was a gray stallion that let no horse escape,
And his fortune was perfect.
Your generous-hearted grim hero
Was broader than the face of the earth;
Born in the likeness of a champion,
He would never yield his equal to mortals.
He was a hero who fastened
A stout tassel to his spear.
He guarded the few Arghyn and Kyrgyz,
Letting not even a stirring breeze touch them,
Nor the least chill reach them.
Such was the hero who protected them.
He was a lion and a khan;
When storms came, no wind touched us.
He was the dark, gray-maned hero
Who yielded no livestock to the wrathful.
He was a true brother in valor
To Sultan Manas himself.
To the right were the Russians,
On the frontier were the Chinese,
And ahead stood muskets;
Many were the enemies
Who contended with us.
He guarded us, letting no man with thumbs subdue us,
No creature with claws scratch us,
No winged thing strike us,
And no wrathful foe attack us.
Such was the hero who tended us;
Born in the likeness of a champion,
No equal to him would arise among mortals.
While this grim hero was with us,
Wearing sable hats from the caravan,
We would turn our racehorses’ heads;
By day there was kumiss, by night girls,
And we reveled with thunderous laughter.
We played knucklebones and raised dust,
And when excited, competed at ordo,
Throwing ourselves heartily into the games.
We lived and played at ease;
A whole beaver pelt
We would cut in half
And trim our trouser cuffs with it.
Strutting about as heroes,
Swaggering as though we were gods,
We ate frozen goat fat,
And in our excess devoured barren fillies;
We crunched sugar like snow,
Sipped strong black tea,
And drank distilled liquor.
We camped all along the valley,
With no one to command us,
And lived entirely as we pleased.
We tethered milk-fed camel calves,
And elegantly boiled
Fat lambs and choice heads.
Wearing costly hats of blue lynx,
With swaying ornaments beyond compare,
We spent the days in laughter
And passed the nights in revelry.
