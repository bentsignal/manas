“All my flesh and bones
Are about to burst apart.
Your hands bring healing to all they touch;
You seven have come as holy women.
Lay hands upon the place that hurts,
Mothers who master every remedy,
Open my eyes, swollen like lumps of dough!”
Thus Aichürök spoke
And closed her mouth.
From the kumis mixed with poison,
Seven bowls of kumis
The deft and capable Karachach
Poured for the old women.
Immeasurably nimble,
The quick-moving poor girl
Steeled herself like a hawk,
Fawned upon the false old women,
Measured poison by the ladle,
And gave them water by the spoon.
She served all seven alike,
Quickly, betraying no suspicion.
At dusk, as the sun went down
And all the people lay sound asleep,
She made every arrow ready in the quiver
And sent the seven ill-fated old women
Out of this world.
Beside the hearth
She dug a pit,
And the accomplished Aichürök
Managed to slaughter all seven.
Folding them double at the waist,
At the edge of the hearth
She laid them in a hidden grave.
Without a single person knowing,
Without anyone seeing,
The seven witches died;
The seven troublemakers went
To the true abode from which none return.
Their seven lives were stilled,
And the world was rid of all seven together.
When roaring leopard Manas
Raided Künjüt long ago,
This poison had brought merciless devastation;
It was an evil that slaughtered men,
A poison called “Soundless Death.”
Whoever smelled it died;
Whoever tasted it saw
The true abode from which none return.
Its odor carried as far as men,
And when anyone breathed it in,
His gall would burst and he would die.
Whoever came within its scent perished;
Any soul who came with evil intent
Received his rightful punishment.
Aichürök, truly laboring in birth,
Had poison ready,
And meant to raise a terrible uproar
Among the seven witching crones.
She ordered her maid:
“Rise, Karachach, rise!
These mothers have come thirsty;
From the fresh golden kumis
Pour some for your mothers.
Let their thirst be quenched,
Let their swollen eyes open.
Hunger brings on illness,
But a full belly restores good sense.
These mothers, healers of suffering,
Bestow great blessings;
From death and agony
They wrench us free.
Their hands grasp the wrist and know their craft;
They have two clear-seeing eyes,
And speak true words when asked.
God creates the soul—
You seven, find some remedy,
For my soul is leaving me!
My eyes stare dimly,
My bones hang limp,
And my fly-sized soul shrieks in pain.”
