Disregard this and shame will follow.
This walnut I have brought
I give to Akbermet and Seytek
As a gift held in sacred trust.
This Kertulpar standing here
Was born by supernatural power and has journeyed here;
It is destined for him to mount in war.
For the fierce hero Kenen
This was written upon his brow.
A three-year-old this year, a two-year-old last,
Every mark of a tulpar is complete.
Born by magic and raised in the desert,
Raised upon a dark and rugged ridge,
Raised in a place where neither human, beast,
Nor any living creature ever looked,
It raced the wild asses stride for stride.
With the steeds of bygone mighty Kalmyks—
Kongurbai’s Algara,
Orok Kyr’s Kylkara,
Börüköz’s Kertelki,
And Jaidaksur ridden by the commander—
The warhorses of the giant warriors,
It was born of the same stock
And raced beside the wild asses.
It drank from the Kaimana,
And grazed upon sacred herbs.
The copper hag among horse judges
Who tended it on Mount Barbar
Declared, “This is a true tulpar.”
“This mad creature is like a whirlwind;
Might it take flight?” she said,
And that peerless judge approved it.
Its hoof does not sink into water;
My far-seeing ancestor Körögöch
And my wise father praised it.
When it gathers itself, its stride stretches wide;
By God’s decree and fortune’s aid,
It has been bestowed upon hero Kenen.
Kertulpar will grow into a mighty steed;
Whoever asks to take it will be a stranger.
Broad-lunged and copper-sinewed,
With a wild ass’s haunches and lean feet,
This is the very image of a perfect tulpar.
Roman-nosed and high-crested,
It will not flinch when mounted for battle;
Broad-chested and low-maned,
It will not falter before an open foe.
If, after hardship, its rider cries, “Go!”
No living soul can flee and escape it.
Fail to tell this plainly, and shame will follow;
For Kenen, who is yet to be born,
You must give it, Joltay, as a sacred trust!
The man who had spoken thus
Now vanished from sight.
Leading the entrusted Kertulpar
Unsaddled beside him, he pressed onward,
Praying to the Creator.
That supremely wise sage,
Who had seen distant lands from afar,
Your venerable forefather Joltay
Along a separate, low-lying road
Now turned back—behold him.
Joltay journeyed on and came;
The sheep rustled in their fold,
Light spilled over the earth,
And the world appeared radiant.
Dawn broke as though the earth had split,
Sunlight touched mountain after mountain,
The nightingale sang in the forest,
And the drifting haze dispersed.
Marked waters, stones, and everything else
Stood as plain as five spread fingers.
Just as the world appeared so,
To the palace with its stout gate,
To the fortress with its portals,
Valiant Joltay came.
Without uttering a single word,
He hastened in at a run;
From atop his mountain-like gray horse,
Joltay rushed onward without pause,
