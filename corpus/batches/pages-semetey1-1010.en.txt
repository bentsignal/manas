The free body of your lordly companion
Shuddered and trembled through its length.
The mud rolled like a gray turban;
Heroic Kulchoro
Drew back, invoking God.
When he looked at Great Urgench,
It was filled with spectacle.
It surged and thundered in billows;
Its middle ran violently fast,
All its waters flowing together without circling,
Flowing far greater than a sea.
Its center rushed beyond all measure,
And avalanche snow flowed at its edges.
The sight opened the heart;
It appeared like a lake and river together.
If you would see a wonder, see it here:
Young firs and pines
Were torn out by their roots,
Struck against the bank,
And stripped the cliffside bare.
Watching it, Kulchoro
Felt his whole age shrivel.
Every gully and slope was flushed red;
Crosscurrents strong enough to sweep away horses
Spurted from every place,
Joining in a thousand channels.
Pines and rafts swept along,
And birches and willows floated;
They gathered into a flood
And struck against every bank.
The companion watching it cried,
"May God preserve me!"
Far from being able to cross it then,
His whole age shriveled.
Foam fluttered upon its surface;
House-sized stones from its bed
All rolled onward with a clatter.
It gathered force like the cuckoo's call;
Great Urgench rushed and roared,
Taking on countless different forms.
The earth split and cliffs collapsed,
And all the water turned to mud.
Kulchoro, watching,
Clutched his collar and invoked God.
A gray roll of mud as large as a house
Fell in and churned away;
Kulchoro watched closely,
And his whole age shriveled.
If he cast a bridle into the rebounding foam
That flowed along its edge, it could not touch bottom;
Even a swift fox could not range across it.
Kulchoro did not think,
"I shall reach the farther bank."
If he cast a rope into the foam
Kicked upward by the torrent, it could not touch bottom;
Even a fox could not range its high banks.
From the force of Urgench
A roaring windstorm blew.
Birches and willows, roots and all,
Swept past in a thundering mass.
A person saying, "I shall cross"—
It would reach up to his kidneys
If this flood struck him,
It would tear him limb from limb.
All the glaciers had broken apart
And poured down in an altered flood.
Spreading wide, flowing, and spilling,
When one looked at Great Urgench,
To the companion standing there
It appeared like a devouring beast.
Urgench had entered flood like this
Because your discerning sister-in-law Churok,
That troublesome poor woman,
Knew that her lord would come.
"If that gray wolf is a hero,
Let him ride forward and cross the water;
If he is a coward, let him falter
And flee back the way he came,"
