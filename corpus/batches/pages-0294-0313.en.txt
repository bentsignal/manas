He simply seizes and gouges, they say.
Before the dragon,
He seizes and casts them, they say.
If his fury truly takes him,
Samarkand and Kokand,
He smashes them to fragments, they say.
That flame-cursed boar,
If his fury takes him again,
A mortal who goes before him
Does not return living without dying, they say.
The mortal he takes in wrath
Does not come back safe, they say.
Flame-cursed Alöökö,
From his deeds I hear he is a mighty king;
All those who approach him,
He slaughters and butchers, they say.
Such are the wonders we hear!
He has set up his marvelous net;
I hear his imagination surpasses others'.
Namurut, who passed away,
They say his aspect surpasses even him.
That swine has taught them speech,
His rhinoceros and his elephant.
We lack the strength to contend with him.
Come, migrate here, my bloodthirsty one;
Heed my words now!
Wonder-workers and sorcerers,
He has knowing men beside him, they say.
When he grows angry, he slaughters, they say.
The Muslims, small as a palm,
He utterly ravages, they say.
Dust rises from his army;
That swine whose house should burn,
Souls flee from his wrath.
Unable to endure his tribute,
Many people panicked, they say.
Of Alty-Shaar and Kokand,
All the people fled, they say.
He plundered their goods, they say.
His great throne with its treasury,
He established it in Margalang, they say.
That swine, living with a white yurt,
Became a king, they say.
A gateway with steel doors,
He became a mighty king.
Upon the mortals who fell into his hands,
He wrought many dreadful wonders, they say.
Unable to withstand his wrath,
How many mortals panicked, they say.
Levying tribute, this unbeliever,
His spirit-power weighed upon the people, they say.
Those who fell into his hands,
He scattered them crying in distress, they say.
If they could not find and give his tribute,
He did whatever he wished, they say.
The mortals who went before him,
Without mercy, that swine,
He slaughtered many of them, they say.
For orphans, widows and the destitute,
He made hell as high as a mountain, they say.
A glowing coal sprang from the hearth;
The sheikhs with courts, the staunch retainers,
Mighty Kokand and Margalang,
Ruling over them,
A giant named Alöökö arose.
Against this warrior Alöökö,
This unbeliever full of might,
We could not match him in a clash;
No mortal could defeat him face to face.
He brought unrest, I heard!
‘Let him obey what I say;
Without exchanging blows, peacefully,
Let Koshoy pay tribute,’ he said,
And sent word to me as well.
That swine whose house should burn,
His envoy came and stood here.
His arm guards spreading broad,
His khan came with him.
Their jackets bulging,
His mighty men came with him.
‘The khan's order has come,’ they said,
‘Without answering back, my Burut,
Pay your tribute,’ they said.
Raising a white standard and banner,
Raising a shouting clamor,
Demanding skilled craftswomen among the women,
For his tribute Alöökö
Demanded only beautiful girls.
‘Quickly obey what is said,’ they demanded;
‘From your living livestock,
Quickly pay your dues,’ they demanded.
‘To load with gold and gold coins,
Give red-tailed nar camels,’ they demanded.
They stood pressing hard.
Saying, ‘I entrust myself to fate,’ girding my waist,
Without delay, your elder Koshoy
Fought with his envoy.
Kudaynazar the bare club,
I had bound him about my waist as support.
The hearing ear, the seeing eye,
Kutunay on his pale tulpar,
I had taken him as my mainstay.
Girding on great armor,
Against the champions who would take tribute,
We fired and attacked.
Mounting stallions from the tethering lines,
Taking yurt poles from the ground,
Crying, ‘Merciful Lord!’ as we fought,
We had a terrible battle.
There was no single one to contest on horseback,
There was no forehead left unsplit.
Striking with whips,
Hacking with battle-axes,
Thrusting with spears,
On level ground, in the open plain,
We wore one another down.
Without heeding his demands,
Without paying that tribute,
Swords shattered, flashing;
Battle-axes clattered upon heads.
I laid low their renowned ones;
Smashing those who had come,
I drove them toward Margalang.
I began many dreadful things;
His champions who came between us,
I slaughtered them without mercy.
Such disorder I had brought;
With this Khan Alöökö,
I had come to blows.
At the envoy who came between us,
I, your aged elder Koshoy,
Roared and thrust my spear.
With that dog of corrupt appetite,
I had exchanged battle cries.
At the fool who boasted of his strength,
I thundered and thrust my spear.
Those in jackets, those bearing stones,
I had put many of them to death.
