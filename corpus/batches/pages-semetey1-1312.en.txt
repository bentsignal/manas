Peerless courser Surkoyon,
When he came downhill,
Stretched his forelegs like a roe deer;
When he leaned into an ascent,
His mane and tail spread wide.
Flattening his ears like a kulan,
He swept the earth like a whirlwind.
That lion-like man,
Making the earth's surface thunder,
Urged Arbuu rul onward,
Putting grass on the gray trail,
And pursued him relentlessly.
Putting grass on the yellow trail,
He pursued until he turned pale.
Taibuurul beneath him
Arched his neck like a stag.
Without slowing, he leaped
Ravines a spear's length wide.
His neck bent like an argali's;
When a ravine a rope's length wide appeared,
He gathered himself and cleared it unfailingly.
When brave Toltoy's Surkoyon
Reached the seven ridges,
Semetey came from behind,
Faster than an arrow.
When Semetey caught him,
He seized the reins then
And took him away as spoil.
He pursued and caught him;
His anger flared greatly.
The lion-prince, that brave man,
Thinking of nothing else,
Put on the splendid Kyrgyz saddle.
Because of this, Surkoyon
Kept vengeance in his heart, saying,
“I will meet you in a place of hardship.”
Taking the narrow road as a shortcut,
Leading Arbuurul,
Their elder brother came escorting them.
When the two choros saw him,
The two choros begged for a gift,
Their knees bending toward the ground,
And both ran side by side.
Their stirrups struck together.
“Uncle whom I hold in a father's place,
My uncle like a father, give him to me,” they said.
“Placed before me, he would be good fortune;
Placed behind me, he would be like a vast army.
Uncle, give him to me as a gift,” they said.
Both ran side by side,
Bounding like roe deer,
And the two raced up.
He swung the knotted reins
Back toward Külchoro
And tossed them.
Before Kanchoro, Külchoro
Was the first to take the reins.
At this, Kanchoro
Thought, “Father, curse you,”
And resentment remained in his heart.
“He does not regard me as he does Külchoro,” he thought;
“Father, empty-headed butcher, curse you,”
He muttered without saying it openly,
Seething behind them.
I will find the waist you have girded,
Find the place where you lie entrenched,
And destroy what you had before;
I will unleash calamity upon you!
I will set fire to your home;
Brooding and waiting,
My butcher, one day I will reach your end!
I will break open your treasury;
Upon Buurul whom you deny when asked,
I will cast a cold lasso.
I will tighten your tether,
And tirelessly load your burdens.
I will abandon you and get away,
And rummage through one of your bags.
I will strike your caravan from Keng-Kol,
