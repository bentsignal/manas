With buttocks six palms wide,
Her name was Orongu.
Can a human be like this?
Grim in appearance and fierce in wrath,
Her lineage was from the Black Chinese—
Can a woman be like this?
This infidel felt no shame;
Would she spare any living soul?
While people gathered,
Swarming like black worms,
Orongu came out naked.
She prepared every kind of beast;
Following an ancient rite,
She had hair two fingers long
Tied behind her.
Orongu strode forward;
Hero Ürbü, son of Taz,
Mounted her like a stallion.
Neighing like a stallion,
Bleating like a he-goat,
Surging like a ram,
Straining like a bull camel,
Hero Ürbü, who made the people laugh,
Upon that woman Orongu
Became like a stallion.
Could any woman's boldness
Match Orongu's?
When Ürbü drew near,
Saying, “I shall untie the camel,”
Orongu hurried onward.
Gripping her firearm and bleating,
Hero Ürbü behind her
Neighed like a stallion.
Bending toward the camel,
Orongu began the rite.
Hero Ürbü shoved like a stallion
And flung her over.
Those who valued modesty
Fled out from among the people.
Watching in amazement
Stood lion Manas.
Orongu's strongman,
The black male camel in the pit—
Bending over, she untied it.
The woman Orongu would not prosper;
Before all those people,
Her face did not even pale.
Orongu untied the camel
And created another spectacle;
Among women, Orongu
Was a champion without shame.
She untied the camel;
The woman Orongu proved a prince
And remained there enriched.
After the camel-untying contest passed,
After this spectacle had ended,
Bokmurun of Kökötöy,
Scattering his wealth,
Making every people laugh,
Made his riches known
To the whole multitude,
Infidel and Muslim:
“Among the Muslims, Khan Koshoy,
Lion Sultan Khan Manas—
Your people stand here;
Your fierce and formidable
Giants stand here;
People of the Muslim faith,
All of you stand here.
I have counsel and words;
