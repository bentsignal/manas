These Kyrgyz are beyond the wind’s reach.
They have ruined your undertaking,
And struck your strength from your waist.
They have laid you in your grave.
Brave Orozan, lord of Chokon’s people,
They have undone the people you set in order.
There is no man left; they have grown numerous.
The enemy behind you has become few.
My strength that had girded itself,
My manly guardian, hold firm.
You will come into this world,
To the resounding shield,
You are a keen and deadly marksman.
You were a wrestler and a champion.
You alone were the one I trusted.
Even if the earth fell before you,
The wind could not overtake you in battle.
Come after binding the people’s hands.
Gouge out both their eyes,
Cut out their tongues without dragging them,
And slash them from the nape.
Exact the Kalmyk’s grief by force.
Kick him in the ribs,
Mount him upon the iron,
Drive him on in humiliation,
Shove him away and burn him,
And drive him here before me.
You were a brave man and a champion.
The broad son of the Burut,
The towering Sungai falcon,
The broad one from Kök-Chalkan,
Your champion who rode out to the contest—
Do not make the road of his horse a drove-road,
His neck was vast as Ala-Too.
