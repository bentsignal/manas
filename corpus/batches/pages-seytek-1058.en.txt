Could it be a land where one never mounted
A courser that flew like a cloud?
Could it be a land where brave men never roamed
And cast off their sorrows?”
The heart of whoever sees it is carried away;
This radiant Keng-Özön
Seems the equal of paradise.
One’s cares scatter away,
And at the sight of this radiant valley
The heart opens in joy.
At the valley’s lower end
Lies ancient Turpan beyond;
Behind it stands great Barbar,
And the River Op pours down overturned.
What use is admiring this land
If it is not destined for us?
Beautiful, wondrous Keng-Özön—
A valley blessed with perfect fortune.
Along that valley
Stand incomprehensibly vast plane trees.
When one looks upon those trees,
There are truly wondrous sights.
On one side of a plane tree,
Pale-grey falcons have bred;
On another side of the tree,
Soaring starlings have nested.
Among the branches of the plane tree,
Pied crows have nested,
And white-necked starlings have nested;
Every living creature has bred there.
“Could it be a land where one did not shoot
The lion among all the fanged beasts?
Could it be a land too vast
For every kind of marvel?”
So spoke Akjoltoy,
Whose majesty equaled a vast army.
Ninety beasts were slaughtered at his feast;
That he might find a road wherever he turned
And live as a hero,
He had been named Akjoltoy, “Blessed Way.”
He was tempered like keen steel;
Whenever he rode forth, a road opened,
And doomed enemies were overwhelmed.
If foes came out shooting,
They were trampled beneath his feet.
He crushed everyone he encountered
And struck down mighty leopards.
His prowess was beyond compare;
He bound Kulunsur
After charging with his spear.
When enemy warriors came out shooting,
He drove them before him,
Took them in his jaws and chewed them,
And destroyed them in an instant.
His majesty surpassed all other men,
And he barred even the lion’s path.
Then Akjoltoy,
Exhilarated by the land,
The consummate grey-maned hero exulting,
Climbed the heights of Keng-Özön,
To a black-peaked ridge,
Across plains untouched by living feet,
Through rough and broken gullies,
And reached Karool-Döbö below.
Akjoltoy climbed to its summit.
When he looked beyond it—
There was haze farther than sight could reach.
When he looked to this side—
There was mist beyond comprehension.
It dazzled human eyes,
And whoever encountered it met misery.
There lay rumbling Kara-Shor,
Where earth was heaped mountain-high.
Any creature that trod there
Lost its allotted fortune that very day,
And heaps of tail-like grass were piled there.
Any beast that walked there
Lost its allotted fortune that day;
Whenever its kulans came there to play,
Their bones piled up like mountains.
