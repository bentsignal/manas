So no one could approach and enter.
Human noses and ears,
Worn as amulets,
By seventy executioners;
Your skillful uncle Bakay
Reached and entered the palace.
The great gate in the wall
Opened its broad mouth.
When exemplary Bakay arrived,
Within that palace
Apples and raisins were scattered,
And his elder, old Bakay,
Was taken toward the guest palace
And received as a guest.
They slaughtered a colt and set out tea,
And laid out the food in abundance,
Heaping sugar like snow,
And piling up yellow butter.
They boiled and served
A fat lamb and a choice head.
When they had eaten their fill,
His elder, old Bakay,
Spread his hands and said, “Amen!”
And gave a new blessing.
Your dear mother Kanykey,
Her saintly nature showing,
Wondered what fate awaited the youth
Whom she had seen when near death;
Her tears poured down:
“To Beijing, whence those who go do not return,
Elder, old Bakay,
If you take the boy as your support
And set out upon the road,
Beijing is a place of calamity.
No one has bowed and conquered it;
Whoever attacked it did not survive.
To Beijing, whence those who go do not return,
Elder, old Bakay,
If you set out upon the road,
The one you fight is Kakan-Chyn,
Who never retreats when attacked.
What people have inhabited Beijing?
Those who faced it did not return safely.
To Beijing beyond a saint's reach,
My dear uncle,
It would be terrible if you let the fine horse stumble!
My legacy left by the hero,
My hope needed by the renowned people,
My dear uncle,
I entrust him to you!
My youthful son left by the butcher,
Royal uncle, I entrust him to you!
Following your imposing figure,
You have begun a campaign against the khan.
The small Argyn and Ogoy people,
Elder, lion Bakay,
You have again left without protection!
To Beijing beyond even a holy man's reach,
Uncle, it would be terrible if you lost him;
My only one left by the magnanimous hero,
Uncle, I entrust him to you!
Return without letting him enter battle;
Royal uncle Bakay, hear my words!
Kakan-Chyn's Beijing
Has a teeming people;
They leave no attacker unharmed,
And have a custom of drinking his blood.
It has roads unknown to man,
And its strength reaches across the world;
It has the renowned mighty Kongur.
