Wild sheep upon its hills,
Ibex upon its terraces,
Kulan on its pale plains,
Silver on its sunny slopes;
Its springs flow in abundance,
Its nightingales sing sweetly,
And a grieving person finds relief.
The tops of its willows bend,
Lines are drawn across its sands,
The gold within its earth is revealed—
May God show me my settled Keng-Kol,
My broad land of Talas!
If the time comes for him to grant it,
If fortune and happiness fill my path,
If God sets what lies behind us right,
I shall lower my spear
Against the wrestler who challenges me;
Making the spear my divining token,
I shall cry out and strike
The great wrestler Küzhürmön.
God, hear my tears;
If you do not wither my head,
Let me cast a sure divination.
Making my own head the stake,
I shall spear Küzhürmön.
If I chance to spear Küzhürmön,
If by chance I hurl him down,
My work and fortune will prosper;
The sister-in-law I take as my support
Will bear a son.
If I am thrown and fall from my horse,
If Küzhürmön spears and strikes me,
Then let me read that omen:
Rather than be called a captive slave,
If Küzhürmön casts me down,
Let me die before my appointed death.
Let this wretched life behold
The true place from which none return!
Küzhürmön, who came out to joust,
Was a lion full of courage,
Merciless, unmatched on foot,
And a master upon a horse.
He never fled from fire or attack,
And thrust his spear from afar;
Formidable wrestler Küzhürmön
Had won in many contests.
In wrestling he spared no wrestler;
One young horse nursed by a barren mare
Could not satisfy him for a single meal.
When Küzhürmön became aroused,
Many men trembled,
Lost their senses and stood still,
Their two eyes widening.
He was a terror surpassing the world;
Whoever approached him stopped.
Taking a spear with a steel shaft,
This villain had long prepared
To shed blood—
Such was Küzhürmön's nature.
When unbeliever and Muslim
Clashed in battle,
He performed dreadful deeds.
When a Chinese giant ravaged
And slaughtered the Kyrgyz people,
Riding a black rhinoceros,
The dark, stubborn champion on foot
Had come out in single combat;
The hero Küzhürmön
Thrust his spear in that duel,
Toppled the black, stubborn warrior
From the back of his rhinoceros,
And cut off his head.
The khan and all the people marveled:
“Küzhürmön is this mighty;
He has destroyed the giant he fought
And driven that terror into the earth.
He is both a hero and a lion;
