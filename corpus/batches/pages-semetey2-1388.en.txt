The feeble old and foolish young—
And inflict such tyranny,
Plunder and destroy us,
And wipe us out completely,
Shouting as you thrust your spear,
Slaying the khans outright,
Killing people on every side,
And utterly erasing our line?
Were we born on a barren black cliff,
Or are we less than a khan’s children?
Were you the one burdened with grief?
Were we not the Chinese who butchered
Your bloodthirsty father Chubak
And left him lying dead?
Your vaunted courage has no equal,
But your mother’s vengeance is not upon us;
Your swaggering has no equal,
Yet no vengeance for your kin lies here!
Your father was a roaring beast of a slave,
But did we slaughter his whole village?
Did we settle the score without measure?
Did we carry off the world,
Or scatter silver coins in its corners?
Did we flee in pursuit of wild asses,
Or scatter coins from our saddlebags?
Did we ruin your standing grain,
Or slaughter your forefather Akbaltay?
Accursed madman,
You drove my caravan from Keng-Kol;
Alas, before this swaggering slave,
Accursed be my womanhood—
I cannot take my revenge!
You slaughtered my people in Talas;
Slave and beggar, you have cruelly
Stripped me of all dignity!
By heaven, I shall scatter your caravan from Chatkal!
Allah, Allah, Creator of the open plain,
When shall I ever take my revenge?!”
As the woman wailed,
Kanchoro did not look at her.
As the young wife lamented,
He remained utterly unmoved;
However many words a woman spoke,
The ill-starred Kanchoro
Did not value them a scrap.
Then Chachykey within the fortress,
Your great sister-in-law with a rump broad as an embrace,
Shouted down from the tower
And summoned the forty wrestlers;
Chachykey issued her decree.
Because Akbala had spoken,
Chachykey dealt harshly with her:
“Slit the soles of that cackling infidel,
That woman called Akbala, and rub in salt;
Cut open her crown and put worms in it,
And let them fester there;
Dress her in a rough homespun coat,
Mount her on a lanky mare,
Send her after the sheep,
And make the wretch tend livestock.
Break the former fury
Of that rude and reckless woman,
That bellowing infidel.
Look upon our Khan Kanchoro,
And look upon the insolence
Of that bellowing woman!
Gather all your strength against her;
Break the swelling fury
Of that howling infidel!
Break the withered spirit
Of that strutting infidel!
Stretch her out and thrash her like a hide!
Let her gather firewood and carry water;
Give that worn-out harlot
To the herdsman!”
The many wrestlers standing there
Cried, “Yes, sister-in-law, yes!”
And brought great misery upon the woman.
