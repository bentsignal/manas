His armor was steel, his poison fierce.
It was the hour he cut down whoever met him.
His nose rose like a rugged peak.
His eyes and coat were hard as iron.
Alas, submitting to God’s command,
He drove a people numbering sevenfold.
The beautiful maiden stood apart.
His terror was singular and his venom fierce.
O God, set me straight.
In the measure of the present age,
Saying, “I am gone,” he abandoned hope of life.
As though a savage dog had sunk its teeth in,
Between his two shoulder blades,
Idle boys cast knucklebones.
Many came near him.
The grassy meadow held grazing livestock.
Wrapped in calamity,
He lay as a pale wrestler might lie.
Black brows stood over his eyes.
Even a horse herder had never seen his like.
Beside him lay a rope of measured length.
He was a servant of Almighty God.
The Creator makes every kind.
On the earth he rode a brocade-clad horse.
His nape was like a stallion’s, his coat mysterious.
Then another man emerged.
Two wealthy souls appeared.
With two breaths they cried, “The enemy!”
He became chief among the heroes.
A creature that lapped blood from the earth,
A companion who rode upon the ground,
Or perhaps it was Badirek.
The dominion of earth and water,
Was he one whom the earth itself touched?
Kaisan kept him captive.
His eye was of black stone.
Let us leave aside whoever encountered him.
