They stood face to face,
Against the khagan of Chinmachin;
If they faced him and fought,
How could they battle me?
Do not pant so, Karagul;
These words of yours are foolish.
If someone stands confronting me,
If some living man presses in,
I shall fire my gun and draw my bow,
And fight him with a roar.
There is no boat on the black water;
While my precious head remains,
No one can strike the khagan.
There is no boat on the open water;
While I, the Kalcha, stand in wrath,
No one can face me.
To be robbed of your beard—
This is a calamity for a man.
You will not rest without telling your pain;
Calling him the son of Manas,
Why do you praise so highly
A wandering orphan?
A man grows drunk on fat;
While you lay sleeping in drunkenness,
He must have ridden over you.
The wretch's young hero,
Saying, “The fool has fallen into my hands,”
Did whatever he wished;
Your beard and moustache,
He tore out every hair.
Ah, Karagul, what shall I do?
The hollow behind Beijing
Was a road I myself had scouted.
His father, that fool Manas—
Have you heard the report?—
Was a slave who deceived six times.
In the marsh by the black water,
Facing toward the qibla,
The falcon Manas knelt down
And was making his devotions.
Driving Algara hard,
I stalked him from behind.
When his brow touched the ground,
Above the back of his waist,
That accursed blood-drinker,
I stabbed him with my spear.
The doomed man, with his left hand,
Seized the spear as he weakened;
The trick he worked upon the spear—
When he took hold and pulled,
My spear broke apart.
Riding a horse like Kartküröng,
Wearing a robe with a flowered collar,
Azhybai, son of the khan of the Argyn,
Below my breast,
With his fringed steel lance,
Struck without looking back.
At times I came to my senses,
At times I could not gather my wits;
As I reeled to and fro,
Before any time had passed,
All the forty companions,
Who rejoiced at the word “slaughter,”
With their gray-headed elder leading,
Shouted and leveled their spears.
From all the forty companions,
From the midst of that fire,
It was Algara's strength that saved me.
Algara beneath me
Carried me off and saved me,
Fleeing over hillock and hollow,
Galloping away and saving me.
From those who came I hear the tale:
The spear lodged in the bone
At the back of his waist;
When told to pull it straight, they drew gently
And pulled the spear free, they say.
The spearhead was jagged;
