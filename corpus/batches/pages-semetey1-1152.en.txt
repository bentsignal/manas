When they were about to finish them utterly,
When they were about to slaughter
All the grim-looking black Kalmyks,
Orokkyr and Muzkindik,
Hero Kongurbay and Ezkara,
Dismounted from their horses
And held the horses back.
Dear hero Kongurbay
Then began to speak:
"Here are the Burut people with a leader;
Here are we—do with us as you will!
If you ask our station,
Each of us is a great lord.
Having heard word
That it was Khan Kökötöy's feast,
We rode here from Kakan.
We came to your feast,
And after eating our fill,
We grew unruly!
Planning to unleash turmoil,
We have fallen into hell like this!
Coming to your feast and using force,
We have become guilty!
We came as guests, tiger,
But humiliated you harshly.
Forgive this offense;
If you will not forgive it,
Here is the head—cut it off!"
The bald men of the jewel-wearing Chinese
Said this;
The strong men of the coat-wearing Chinese
Said this.
Their knees bent to the ground;
As though Esenkan had come,
They bowed before hero Manas.
Among the black Kalmyk people,
There is the custom of bowing like women
Before great lords;
Such is the infidels' tradition.
"Why should we quarrel with black Kalmyks and Manchus?
What inheritance would we gain?
Why should we behave roughly
Toward infidels who came to the feast?
By attacking amid the feast,
What inheritance would we gain?"
The stalwarts Koshoy and Bakay
Stood between them.
When they were about to reach the very end
And utterly plunder together
The black Chinese and many Manchus,
The tiger, bloodthirsty Manas,
Stopped as though he had drunk poison.
Infidel and Muslim alike—
Saying it was Khan Kökötöy's feast,
A spectacle arose.
Saying it was the place of the great gathering,
The feast of Khan Kökötöy,
With bloodthirsty Manas among them,
Infidel and Muslim alike
Raced like the qibla wind,
Made peace once more,
Abandoned words of disorder,
And began words of entertainment.
Infidel and Muslim alike
Gathered around shared cauldrons;
Their clashing subsided.
They gathered after their disorder,
And all the trouble ceased.
The people reconciled and prospered,
And merriment began again.
As the people stood thus,
Kökötöy's Bokmurun
Regained his spirit,
Came back to life,
And recovered his speech.
Maaniker seemed to run
Over hill and mountain.
