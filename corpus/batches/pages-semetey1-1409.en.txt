I had not lived to see such a thing;
My eyes reddening like a raven’s,
This wretched soul of mine until now
Had somehow gone on without dying!
My horns broken, left blunt,
I have become hornless!
Bereft of mane and tail,
I have become a mere stump!
Alas, Manas, damn you for dying;
Would that Bakai had not lived to see disgrace!
My sire died—what a day of sorrow—
And a camel’s load was laid upon a yearling;
When my lord passed from sight,
I herded camels to survive—
Why was I cast into such hell!
Failing to die beside my companion,
I have lived on in ruin;
Parted from my comrade,
Out on the empty steppe
I have been left to howl!
When I fitted a head
To the black spear Zulpukor,
While leopard Manas lived,
When infidel and Muslim alike
I led at the head,
Why did I not die then!
When I swung the curved blade
Against my forearm,
When I shattered
And took the face of the earth,
In the full strength of my courage,
While my bloodthirsty hero lived,
Why did I not die then!
When I hung a battle-axe
Upon the lattice wall,
When Manas had need of me,
At the earth’s edge, Zhelpinish,
When I knew that land,
Why did I not die then!
I did not suffer the grave’s torment;
Beneath my gray wolf
I did not die as a sacrifice!
When he rode out to battle,
Loading bullets upon the elephant,
Gathering the Muslim people together,
Driving the people in surging ranks,
Massing the giants in firm array,
Leading all the Muslim nation,
When he brought his wrath upon Kakan,
When he battered
The Black Chinese Manchus,
And seized their khanate,
Why did I not die then!
When my anger made blood flow,
When I held them under siege,
When I beat down the strong men
Of the coat-wearing Chinese,
When I thrashed them,
Why did I not die then!
Alas, Manas, damn you for dying!
I was left behind in disgrace;
Do not let Bakai be overcome—
My lion Manas, take me with you!
Companion of the other world,
Did you cast me behind you
To be shoved about by everyone,
And leave me to be slaughtered like this?!
You were a leopard-born warrior;
Manas of the dark day, damn you for dying!
While Manas lived,
I would carve the elephant’s flesh and eat it;
Pride had carried me away,
And I would say, ‘Let calamity remain far off!’
While Manas lived,
I was a great man, I was a khan;
To every people who saw me, I, Bakai,
Was a man like a prophet.
My lion Manas, have you truly gone,
