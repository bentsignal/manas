This comely infidel woman—
If blood should drip upon this black earth,
This infidel, white as snow,
Is surpassingly beautiful and bold;
There is no such marvel in the village.
With a handspan waist and moonlike face,
That celebrated beauty, that wanton woman,
Is full of guile and fierce with venom—
Aychörök, fertile in cunning,
This mischief-maker, fertile in cunning.
Friday alone knew when she walked;
The twilight night knew when she flew.
She swept across every quarter,
The whole wide world,
Flying as she roamed.
No mountain pass remained uncrossed,
No man remained whom she had not visited;
Chörök has seen a great deal.
She is no Chörök, but simply
A scourge who brings nations to ruin.
Take that unruly-minded woman,
That glittering infidel woman
Who learned the whole world in flight.
There is no ridge she has not flown beyond;
There is no man who does not recognize
The outward face of that black-hearted wretch.
Damn you, valiant Kiyaz!
Kiyaz, you witless fool!
Aychörök, the accomplished one among women,
That tumultuous woman,
Was Khan Akun's daughter.
She was the subject of my words,
And she was your sister-in-law;
You truly are shameless, Kiyaz.
You have courage but no sense;
You are a blundering Kiyaz.
Why do you not unleash
Utter slaughter, Kiyaz?
Why do you not take Aychörök
As plunder, Kiyaz?
Look there: the peerless woman Aychörök
Is, after all, your wife;
If you truly contest her, she is your widow,
And if you exert yourself, your own spouse.
You destroyed that swaggering ruin-bringer,
You demolished the Grey Wolf's fortress.
You are a hero, you are a lion;
Yet you are a foolish Kiyaz.
You are the man to take the beauty.
Think it through and raise the tumult;
Show Talas the strength of your arm.
Take the woman as a prize,
This beautiful woman Chörök,
This strange-minded infidel,
This graceful, shimmering woman,
With thimble mouth and camel-calf eyes,
With rolling black eyes.
There is no need to ponder such words.
Ah, God-forsaken valiant Kiyaz,
Now is the time to take Aychörök.
Take Aychörök as your spoil
And return, Kiyaz!
Why not hold a grand spectacle,
A mighty feast, Kiyaz?
Meet her face-to-face and speak,
Saying, “Sister-in-law, come with us.”
Make the infidel Aychörök,
That woman, your plunder;
Devise a plan no man could imagine,
And hold a feast without stint.
Open up all the livestock;
Let the revelry be like this:
Parade her through many towns,
Take her and display her before the people;
Heap meat as high as Ala-Too,
Pour broth as vast as Ala-Köl,
Raise dust along the lower road
And amaze all who see it.
