I found and chose my own husband.
Putting aside my wounded pride, poor wretch that I was,
I entered and moved among your village.
I placed my wishing necklace around my neck;
Saying, "Let him stay with you for six days,"
I pleaded with his wife.
Though I pleaded, the woman would not consent
And would not listen to my words.
Still aggrieved, poor wretch that I was,
I went to the edge of Ala-Kol
In the form of a white swan.
Thinking, "If my dear one is a man,
He will come in search of me,"
I remained beside Ala-Kol
In the form of a white swan,
And carried off
Your noble one's Akshumkar."
Having said this, Aichurok,
Her white throat gleaming,
Her moonlike face shining,
Took your sovereign's Buurul
To a plane tree stirred by the breeze
And tethered him securely there.
Without looking up or down,
Without thinking she might die,
Without looking all around,
Without valuing her life by so much as a hair,
When the laughing game began,
The boisterous young lord,
The champion upon whom the day had fallen,
Now lost all memory of himself.
The splendid one who had never seen a maiden
Saw the maiden and grew excited;
Calm and ringing with vigor,
Without valuing it as much as his boot-tip
That his mighty lord remained outside,
The overwhelmed Kulchoro,
Among thirty brides and thirty maidens,
With Aichurok in their midst,
Played on without concern.
The maidens' talk became a marvel;
As he sat playing in delight,
His lord passed out of his thoughts.
Let us leave the boy
With the maiden who delighted him,
And turn our story to the thoroughbred lord Semetey,
Who had given away Buurul and remained on foot.
Let us begin the news of him.
Upon the ridge of Agynek,
For that swaggering unfortunate man,
The sun before him darkened in the sky;
Sixty anxious thoughts
Came at once upon Semetey:
"Kulchoro, who was my shelter,
Has not yet passed twelve,
Has not come anywhere near fifteen;
The milk of Kanykei's breast
Has not yet left his palate.
He was a child whose understanding had not ripened,
Still awkward and unformed in himself.
Did he come to a high mountain pass
And fall to his death?
Did he come to a river with a fierce current
And drown in its waters?
Did he fall into a net
Bound round with iron rope?
