“You are the support on whom I rely,
The marksman who is a shield
For the Kakan of Chin-Machin.
Even if Jeljetpes descends underground,
Capture him and bring him here.
Bind both his hands;
Gouge out both his eyes.
Cut out his tongue so he cannot speak,
And pierce him through the nape.
Put a billowing robe on him,
Seat him backward on a mule,
Letting him sway helplessly,
And drive him here to me!
He too is a champion and a lion.
As for Kenen, chief of the Buruts,
Pure as sparkling water,
The precious bringer of good fortune,
Huge as the Ala-Too,
A fortress like Mount Kap,
Who consumes those he fights,
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
A massive, well-built terror,
Formidable as Alaskar,
The finest of the white-capped people—
Go and destroy Er Kenen;
Find a way and capture him!
No bullet pierces what he wears;
No arrow catches what he rides.
Kertulpar flew like a cloud,
While the champion wore his armor,
And circled all Five Beijings.
He is a beast fit for
Your champion khan Chyntengir to ride;
Winged Kertulpar
Is a creature worth taking as plunder.”
Saying, “Remember these words,”
He spoke thus to his marksman,
Gave him this terrible sign,
And firmly sent him against Kenen,
To bring distress upon the champion,
The khan of the Ala-Too.
Chyntengir took pride
In having champion Yengkor.
When Yengkor chose his shot
And watched for his mark,
He ordered the host to surge past without stooping,
To encircle him,
To reach his very foundation,
And spill his blood by the spoonful.
He ordered them to press down from the crown,
And gave him a countless army
Of iron lariats and nets,
And men armed throughout with daggers—
Gave lavishly, holding no troops back.
The champion became the marksman,
And came toward the hero.
How great was his strength and skill
As he came to see Er Kenen,
To fight and quarrel
And deliver his punishment by hand!
He covered the earth in fog;
Camels passed, crushing stones,
And crossed water without tiring.
Watching ridge after ridge,
He saw Kertulpar beneath Kenen,
A swift, well-equipped beast.
Unless his limbs were broken,
Unless he died and his life grew still,
Unless a thousand disasters befell him,
Unless only a handful of reins remained,
He would never stumble into disaster;
Though crimson turmoil and great calamity raged,
For however many days he rode into slaughter,
He would never yield to the enemy—
The formidable racer Kertulpar.
Jeljetpes, the blessed lion,
Attacked the enemy without looking back;
It was his time to make slaughter.
On the qibla side Er Kenen
Wounded everyone he faced,
Threading up and down
Through the countless host.
He thrust his spear without turning,
Drenched them in bloody gore,
And fought without retreating.
He passed swiftly through their midst,
Spearing those he reached,
Searching for Chyntengir.
Having lost the two lions,
Ebegey and Kogoy,
His ribs seemed broken apart;
The world appeared false.
Black night fell upon his head,
And a merciless day came;
The night was caught in cloud.
Er Kenen, lion against the foe,
Was then surpassing all
In strength and courage.
