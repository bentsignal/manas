I deceived and escaped
The great stone-buttoned lords of China, my elder.
I surveyed their city from the ridge;
I have learned, my khan and elder,
The true measure of Kongurbay.
The nobles of Great Beijing and Dyngsha
I brought around to my word;
My khan and elder, I deceived this multitude and returned.
Through tireless effort, your brave companion
Has shown his resourcefulness.
‘Pay the blood-price of Manas,’ I said;
‘Consent to these terms,’ I said;
‘Pay tribute safely to the khan,’ I said;
‘Negotiate between both sides,
Make peace between the peoples, and return,’ I said;
I never wearied of mediating.
With skill I deceived
The old Kalmyk Kongurbay,
That soul who had fostered an ancient grudge.
You elders, led by Bakay,
Go, every hero among you.
My heroes, my elders, my sultans,
Do not say that I am raging;
Heed the words of your servant.
All you giants, loom over him
And dismount from your horses.
When you stand face-to-face,
Lay the savage, raging Kalmyk
Flat along the ground.
I shall ride between the two sides
Upon the winged steed Arbuurul.
Ask the wretched Kalmyk
Where the champions have gone,
My bloodthirsty elder Bakay;
Question him closely.
Ask what will become of the people
And what danger Ezkara intends.
Ask where Almambet, Chubak,
And the hero Manas have gone;
From the snarling Chinese,
My precious kinsman Sarykan,
Demand an answer!”
When Külchoro had spoken thus,
The bloodthirsty warrior came forth prepared;
Khan Bakay, my khan and elder, stepped forward
And pleaded with Külchoro:
“For a son like you,
I, your elder Bakay, would gladly sacrifice myself!
Ah, my champion Külchoro,
If you lead this great onslaught,
Capture Kongurbay,
And give us that spectacle,
Then in Bakay’s old age
I shall present you from my herds
My treasured beast Kökcholok.
If that provision is too little,
Pour my blood into a leather flask and take it;
If need be, Külchoro,
Take your elder’s very life.
Charge straight at the brooding Kongurbay
And thrust your spear into him;
Free my soul
From this grievous hell of torment.
Charge upon the swaggering Kalmyk
And drive your spear through him;
For the sake of the great Kyrgyz nation,
Cut the throat of the infidel
Who mines the veins of gold.
From the vast nation of China’s forty tribes,
Avenge the Kyrgyz.
What lion is fit for such a feat?
A hero like you is fit for combat.
Strike the black-worm infidels
With a mighty blow.
My dear Külchoro,
A lion like you is fit for such a feat;
Avenge the bloodthirsty heroes of old.
You have always been resourceful and lionhearted;
