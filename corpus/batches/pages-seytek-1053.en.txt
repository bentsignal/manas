“If only the one God
Granted a man his heart’s desire in this way,
What regret could remain in a young man?
If, by a man’s good fortune,
Such a peri beauty came to him,” they said.
The people’s cries gradually subsided,
And the crowd grew calm and quiet.
Into the echoing palace
The young men came running,
Throwing themselves into saddling the horse.
The leopard Seytek’s fierce nature
And heroic spirit became manifest,
While the immeasurably swift
Attendants rushed in every direction.
A white halter made by a maid,
A halter fitted to Kulansur,
A white halter tied with rounded knots,
Fitted for the mighty stallion—
They fastened it upon Kulansur.
An impenetrable pure-white caparison
They draped with fluttering skirts
Across the prancing thoroughbred.
Steel shoes with silver nails
They hammered onto his hooves.
They saddled him magnificently,
Striking awe into everyone who saw;
A leather saddlecloth and blue saddle pad
They placed upon the thoroughbred.
They had brought from the palace
The white saddle inherited
From Almambet of old.
Calling it a legacy of the noble hero,
They filled the people’s hearts with wonder.
The reason their hearts were satisfied—
If you ask about that saddle—
Forty craftsmen and Bölökbai
Had fitted it without the slightest flaw,
Assembling it piece by tiny piece.
When its value became known,
Many khans asked for it.
It was never given to those who asked;
Far from giving away the saddle,
They never let an impure man ride it.
Its front bow was of white gold,
And its rear bow of yellow gold.
So that it might shine on moonless nights
And delight the heart of its rider,
They had every part of the saddle
Ornately carved.
Upon its front and rear bows
They had rubies and precious jewels
Set in clusters the size of bowls.
The white saddle was of matchless beauty
And satisfied the heart of everyone who saw it.
The winged tulpar Kulansur
Filled all the people with awe.
They saddled the moon-browed Kulansur
With the golden saddle,
Moving with immeasurable speed.
They quickly finished saddling the horse,
Then laid on the bearskin crupper and white breastband
And had the straps drawn tight.
Leading the horse straight ahead,
They made him stride proudly,
Urging one another onward.
When the leopard mounted his horse,
They surged as though an enemy had come.
Clad imposingly in a golden coat,
He loomed like a white, rocky mountain;
His splendor appeared mountain-high
And captivated all who saw him.
Straining like a hungry lion,
The magnanimous hero rode forth,
Equipped with all his weapons.
At his side hung a scabbardless saber;
He took a spear with a steel shaft and sharp point,
Its blade a full handspan long.
Wondering what spectacle would unfold,
The gathered people lined the way.
