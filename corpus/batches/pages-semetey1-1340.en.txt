If fate reaches you, fall to a bullet!
Does a hero not die at an enemy's hand?
Ah, my gray wolf Semetey,
Will those who remain not live out their days!
Beijing is that formidable stronghold;
If you face it, do not remain alive.
Kongurbai, khan of Beijing,
Is the lion who slaughtered your father.
The son of the great lion said:
"Is it right to fear war?
Can there be a man who will not fight?
Unless you pull down the golden pillar
Of hero Kongurbai's tent,
Unless you tear out its pillar,
Unless you finish him and unleash strife,
Lion Manas went to the other world;
Unless you avenge him,
May you sink without offspring,
And, never being called Manas's son,
May you die rather than live!
The reason I speak thus is this:
Did mighty Manas not gather
The whole world under his rule?
Did he not ask the one Almighty Allah,
Saying, 'Grant me a son who will avenge me,'
And receive you in answer!
Break the Chinese and seize their migration;
If you are a man, my only one,
For your father gone to the other world,
Take vengeance upon Kongurbai!
The prince's only son—
Without campaigning against Beijing,
Is it right for him to wander grieving,
Without fighting against Kakan?
Can this behavior of yours be right?
Your father Manas was a lion;
Almighty Allah will never
Create and grant another mortal
Like your father!
The father of your father Manas, the gray wolf,
Yakyp, was driven away,
And Manas was born in Altai.
When he reached eight years,
He killed twelve boys
Among the Black Kalmaks and Manchus,
And threw those people into turmoil.
At nine he stood firm;
When he reached exactly ten,
He pressed down and devoured
Every mortal who faced him.
When he reached eleven,
There was Esen Khan's envoy,
His appointed frontier scout,
Who stood at the blood-red throne,
A master jeweler of many wonders,
The swaggering champion of the Kalmaks,
Named Döngö,
Esen Khan's treasury wrestler.
Yesterday, when he came to oppress
The Kyrgyz of forty households,
Saying, 'We shall drive away
All wealthy Yakyp's countless cattle;
Esen Khan requires them,'
When Döngö came from China,
When hero Döngö with his whole force
Brought grievous hardship,
When he raided all the countless cattle
And drove them away in confusion,
Hero Manas on Toruchaar,
At twelve years of age,
Confronted them in Altai;
Your lion Manas, the gray wolf,
Unleashed an upheaval.
There in Altai
He wrought utter turmoil;
With hero Döngö at their head,
On that campaign in Altai
He slew two thousand champions.
