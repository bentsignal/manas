My son, does it stand safe and sound,
Your tethering line set up in Talas?
And aged Chyyyrdy,
And dear lady Kanykey—
Does she live safe and sound,
Your mother who suffered torment?
Is your divided people still there?
And Bölökbay, who guarded
Manas's treasury and was
A friend to the giant Dөөтү—is he alive?
Did you safely find
The remnant of the scattered people?
Did you safely find
The descendants of Almambet and Chubak?
Did you see within Talas
Your fox like a gray hare?
Did you take back without scattering any
Your property left by Manas?!
If we follow the words of tradition,
If we ask those who know,
We heard news of you from Bukhara.
We have kept safe
Your wife, Aychürök.
Because of where she had gone,
Because of your bride,
A lamb's meat seemed a fur coat to us;
We saw hell loom terribly great.
Because of Aychürök,
Embers sprang from the hearth;
Demanding, 'Give us Aychürök,'
Mighty Chynkozho and Toltoy arose!"
Beginning his account with Chynkozho,
While he stood saying this,
The young prince Semetey,
That dear lion, proud as a cock,
Stirred and began to speak;
The dear hero returned the greeting:
"The people are safe, the khan is safe,
Lion Bakay too is safe;
The land of Zhylygnduu and Keng-Kol is safe,
The happy Kyrgyz people are safe.
Kanykey and Chyyyrdy,
My two mothers, are safe.
Let us greet one another, elder in-law;
You whom I have newly seen are safe.
Our fox that goes forth like poison,
Our inheritance left by our father,
Is lying safe and sound.
We have come to act as intermediaries;
Our intentions are pure.
Do they live safe and sound,
My two elder in-laws,
Your Afghan people at Opol?
Our influence reaches our Kozho;
Do your Afghan people here
Remain undisturbed and safe?"
From atop the golden throne,
Your gray-maned lion, sitting unmoved,
Leaned forward and stretched out his hand.
What else could Azhybay and Tümönbay do
But panic?
Both stretched out their hands,
Struck by God in their haste.
Semetey, son of Manas,
Stretched his hand toward the two;
When he grasped them both together,
They did not even fill his palm.
"Would a hero fail to take interest
In such feeble men?
If two thousand weaklings like these
Came together as one,
Would my poor head be satisfied?
Would Toltoy and Chynkozho fail
To heap humiliation upon
Weak men like these
And shut them away?"
Your young champion, son of the blood-taker,
Held both men's hands at once,
