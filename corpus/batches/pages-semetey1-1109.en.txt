He gathered mixed clans and made a people.
He gathered stray birds and made hunting birds;
He gathered wanderers and made a people.
Once poor, he became rich;
Once alone, he became numerous.
Give hero Ürbü my greetings.
Tell him to come to my feast
And see this spectacle.
If he does not come to my feast,
If he does not see this spectacle,
If I should take him into my wrath,
I will turn back and raid him
And beat out his old madness.
On this side of there
Are Khan Tekechi, Khan Shygay,
And Zhamgyrchy of the Eshtek—
The peoples of three khans lie side by side.
Their stock is Kyrgyz, their sons true;
Give all three khans my greetings.
Say Kökötöy's many livestock
Have filled Ükürchü;
Say they surpass the Muslim peoples;
Say they dug cooking pits
Along Üch-Kapkak.
Say that Bokmurun, Kökötöy's son,
Has camped at Üch-Karkyra;
Say that Kökötöy's countless riches
Have filled the face of the earth,
And the time has come to give the feast!
Tell them to come to my feast
And heed my words.
If they do not come to my feast,
Tell them to behold Kökötöy's thousand champions
From atop their own houses!
Dwelling in Wide Ili,
They are imposing heroes.
The two khans Edigey and Teyish—
There, as we can see,
Lie Muslims beyond counting.
Give those two my greetings;
Tell them to come to my feast
And see with their own eyes
Kökötöy's great prize.
If they do not heed my words,
If they remain away from my feast,
I will take and slaughter their mares as booty;
I will bind and seize their foot soldiers,
Make them shoulder and carry firewood,
And thus settle their account.
Those who answer me defiantly—
I will punch out their brains.
When you go farther on,
You will see another young man,
In the broad valley of Sary-Arka,
Sprawled imposingly there—
The champion of the Kazakhs.
He husks his millet, stones and all;
He praises his wife like a maiden.
His lattice walls are of wood,
And his elder brother is of the Alach.
Hero Kökchö, son of Aydar Khan—
Who has ever stood beside me!
The heel of the blue boot,
Convey my many greetings
To Aydar Khan's Kökchö.
Dwelling in Sary-Arka,
Tethering his piebald-yellow herd,
No enemy went against him in combat.
His manner and conduct are brusque—
Poor lord Kökchö of the Kazakhs.
Kökchö, khan of the Kazakhs,
Give Khan Kökchö my greetings.
