Never surrendered their comrades’ lives.
From the day I became Külchoro,
From the day I mounted the steed Külüstan,
I wheeled my spear and broke the ranks,
My fortunes were set right by the unseen,
And anyone who fought me vanished.
When I stood glaring in fury,
No enemy dared confront me.
Of all the sons born to mankind,
None remained who dared oppose me.
Black within and blue without,
I did not meet my doom and die.
Never in all the world had I seen
Such an enemy as this infidel,
This son of a monster.
Brave Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
He too is of Kyrgyz stock.
When he girded his waist firmly,
The two khans Chynkozho and Toltoi,
When they came to exact this blood-price,
Loosed arrows like falling rain,
Firing shots and starting strife,
When they came bringing calamity,
Our hearts burned and flames engulfed us,
They raised their banner on grassy ground,
And Kanchoro was our own kinsman.
That vicious swine began the quarrel,
Opening our flank and ruining everything,
Bringing disgrace and dividing the people,
Our people fled and scattered,
Our fortress was shattered.
Our hearts were trampled by evil,
And before the eyes of many enemies,
We now walk exposed like this.
When my noble uncle Semetey
Found the two of us,
He had ninety beasts slaughtered for a feast,
And gave us matching names,
Feeding us rump fat and horse mane,
Calling us inseparable brothers,
He had us equally suckled
At Kanykei’s breast,
And called sons of the noble one.
Yet Kanchoro, who had lived so,
Came out of the ravines,
Ravaging the land and slaughtering the people,
That swine rose as an enemy,
An abomination arose from our own hearth,
An enemy arose from our own kin,
Steeping poison for my destruction,
And thus brought about my downfall.
He told Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
“Drive so-and-so here.”
God must have struck the fool.
Shamelessly exposing himself and ruining all,
He had become an enemy.
To the Zhediger of Zheti-Özön,
A peaceful people resting in their land,
With six fleet horses
And a letter no larger than a palm,
He sent word to wage war without delay,
So mountain-like Toltoi and Chynkozho
Would come and take revenge,
So the honorable would witness it,
So brave Kyyaz would cease to be a man
And die rather than remain alive.
Yesterday, when the Zhediger overran us,
And the people were devastated,
Saying, “My own younger brother became my foe,”
The magnanimous lion took offense.
When my elder brother was beguiled
By Kökmonchok, daughter of a peri,
And vanished into the unseen,
I was entangled in a net of ropes
And fell into the enemy’s hands.
Truly ensnared, I braced myself,
But fell into Kanchoro’s hands.
My own kinsman became my enemy,
And grief filled my heart.
