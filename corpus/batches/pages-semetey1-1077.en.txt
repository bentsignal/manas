Born as your own kinsman,
When was I ever a son of Manas?
You were traced from the very loins
Of noble, generous Manas;
You burst forth, tearing the womb
Of my noble mother Kanykey.
When was I ever a son of Manas?
If I should die,
I was a dog-hide slave;
While I remain alive,
I am a sheepskin slave.
Do not grieve without cause;
I was one of the acknowledged servants.
My last request to you, elder brother:
If I die at Toltoy's hands,
You will recite the Quran and bury me.
If I return having killed Toltoy,
My name of "slave" will disappear,
My poor soul will be restored;
So that I may become a sultan,
At the very least, leopard,
You will give me the Argyn people.
Farewell then, leopard!"
Speaking so, Külchoro
Seized the nearby prince
Around the neck.
The gathered folds of his shirt
Burst apart, and Semetey
Tore every one away.
Beneath his chin,
Above his beard,
He kissed him firmly with a roar;
"Farewell, elder brother!" he said, kissing him.
As the boy stood so,
Khan Akun and his city cried aloud;
Every soul who saw him
Shed streaming tears from their eyes.
Semetey could not bear to watch.
The Kasaba banner floated in his hand,
Syrnayza flashed in his hand;
The hearts of those who saw were divided.
Appearing like Opol Mountain,
He caught up the black-jawed whip
And swung it around;
He pulled fairy-like Buurul
Sideways and let him go,
Breaking the face of the earth.
Külchoro rode away at a gallop
Toward Toltoy.
Your prince could not remain still;
He now took a spyglass in his hand
And trained it after
The ill-fated Choro.
A black-striped leopard
Strained along at his flank;
The spirit of his khan and father Manas
Had taken him under its protection.
A short-maned blue lion
Strained beside him;
Almambet's spirit
Had taken him under its care.
The Forty Chiltens and Khidr Ilyas,
Each becoming a host of a thousand,
Shouted encouragement behind him.
Nine patron spirits
Readied themselves at his armpits;
Dock-tailed blue wolves,
Appearing as some forty living beings,
Closed in behind him.
The giant black bird, the Simurgh,
As though about to carry him away,
Reached its feet down from the sky,
And it too stood supporting him;
Roaring, it stretched out its feet,
And it too stood supporting him.
Seeing his protectors revealed,
Dear Semeң rejoiced
And stood stretching tall:
