When his wrath was roused,
He killed his own father.
Hot blood reached his nostrils;
From his neck and jaw
Blood streamed like a saddle-strap.
He flared higher whenever diminished,
And multiplied whenever slain—
The accursed one’s own son.
When the lasso struck his head,
He shouted in fury,
Invoking the name of Manas,
Who had gone to the other world:
“Frenzied slave, is it you?
May I die if my strength does not suffice!
Wrathful Karagul,
Let me test myself against you.
I will pull the reins
And turn the ever-ready Taibuurul,
Then drive you toward Beijing.
After making me pay beyond all measure,
After splitting my head open,
How can I remain alive!”
The splendid lion, proud as a rooster,
The noble hero who would not be moved—
After his father Manas died,
He settled and took possession of Talas;
Living as an orphan, he grew to manhood
And became hero Semetey.
He had not yet died of shame,
And had never before seen
Such humiliation upon the earth.
Without looking around him,
Without sparing his life at all,
Without glancing about,
Your champion, son of the mighty man,
Did not value his life as much as a hair.
He hurriedly put on bulletproof Akolpok
Without even shaking it out,
And mounted the playful Buurul
Bare-backed at a bound.
He seized and swung
The white whip with a black handle,
And struck Buurul’s flank,
Making it ring out, “Kang!”
With no spear and only a whip,
The leopard’s offspring
Set out to chase Karagul almost naked.
He looked neither uphill nor down,
Gave no thought to dying,
And raced in fury.
As though the horse could understand speech,
He pleaded with Buurul:
“My beloved Arbuudan,
Lower your head toward the ground
And take a different kind of stride.
To the one who has never squatted down to urinate,
Whose sleeve has never touched the ground.
That infidel son of Katal—
Bring me up to him once.
Lower your neck toward the earth,
My beloved noble thoroughbred,
And take a stride beyond all measure.
That infidel son of Kaldai,
Hero Khan Kongurbai—
His Black Horse ridden on patrol,
Bring me up to it once!
I raise argamaks and horses;
May I circle in devotion, Buurul,
Around a thoroughbred like you!
I gathered champions and heroes;
May I circle around a kinsman like you!
When he made me pay beyond all measure,
When he split my head open,
If only he had turned his neck before me,
If only Külchoro had stood and watched—
Would that thundering Karagul
Then have dared commit this deed?
The dream he saw had turned baleful—
