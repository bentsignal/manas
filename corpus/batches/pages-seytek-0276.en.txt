You need a dark gray-maned lion
Who is never sated by countless armies
And spares no life when facing the foe.
When enemies meet you in battle,
You need a lion like me.
Set our meeting by the morning star
And take your uncle Kül as companion.
Heed my tongue when I offer counsel;
Thinking, ‘He will be a heavy mace,’
Keep me hanging at your belt.”
As Külchoro spoke thus,
The orphaned son of the hero
Revealed his valor;
As though stabbed in the heart,
He had gone away in wrath.
“True-born hero Seytek,
Braver than Semetey,
Has proved unworthy,” would they not say?
“The wretched orphan did not take after his father
And has come to nothing,” would they not say?
They would spread rumor and clamor,
Raising a great uproar that never fades.
“When driven into a trap, the stubborn one
Became as weak as straw,” would they not say?
“He lacked courage and did not take after his father;
He turned out worthless,” would they not say?
Like a hazy star in heaven,
He chose his appointed companion, would they not say?
“He means to attack the Chinese
And has taken as companion an uncle
Whose figure looms like a mountain,” would they not say?
Would not the people’s gossip devour him?
“He lost his nerve, unlike his father;
His life fled as though struck from above.
Unable to find the road through the dead end,
He wanders in confusion,” would they not say?
“Mighty Külchoro
Leads him by the hand,” would they not say?
Is there any yellow, hilly mountain
A hero cannot cross when he spurs on?
Wherever he went, your heroic father
Would take a companion.
Let me go with you as well.
Renowned Keң-Kol and great Talas—
Our forefather, brave Manas,
Found that land for us.
The fortunate Kyrgyz people there
Drank arak as though it were water.
It is the land my father proudly rode across,
The land where my mother bore me in labor,
The land where the blood was washed from my shirt,
The land where my umbilical cord was tied.
Broad Talas is where I wriggled
And crawled upon the dust,
Where I rode a switch naked
And played as a child.
Like the morning star Venus,
Let me go as your appointed companion;
My good fortune Seytek, let me
Go beside you as your companion.
Like white wheat flour,
Let me mingle closely with you;
Like the hair of a white gazelle,
Let me be drawn out beside you.
Knowing none can prevail against fate,
I give you this final counsel.
I speak it to you, my colt Seytek:
You need an elder brother.
My child, I need you to remain safe.
My child, many will narrow their eyes and mock you;
There are many wrathful enemies.
The Chinese stand along my flank;
Firearms face us ahead,
And many enemies oppose us.
What is needed against such foes?
To gird one’s loins, one needs a strong back.
One needs firm ground on which to stand,
Abundant courage and fierce might,
Power to straighten out whoever attacks,
And wisdom to ponder coming and going.
