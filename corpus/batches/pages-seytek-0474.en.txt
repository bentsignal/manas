When our lord was gone from sight,
Our heads sank lower than a kid goat’s.
Kanchoro, whom we raised with lullabies,
Showed us the city of Alai
And wrought countless deeds, I suppose.”
The sage Bakai said this,
And stood sobbing loudly,
Lamenting with a ringing voice.
Before he had finished speaking,
The moonlike lady Aichürök,
Smiling faintly through her pallor,
Her face shining clear as a mirror,
Cried out for her betrothed,
And wept without restraint, sobbing.
She met him in sorrow and lamentation,
Sobbing and weeping, overcome.
Her black hair, untouched by a comb,
Fell apart strand by strand;
Her dear hair, long as horsehair,
Spread across her breast.
From her quail-like eyes,
Separate tears streamed;
From her blackcurrant eyes,
Tears fell in drops as heavy as a whip.
“O blue-maned lion!” she cried,
Sobbing that they had been reunited:
“My princely leopard, surpassing lion,
You increased your nobility,
But made this wretched head endure
Torment, hardship, and calamity.
The Zulpukor blade is supreme,
Yet you let Bölökbai trample it,
And, lost for sixteen years,
You made me guard the foot of the mountain.
My noble blue-maned lion,
My anguish never leaves my thoughts.
Yellowed as though with jaundice,
I endured a hundred grievous afflictions.
I preserved what was entrusted to me
Amid a hundred enemies.
Did I eat the flesh of some great bird?
Crushed and turned to embers,
From that time until this,
Parted from the great ones,
I wandered in misery and confusion—
Was this how you wished me to die?
I burned and baked, becoming an ember;
I was shattered and came to nothing.
I was a fine steed, but I wasted away;
I was a blue-maned lion, but I grew weary.
I burned away and lost
Half the youth of the blue-maned lion.
Steel softened and I became brass;
I faced many a storm,
And was gnawed and devoured by nobles.
They called me gray-bearded Bakai,
And I became a byword among cruel foes.
My wings, my pinions, and my tail
Were plucked, trimmed, and devoured.
My brain boiled where no water could reach,
And I became a barren desert where no grass grows.
Stripped of tail and mane,
I became the wild ass of that desert.
Neither dying nor vanishing, I grieved;
O bloodthirsty lion whom I cherished,
O leopard, I was parted from you.
My eyes became blood-filled sores;
I became a soul no longer needed.
I was once a proud and splendid man,
But became a stooping old man.
My waist was bent like a fox’s;
My vigor gone, my wretched soul
I burdened upon the beasts of night.
From my earliest days, I, Bakai,
Was born to be a warrior;
But when the hero vanished from sight,
I was beset by so many calamities.
My wretched soul was dragged
Like a kökbörü carcass among strongmen.
Our years stretched long in mockery.
