Unless not a handspan of rein remained,
Unless forty disasters struck,
He would not let his rider be taken by the enemy.
Such was this Black Horse.
Born in the desert and raised among stones,
The accursed one never became desert-worn;
Raised apart by the unseen,
The accursed one, in his third year,
Raced with the kulan.
Have you heard of it?
Kyz Saikal, daughter of Karacha Khan—
The horse Kyz Saikal rode,
The mighty tulpar Algara,
Was born together with him.
When his mouth was released,
When urged forward with a burst,
He had a way of turning into an arrow.
When your uncle tells it,
His tale has no end.
Such was Algara.
His tail and mane were no common things;
Apart from hero Kalcha,
No mortal ever rode him.
When he entered a crisis
And opened his mouth,
The beast flew roaring like a cloud;
When stone touched his hoof,
It burst apart layer by layer.
When angrily urged forward,
His flesh separated like a sheep's,
And his back straightened like a broad ridge.
When Kongurbai grew impassioned,
He tied on his berenji waist-armor;
The great Black Horse, swiftest of steeds,
Circled Beijing of the Kakanchin
In twelve days.
If you ride, the Black Horse is that steed;
From the speed of this beast,
In a fierce spear fight,
Yesterday, when the clash came,
Within the fortress
Where your father gray-wolf Manas fought,
When he entered dire straits,
In the forty-day desert,
Your khan father's Aimanboz,
After going six days without water,
Yesterday could travel no farther.
Six thousand Chinese and countless kaldays,
With white-bearded Kongurbai,
Shouted and leveled their spears;
All the Chinese and many Manchus
Surrounded your oceanlike gray-wolf father.
At a loss and alarmed,
When Aimanboz turned his head,
Saying, “What remedy can there be?”
The gray wolf stood bereft of means.
Then gold-tressed, bright-belted
Almambet, son of Aziz Khan,
His companion like a star at the cairn,
The companion of Khan Manas,
Shared one road in reconnaissance,
And one arm in battle;
They shared food as comrades
And livestock in one enclosure.
Noble Alman, gray-wolf hero,
Mounted stake-necked Sarala,
And made him rear in battle,
Girded his leather belt,
And against hero Kongurbai,
Without making support of his kalday,
Entered the host alone and attacked.
He arrived before the arrow;
This Kongurbai of Alööke
Received Almambet's spear,
Was gravely wounded,
And immediately fared badly:
His backside tipped from the saddle,
And his foot slipped from the stirrup.
