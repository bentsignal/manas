He stooped, and strength left his back;
From your uncle Bakay’s hands
All courage fled, all power departed.
Certain that he would die,
The wretched old man mounted in this state.
Sweet words had left his mouth;
The wide, staring eyes
Had left your ruined uncle.
All vigor fled from Bakay;
This was when he sank deep into misery.
He had no life beyond lying still;
This was when Bakay lay upon his side,
His only loved one gone,
While Kanchoro dragged him as a kökbörü carcass.
Brave Bakay went rolling
Along the path of the stallions;
Brave Bakay passed through the hands
Of seventy Jediger wrestlers.
The broad world became narrow;
Great Bakay could no longer join counsel.
Parted from his children,
He was subjected to peculiar disgrace.
That costly cap of blue lynx—
Who had not worn it jauntily?
When people saw the lion Bakay,
All beneath moon and sky would tremble.
Now an iron cage surrounded him,
And thick misery lay upon Bakay Khan.
Bakay the lion, victor in many races,
Became their kökbörü carcass that day.
“While my fortune was intact,
I was a man of lofty purpose.
Akbaltay, forefather of this servant,
How long were you there?
Alas, may my only one survive,
May that soul never witness such humiliation!
In the prime of strength and longing,
While my only one remained alive,
Tall, broad-shouldered,
Both wrestler and lion,
He was a glowing red ember,
Peerless in my world;
My Morning Star companion of the moon,
My honored sultan,
The light for my grieving soul,
My keepsake left
By Albambet who passed to the other world.
He girded his belt with skill;
If seven divisions of black foes appeared,
He entered alone and attacked.
The choicest of the falcons,
My Külchoro was precious.
May my sole loved one not die;
As my final journey draws near,
May my orphaned loved one not die,
And may Bakay not see this grief!
Ah, have I, Bakay,
Eaten the flesh of the mythical zymyryk bird?
Parted from my champions,
Did you decree long ago
That I should burn to death?
In old age, have I, Bakay,
Fallen into a clamor unlike all others?
Did I drink the water of immortality?
Wretched soul who has strayed so often,
Did you eat the raven’s eye?
At every time and in every age,
My beloved Creator,
Did you decree that Bakay should wither and die?
My gentle gray-mottled falcon,
My Morning Star, my gray hawk,
My one plane tree before me,
The misty tower above my head,
My one leopard, do not die;
May this frail soul never see such grief!
While fortune was upon my head,
I, saintly old Bakay,
Was known as a hero.
