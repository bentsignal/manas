Let kinship be sacrificed,
I have no one to look to but those two.
One day it shows its face,
Another day it shows its flesh—
Such is this filthy world.
I thought she was my good mother,
But she was a shaman mother.
Her speech is plain when she speaks,
Her eyes see beyond this world;
She was my shaman mother.
You were good, dear mother...
But you began your wickedness,
Abandoning to ruin your people,
The many Kyrgyz of Ala-Too.
Knowing and seeing through divination,
You reached your desire long ago.
To the city of Khan Karakan,
You fled, wandering in disgrace;
For this you went roaming away.
You settled on a sheer cliff.
Branded a sorceress,
You earned an evil name.
My gray-wolf mother Kanykey,
Your great knowledge brought you good.
Our honor has departed—
No such tale has ever been heard.
“The child sucked my breast,” you say,
“He sucked it far too hard,” you say,
And make an omen of it,
You wretched, shameless mother,
Creating such a commotion.
Oh child, who can know whether
The baby in the cradle will become a lord?
Who can know whether
The child in the womb will become a khan?
Would the son of this khanly prince
Ever fear a clash of arms?
Child, would the son of a noble prince
Ever be afraid of a child?
Shame, mother, shame—let all hear!
This troublesome, ill-fated boy,
While still too young to grow,
Let us put him to death.
Let us crush his belly beneath our feet,
And renounce all kinship
With this wretched boy.
Let us pour sand into his mouth;
If our hands cannot kill the child,
Let us destroy him utterly,
Make all his days dark,
And find some way to do it.
Then the affairs of the age will prosper,
And such crookedness will vanish.”
Saying these words then,
I sharpened my black tongue
And spoke out before the khan.
“She is only a woman,” they said,
“From time immemorial,
A woman’s deeds have been flawed,” they said,
And hobbled my thoughts short.
“They have become enemies,” they said.
Why should my wretched, dishonored mother
Regard as an enemy
A child still in the womb?
I have no steed but my gray horse,
And apart from Külchoro and Kanchoro,
I have no other kin.
When I stoop, I have no saddle,
And to accompany and support me,
I have no nephew born of an elder sister.
I have no elder brother to uphold me,
No maternal uncle to lean upon,
No younger brother to be my companion.
While Külchoro and Kanchoro,
Those two with rhyming names, remain,
I have no brothers but those two,
And without them I have no life.
What lives today is gone tomorrow;
My heart is aflame, my breast is embers,
All for the two boys with matching names.
