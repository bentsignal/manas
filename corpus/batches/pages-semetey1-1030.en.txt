Understand my babbling words;
My fortunate elder brother, heed what I say.
Silver-eyed and sun-faced,
My thundering elder brother;
Golden-eyed and moon-faced,
My dear lordly brother—
Before us lies this water,
And here is a manifest enemy.
Let us not be swept away by chance,
Overtaken suddenly by death,
And perish in destruction.
Precious lives that did not die by an enemy—
Let us not lose them to speechless water!
My dear khan and elder brother,
Let us line up the fourteen horses;
Along this immoderately rushing river,
Let us search apart for a crossing,
Find a shallow channel,
And somehow make our way across."
When Kanchoro had said this,
His lord's anger rose;
He began a tumult
And nearly swallowed Choro
Together with his horse:
"Damn you, Kanchoro!
Is it right to fear death?
In a world where none lives forever,
Can anyone have a spine of iron?
You fear the rushing water;
If ten thousand human enemies appeared,
How could you not fear them?
You fear the swollen water;
When spear points sway,
When human heads roll,
If sixty human enemies appeared,
How could you not fear them?
You have feared the floodwater;
May God strike you!
Does the angel Gabriel,
Thinking, 'These men will cross the water,'
Spread out his five fingers,
Ready his knife and loom there,
Watching from the farther bank?
You fear the rushing water;
Do not babble, you rascal—
Turn back and go away!
Will thirty brides and thirty maidens,
With your sister-in-law among them,
Not say, 'White divers have come,'
And devour us without mercy?
Will they not say, 'Caravan men have come,'
And torment us with mockery?
Will they not sing their taunting song,
And will brave, manly warriors
Not suffer disgrace from shame?
May God strike those maidens—
Will they not mimic us endlessly?
Will they not recognize our cowardice
And laugh at us in scorn?
'Kulchoro was a falcon;
He entered the water straight on,' they will say.
'Those men did not dare remain;
They wandered seeking a ford,' they will say.
'Kulchoro was a hero;
He rode directly into the water,' they will say.
'The caravan man with an aching head
Wandered seeking a ford,' they will say.
Will they not say, 'Weaklings have come,'
And devour us with shrill derision?"
Saying this in fury,
He hemmed in the horses;
Without stopping, making the blows crack,
He began beating the horses.
The bloodthirsty man unleashed his rage;
Your sovereign utterly beat
The fourteen horses from Talas
And left them slaughtered.
