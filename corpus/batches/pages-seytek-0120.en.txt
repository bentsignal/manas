On distant journeys, our road will be one;
When battle begins, our arms will be one.
My noble uncle Külchoro,
Falcon against the many-shaped foe;
My noble father’s hero,
Foremost among my champions—
If I do not bind my falcon, my lionhearted uncle,
Close beside me at my waist;
Then make Keng-Kol our frontier
And set all before us ablaze;
Smash their ranks and bring them suffering,
And, if true fate comes, fall to a bullet;
If I do not grapple with them and wreak slaughter;
If I do not rescue and reunite
Old Bakai, left to me by my forefather,
And my poor mother Kanykei;
If I do not restore my noble sister-in-law Akbala
And give her back to my uncle!
If I do not gather without fail
The scattered Nogai and countless Kyrgyz;
Bring back all that was lost,
And make good all that is lacking;
Place my golden crown
Upon my long-imprisoned uncle;
Subdue the whole world
And proclaim it to the people;
If I do not raise my uncle as khan
Over every last one of those dogs!
If I do not make him a fortress upon the black earth,
A lord who governs the people,
A blessed and sacred man,
A khan before all who behold him;
If I do not make my heroic uncle Külchoro
My sovereign!
If I do not breach the enemy’s bounds
And wreak immeasurable slaughter;
If I do not grapple with treacherous Kanchoro
And gouge out his eyes;
If I do not hunt down
And torment all who became our enemies,
How could I remain a fugitive?
A man is born and slaughters sheep,
Heroes feast and slaughter for celebrations,
The people cry aloud and give him a name;
A prince’s only son
Cannot pass his days a captive.
The offspring of a noble prince
Cannot wander in flight like this.
When I stiffen in wrath,
I shall destroy the hostile foe.
My precious uncle Külchoro,
The inheritance left by my heroic father—
If I do not bind him close beside me;
If I do not earn the name “son of the formidable one,”
And feed the hostile enemy’s countless herds
To the people in Talas;
If I do not raid caravans and seize their trains,
Contending until I take revenge;
Strike their paradise and seize their migrations,
Fighting until I take revenge;
I have stiffened in wrath—
May I perish if I do not wage bloody war this year.
If the enemy provokes my rage,
May I perish if I do not take his head by the sword.
If I do not plant the banner with a shout
And turn my warhorse’s head toward battle;
If an enemy appears before me
And I do not destroy them all at once;
If I do not take up a sword bare of sheath
And spread slaughter through the age;
If I do not crush my enemies
And take their heads this year,
May I die unworthy of the name Seytek,
May I sink away as though never born.
Confidant of my formidable father,
Companion of the prince,
Their sheep shared the same fold,
Their food shared among comrades,
Their gains gathered together—
He was Semetey’s own contemporary.
