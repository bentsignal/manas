Kökötöy's vast riches
Were plundered and taken by the Chinese;
That the many Manchus tirelessly
Had begun the spectacle.
Before Chinese as numerous as cow hairs,
We bristled up against them.
Then tiger Manas,
Burning in anger,
Donned Akolpok,
Girded on his gray cartridge belt,
And prayed to the one God.
Saying the people had been ruined,
Bloodthirsty Manas prepared;
Alarmed for the Muslims,
Hero Manas then
Saddled Akkula.
If you ask of Akkula's nature,
It was a true racehorse:
Its splayed hooves were like ravines,
Its forehead bore a white blaze,
Forged like steel.
When its hoof struck the earth,
It was like an overturned basin.
The breadth of its croup
Was like a field where colts raced.
Your hero's Akkula
Was the tiger's very self,
Perfectly formed and swift,
The foal of a yearling mare,
A tulpar among black horses.
It did not falter when ridden into battle,
Nor grow sick when ridden with the baggage train.
They dressed it in all its gear
And mounted the enraged bloodthirsty hero
Upon Akkula.
The spirit of whoever saw him failed;
The gray-wolf lord, the tiger,
Appeared like Mount Opol.
Dressed in a yellow buulum robe,
Kanykey of Khan Manas
Had a black-blazed argamak
And mounted the argamak.
To see Kökötöy's vast riches,
To see the great wealth,
The beautiful lioness Kanykey
Went along
To direct the feast.
Azhybay and Bakay were twin khans;
Almambet and Chubak were twin heroes;
Serek and Syrgak were equal heroes.
All the forty companions were there,
The most skillful spear-thrusters,
The finest battle-axe wielders.
If attacked, they brought ruin upon a people;
Where they swarmed was red fire.
Manas surged forward
And set out for the feast.
Before a gray wolf great as a mountain,
Could a mountain stand firm?
Could any mortal meet and defeat him!
Khan Manas's nature was of another kind:
His brow was high, his venom strong;
He leveled whoever attacked.
Have you heard the tale?
Born in Altay, he grew mighty;
Whoever exchanged fire with him
Was humiliated.
He never stood idle without fighting;
When he heard the news,
The Kakan's khan wept.
The sorcerer who came from Beijing
Judged the hero from his lineage.
When he reached twelve,
He broke a citadel and took booty,
And brought conflict to fortified peoples.
He never watched without drinking blood;
Calling him the bloodthirsty Burut,
The Kalmyk khan wept.
