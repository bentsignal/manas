Would a people with a prince flee?
You old men in white turbans,
All of you have fled;
You resourceful old men,
All of you have fled!"
It was the time when Chynkojo
Surrounded the great Black Zhediger
And drove them before him.
Whoever resisted,
He threw belly-down across a horse
And bound fast.
He inflicted every kind of wrath;
Chynkojo drove away the Zhediger,
Leaving not one behind.
Those fated to die perished;
Those who survived Kojo, son of Shygai,
After the deaths,
Suffered utter humiliation.
Roaring in his fury,
What did dear Kojo do?
He began an upheaval.
Unable to withstand his wrath,
The great Black Zhediger
Were slaughtered and cast down.
He heaped humiliation upon them like sheep;
What did dear Kojo do?
He drove away
Every one of the Zhediger.
Unable to find any recourse,
Many of the Zhediger's great host
Rushed about in panic.
Many more fled,
Trying to protect their lives.
To flee away together,
One called to another, "Come!"
Many went urging one another.
Many cursed their fathers and grandfathers
Without letting Kojo hear.
Many grieved
Without speaking a word.
Many despaired then
Of their lives held in trust.
Many said, "We have suffered humiliation
At the hands of dog Kojo";
Many said, "Semetey slaughtered us;
We are dead."
Many horses then
Bucked and plunged without stopping.
Many went weeping, saying,
"Alas, we shall die without seeing
Our wives and children."
He unleashed an upheaval;
Dear Kojo drove them
Before him under the whip.
Then a khan like Chynkojo
Arose among the Zhediger;
When they saw Chynkojo,
Life left the Zhediger.
Among the Zhediger,
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Among the Zhediger,
Was the most skillful of spearmen;
He subdued the whole world—
That mighty Chynkojo.
White sacred banner and red flag—
The helpless Zhediger marched on
Amid shouting and uproar;
Blue sacred banner and red flag—
They now saw things never seen before
Amid the uproar.
Many could not then
Find any recourse.
"That fool who fled his village
Has truly done this to us,"
Many said, reviling him.

To a narrow pass like a saddle-back;
