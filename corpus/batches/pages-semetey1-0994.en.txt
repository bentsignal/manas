He summoned his soul-companions,
And laid out the stones of the hearth;
Upon the stone hearth
He had the traveling cauldron boil.
He had the camp enclosed all around,
And a hitching post made of bronze
Planted firmly in the ground.
He spread out all their possessions,
Had Buurul stripped of his tack,
And tethered in the breeze.
He had deer-like Buurul
Roll splendidly in the sand,
Then had him tied apart
In fine fashion at that post.
The golden tent with silken cords
He had pitched in a breezy place
For his lord to sleep alone.
He had cushions of fine velvet
Laid thickly upon the ground.
No enemy could draw near
The gray wolf beset by peril:
At the hero's right hand
Lay a silver-cheeked battle-axe,
And at the hero's left side
Hung a gold-covered belt pouch.
The noble champion was worth the world,
His splendor unlike that of other men.
They kept the traveling cauldron boiling,
And spread all their goods around.
Your festive gray wolf
Had the tablecloth spread,
Had every delicacy brought,
And had a yearling lamb and young foal
Slaughtered with a slanting cut.
He had sugar heaped like snow,
And yellow butter piled in mounds.
They gnawed sugar white as snow,
And drank honey as though it were water;
They sliced kazy and ate the crest fat,
And ate bread dipped in honey.
My poor mother Kanykey,
A woman who understood honor,
Thinking, "He will need it on the road,"
Had employed all her wisdom:
Along the broad Talas
She had filly-mares tethered,
And strong liquor prepared.
Saying, "Let my son drink it on the road,"
From the honeyed kumis of those filly-mares
She had distilled this liquor.
With noise, delight, and revelry
They made exceeding merry,
And into the traveler's drinking bowls
They poured it to the brim.
They drank the liquor and grew merry,
Then slept propped upon their elbows,
Dozing briefly and resting,
Lying until their hips grew sore,
And reclining until sleep was satisfied.
They had the thoroughbreds brought in.
The cold dawn wind roared,
The tips of the grass swayed,
And the birds of morning chirped.
The earth seemed to split and open;
All the twilight mist
Scattered toward every quarter.
When one looked upon the face of the world,
It opened bright as a mirror.
Then gray wolf Semetey,
Telling tales for diversion,
That poor hero traveling the road,
Now raised his spyglass to look.
