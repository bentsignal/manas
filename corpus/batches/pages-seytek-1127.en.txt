And in life his eyes were open.
Bilgich was famed among men,
A peerless judge and seer.
He watched Joltoy warily,
Knowing that the tiger Joltoy
Had the valor to crush
Whomever he encountered.
“Ah, Joltoy is a true hero,
Standing proud and pouring out his wrath;
The hour has come to face his foes.”
So saying, Chonbilgich
Spoke thus to his retainers,
Warning them to tend the horses,
And never surrender their honor
Until their noble heads entered the earth:
“He who has no honor has no shame;
The Burut nation is your foe.
We came stalking from the Kakan
To humble the Kyrgyz spirit
And open the road for China.
Do not twitch in irritation;
My warriors, do not stir.”
He said this to his comrades,
Then charged the fearsome Joltoy,
Prepared to die if death had come,
For honor and for his people,
And to answer Joltoy.
Raging, he rushed forward,
And there they clashed.
Akjoltoy was his opponent,
A matchless champion like Mount Kap;
Without fluster or concern,
Even if not Bilgich alone but all Beijing
Shouted and leveled their spears,
He would not reckon them equal to his boot-tip.
His tiger nature showed;
When he saw Chonbilgich,
He laughed in eager delight.
Without fuss, Akjoltoy
Fought with measured force—
Who had ever seen such a deed?
The mountain-like steed Külansur
Halted and turned its head.
Your Joltoy, who came alone from the host,
Reached the feather-grass plain;
No dust rose beneath him
Where the earth itself seemed to speak,
And now the mountain-like Külansur
Was reined in and turned.
Rejoicing at the word “foe,”
Clad entirely in iron,
He chose a steel-tipped spear,
And girded on his naked sword.
Wearing ninety robes of silk,
Hero Joltoy said, “I trust in fate.”
Hero Bilgich was huge as a mountain,
A fortress sprung from Beijing,
A hero of immeasurable might.
Ninety beasts were slaughtered at his feast;
Orphaned of his father at six months,
He was given the name Bilgich.
He had slain head-shaking giants,
And crushed armies on the march,
Proving the lion in his valor;
Throughout Beijing, he brought the lands
Of forty khans beneath a single will.
He became master of Kakanchin
And a giant to the whole world.
His menace was one thing, his venom fierce;
When he roared, even a dark-hearted man
Despaired of life, crying, “I am dead!”
His mustache bristled hair by hair,
His many peoples gathered in one place.
Here was courage, here was the lion;
Akjoltoy was his opponent,
And this was where Bilgich had come.
When he saw the hero Akjoltoy,
He began a savage quarrel,
Raging and roaring,
As though to crush heaven and earth,
