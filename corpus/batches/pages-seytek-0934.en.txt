The souls of the living burned with anguish,
And whoever looked upon that age
Saw its falseness revealed,
All scattered in confusion,
Crushing without respite
Those they had marked for their ranks,
Engaged in riotous slaughter.
Many a splendid warhorse,
Wandered riderless and bare of saddle.
Many a fleet courser,
Neighed while dragging its reins,
Its riderless heart grieving.
It was a dreadful, mighty conflict,
Many among the people witnessed it,
And many mortals lay dying.
All the warriors trembled,
The people cried out one by one,
Thrusting and fencing with spears,
They kept slaughtering one another,
The great conflict tightened its coils,
Becoming a chaos beyond understanding,
As they continued their strife,
A terrible massacre took place.
When Sarybay’s troops saw brave Seytek’s bearing,
They surrendered,
Some heroes after others,
Their knees buckling,
Ran after him and bowed,
Taking reins knotted with loops,
And hanging them around their necks,
While one man from another
Contended to offer him a warhorse.
Hurrying and trotting forward,
They came as envoys,
Submitting to the hero,
And vying to offer livestock.
They began according to ancient custom,
And thus brought the fighting
And conflict to an end.
The two armies that had clashed
Made peace that day,
They began speaking to one another,
Gathered their wits and opened counsel,
And began their sorrowful plea:
“Because of what has happened,
Because of Sarybay,
We became an army tens of thousands strong,
And the whole world was stirred.
Sarybay drove us here,
Though he had neither rope nor cord,
Sarybay brought us here in bonds.
He unleashed his terrible wrath,
And even before Saikal’s many daughters-in-law
Could look back toward their village,
We suffered misery and innocent deaths,
Though our livestock had grazed freely,
And men despaired of life.
The cause of their despair
Was an army vast as a hearth-fire,
Which devastated the people without respite.
A tyrant named Sarybay arose,
Gathering the people and mustering an army,
Amassing the entire host in abundance.
When his khan gave the command,
He stiffened and poured out his fury,
And while he stood enraged,
Could the Saruu voice any grievance?
When your lord bellows from his throne,
Is there a horse among the people that a camel could stop?
Not a single head of livestock remained in the pens,
Nor strength remained to turn and flee,
Not a shod yearling remained,
Not a living soul among the born remained.
He began such outrages,
And anyone who resisted
He slaughtered without leaving one alive.
He committed notorious atrocities,
And brought every mortal low.
Seizing his moment, he scattered us,
Raging and shouting,
