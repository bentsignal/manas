Of the great quarrel with Sarybai,
She began the epic, revealing its secrets.
The vast ordeal, the deep conflict—
That wondrous, mighty epic,
She told it all.
When God granted her wish,
The desire and purpose she had sought,
When God freely granted it,
She drew the reins of fleet-footed Jeljetpes
And turned his head.
The one she would grapple with was Sarybai,
Such was the savage enemy.
Gentle in nature and broad of heart,
Such was the champion maiden’s way.
She turned the horse’s head,
And of all the saintly heroes—
Lion Bakai and Kanykei,
And heroes such as these—
She now asked their blessing.
“Toward sunrise with banners streaming
Lay the road I myself once rode.
That infidel son of the ogre
This hand has speared seven times.
He split lungs and severed heads,
Stirred strife and spilled blood;
He was an enemy bearing a blood feud.
Let me wage the battle freely;
For the common folk, for the people,
Against the savage foe Sarybai,
Let me clash in combat.
My lion father, khan Bakai,
My aged mother Kanykei,
If you give your blessing, let me receive it.
For the common folk, for the people,
Let me wage the conflict fiercely.
If true death reaches me, let me die;
If what Creator Allah has ordained
Falls upon my brow, let me face it.
For the common folk, for the people,
For the lost magnanimous lion,
For my lion brother-in-law Külchoro,
Above all, for you,
For the countless downcast host,
For the blue-maned boy Seytek,
Let me face the true decree;
If true death reaches me, let me die!
For all the heroes, for you,
Let me sooner behold
The true realm whence none return.
If I am granted days of honor and success,
Let me grapple with Sarybai, whom I hunt,
And spill his blood.
Against the enemy who brought my father calamity,
Let me strike without mercy.
From the enemy who oppresses us,
Let me avenge six generations of my fathers.
If I find the means, let me plunge the villain
Into grievous misery.
If the Creator does not will it,
Let me die at the infidel’s hands.
Infidel and Muslim alike—
How many people have been beset by war?
The one I shall grapple with is Sarybai;
Such is the infidel’s nature.
God knows who shall die;
God knows whether one will turn back
And return safely from the villain.
His calamities are many, his venom strong;
He lays flat whoever encounters him.
His strength is extraordinary,
And his heroism is manifold;
His venom is potent, his nature harsh—
Such is the grievous work
Of this blazing Sarybai.
Once my bloodthirsty, blue-maned father-in-law
Was slaughtering the Chinese
At the height of the massacre.
He trampled the whole universe
And cleaved through it.
