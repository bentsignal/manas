Dear Bakai and Külchoro,
Retainers of the bloodthirsty hero.
Heading to Beijing
Beren Semetey, the gray wolf,
Set out from Talas.
The Kyrgyz and Argyn people wept,
And both mothers of the gray wolf wept.
The Argyn and Kyrgyz then,
Raising their palms and wailing,
Prayed that the noble hero be kept safe.
They prayed, sobbing.
The splendid lion, son of Manas,
Wore a coat no bullet could pierce,
And rode a horse no bullet could catch;
He turned Buurul obliquely
And knotted his horsehair tail tight.
Buurul was the steed beneath him,
And he had the strength to strike Beijing,
Which no raider could touch, alone!
Akolpok shines on his back,
When set to shooting, the heirloom
Akelte shines on the back,
The sight arrested the heart;
Half bronze and half gold,
A gray pouch is visible on the waist,
The white sighthound barked behind him,
And Akshumkar cried out upon his hand.
A golden-cheeked battle-axe,
Forged of steel and sharpened keen,
Its handle wound about with yellow gold,
Kakanchyn's Beijing
To strike in the battle for Beijing,
Had been repaired by smith Bölökbai.
If you ask about the sword,
The six-edged blade fallen from heaven,
Zulfiqar, left as a gift
By valiant Manas, the gray wolf.
Zulfiqar rang in its sheath;
Semetei who took the sword
Stood ringing like Opol-Too.
Standing erect and untroubled,
He reckoned the Black Chinese he would fight,
And the thought that he might finally die,
As less than the hem of his robe!
Heroism and leonine courage
The Almighty Allah
Had granted the lion's son
Without measure.
Heading towards Beijing
Riding hard and covering much ground,
Traveling not a little but far,
Crossing rounded, arching passes,
Traversing many kinds of country,
Past straight-flowing springs and clear water,
He journeyed without stopping or resting,
Riding hard and pressing far,
Wearying horses and scouting the land,
Running hounds and flying birds—
Your young son of the Bloodthirsty One
Was journeying toward Kakan.
He crossed rugged passes,
Passed over many turning uplands,
And skirted the mountain foothills;
Your gray wolf journeyed on,
Roaming through Ala-Too,
Crossing Kötörmö,
And traversing broad Suusamyr.
By the mountain of Kyz-Art,
For his coming battle with Great Beijing,
Your gray wolf had
All the fine thoroughbreds
Shod with steel.
Along deserted Elkin-Too,
He skirted Elkin-Too;
He bent down and looked
A blue lake appeared,
And the many ducks resting on the lake
Scattered when a falcon struck.
