Let me grapple and test them!
While my ocean-like head remains,
How can I give up my equal companion?
The swine are surging;
Let me fight them alone and test myself!”
Your prince grieved in this way,
Put on the bulletproof armor,
Readied himself toward the foe,
And prepared his weapons.
The blood-drinking gray wolf’s
Wrath spread across his face.
The gray kise he had inherited
From his khan father, the bloodletter,
The bloodthirsty champion buckled on—
He who unleashed havoc,
Who carried off women.
At doom-struck Khojo
He glowered in wrath.
Making ready with the gray kise,
Pleading with the Creator,
When the wondrous thing was slung on,
It was a mirror upon the shoulder;
When borne into clashing battle,
It was comfort for the entrusted life.
Its report had been measured and judged;
Nothing measured could match its form.
In sport it was Ochogor,
In earnest it was Koichagyr—
This was the sharp weapon inherited
From noble Lord Manas.
Its keenness was proven among the people,
An inheritance remaining
From the poor prince and sultan.
Golden collar and copper sleeves,
Double ring mail with lark-like eyes,
Netted outer coat and blue jacket—
No bullet pierced it in battle.
Gray-wolf Sultan Semetey
Now put it on over the rest.
The sight stirred the heart;
When he put on his weapons,
Semetey, wolf of battle,
Loomed as large as a house.
He raised the gold-crescent banner,
Raised the cry “Ai, Manas!”
And your prince surged from the palace,
Wearing the bulletproof armor,
Gathering himself like a lion.
Like a cloud about to rain again,
Wrath covered his face;
His breadth was an armful,
His arms and armor fastened on.
The sight stirred every heart;
Venom poured from his mouth.
He readied himself like a hawk,
And received wisdom from the unseen.
Looking to either side,
As he prepared to enter battle,
He licked his lips and nose.
The sight stirred the heart:
Külchoro, son of the gray wolf,
Appeared with Aktelki.
Külchoro came out in high spirits,
Met Semetey,
And stood as his support.
Beyond him came
Kanchoro, born to surpass
His father, lion Chubak,
Wearing over himself a fur coat
Of blue otter skin;
If an army of six thousand came,
He attacked without sparing himself.
He never turned back from gunfire,
Never fled in fear of death.
Köktelki of Khan Köbösh,
His stallion for holy war,
Came as his support.
