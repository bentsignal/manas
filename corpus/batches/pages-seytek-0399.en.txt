When he was still an infant,
She had the spirits carefully guard him,
And had costly crimson cloth
Laid over him in splendor.
She appointed sixty blood-shedding champions
To shed blood in his defense,
To rout whoever attacked,
And destroy whoever caused loss.
She set young women to guard him,
And slaughtered barren mares and young camels
To provide for his nourishment.
She had Margalan and Kokon searched,
And red raisins and sweet foods
Brought from every city.
Tender lamb and savory head-meat
She had simmered into broth.
Until you reached the age of seven,
She had the young women taste your food
And carefully prepare each meal,
Making every limb of yours whole,
Leading you with her until you became a man.
Across countless roads of no return,
She had the routes reconnoitered,
And horses brought from afar.
She had you thrust spears at the enemy,
Drag your spear through their ranks,
And learn the enemy’s ways.
She made your spirit mighty
And made you sultan and khan of the Argyn.
While your mighty uncle lived,
You became a man of weighty renown;
You kept a royal court and became khan.
You became a worthy hero,
Sound in all your twelve limbs.
While you had such an uncle,
When did you ever lack anything?
Ruling the people and maintaining the realm,
You became a seasoned lion.
As for the remaining Kanchoro,
You anointed yourself with bloody fat,
To remove the wretch
From this world forever.
“Do not let him stir up strife through the ages;
Finish him and be done with it.
Champion of the holy old man,
My comrade, lion Bakai,
Make the child disappear,
And turn him into black earth.
Jamgyrchy of the Eshetek,
Kutunu, the very eldest,
And round-eyed Sarkangan,”—
When the stars shone bright,
At the hour when the people slept,
They took these men as envoys
And called upon the people to mediate.
“This child is my darling,” they said,
And pulled him from my hands.
When God was about to punish
My crazed, unruly mother’s intent,
The wretch’s purpose went astray.
Fearing she would harm the child,
They saved him from brutal torment
And pulled him from my hands.
They did not rightly heed my words,
But regarded me as an enemy
And had him taken from my hands.
They had him securely confined in a palace
Where not even a crow could fly,
Whose walls were guarded by lions,
And which even spirits could not cross.
From a city of Hindustan,
The mother of seventy sons,
The sovereign among women,
The noble matron Adalsur—
They summoned Adalsur.
They hung an amulet around his neck,
And entrusted him to Adalsur,
The holy old man’s Adalsur.
She set his crooked affairs right
And raised him with tender indulgence.
