On the banks of broad Talas,
Below Tört-Kül,
In the garden once planted
By the gray wolf Prince Manas,
The son inherited from his father
The throne upon which he sat,
And his palace was of gold.
When they saw Aichürök,
At the edge of the open steppe,
Near the seer’s dark cave,
Before the golden throne,
The people swarmed like teeming worms
And gathered together.
Some wore wound turbans,
And prayed, crying, “My God!”
They had full, ripened grain
And herds of all four kinds.
They wore mottled robes,
And did not roam about wrestling.
They crowded in from every side,
Their blue felt caps trampled underfoot.
From their encampments their livestock covered the green,
And the people came in mounted companies.
People of every kind arrived.
The reason the people came was this:
Sarybai had been consigned to the fire,
And the long-suffering people
Were delivered on that day.
Their moonlike faces brightened,
Their pounding hearts grew calm,
And true contentment filled them;
Their faces truly brightened.
The stored and guarded treasury,
To every one of the people,
Because fierce Sarybai was dead,
They scattered without withholding a thing.
Dead fires were kindled anew,
The dying returned to life,
The severed were joined,
And the scattered were gathered.
Burning hearts grew calm,
Furrowed brows relaxed,
Their painful ailments subsided,
And the people’s spirits rose.
Multitudes of every kind arrived,
Many coming from every tribe.
They devised wonders beyond imagining,
As though surpassing the moonlit world.
The great feast they held then
Seemed to cover the face of the earth.
They heaped up glowing red embers,
And the encamped people voiced their grievances.
Those who knew and those who had seen
Heard it from long-eared elders;
From all these countless people,
A great assembly formed,
And the uproar split the sky.
Dust flew into the heavens
As the people gathered in throngs.
It became a most joyous feast,
And the grateful people could not rejoice enough.
“When Sarybai held us captive,
Unable to stir from one place,
We endured six or seven months;
We suffered exceedingly,
All because of Sarybai,” they said.
From Sarybai’s venom
Red-tailed riding camels died,
And the finest red-necked champions,
Souls whose like could not be found, died.
He fought to possess this Talas;
Many men and many lions
He drove away, estranged from their people.
He extinguished the burning fire
And carried off the plunder in one night;
Thus he brought the people to ruin.
He made mankind grieve,
Made every back stoop,
And made dear eyes bulge with anguish.
