My elder, wise old Bakay,
My saint left above all others
By my father Sultan Manas!
My father's confidant,
The eternal companion
Of my lion father, the hero.
They shared death and shared life,
Companions gathered in this world,
Faithful companions sharing death.
When scouting, they shared one road;
Entering battle, they shared one army.
Lion Sultan Bakay—
I choose him and send him on the road.
Great Kalmyk Khan Kongurbay—
If Almighty Allah supports me,
I will raid his horses.
Yesterday, while my lion father lived,
While the falcons remained safe,
Butcher-prince Bakay—
Was he not the uncle who went there?
Calamity upon the khan—
Was he not the uncle who brought it?
Gathering tribute from Beijing,
Was it not Bakay who took it?
The gloomy city of Tüp-Beijing—
Was it not Bakay who besieged it?
Of Beijing's Manchu Chinese,
Their strong men in armored coats,
Their bald men who girded stones—
Was it not Bakay who beat them?
Riding a courser and grasping the banner,
Was it not Bakay who went forth?
Was it not Bakay who fought six months without cease
And drove them before him
While lion Manas lived?
Was it not Bakay who brought
True slaughter upon the khan,
In the days of true Manas,
The golden throne ridden by Chylava—
Truly entering Beijing,
Was it not Bakay who seized it?
Armor that no bullet could pierce—
Was it not Bakay who wore it?
The teeming infidel,
Warrior Khan Kongurbay—
Was it not Bakay who surveyed their land
And traveled knowing it well?
The road to Beijing has five branches;
Marking and distinguishing them all,
Was it not Bakay who knew them?
The blue courser's head—
Was it not Bakay who once turned it?
Thrusting his spear into the many foes,
Was it not Bakay who stood?
Shattering those who opposed him,
Was it not Bakay who slaughtered them?
When bows poured arrows like rain
And guns fired bullets like hail,
Was it not Bakay who stood unmoving
Beneath the banner?
Beijing's daughter as tribute—
Was it not Bakay who took her?
While his dear Manas lived,
Slaughter upon those who challenged them—
Was it not Bakay who brought it?
My uncle Bakay, my falcon,
The inheritance left by Manas;
The one I will fight is Kakan-Chyn,
The one I will confront is Bakburchun.
My heroic uncle Bakay—
If I make him my renowned companion,
Kongurbay is indeed a hero;
I will face him, fight six months,
And bring conflict upon that swine.
The road to Beijing has five defiles;
My renowned uncle Bakay
Will lead me straight against the enemy.
Great Beijing, ancient people,
