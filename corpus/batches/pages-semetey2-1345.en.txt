They fired bullets into the palace city,
Becoming enemies of the bloodthirsty khan.
This infidel Kanchoro
Was the foster son of that bloodthirsty lord;
They had kicked together like colt and yearling,
And quarreled as they sucked the same breast.
Behold that kinship!
Behold the treachery of the choro
Who turned against his kinsman like an enemy
And died corrupted!
From the top of the tower,
Poor Aychürök, the woman,
Her proud dark head bowed,
Her bloodied face torn open,
Burning and blazing into embers,
Turned by grief into fire,
Her knees bending to the ground,
Shrieking, weeping, and screaming,
Burst in upon true Kanykey,
Wailing and sobbing.
When she saw Aychürök’s condition,
Her grandmother Chyyyrdy,
That elder wife, understood
That her one cherished man had died.
Thinking, “How can I live bereft of my only one?
I will drive a knife into myself,”
Within a white saddlebag,
Bought for six thousand camels and a thousand colts,
Was the white blade she had purchased.
She seized its handle
And drew it from its sheath.
“It is the pennon fixed to the spear,
A special keepsake left
By magnanimous Manas, the radiant one,” she said.
She shrieked, wept, and grieved,
Bereft of two sons in succession.
“How can I remain now?
Though God may refuse to take me,
I will go in this way today.”
The fine blade, solid steel with a golden hilt,
Was steel through and through;
She grasped and turned it in her hand,
Then plunged the white blade
Into the thick flesh of her living heart.
Chyyyrdy herself
Cut herself open.
Poor Aychürök, the woman,
Saw with her own eyes
That her grandmother had died.
Her mind was pierced and split apart;
Twelve afflictions and a thousand cares
Appeared above her head.
Fire that burns through the grass is terrible;
Worse is Kanchoro sprung from one’s own people,
An enemy gone rabid.
When the cruel enemy surged in
And plundered all beneath them,
When orphan, widow, and aged soul
Were thrown into a dug pit,
“The dogs will take her if we plead;
Before the five bright stars,
Golden rubies in the radiant firmament, have set—
My mother who suckled me on five separate journeys,
Who widened her narrow womb,
Who emptied her stone-hard breasts,
And raised us to maturity—
When they plunder us,
My dearest mother,
If she suddenly disappears,
If by chance she lies unburied,
If Kanchoro sees her,
Shameless before breast milk and salt,
Will he acknowledge my mother?
Will he call her kin to the leopard,
Or an old raving infidel
Who has lived too long,
Drag her along the road,
And hand her to the Dangyt?
