Its tail streamed even as a yearling;
Knowing it would be an heir in hard times,
Your wise mother Kanykei,
That discerning woman, had tested it.
She had it covered carefully with a blanket
And raised out of the people's sight.
Such was the gray five-year-old's story.
When the gray-maned hero vanished,
Kanchoro seized Külchoro
And gouged his right shoulder.
When sixty women and widowed sisters-in-law
Were violently plundered,
The hero Kutu of the Zhediger,
A man of honest heart,
Without letting any person know,
Without letting anyone touch it,
Without letting anyone consume it,
Without letting it be called another's booty,
Dressed Karadöö in the bulletproof mail
The gray steed no bullet could reach,
He mounted Karadöö upon it.
Thus the hero Kutu took him in,
Named him Karadöö,
And raised him.
Look upon Karadöö!
If you ask about Karadöö,
He was the very son of the hero Kutu.
There is much that could be told;
Within Karadöö
Were words as vast as Alaskar Mountain.
If you ask about Karadöö,
Common iron is not keen steel,
And a falcon raised elsewhere is no true kinsman.
If you ask about Karagul,
Ambush lay within his words,
And mischief moved in his eyes.
Malice moved in his eyes,
Crookedness dwelt within him,
And his gaze was askew.
If Karadöö found the opportunity,
He meant to strike the hero Kyyaz himself.
Then Karadöö
Girded on a bare, sheathless sword;
Like a cloud returning with rain,
Wrath spread across his face.
Thick-wristed and stone-hearted,
That perfectly built champion
Raised his cavernous spear
High overhead.
When he drove it home,
He crushed a man into ground the size of a sheep;
When he charged in close,
He never opened his armpit in fear.
His stratagems were stern, his venom strong;
He laid flat anyone who challenged him.
His youth was extraordinary,
His courage immense—
Ask no more about Karadöö!
His tread raised dust;
His voice drove life from men.
When he advanced he scattered nations;
His swooping was like an eagle's.
Look upon his gray-maned valor:
It was like the very eye of Kökchö.
Karadöö, a mass of calamity,
Was just like Semetey himself.
His early word had come late:
Against Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
He now stood ready to meet him,
Start the conflict,
Match him and take revenge.
If his strength sufficed, he would fight;
Karadöö's purpose, his heart's desire,
Was to take his distant vengeance.
He mourned Semetey,
Would make war upon the hero Kyyaz,
