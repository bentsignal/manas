We used to set up ninety mattocks,
And test our strength with thunderous force,
Plunging into the tumult to seize them;
We would enter contests
Of endless, thrilling uproar.
While your great champion lived,
We reveled in games to our hearts’ content;
Birch trees growing on the open plain
We tore up by the roots.
We used to play kökbörü with a camel;
While the princely gray wolf lived,
Whenever roused, we went to war
And tested our strength.
The mountain ibex of Daңduu-Bash,
The cliff goat with its thick hide,
We would put into the tanning vat.
Before my fortunate mother,
Standing sole to sole and pulling,
We tore them apart piece by piece;
Our muscles were each as large as a fist—
Such was our former strength.
We held feasts and revelries,
Drinking kumis and courting girls,
Playing orompoi and winning prizes;
Such boundless games we played.
In the safe days of happiness,
While the joyous gray wolf lived,
When the multitude camped in delight,
Their cares forgotten,
With pleasure by day and games by night,
In those flourishing times,
When we had drunk our fill of kumis,
Red-dressed and slender-waisted,
The most graceful beauties—
When yesterday the girls and young wives watched us,
If bored, we went hunting roe deer;
Through ninety mattocks in a row
We shot our arrows,
And plunged into such delights.
When your vigor is still sound,
Then enter battle;
If your strength is whole,
Then fall upon the enemy,
And learn the measure of your prowess.
Take only what your power permits,
And exert your warrior’s strength.
Now put your vigor and might
Fully to the test.
Show courage and test your strength;
Test every bit of your former might.
Then Külchoro said:
“While the hero still lived,
When all my strength was at its height,
When I scorched sand and set stone aflame,
In the days when I raged,
Destroying lives and spilling blood,
In the days when I thirsted for battle,
When I grasped my sword and took up my spear,
And laid the enemy low,
Subduing all who faced me
And driving them before me,
When I sharpened
My red, Zulfiqar-like sword,
And crushed the hostile foe,
Chewing them to pieces,
When I spread tumult wherever I went
And threw all who resisted into panic,
When I wore armor
No flame could pierce to my flesh,
When I subdued the foes who shot at me
And drove them round—
Your uncle was strong as a lion;
Your poor uncle Choro
Had every power needed for combat.
While my lionhearted uncle lived,
We brandished battle-axes
And stood testing our strength;
When would we ever strike from afar?
Taking matchlock and bow in hand,
We tested our strength side by side,
