When mortal men
Beheld him, their minds reeled.
A prince among humankind,
Choice among many lions,
The finest of living souls,
Perfect in wisdom and strong in might,
He leveled all who grappled with him.
Those who faced and leveled spears at him
Saw their age brought to ruin.
Those who crossed blades with him
Were uprooted entirely.
His lionheartedness was renowned;
When word of his wrath was heard,
Chylaba, khan of the Khakhan,
Wondered how he could wage war,
And the lord of the Black Chinese
Despaired of his dear life.
He raised piercing cries and tumult,
Bringing strife upon all he encountered.
Chubak, son of Khan Balta,
Appeared just as in life
And joined them as a comrade,
Raising a mighty shout.
He became a companion to Kuyaly, mighty Külchoro,
And gray-maned Seytek,
Joining all three.
Their opponent was Sarybay,
A fierce enemy such as this.
A dreadful and oppressive deed arose,
All who confronted him were leveled,
His weapons numbered eighty-four,
And wherever he advanced became fire.
In their battle frenzy, the heroes
Loaded these three:
A heavy matchlock, a musket, and a long gun.
The black earth did not overturn,
But halted beneath their multitude.
Their adversary was Sarybay,
A calamitous enemy such as this.
The gray-maned heroes were filled with ancestral spirit;
At the sight, the mind reeled.
Lofty in form, their eyes were embers,
They loomed like high blue mountains;
When the fierce champions moved,
They seemed able to conquer every age.
When the lions grew angry,
They seemed able to drive down and swallow
Not only Sarybay, but the entire world.
Such were their form and appearance.
Their majesty was great, their wrath was strong;
They loomed like that towering mountain.
Warriors mighty in wrestling,
Heroes who knew the enemy’s ways,
The three stayed united and did not scatter.
Against the infidel son Sarybay,
Not sparing their threadlike lives,
They began their fury in earnest.
That precious life needed divine favor;
The fierce champions, without the least regard,
Bound it by a thread and cast it aside.
They shouldered their fine, sturdy long guns,
Their jezails upon them,
And prayed to the Creator.
Invoking Babedin, they gave their blessing,
And vowed a moon-hoofed horse
To the forty saints and Kydyr for Kyyaz.
The heroic companions and brave Seytek
Still dwelt in Talas.
The lineage of Manas was not yet spent;
May they not break and scatter the banner,
Nor turn the eyes of the surging lion’s descendants
Toward the torments of hell.
Do not turn the ravine ridge into their road;
Do not let the princely children of Manas
Be disgraced by the wrathful.
When the time comes for their road to open,
They will destroy the hostile enemy,
When God grants the hour,
And it was said their destiny would be
Like the stars in the sky.
