I do not even have a wound to show.
Unable to match Toltoi’s strength,
Was I to return crestfallen?
So this is what the filthy world is.
Wishing to die but unable,
A strange ordeal befell my head;
I could not take my own life.
How can I go without shame
To my mountain-like uncle
And say, ‘Toltoi escaped’?
Speaking thus in this way,
His heart was grievously torn;
Unable to cease his sorrow,
What could dear Choro do?
Tears poured from his eyes.
Perhaps I should take
The gold-cheeked battle-axe now,
Smash my own head,
And leave myself wounded.
I could reach my uncle moaning,
Saying, ‘Toltoi struck me without mercy!’
When I come groaning in pain,
Would that valley-spring, sheer cliff,
Learn by chance that I struck myself
With the axe?
That double-wiled shrew
Was a wondrous servant woman,
One of the overbearing ones,
A person of sacred power.
She knew today
The suffering that would come in six months.
Rather than that, let me go to my uncle
And tell the truth,
Saying, ‘The hardship happened this way.’
Like a prince sunk in sorrow,
Young Külchoro grieved.
As though his khan father, lion Almambet,
Had died,
Unable to cease his lament,
He turned back toward Afghanistan;
Having let Khan Toltoi escape,
Your young man returned grieving.
His heart remained torn apart;
A spirit descended upon the orphan,
And he looked as large as Mount Opol.
Beneath him, the great Buurul
Pounded onward with a rolling gait,
Loping in great bounds.
When he drew near the city,
A star separated in the sky;
Dawn loomed into view,
Clouds scattered in the heavens,
And the face of dawn opened.
As dawn drove back the dark,
And hazy light touched the earth,
The cold wind of morning whistled,
Young larks chirped,
The face of the steppe bent in light,
Lines appeared upon the sand.
The earth appeared and dawn broke;
When he drew near the city,
A gaunt figure came into view.
When he saw that figure,
Lion prince Külchoro’s
Heart was grievously torn.
Your gray-wolf uncle Semetey,
Unable to calm or endure his heart,
Appeared coming to meet him.
“My twin lamb, my matching half,
Parted from my twin,
How can I go on now?

Over the twelve-year-old child,
