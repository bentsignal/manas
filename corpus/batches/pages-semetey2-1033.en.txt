I have come to launch an attack!
Yesterday, while that bloodthirsty leopard lived,
He stirred every conflict he knew;
Leaving not a single man behind,
He gathered hosts in their thousands.
Driving them onward to Kakan,
How many did he slaughter?
Had my father not gone,
Bound in humiliation and driven with the army,
Had he not gone there,
To conquer Beijing,
To bring calamity upon Beijing,
Rousing the nation and driving forth the host,
Driving such an immense army,
Humiliating some,
Pouring out his wrath and oppressing them,
Blunting their white-steel blades,
Leading all the giants and champions
Until they perished—
He began that dreadful tumult.
Driving all those mighty warriors
Deep into China,
He left them slaughtered there.
Agitated, seeking the spirit
Of my father who truly died,
Raging as I demand his blood-price,
I shall cry out and level my spear;
I shall slaughter many of you,
And stand against you in fury.
Our khans who deem themselves mighty,
Our many young and old—
Valuing our fleeting lives not even as a hair,
Today we shall all be slain!
Heed what has been said.
Do not quarrel or raise strife,
Do not fall into frantic confusion,
Do not cause an empty uproar
And fight like the Chinese.
Before the people are ruined and heroes die,
Before survivors can no longer endure life,
Heed these words:
Pay the blood-price of my father, Khan Köksö!
When he rode against teeming Beijing
With his spear leveled straight,
When the day was ending in a storm of bullets
And battle raged,
He found the suffering unbearable.
It was for this that my noble father
Entered the fray against Kakan,
And for this cause he died.
How would he ever have suffered such wrath
Had Khan Manas not unleashed his anger,
Had he not driven my father
To that Beijing?
How else would he have suffered such wrath?
When would my father, the khan and sovereign,
Have died, leaving his children orphans
And his wife a widow—
When else would my valiant father
Have died?
Heed these words of mine;
As recompense for that father,
Give me as much as I desire!
Without fighting or quarreling,
Without throwing the whole country into commotion,
Without throwing all the people into turmoil,
Heed what I have said;
Saying, ‘Such is the blood-price,’
Pay the price for my father the khan!
Give countless dark-brown camels,
And bales of robes of honor.
Upon the gelded camels
Load red gold and crimson silk,
Saying, ‘Take them without contention,’
As the price for my mighty father.
If Semetey will not fight,
Let him endure this great demand.
Let him give countless bald-faced mares;
