“He was a deep-dug defensive trench.
Against the enemy he faced,
He was a host of sixty thousand dark-clad warriors.
He was my shelter and support,
A lion who never grieved.
Almighty God, protect him”—
He spread his palms solemnly,
And cried, “Amen!”
The hero opened his hands in prayer.
Crying loudly, “O God!”
A gray prayer-sash descended from heaven
And settled around his neck.
Turning his face toward the qibla,
He raised his hands, crying, “Almighty!”
And your valiant hero gave his blessing.
Receiving his elder’s blessing,
Külchoro, protected by the spirits,
Set forth toward Sarybai,
Ready to die if fate had come,
To test himself against formidable Sarybai,
The infidel in the fullness of his strength,
The hero of abundant courage,
And meet him face-to-face.
If the true God so willed,
He would surrender his fly-like soul.
He took a deep-blue steel spear,
And donned armor of blue iron,
Buttoning it along his entire body,
The precious armor made by Kanykei,
Wrapping it snugly around his frame.
He pressed onto his head
A helmet no axe could pierce.
Crossing its ties,
He knotted them beneath his throat.
The kandagai worn against bullets
Was such that no bullet could pierce it.
A marksman who could shoot
Anything born into this world
Had been summoned and sent by your mother.
He smoothed the horse’s flanks,
For he was both valiant and leonine,
And all his fortunes were complete.
My looming presence at the fore,
Created by God the Sovereign—
Keep watch over the hero!
Külchoro, my falcon,
Was renowned, and was my support.
Born greater than I,
He would plunge into the facing enemy
And cast them into turmoil.
Full of cunning and fierce in wrath,
He was a merciless lion,
A hero protected by the spirits.
Hero Külchoro, my lion,
Was the one Almighty God alone
Had written upon my brow.
His heroism was extraordinary,
His strength beyond measure;
He was both noble and leonine,
Perfect in all twelve limbs.
He was given the name Külchoro;
Surely God alone
Would never create another like him.
His valor was renowned,
His gray-wolf courage plain to see.
We joined when no bigger than thumbs,
United when tiny as lice,
And met when small as ticks.
We encountered each other in childhood;
He was written upon my brow,
And found as my blessing.
This peerless Külchoro,
His gray-wolf courage was unlike any other’s.
We kicked together like colt and yearling,
And quarreled as we suckled at the breast.
I carved him choice fat and fed him;
I had this boy suckle
At my mother Kanykei’s breast.
To enemies who bore us malice
He was a cauldron of bitter brine,
