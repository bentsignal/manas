In the beginning, your forefather Bakai
Was mighty throughout the whole world;
Yet at another time, mighty Bakai
Was humbled into tending camels.
Spreading his hands and saying, “Amen,”
Dear Bakai collapsed;
Desolating old age
Struck him down and let him rise no more.
These were the lofty honors
Of your aged forefather Bakai:
Though mortal, Bakai’s living spirit endured;
Upon your prophet-like forefather
A golden crown was placed,
And the golden throne left by Manas
Was mounted by Khan Bakai.
Endless fortune and broad pastures
Were inscribed upon the brow
Of an elder such as Bakai.
When Bakai fell,
Your dear mother Kanykei
Sprang to action like a hawk;
The venerable lady herself
Suddenly found a plan.
Arming herself with the jezail,
She appealed to Kuyaly,
The lionhearted warrior daughter of Kaiypchal,
And began her speech at length,
Speaking softly but with firm resolve.
Your fierce, ill-fated mother,
Most graceful of the long-haired,
Mightiest among women,
Purest of those in flowing gowns,
The foremost of womankind—
Such was that blessed soul,
Deeply wondrous and rich in wisdom.
Her eyes were Venus, her tongue was honey;
No such soul is born among humankind.
The venerable woman who bore a hero
Was a saint among women.
Her words were sweet, her tongue was honey;
However long you speak, none like her appears.
Such was the sacred power
Of your dear mother, the venerable lady:
She joined together what was severed
And gathered what was scattered.
At night her treasure-chests loomed like mountains;
While her gray-wolf mountain yet stood,
Her command reached across the world.
Your noble mother Kanykei
Spoke plainly while alive,
And kept her eyes open until death.
Your dear and noble mother
Said this to the warrior maiden,
Telling her child a wondrous secret:
“Warrior maiden, my child Kuyaly,
When I still possessed sacred power,
When I once surged like fire
And flourished in my prime,
When abundant wealth and great fortune
Still bade me journey on,
On the Night of Destiny in the holy week,
I lay down and dreamed a dream:
Its collar was fine and its tether golden—
In my dream I caught a falcon.
Saying it would accompany the lion’s son,
I tethered it fast and secure.
Then, through God’s mysterious doing,
Barefoot and bareheaded,
Named Khizr and noble in essence,
An old, white-bearded dervish
Appeared before me.
The white-bearded one came and said:
‘The towering golden Ala-Too
Cannot be overrun by an invading foe,
Nor seized by any man who marks it well.
Until the end of time,
Here is the land where descendants
Will have their soiled shirts washed,
And the blood of their birth-cords staunched;
This is the land where they shall remain.
