As the Kalcha reeled,
Hero Alman seized him
From the lower skirts.
He was about to stretch him out
And slaughter him as a sacrifice.
When the crisis came,
This accursed Black Horse
Turned his neck like a spirit,
And flew like an arrow
Across cliffs a rope-length high.
Because that Black Horse was there,
His extinguished fire was kindled,
And his dead soul revived.
Saying this was Kongurbai the Kalcha's horse,
Karagul said: “My dear foal,
Choose it and ride it.
Present it as a gift
To your aged uncle Bakai.
That foolish hero Kongurbai
Has no other livestock,
No other life than this.
Once he is left horseless,
Enter straight in and make war;
This is what I say—afterward,
Take that slave's head.”
Thus Karagul then
Set about deceiving him.
Upon the foolish son of Manas,
Saying, “This is Kongurbai's armor,”
He put the mail coat;
Saying, “This is Kongurbai's horse,”
He mounted him on great Kyyduukara.
Karagul, son of Katal,
Revealed his monstrous nature.
The Sary-Suu of Kürpüldök—
Here was the dark hour.
Forty ropes lowered into that water
Would not reach its depth;
At the ford of that water,
Even a tulpar could not pass.
Looking toward its edge,
There was brine rising to the knees;
Looking toward its middle,
There was a great excavated trench.
Your hero, brave but without sense,
Born of a warrior,
Entered the water like fire.
Looking behind him,
Karagul, son of Katal—
Here was the dark hour—
Had remained there
With the shaggy, sore-backed five-year-old mare.
When Semetey saw it,
He raged in anguish,
Pleading, “Uncle!”
“My dear uncle,
You dug my deep trench
And remained on the farther bank,
Making my misery boil.
Among that vast, desolate herd,
Uncle, was no horse allotted to you?
Among those many desolate possessions,
Was no coat allotted to you?
While there was a tulpar fit for campaigns,
Why were you humiliated by a sore-backed nag?
While there was a tulpar with a good gait,
Why were you humiliated by a mare?
When we raided
That vast, desolate herd completely
And set it on the right road,
When they ask, ‘Where is your uncle,
Your father Manas's confidant,
His companion for the afterlife?’
What shall I say?
Leaving you behind and driving the herd,
Uncle, what inheritance shall I attain?
My dear uncle, met through providence,
My dear, beloved uncle—
