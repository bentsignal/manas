He brought great calamity upon the Kyrgyz
As he went on his way.
Driving off the horses with a roar,
He bellowed menacingly
And summoned his champions.
Swaggering and boasting,
He seized a great many Kyrgyz horses,
And became laden with vast wealth.
Up Suusamyr’s steep ascent,
Straining against the slope,
He dragged them back and forth;
Any foal or yearling unable to walk
He had his champions slaughter.
He drove away the entire herd,
Taking every last horse.
He made the countryside thunder
And set them upon the straight road.
Let us leave that matter here
And begin the tale of the peerless champion Akzholtoi
And the indomitable gray-maned Seytek,
Telling what befell those two.
One herdsman, left for dead,
Brought them word;
Most of the herdsmen
Had been overtaken by death.
Erebai, son of Emil,
Had barely escaped with his life.
His head was split in seven places,
And he had been stabbed in seven places;
His senses failed and his strength was gone,
Yet he escaped when they thought him dead.
He cried out as he went down the Talas,
Calling to his heroes:
“They have reached the very heart of our land together!
They have driven off the whole herd,
Shattering our sleep completely;
They spilled your blood like water
And cut your lives like thread.
They struck your horses upon the hills,
And grievously destroyed your wealth;
They brought disaster upon your lands
And rode straight through your people.
It was the hour of a devastating raid;
These Kalmyks, making everything ring,
Launched their assault.
He is the keen eye
Of the courageous heathen Manchu Chinese,
A hero who never retreats,
And the very embodiment
Of our mortal enemy.
He scoured the land in a dreadful state
And has now ridden away.
He has the thoroughbred Kerik;
If you would fight, that is your foe,
And he alone has the strength of a thousand men.
Would he refrain from bringing grief?
That snarling infidel’s
Battle cry is ‘Kakanchi!’
Would such an enemy stand idle?
That terrifying villain’s
Battle cry is ‘Bakburchun!’
He speaks a strange, rambling tongue;
I understood nothing of it.
My hero Seytek, hear me!
He seized the livestock grazing in the mountains,
And took the lives of multitudes;
He killed the men who guarded the horses,
And took possession of the broad land,
Killing many splendid warriors.
Here I have finally arrived!”
Erebai gasped these words.
“So this is your report, Erebai:
The forces of Beijing have come!”
The power that threw the nation into turmoil—
For from ancient times Great Beijing
Had begun disputes and stirred up strife,
A custom inherited from its forefathers.
The enemy who would never come when sought
Had now come of his own accord to attack;
Without tormenting themselves, they had found him.
