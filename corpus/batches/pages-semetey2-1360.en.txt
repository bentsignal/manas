Settling freely along the place of honor,
Living together as husband and wife,
She was Chürök whose every manner
Surpassed all others in the world;
Chürök whom a man would love
And draw against his breast;
Chürök who would enter
Countless delightful games;
Chürök who could cure
Ancient grief and great affliction;
Chürök who should not go as spoil
Into a broad foreign palace;
Chürök who should not be placed
At anyone’s unrestricted disposal;
Meeting face to face through one gate,
Eating meat from one platter,
She was Chürök who could enter
The deepest pleasures and games;
Would Chürök not lie with
The poor man who swayed toward her,
And satisfy his first longing?
To that sidelong-glancing beauty,
Ah, to that quivering creature,
Would Chürök not place into his hands
Everything he asked of her?
Would Chürök refuse to consent
And submit to what he said?
If I do not spend my strength
For matchless Chürök,
If I do not scatter my gathered herds,
If I do not spend beyond livestock,
Sending men through many cities,
If I do not open the treasury,
And lavish upon Chürök
All the wealth I have found and amassed,
Scattering countless riches!
For beautiful Chürök to enter,
If I do not commission a palace,
Commissioned with a golden roof-ring and copper ropes;
If, upon that roof-ring,
I do not have gold richly plated,
Spending freely from
The Jediger treasury;
If I do not gladden the people with celebration,
Making all the world proclaim,
“Kyyaz has won Chürök,”
And gather every living soul,
Holding a feast for multitudes;
If I do not make a wonder for the ages,
As though taking a maiden in marriage;
If I do not attend her like a chief wife,
Begging her no fewer than forty times,
Preparing to serve her
And standing before her!
Moonlike Aichürök,
If I do not obey your words,
Go wherever you direct me,
Consent to what you say,
And carry out your commands,
May the hairy-breasted Earth strike me,
May the overarching Sky strike me,
May holy Ak strike me in disaster,
May the spark of the white matchlock strike me,
May the bullet of the white-barreled gun strike me!”
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Stood there in distress.
Without approaching her, brave Kyyaz
Stood pleading with Chürök.
Your mother, moonlike lady,
See every outrage she commits:
Her heart burning to embers,
Her eyes staring without dying,
Parted from the bloodthirsty lion,
Her dear hair like willow silk,
Her dear head cast onto the plain,
Rather than suffer such disgrace,
She wished to die before her appointed death,
And see the true abode from which none return.
