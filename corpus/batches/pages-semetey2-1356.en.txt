His two hands, once his greatest servants,
Hung paralyzed and would not rise.
The Zhediger fought and quarreled,
Inflicting humiliation,
Bringing misery upon his poor soul,
And committing violence.
As twilight came and the sun set,
The Zhediger forced their way in.
The Zhediger entered and seized
Aichurok's White Palace.
May God never show such a sight!
They traveled in turmoil,
The army massed thick as fog,
And the Zhediger entered in ranks.
Gold, silver, countless coins—
The Zhediger plundered much.
Akzholtoi, now alone,
Grieved in sorrow,
Having lost the gatekeepers
Who had served as his support.
His elders were soaked in blood;
His guards and lions,
The heroes bound at his waist,
Were taken by the Zhediger.
Though shot, no bullet pierced
The accursed Kanchoro.
As though he had prospered,
He had drawn on and worn Ak-Olpok,
With golden collar, copper sleeves,
Silken weave and lark-eye rings.
When merely brandished, it set things ablaze;
When truly fired, it blasted its target.
When he slung it on, it was
The answer to every turmoil;
When the wretch aimed it,
It became the companion of his flylike soul.
Akkelte, refused to any who asked,
Was in Khan Kanchoro's hands.
Seeing this misery,
Finding no pass to cross,
Finding no lion to support him,
Despairing of his precious life,
He held the short gun Chortkelte.
Gripping Chortkelte, he ran.
As twilight came and the sun set,
While the people lay utterly still,
Before stars divided the sky,
His eyes dimmed and night fell;
Nothing at all could be seen.
Then Akzholtoi,
Toward Kaiypchy Pass,
Toward the ridge of Kanzhailak,
Gathering strength in his flylike soul,
Scattered all his worldly hopes.
One gray wolf, unable to endure,
Fled toward Kaiypchy.
Before the Zhediger could seize him,
Before they could torment him without mercy,
Before they cut off his head like a sheep's,
Before they brought him utterly to ruin,
Before their flood trampled him,
He, the son of the smith Bolokbai,
That extraordinary weather-working servant,
Made rain fall upon the uplands
And drew fog over the foothills.
The weather broke and the sun was veiled.
Running on foot day and night,
Toward Kaiypchy's Kara-Too,
Toward the pass between twin peaks,
The route by which his father Bolokbai escaped the foe,
The ridge used for flight,
While the weather broke and the sun was veiled,
Akzholtoi fled and escaped.
Akzholtoi became invisible;
No human being approached him.
From Kanchoro's wrath,
Akzholtoi vanished.
