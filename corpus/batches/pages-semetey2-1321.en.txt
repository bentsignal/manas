Make your mourning head a man,
Make your woman's head a banner;
Against Kanchoro and Kyiaz
Raise an uproar no man has seen.
Drive your caravan forth,
And take revenge without grieving
On those babbling fools.
Lead your migration from Talas;
If you are a tigress, a lioness,
If you truly are Aichurok,
Take vengeance on the traitors!”
Thus Akjoltoi
Spoke these words to her.
Kulchoro, who was your wing,
Had his temple pierced and was made a slave;
The warrior's shoulder was cut,
And he was enslaved as a herdsman.
Kanchoro sharpened his keen broadsword
And slaughtered all the rest,
Leaving no one alive.
He spilled and churned their yellow blood;
My forefather, old Bakai,
Suffered outrage upon outrage,
And they dragged him in the kok-boru game.
In fury and anguish,
The many Argyn and the Kyrgyz host
Despaired of their precious lives.”
Ah, when Akjoltoi
Had said these things,
Old Chyiyrdy
Tensed like a hawk,
And pleading with Kanykei,
For six thousand camels and a thousand colts,
She had bought the white dagger;
She drew it from its sheath.
Shrieking aloud,
“How can I remain after my darling?”
She aimed it at her breast.
The old mother herself
Drew it across her body.
His unfortunate mother Kanykei,
Wailing and sobbing, could not stand,
Yet could not take her own life;
Her color fled and her senses reeled
As they struggled over the white dagger.
She screamed and wept aloud,
“Ah, my only one!” she sobbed.
From her quail-like eyes
Uncommon tears of blood flowed.
Her strength left her as she screamed;
As she screamed, from her eyes
Dark blood poured.
“My morning star!” she cried,
“My only one!” she sobbed.
Her ribs seemed to split apart,
And tears poured from her eyes.
The palace ridge where she stood
Was washed by the tears from both eyes,
Which spread like a flood into a pool.
Your noble mother Kanykei
Sobbed, “My dear foal!”
And cried, “My cherished one!”
Calling him her ash-gray camel calf, she sobbed:
“Late in life I braided my hair,
After long yearning I saw your face.
Now before my eyes
Lies only pitch-black night.
My sole hope and support—
Whom have I besides you?”
Thus Kanykei
Stood weeping in anguish,
Lamenting and sobbing,
Unable to catch her breath.
If he was dead, he was not in the grave;
If alive, he was nowhere among the living.
