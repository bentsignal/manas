Biting like stallions,
Gnashing like hungry male camels,
Gripping one another's trouser hems,
Tripping each other without sparing their lives.
That Ak-Balban of Bukhara
Grew weak;
Too-Balban of Kokand
Lifted him and dashed him down.
When all the many prizes of Bukhara
He drove away,
Wrestling with Too-Balban,
Semetey put him to torment.
A champion such as Too-Balban
He lifted and dashed once to the ground;
When he tore his arm out,
Through this, Too-Balban
Met his death.
“He will surpass Manas,”
All the people began to whisper.
Not content to stop at that,
The mount beneath me
Was Temir Khan's Aktulpar;
What I held on my hand
Was Ysmayyl's falcon.
I went out flying the falcon
And heard the bald charcoal burner's words.
“Your great people are the Kyrgyz,” I heard.
“Your horse has a white blaze,
Your father is someone else.”
Sarytaz said a word;
He put a troubling word into my thoughts.
I heard the words of that damned bald man:
“My people are the Kyrgyz,” I heard.
“Manas is your father,
Talas is your land,
Kanykei is your mother,
Ysmayyl is your mother's brother;
Abyke and Kobosh in Talas are your elder kinsmen;
Akun Khan is your father-in-law,
Aichurok is your wife.”
Sarytaz said a word;
He put a troubling word into my thoughts.
I chose to mount Aktulpar;
[HOLD]
From far away I came of my own will,
Saying, “Abyke and Kobosh are my kin.”
You whom I regard in my father's place,
Abyke, on whom I lean,
Hear the words I mean to say.
Kumaiyk, left by my father Manas,
That keen hunter known to the people,
The inheritance left by my father Manas,
From the banks of great Talas
Has strayed into your keeping.
Where is that treasure, the white sighthound?
Tell me what has become of him.
Long ago, the bird a vulture hatched and raised,
Which God Almighty brought to my khan father,
To Khan Manas—
His talons iron, his jess fastener copper,
Two cubits in girth,
Whose striking talons never missed,
Whom no keen hunting bird could match,
Black-eyed, with speckled legs,
Never shrinking from what he saw,
[HOLD]
He would not fold his wings and swoop;
He would not let a goose fly from the lake.
He would not gather his tail and swoop;
He would not let a swan fly from the lake.
He was a bird to take hunting;
He was a possession left by my father.
Where is the white falcon, uncle?!
Long ago, into looming Great Beijing,
When he went straight in and thrust his spear,
From a hell of grievous suffering,
When he freed lives.
