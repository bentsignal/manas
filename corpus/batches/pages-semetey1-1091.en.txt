Among them
Was the hero Koshoy as well.
At Kökötöy's mourning feast,
His people surged together;
I saw the place where they buried him.
For many days the people gathered;
I saw the place where they camped.
I saw with my own eyes
Kökötöy's grave prizes.
Adorning them richly with gold and jewels,
He staked all his wealth
In equal shares, without favor,
Staked a thousand dark camels,
And a thousand black-blazed mares.
The racers that were to run
Had their hair tails braided,
Their forelocks adorned
And tied with blue silk.
Their waists were formed slender as a girl's;
They streamed uphill and down.
Kökötöy's many prizes
Delighted every soul who saw them.
Their fangs protruding,
The swift horses grew restive;
Their mustaches bristling,
Giants and heroes grew excited.
They covered him and heaped the earth;
Saying Khan Kökötöy had died,
The common people gathered in a mass,
With their khojas and men of standing.
For Khan Kökötöy's death,
There were peoples who had set out and come.
There were heroes and giants,
Heroes each worth a thousand,
All of them gathered there.
His white beard flowing,
He who had fathomed the depths of wisdom,
At whose stern face men trembled,
Who had studied under saints—
Mighty Koshoy too was there.
He made the unknown known,
The learned elder Koshoy.
Do not ask of Koshoy's station:
To receive the highest instruction,
Hero Koshoy entered Egypt
And studied in Egypt for five years.
To comprehend all the world,
He studied in Baghdad for five years.
Traveling the lands before him,
Hero Koshoy entered Mecca
And studied in Mecca for five years.
He received a letter from the caliph,
Pledged his hand to the twelve-in-one,
And was called Sayypburush.
His white beard flowing,
That elder, your uncle Koshoy,
Had examined every counsel.
Before that Koshoy,
The people were gathered.
The rich man's son Bokmurun,
Saying, "My khan-father has passed away,"
Wept before Khan Koshoy:
"My uncle, elder Koshoy,
My father Kökötöy
Was indeed the true Kökötöy.
My dear father's countless riches
