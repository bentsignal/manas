His fame remained beside Manas.
Wise and ingenious in stratagems,
Lion Bakai was a hero;
In wisdom and cunning
He was no less than Manas.
When he battled with us,
Bakai’s beard was mottled gray.
That Bakai must have died,
Must have beheld the face of Judgment Day.
If that Bakai has not died,
If he has not beheld the face of Judgment Day,
He would lead Semetey
And bring him here.
The reason he would bring him is this:
Was it not Bakai
Who brought chaos upon the Kakanchin?
Was it not Bakai
Who seized the khanate of Chet-Beijing?
Was it not Bakai who mounted
The golden throne where the khan sits?
Was it not Bakai
Who journeyed through Beijing on many campaigns?
The roads to Beijing have five branches—
Distinct as the five fingers of his hand—
Was it not Bakai who knew them?
Was it not Bakai who led campaigns
Against the Kakan and traveled
Through this land many times?
That Bakai will not disappear;
While that Bakai does not disappear,
Affairs in Beijing will not prosper.
Bakai, the infidel of the Burut—
Karagul, you are no less
Than that Khan Bakai in cunning!
He joined what had been severed
And gathered what had been scattered
By that bastard Bloodthirsty One.
With that Bakai leading the way,
If it pleases the sly Burut,
May he not come here on patrol!
You have your superb Black Horse;
Were it not for your old age,
You would be a mighty and renowned man!
Keep watch from Altyn-Ken over there;
Sleep was never your kin,
And lying asleep never brought good.
Seek news from Kuukulzha.
Why should I remain here now?
I will add the bereft Algara
To the black-cloud herd
So that he may grow stout,
Then leave him behind.
If you are caught unawares, disaster will follow;
May you perish together with your herd—
Algara is entrusted to you!
Why should I remain here heedlessly?
I will depart toward Beijing.
This year the people of forty khans have gathered;
They are opening Chagaan and holding a feast.
Now that my mustache is turning gray,
I have not had the least fill of pleasure.
Six teitei and forty shurkut—
Those are the wives I took;
I will sport with them.
I am hero Kongurbai;
My valor stands apart from the people,
And I am the protecting fortress of Beijing.
With the six teitei and forty shurkut,
Lying a few days in their embrace,
This is the time to take my pleasure.
Why should I give more counsel now?
You are yourself an exceptional man;
I will depart toward the Kakan.
If I keep watch, I am myself a great noble;
I roam through Beijing.
My renown is that of a peerless great noble;
I roam through the Kakan.
