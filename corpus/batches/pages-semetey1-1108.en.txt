Has camped at Üch-Karkyra, tell him.
Say there is meat like Ala-Too,
Broth like Ala-Köl,
And the time has come to give the feast.
Say there are all kinds of people
And wonders without end.
Tell him to come to my feast
And see this spectacle.
If he does not see this spectacle,
He is a woman-hearted man;
If he fails to take heed,
If by chance he does not come,
Tell him to behold Kökötöy's red banner with its golden crescent
From atop his own house,
And give us all the palace maidens
As booty!
When you travel farther,
Beyond Great Bukhara,
In a plain with a great river,
Dwelling among rugged hills,
Whose tale is extraordinary,
Called a merciless hero,
Is Muzburchak of Khan Buudayyk.
He steeped his spear in blood;
The man who came to grapple with him
He pressed into the black earth.
Meeting him head-on,
No foe escaped and emerged.
He girded his waist tightly,
Born greater than his father;
When his anger rose,
He cast the enemy into turmoil.
His weapons formed an armful upon his body—
Muzburchak of Khan Buudayyk.
No man defeated him in wrestling;
No one came facing me,
And he yielded his companion to no one.
He sowed his rice as an autumn crop;
When he wrestled an enemy,
He swallowed and chewed his windpipe.
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
That elephant-built brute,
Muzburchak of Khan Buudayyk—
Give Muzburchak my greetings.
Tell him to come
To Kökötöy's renowned great feast
And see where unbelievers and Muslims
Gather together.
Tell him to see the poles crack in their hands
And bend.
Tell him to see the heroes charge on horseback
And joust.
Tell him to come to my feast
And see this spectacle!
If he does not come to my feast,
If he does not see this spectacle,
I will find a splendid quarrel.
Until the bylkıldak stalk ripens,
Until the autumn gossamer flies,
I will fight hard for six months.
I will load gunpowder
Upon Kökötöy's red-tailed black camel.
If he does not heed my words,
I will shoot from atop his house
And make Muzburchak buckle.
I will take his craftsmen as booty
And make him taste my salt!
When you travel back from there,
He summers in Eki-Kemin,
Tethers a pair of black horses,
Makes the dense forest his livestock,
And the black cave his home;
He sharpens his eloquent tongue
And speaks before the khan—
Hero Ürbü, the bald Eybit.
He knows the tongues of seventy tribes,
A fully accomplished orator.
He gathered kites and made them hunting birds.
