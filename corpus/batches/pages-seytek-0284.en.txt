My mother entrusted it to you!
If you bring it back to me,
Bring my venerable white-haired mother
And let my eyes behold her.
Let me gaze upon her face,
Breathe once the scent of her breast,
And only then let me die!
My black crag no goose can circle,
My great mountain left by the departed,
Light of my seeing eyes,
Strength of my heart,
My only son, fare safely.
Your mother has no strength left.
Go in safety and return unharmed;
Do not leave your poor, wretched mother
Longing—come back quickly.
Go safely and return sound.
My orphaned son, if only
Once within seven days
I may safely see your face.
Saying, “My cherished orphan will return,”
I shall grieve for six days;
But if you do not come within six days,
If I do not see your radiant face,
I shall die before death comes.’
Moon-like Aichürök,
For the son she bore at death’s door,
Let her thoughts scatter far and wide.
Amid universal anguish and hardship,
Behold the plight of her only son.
Her heart was deeply troubled,
And tears poured from her eyes.
As though her son would never return,
Aichürök grieved,
Though he was merely setting forth
Upon a campaign.
So great was the poor woman’s anguish,
As though they would never meet again.
The woman screamed and shrieked,
Unable to bear it, she wailed.
There is no end to my misery.
The one who unleashed volleys of fire,
Who rescued us from calamity
More terrible than any battle cry—
My noble son is not here.
The support on which I relied,
The one in whom I placed my hopes,
My valiant son, is not there.
My heart burns; I have no courage left.
The enemy has torn away my wing;
My heart burns beneath the vast blue sky.
Had you been there then, my son,
Would they not have spared my blood?
Would they not have left my herds unscattered?
Would the raging Jediger
Not have spared my life?
Across the lower valley of Talas,
Would they have dared draw their boundary?
Would you not have faced them in battle
And crushed those evildoers?
Yesterday, when the enemy surrounded us,
When our people were left helpless
And suffered bitter torment,
I failed to do what was right—
Curse my womanhood!
I let them seize my livestock,
And let my life become their spoil.
I let them cut down my silken banner,
And let them drag away
The mother granted me by fortune.
If you encounter my mother,
Go and exchange greetings with her;
To be my strength and comfort,
Bring your noble, blessed mother
Back this way with you.
Let my eyes behold her;
Bring me the one I revere above all people—
My son, bring her back to me.
If you abandon her, shame will follow.
My heroic son, heed my words:
