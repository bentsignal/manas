Not even a stick for a horse-catching pole remained.
Khan Manas's royal encampment,
Kanykei's white palace,
Its gate of solid tin,
Was a place no person brought news to,
And no soul visited.
Of anyone who went without an answer,
Not one remained alive.
Before its door,
Standing sternly with swords in hand,
Were exactly forty wrestler guards;
Its gatekeepers were beyond number.
They wore human noses and ears
As amulets,
And were covered in the hides
Of seventy executioners.
At the mouth of that doorway
Was a war drum for announcing news;
When any enemy approached,
His wrestlers beat it.
When they heard the drum's sound,
With brave Manas at their head,
When all forty companions came forth,
The courage utterly left
Anyone who drew near.
They had made Talas their homeland
And settled there, he thought;
Young Aydar stood amazed,
Thinking they had become a whole world,
Let alone merely Chinese.
Manas the keen-eyed leopard
Was at the point of unleashing
Festivities upon the Chinese.
If nocturnal Beijing and Kakanchyn
Attacked with all their might,
He was at the point of taking them all,
Leaving not a hair behind.
Young Aydar saw Manas
And, saying, “He looks fearsome,”
Rejoiced with all his soul!
“Brave Manas the leopard
Is broad across the right shoulder.
For the mere handful of Muslims,
He is a lion who will remain
As their supporting pillar!
Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Has grown arrogant,
Failing to send word to the gray wolf!
I wore down my horse's flanks;
Among all people upon the earth,
I visited every one.
When I saw Manas's form,
My mind was deeply shaken.
Among infidels and Muslims,
Among all the giants on earth,
Where has another like him appeared!
His fortune is complete;
He is a hero who receives and protects
The wretched people without making them wander.
His brow is high and his face venomous;
What soul survives when the bloodthirsty one kills?
His face is like oiled wheat,
His eyes like twilight mist.
The madman who confronts him—
The way he faces becomes a red fire.
His courage is formidable, his venom strong;
He lays flat whoever attacks him!”
His prince sat firm as a camel;
With mighty Bakai at their head,
Twenty were on his right.
Each was worth a thousand,
All gray-wolf champions of noble stock.
With Khan Almanbet at their head,
Twenty were on his left.
Their waists girded for single combat,
Against an army of seven countless hosts,
They charged alone through its midst,
A brood like monsters.
