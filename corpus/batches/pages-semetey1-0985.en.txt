Sent them out as mediators:
One was the khan’s Körpöjan,
One was Sarykan’s Tynymkan,
And one was Jamgyrchy’s Jaynake.
The three senior wives went together,
Stepped between the two sides,
And went to obtain an answer
From the pampered woman.
Chachykey’s white palace
Had a gate of solid iron and tin;
The pampered daughter-in-law’s
Splendor was exceedingly great.
No one dared approach it,
And whoever did was amazed;
No traveler dared go there,
And whoever looked was amazed.
At Chachykey’s encampment,
Acting as mediators there,
The three senior wives entered
To learn what had become
Of the falcon that had flown away.
Körpöjan, wife of Khan Bakay,
Moved like a goose with young,
Walked inside, and began speaking:
“You were once a younger child;
Ah, delight who shares my life!
Now, my child, tell the truth.
They opened their horses’ stride;
Along the shore of Ala-Köl,
Prince Semetey the lion
Lost Akshunkar.
Take wisdom worth red gold;
My precious child, heed my words!
We followed the chain of tradition,
Asked the learned men,
And heard words from men of counsel:
My colt, we heard that you know
Of the falcon that was carried away.
Ah, my colt Chachykey,
Tell the song you have sung,
And speak your secret faithfully!
If you tell your secret, you will prosper
And become a pure senior wife.
If you are human, tell the truth:
How did you sleep, and what dream did you see?
What manner of event did you witness?
Tell the truth, my colt!
Think upon what came before and after;
If you do not tell it, your blue lord
May mock you for nothing.
A man’s work is solid iron,
My child, but your name is woman;
One needs skill to melt it.
If you do not tell, shame and reproach
Will fall upon your own head.
If you do not tell, my colt,
What can I say of every deed
Of your dark-hearted prince,
The true son of the tiger,
Descendant of the mighty
Who smoked whenever he was spent
And grew fiercer whenever he died?
If you do not tell, suddenly,
When that desperate man’s anger rises,
A man’s affairs are perilous.
May he not stretch out the hair at your temples,
Or give you the divorce of a cast-off wife!
Do not be obstinate; hear my words.
My daughter-in-law, heed me now.
If you disobey and your sorrow boils,
He may put a kementei upon you,
Mount you on a bay mare,
And drive you toward your father.
When your father gave you,
When you came here as a bride,
You shone in a kete coat,
Rode proudly upon a bay pacer,
And shone in a supa coat,
