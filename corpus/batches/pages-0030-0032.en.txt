Now they plundered everything in their path.
They pulled down what had been built,
They struck down whoever they met.
Your honored Kyrgyz people’s
Brides and daughters cried out now.
The Chinese swept in like a flood;
Those who offered defiance
They trampled into the black earth.
Unable to find a way to save their lives,
Countless Kyrgyz were panic-stricken.
“Will my life be spared?”
Stumbling along the mountains,
Countless Kyrgyz fled.
All the old people,
Their knees buckling,
The poor ones without strength,
Before the Chinese
Bowed low like brides.
Then Alöökö the Chinese
Laid waste the royal camp and plundered it.
All the women
He made widows in black headdresses.
All the strong men
He drove away and made slaves.
Karakan’s treasury,
The five palaces that housed his guests,
For all their mighty strength—
He tore their pillars out now,
He brought calamity upon them now,
He broke into the treasury now,
Thus he brought them to utter ruin.
Alöökö Khan and Molto Khan
Had forces beyond human knowing,
To bring mankind to ruin;
Swarming like ants,
An army stood behind them.
The great army swept in;
Now bitter was the Muslim people’s fate.
Within the great army
Was Döngö, champion of the Chinese.
Do not ask about Döngö:
His head was like a great cauldron,
His brows like dogs lying stretched out.
His face was like wheat glistening with oil;
His valor was unlike others’.
His eyes were like open pits,
His bulk like half a mountain.
At the army’s head stood huge Döngö,
Ready to devour lives when he stirred.
He was skilled at striking with an iron-headed mace.
That great swine, sprung from the Chinese,
Was a mighty one.
A sword without a sheath hung at his side.
As though he would swallow whatever he seized,
His wrath gathered upon his face.
Grim, he raised his battle cry,
Girding himself with twisted iron.
He was a warrior of long standing.
In Beijing, home of the kakan,
They called his name Döngö.
He ate seven batman of wheat,
A warrior smelling of grain.
He killed everyone within his reach,
A bondsman smelling of blood.
He spared no one he encountered;
The meat of a whole boar
Would not fill him at one sitting.
From the Old Khan of Beijing,
From his city, he had received command.
To carry out the khan’s plunder,
That mighty man had made ready.
The Black Burut, the Kyrgyz—
To slaughter them all, leaving none,
That mighty man had made ready.
Do not ask about Döngö:
From Chin-Machin, from Beijing,
From the true kakan himself,
He gathered troops and took an army,
Took great numbers of every kind.
Clad in blue-steel armor,
If you could see huge Döngö—
Lunging like a blue-gray tiger,
Sounding his trumpet in uproar,
With dust whirling into the sky,
His Torguts, Sibe, and Manchus
Gathered together, every one.
The Chinese war drums
Were all beaten together;
All their champions surged forward.
Molto and Alöökö
Gave Döngö their command:
“They speak another tongue,
Those damned Burut;
In their very nature they are our enemies.
Slaughter them and trample them down!
Warrior Döngö, sweep over them!
Kill them and trample them into the earth!
Back when the Burut Ogoy was alive,
The countless cattle put out to pasture,
The myriad livestock taken as customs dues,
He raided and drove off every one.
Thus he brought me to utter ruin.
My vengeance will never be exhausted.
The Burut whose ruler is dead—
Let us tread upon their heads,
Scatter their livestock of every kind,
Set our cauldron in their place of honor!
Let us bind and slaughter their khans,
Plunder their herds and take our ease.
Everyone who offers defiance,
Let us slaughter them all, leaving none!
Those damned Burut—
Let us enjoy their good things.
If they rear up, let us crush them,
Seize their scalps and slit them.
Drive on, my Döngö, drive the army on!
Drive all the troops forward together!
Let us surround their strong men;
Their girls and brides, sparing none,
Let us seize from their very homes.
Those damned Burut—
Let us quench their burning hearths;
Not even a cluster of three households spared,
Let us drive them all away together.
Spur on, my Döngö, spur on!
Let us lay great suffering upon them.
The Burut girls and brides,
Let us seize them all, leaving none!
My wrathful Döngö, hear my words:
Let us slaughter their strong men,
And the poor ones without strength,
Let us take as plunder and rest.
Let us press them hard and levy tax.
To the edge of their people’s lands,
Let us bring Alöökö to hold hostages.
From those unruly Burut,
Let us exact the tax in full;
Their ancient people, sparing none,
Let us destroy and plunder utterly!”
Raging, Molto Khan
Spoke these words to Döngö.
The army swarmed across the earth,
Its troops too many to be counted;
Dust whirled into the sky.
