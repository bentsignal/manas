And into the heart of hero Muradyl
He planted a foul word.
Muradyl assembled his wrestlers,
Ordered his warriors to prepare,
And, parting from the Soloons,
His bulk appearing like a mountain,
Climbed the dark-necked ridge,
Up onto its looming spur.
There Muradyl took his place.
When he went out to keep watch,
Hoping his people remained safe,
The tent showed pale in the distance.
When he saw that tent,
Muradyl stood astonished,
And his mind was sorely troubled.
In shape and splendor,
The figure within the tent
Appeared to be the bloodthirsty one’s son!
Muradyl pondered his course.
Against a bloodthirsty man like his father,
Against a trickster like his father,
He could not prevail in combat,
Nor would his strength suffice in wrestling.
If he matched himself against that killer,
He thought it would carry him to his very end;
If the blood-drinker’s descendant
Should chance to capture him,
He would bind him and take him to the Buruts.
Muradyl thought through all of this;
He did not leave any of it unconsidered.
Standing there, Muradyl resolved
To unleash an upheaval:
He would make snow fall
And raid away their horses.
What did hero Muradyl do?
He began to enchant the weather.
Taking the weather-stone he had chosen,
He recited its true spell many times
On the heights of Kyzyl-Kyya;
At Lake Turuna,
He struck the weather-stone into the lake
And began enchanting the weather.
The cloud’s name was Buurakan—
He called it by its name;
The wind’s name was Zhelshamal—
He called it by its name.
A cloud that traveled in winter
He seized in the middle of summer.
The great Chinese warrior Muradyl
Worked measureless weather magic.
Mist formed in the ravines;
Above the mountain peaks,
Black clouds gathered in fury,
Shrieking along the lower slopes,
And black rain closed in.
Above Orgochor,
A towering cloud appeared;
It swept howling over the foothills,
Pouring down blue-blazed hail.
The earth rumbled and shuddered;
From the sky the blue-blazed hail
Came whispering down.
The sullen cloud split apart,
And hailstones as large as bowls poured from the sky.
Nothing could be discerned by sight;
A dark ground fog moved about,
And the face of the earth disappeared.
The ground broke apart with a roar;
Through ravines and hollows,
A flood surged, thundering.
Black cliffs collapsed and crags flowed;
The torrent covered all, and the mountains flowed,
Choking every hollow
And breaking down the riverbanks in heaps.
He turned the summer day to winter;
Before your lord,
Ice stood four fingers thick.
