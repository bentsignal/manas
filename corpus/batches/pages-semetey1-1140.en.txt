They satisfied whoever saw them;
They fitted yellow brass
As heels upon their soles.
When you go among the multitude, wear them;
Know that your mother is noble,
And recognize this labor of mine.
Your accomplished sister-in-law Kanykey,
Displaying her mastery,
Dressed the executioner-like companions
In coats for the swift.
The forty companions' forty racehorses—
Your precious sister-in-law Kanykey
Had tethered them in a row
In your magnanimous lord's stable,
With Akkula at their head.
She fed them selected rice grain
And drew their sweat each day;
She fed them rich barley grain
And drew their sweat each month.
The learned woman Kanykey,
Knowing Kökötöy's feast would come
And the people would fill it seething,
Knowing lance shafts would bend
When heroic men jousted,
Feared the horses would grow too fat,
Would become "fat-swallowed,"
And calamity strike the racehorses,
Bringing mange upon them as well;
They would lose mane and tail,
Bringing disgrace,
And all together
Would mount crippled horses.
She feared incurable disgrace
Would come from that.
The forty companions' forty racehorses,
With Akkula at their head,
She had brought from Khan-Zhaylak,
Had their reins fitted firmly,
Had felt coverings spread over them,
And had the horses raised unseen.
Such are the deeds I have done.
Hear of my labor;
Know my precious worth.
Each of you mount, dear boys,
His own horse.
When khans swarm together
Like black worms,
What will the feast be like?
What will become of the leaders
Of the black Chinese and Manchus?
A guest who brings calamity bears obligations—
What will the feast be like?
From within broad Talas,
For riding if conflict began,
He took six swift horses
And had ammunition loaded on elephants.
He received the pledge of young Aydar
That the handful of Muslims
Had been utterly plundered.
He heard words from the messenger:
At Khan Kökötöy's feast,
The black Chinese and Manchus
Had utterly slaughtered
The handful of Muslims.
He heard that the handful of Muslims
Had been plundered;
He heard that, beginning with Khan Koshoy,
They had been beaten.
He heard that the Chinese had inflicted
Humiliation upon noble Koshoy;
That hero Töshtük, son of Eleman,
Had lost his wits;
That Bokmurun, giver of the feast,
Had fled from among the people;
That the black Chinese had seized
All the vast riches;
That they had begun the spectacle;
