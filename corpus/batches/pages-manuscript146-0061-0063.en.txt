The affairs of the great host were secret,
Yet he had learned them all,
The command that Chaan had carried out,
When that troubled time arose among the people,
My elder Etек acted,
And learned its immense secret.
With a name of bitter wrath,
He had arrived at that place.
To the son of Heaven,
He related it without holding back.
Not leaving out a single word,
He told the truth.
Of the lineage of the Black Kyrgyz,
He disclosed its secret.
One could not bear to see another,
One would not heed another’s word,
Now of those people and that nation,
He disclosed the secret.
Of the small force that knew how to destroy,
He told the truth.
Of the camp of the warrior Esen,
Börköy gave an account,
Of his many clamoring people,
He gave a full account.
The horse he rode was a bay thoroughbred,
He was a man of fierce courage,
Called the hero Kulan Saryk,
He had two sons.
The younger and elder brothers lacked unity,
Such were those people.
To the warrior Esen concerning this,
He was a man regarded as a warrior.
Saying this, Chañ Buzer continued,
To the sovereign of Heaven,
He related everything.
From the Amroolu Kyrgyz,
He brought and delivered the report.
Of the small Amroolu people,
He turned their grievance into conflict,
Coming into the fire and making a secret pact,
If they falter, we shall demand and take it,
If they seize a single soul on the plain,
We shall bring down their young and seize them.
The unruly Kyrgyz lineage,
We shall drive them on and exact tribute.
If by chance they strike the frontier,
We shall fight them in the Kyrgyz manner.
Closely surrounding them on four sides,
We shall shout and cast terror upon them,
On the dire day when they are hemmed in,
Warrior, we shall take the fourfold blood-price.
With neither complaint nor quarrel,
With neither guile nor hidden secret,
Without the least corrupt dispute,
We shall take the realm of forty khans.
If they rage and fight,
If they scatter and fight,
We shall spill their wrathful blood.
We shall advance under cover,
Without delay, we shall open their eyes.
Ala-Too is no land cheaply won,
If one should assail it by chance,
Those Kyrgyz are no easy people.
All twelve months, in fair weather and winter,
You take the moon’s breeze,
When the people set out for the uplands,
In the season when the horse’s tongue turns black,
When the riverbed fills with water,
When the nightingale alights upon the tree,
When the cuckoo calls at dawn,
When roe deer cry from mountain to mountain,
When the snow breaks loose from Mongul,
When the river surges and the waters swell,
When embers from the khan fill the land,
Then livestock and people take courage.
When strength enters him as he bends,
Guests will not remain on the uplands,
When the time comes to camp on the khan’s summer pasture,
How much shall I see again?
I will divide his people, leaving none behind.
I will slaughter those of his paradise,
Let it be restored after one lord has died.
After his treasury has perished,
I will scatter his abundant wealth,
If they rebel, we number thousands.
The fleet breed of the Kyrgyz,
We will strike one of their petty lords.
If we rise stiffly in wrath,
True Tengir will strike as he has said,
We will unleash a single great tumult.
The wrathful one’s people are far away,
We will plunder them, leaving nothing,
We will hand over his paradise,
You saw the paradise of those people,
We will burn their champions,
Their brave men and lions,
We will drive and gather them in the valley,
I will make it an utter scourge,
I will bring disaster upon their heads,
Destroying all their people,
I will be sated with one haul of booty.
Not merely six enemies, but a hundred enemies,
If it stands, its world is an arrow.
Hero, when his savage frenzy seizes him,
One man’s demon is his wrath.
That one is of the Kyrgyz stock,
Let us find one oppressor.
Calamity and affliction,
We will set them upon head after head,
The chief design of the Manghit,
If we charge in, we will seize it.
