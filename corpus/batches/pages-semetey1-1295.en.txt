Your gray wolf, born a lion,
Fired Ak-Kelte once,
When the gun's report rang out,
Poor Kojo darted aside.
The shot went astray,
Missing him then,
It tore away a mat-sized patch of turf.
Despairing of his precious life,
That Kojo grew enraged,
And stirred up God's weather.
The weather-stone inherited
From noble gray-wolf Alman,
He took and placed in water,
And the spell of Chinmachin
He began to recite in earnest.
Mist arose from level ground,
Clouds covered the mountains,
From the towering mountains
A black wind thundered,
And in Kojo, son of Shygai,
The guise of a weather-maker appeared.
As though poured from buckets,
Blue-speckled hail spilled from the sky,
Low mist crept down,
And then there moved
A red haze and driving sleet.
All the lowlands filled with rain,
All the foothills filled with hail,
Flurries and driving sleet,
Clouds gathered overhead,
The earth seemed to split,
And God struck the heavens.
The spell of Kakan-Chin
Poor Kojo then
Stood boldly reciting.
His fiery eyes blazed fiercely,
The lion prince Semetey,
Truly seized by his bloodlust,
Was about to assail Kojo,
Was about to grapple with him.
As he raised Ak-Kelte again,
As he was about to shoot Kojo,
Chynkojo's Black Horse—
That Black Horse, it seems,
Had wings upon its flanks,
And, when a wondrous spell was read,
Was accustomed to fly.
A mighty sorcerer and magician
Had fashioned this for Kojo:
Beneath him was his racehorse, the Black Horse.
When his precious life was pressed,
Whenever it saw an enemy in battle,
It was accustomed to rise and fly.
That Kojo was a master;
Ask not the full extent of his powers,
Kojo was a lion aided by spirits.
Resourceful, he went flying,
Kojo was a hero of formidable spirit.
Mingling with the mirage,
Chynkojo entered the tumult.
In a deed no son of man would believe,
Chynkojo entered the fray.
Much of it is false, much is true;
No one has gone and seen it,
And unless manifest God knows,
We cannot tell whether it is true.
Half is false and half is true;
Unless the Creator himself knows,
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Kojo whose deeds were formidable,
Kojo with broad right shoulder,
Kojo, a hero surpassing the world,
Kojo perfect in all twelve limbs,
Was angered and enraged,
While the racehorse beneath him,
Circling like a blue vulture,
With jezail gun and steel-tipped arrow,
Kojo girded himself once more,
