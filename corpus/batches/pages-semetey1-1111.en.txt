Go all the way to the Lama and tell him,
Say he must come to this feast of mine,
Kökötöy's festivities—
Say the buried Shibee must see them!
If he does not heed these words of mine,
If he does not consent to what I say,
If he does not come to this feast of mine,
I will slaughter and destroy his people
And leave them to the wolves.
I will shatter those of his people who remain
And drive them away on foot in misery.
When you travel farther on,
There is a gray and misty desert;
Upon the face of that desert
There is a people called the Soloon.
They lock themselves in at night;
They have walls built to bar the way.
The ruler of those Soloon
Is a khan named Orokkyr.
If you ask what the Soloon are like,
Their heads are long like mortars;
Spread across their backs,
They have hair like a pestle shaft.
Their entire wretched custom
Is to roast and eat snakes, scorpions, and lizards,
And any frogs they find
Out on the steppe.
Their khan is Orokkyr;
Take word to Orokkyr.
Say every one of those people
Who live like livestock must come;
Say they must see Kökötöy's festive feast
At Karkyra.
Say Kökötöy's son Bokmurun
Has moved here from Tashkent,
Say he has camped at Üch-Karkyra,
Say Kökötöy's great wealth
Has filled the face of the earth.
Say he must come to this feast of mine;
If he does not come to this feast of mine,
Say Kökötöy's countless spear-bearing champions
Will appear directly above him!
I will overrun and slaughter his people,
And leave them wearing black headdresses in mourning.
When you travel farther on,
There are the mines of Altyn-Too;
If one kept telling of Beijing,
Its tales would never end.
Its city, called Bakburchun in Chinese—
I know much about those people.
They raise a haze over the steppe;
They have buildings made into towers.
The Manchu khan Ezkara
Is another extraordinary hero.
He has a scroll bearing a great rank,
And a horse like Chongküröng.
Those who hear his fame take fright;
He saddles that bay horse.
The sixty Manchu burku
I will hang—
He studied foreign learning for six years.
His courage is of another order,
A peerless champion born among the Manchus.
Both a judge of horses and a warrior,
That lion is the lion you must name.
Say Ezkara, khan of the Manchus,
Must consent to these words of mine.
Say not a soul from the Manchu people must remain behind,
But all must come to Karkyra
For Kökötöy's memorial feast.
Say they must see Kökötöy's many festivities
Before their own eyes.
Say the Manchu baldheads who carry stones
Must come;
Say the strong men of the coat-wearing Chinese
Must come!
With their gauntlets put on,
Let not one of the Kalday remain.
