The sky was clear, the earth was mist,
No figure could be made out,
So dense was the battle’s turmoil
That none could tell who was who.
They surrounded the champion,
And then Chongbilgich
Exhausted every stratagem,
Making every assault he launched
Pierce to the ribs.
He commanded those who remained alive,
Proclaiming to the champions,
And crying out to all the world:
“He is renowned indeed, a true mainstay,
A hero sprung from the Burut.
Though born of woman in human form,
He is one of the demons.
This infidel who came without support—
His cunning is great, his venom strong;
A hero such as this
Is peerless, a true warrior.
He is their most vaunted son,
The lord awaited
By the Kyrgyz of Ala-Too,
The very model of might,
The dark eye of all heroes.
Surround him, champions!
If we seize him, what regret could remain?
Press in and fight him,
And let us take him alive.
To the great hero Kite we shall say,
‘We came after destroying his nest,
We dammed and diverted his water,
And tore out by the roots
The whole line of these babbling Kyrgyz.
We brought calamity upon their hero,
And bound and captured the prince
Who was lord of the Kyrgyz.’
To the heroic son of Kaldai,
Whose wrath is strong as doomsday,
In fulfillment of the fallen hero’s word,
To great Kite himself,
Let us present him as a gift.
Then let us go and claim, without yielding,
The khanship of the forty tribes.
Let us not kill him swiftly,
But put him to death in torment;
Let us not kill him in a lonely place,
But put him to death by force
Atop Beijing’s golden throne,
In its garden of ranked plane trees,
Before the fortunate carpet,
At the famous Zuu pit known to the people,
Encircled by many great lords.
The Zuu pit has a living horror,
A chasm forty fathoms deep.
Let us cast the unyielding Seytek
Into that pit.
From this swine, for not merely one forefather
But a thousand, let us exact revenge.
If Seytek falls, their backbone falls,
The Kyrgyz lose their falcon-lion,
And sacred unity departs with him.
Let us extinguish the fire kindled
By the doomed man who leads them,
Drive all his people away together,
And make them common plunder;
Thus let us take revenge
Upon the swine who destroyed our peace.
Close in upon him, champions!
He is a lord, he is a khan,
He is a sacred soul.
Once we have cast him yellowing into the pit,
Not one of those swine will remain,
Not a drop of blood will be left in their veins,
Not a soul will remain to show courage.
Wage the struggle on a broad front;
Without killing or letting him vanish,
This is my command, champions:
Hoist him upon the Kerik-elephant
And say, ‘Here is the enemy who came!’
