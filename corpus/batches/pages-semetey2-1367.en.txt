Your wisdom will return when the day comes.
Do not overthink it;
Kyyaz, take my counsel,
Take a wife like Aichürök.
Who would not desire and love
Chürök, the peri’s daughter,
Such a radiant wonder!
If you desire dear Chürök,
Do not call her a wife, but a maiden,
That wonder whose path has been barred.
If fear of Chürök makes you hesitate,
Speak your mind more plainly;
If you will not take her, tell the truth!
For your sake, brave Kyyaz,
I made my quiver ready;
I destroyed Semetey,
My own kinsman.
I bound Khan Bakay,
And when he defied me,
I hewed the right shoulder
Of my own kinsman Külchoro.
You will never prosper, Kyyaz:
I have delivered the prize into your hands.
I settled and made Talas my home,
Placing greater hope in you alone,
Brave Kyyaz, than in my own kin.
Heed my words, Kyyaz;
Cease your foolish talk, Kyyaz!
I thought you were a true man, Kyyaz,
But you tremble for fear of a woman—
You are a witless child, Kyyaz!
I settled along the Six Rivers
And stood by you, accursed Kyyaz,
Thinking you closer than my father.
Let what has been said guide you;
Lion Kyyaz, heed my counsel.
Take that woman Aichürök,
That red-robed, slender-waisted,
Quivering infidel;
Reach out your cold hand
To that sly-tempered shrew,
That gleaming infidel,
That exceedingly beautiful woman,
And claim her as your prize.
My friend, move your people
From the land of the Seven Rivers,
Climb the mountain pass and settle there;
Take a fairy beauty and prosper yourself!
Recognize the wisdom in my words;
My warrior Kyyaz, heed my counsel.
We stirred the stars in the heavens;
We became friends unto the next world,
Chewing grains of white wheat,
Licking the edge of white steel,
Settling side by side,
And pledging ourselves for eternity.
Do not babble, Kyyaz;
When your turn comes, wield the hoe,
And if you have abundance, tend the people.
When were you ever brought low
By parting from elder or younger brother?!
Now is the time to slaughter and destroy your foes,
Now is the time for you to be mighty;
Fortune and prosperity have alighted on your head,
Now is the time to act.
Seize the village of that bloodthirsty bastard,
Tearing it down from the ridge,
And take his sun-faced wife!
Take the groves he planted in Talas;
Take the throne from which he ruled the people!
Leave my stepmother and take my sister-in-law;
Leave them both and take the one I offer.
Take his red-tailed camels;
Take his herds of every kind.
Brave Kyyaz, whose fortune has come from above,
Your lord has spoken—heed my counsel.
If Chürök agrees to the match,
Let us place authority in her hands
