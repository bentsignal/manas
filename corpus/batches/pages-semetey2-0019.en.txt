His heroism was of another order.
In Beijing of the Kakanchin,
This is the building he erected;
If you ask about that destroyer,
This is the mighty khan of the monks.
He was more skillful than a spearman,
A mighty one who could subjugate
The whole world.
Born in this Beijing,
He fought without rest in the Altai
Against your father, hero Manas;
That dog raised the turmoil.
Beijing there is a vast, gated city;
It has a thousand learned men.
If I speak of hero Kongurbai,
His tales will never end.
His tiger bore fifteen cubs;
No mortal has gone near him.
Speaking of Beijing, there is disaster:
Skilled on horseback and swift on foot,
It has champions to face mortals.
This year, my son Semetey,
Understand your uncle's words;
My lion, my son, heed me.
This year, without turning aside,
To Beijing
Let us bring a mighty spectacle, my son.
Let us raid every horse
Of that Kalcha slave,
Then grow fat and refreshed.
Yesterday, truly, those few people
Who let themselves be taken to Kakan-Beijing
Must have had weak horses.
Let us prepare swift horses;
Their clothing must have worn out,
So let us prepare strong clothing.
These numerous Chinese people—
Their multitude is like black worms.
Let us consult Chyiyrdy and Kanykei,
Take counsel with Kanykei,
And prepare ample provisions well.
Let us deliberate together;
Then afterward, in Beijing,
Let us create a great tale.
My son, Beijing is no playground;
Gated Beijing with its royal court
Is no place for games.
It has a desert forty days across,
An accursed land such as this.
My son, whoever has inhabited Beijing,
Those who attacked Beijing
Never turned back to see their homes.
Beijing's forty-day expanse
Has a broad river road.
Misery awaits whoever encounters it.
Precisely aimed at them,
There lies a stretched net.
Whoever falls upon accursed Beijing
Has misery from which he cannot recover.
The sand blazes with heat;
Set a cauldron upon it,
And it boils roaring and surging.
Before one can say a word,
In the blink of an eye,
Not a spoonful of water remains.
One reaches the deathless desert;
If you encounter that desert,
My foal, your strength will leave you.
Let us not be caught unawares;
So the sun's force cannot penetrate,
Let us prepare our clothing well.
If your stride reaches those many people,
Let us drive them all away.
With wise Bakai leading the road,
Taking no fewer than sixty thousand troops,
And abundant provisions
For that great army to eat,
Taking plentiful provisions with us,
