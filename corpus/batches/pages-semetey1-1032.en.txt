Without saying, "These are in-laws I have never seen,"
The poor hero grew furious.
He drew himself up in a surge of power,
Scolding Kulchoro.
Your gray-maned hero went onward,
Water streaming from the tops of his boots.
All the maidens and brides
Cried out then
And took shelter in their pavilion.
His eyes reddened like a lion's;
The hero Semetey advanced,
Giving voice to his anger.
Chilled through, the fourteen horses
Reared and played,
Stamping the black earth.
Semetey raged
And then berated Kulchoro.
Furious and surging with wrath,
Without caring that the people stood there,
He scolded Kulchoro:
"You are a ruinous boy!
When will you seek the ford?
Whenever you find a bedraggled maiden,
You linger and play with her!
If you carry on this way, you wretch,
You will bring disaster upon me!
You are a calamitous boy;
Beside a maiden before you,
You stretch yourself out and lie there.
If you carry on this way, you wretch,
You will bring disaster upon me!
Whenever there are maidens riding a swing,
You stay to frolic and play;
Whenever there are maidens digging dye,
You play with them improperly!
If you continue like this, little brat,
You will bring disaster upon me!
Whenever there are maidens gathering reeds,
You stay there playing eagerly;
Whenever there are maidens gathering dung,
You stay there playing foolishly!
If you encounter a caravan maiden,
Without saying, 'My elder brother will grieve,'
You remain there playing senselessly.
If you do not abandon this conduct,
You will bring disaster upon me!
When you mount Buurul, you dash away
And vanish in a frenzy;
Without once turning to think,
'My elder brother, my champion, was left behind,'
You forget him entirely!
You beg deceitfully for a horse to ride
And then begin
An uproar beyond all restraint!
Upon a bare and windswept ridge,
You leave me looming like a frozen camel
And ride away!
When you see a maiden, you whine,
And follow alongside her;
You forget your elder brother, great as a mountain,
In the barren steppe,
And deceive him and leave!
You do not ride your own horse;
You doomed wretch,
You never go about speaking the truth!
You are a calamitous boy;
You begin excessive suffering
And trample your elder brother like a traveler
In the empty steppe!
You ought to think, Choro;
Abandon this conduct, Choro.
There are many dark, rugged mountains,
And many enemies who may clash with us;
There are many treacherous passes,
And many warriors with whom blood will be spilled.
How many enemies are there to grapple with?
Behind us, watching and stalking,
Is Kalcha, who slaughtered your father.
