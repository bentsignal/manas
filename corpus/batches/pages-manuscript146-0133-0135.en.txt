Without delay, this affair fell apart,
His enemies multiplied,
Have we been left bewildered and without recourse?
The condition of the Kyrgyz became plain,
This wretched old Tekeche,
His oppressive nature became clear.
What good is there in blood being shed?
Countless troubles came into view.
Ah, taking command through cunning,
He still possessed the prestige to seize power,
That noble one was Kenen’s son.
He had occupied two lands,
From of old the Chinese had been foes,
As for Ala-Too and the Kyrgyz,
Few remained who could become a people.
Ebegey and wise Kongoy,
I saw Chargyn from afar,
This was precious through all the ages,
Nothing like it could be found on earth,
Of this accursed swine,
If any Kyrgyz fell into his hands,
There seemed no end to what he did.
This land where our cord was cut and our swaddling washed,
This Ala-Too our forefathers tended,
Its true state upon the earth could no longer be seen,
Life would not be set right,
The people who had lived in freedom,
He would not let the people settle in their summer pastures,
He plundered their sustenance and ruined them,
Our lot in this place would not improve.
For this wretch, half the people would spill their blood,
This land would come under his heel,
The people and land of this Kakan remained behind,
His boastful tongue was backed by brute force,
A man must rise to stand against him.
Can a surrounded man prosper?
O dear people, lofty as a mountain,
What course could we take?
Now the enemy is coming,
What remedy could there be?
We have not the strength to fight,
Take heed of what you have been told,
Lion Kenen, heed your words,
I shall turn away from wrath,
Know it, if you agree to these words,
Like the Kyrgyz amid the teeming multitude,
Know that I am one of your people here,
The torment of anger,
Know that you will depart unharmed if you consent.
Ebegey and Köböy said this,
And, having spoken, drew their words to a close,
Hero Jeljetpes then said this,
Look outside, wolf-eyed one,
My enemies will fall and drink blood,
Is the open field my hero's battleground?
Without blood being spilled in that hour,
Without the great conflict becoming famed,
Will he then set about fighting?
If he bows his head before the warriors,
Will all good fortune not depart?
The spirited thoroughbred racer,
The brave man headed by a khan,
Will he not die and pass away in regret?
Let us strike that bristling swine,
Will the fox not catch him in the end?
If an unconquered people bows its head,
Will it not shrink today from what it sees?
Will it not draw near to witness it?
Will it not say that its heart is with me?
He chose his warriors and launched the attack,
Would he not unleash the valiant Kyrgyz?
Would he not cut down whoever resisted?
Would he not send forth warriors sharp as swords?
If one fell into his hands, would he let him go?
Would he not slaughter those seeking protection?
Would he not consume even their mutton?
No trembling khan remained,
Would he not strip the people bare?
Not merely your livestock, but your head,
Would he not make it an object of cruel sport?
What your treasury had yielded,
Would he not seize it before all the world?
Would he not strike anyone who faced him?
His forefather was Aleke,
After him came the warrior Kancha,
Would he not exact blood-price from the Bulgars?
The Kyrgyz cannot attain your vengeance,
Can the small Kyrgyz people not recover?
“Indeed, father, here it is,” he said,
Would he forget the heroes?
Would he not bring down whoever confronted him?
So he spoke of a feud whose end none could reach,
The black host came once again,
Many binding words poured forth,
He told the tale through to its end.
When the warrior Kenen heard this,
His soul boiled like flame,
At the depraved Chinese swine,
You had been an enemy since ancient times,
How could we remain meek?
Humiliating the lion-hearted leader,
Casting Kongur down upon the black earth,
Putting him into Kozu’s wicked hands,
Letting an enemy rule the people.
