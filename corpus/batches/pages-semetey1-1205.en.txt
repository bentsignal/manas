Grain powder roasted in the guns;
The long guns cracked,
The short guns shrieked,
The ochogor guns boomed,
And the syrbarang guns thundered.
The jamby stood unmoving.
With cotton sash and broad boots,
Kongurbay, khan of the Kechils,
Stretched tall and fired;
His archer drew the bow,
But the jamby did not move.
Ezkara, khan of the Manchus,
Advanced and fired;
His shot vanished from sight,
And no one knew
Where his bullet had gone.
The Black Kalmyks lamented;
All the rest
Shouted in the Kakanchyn tongue
And fired again, crying out.
The jamby did not bend
When the infidels shot and passed.
Koshoy charged and arrived;
Under Koshoy's command
Were expert marksmen who could shoot an eye.
With your uncle Koshoy at their head,
The specialists came.
Closing in with a thousand marksmen,
Koshoy kept firing,
But the jamby did not stir or twitch,
Nor shift or quiver.
They fired turn by turn
And lay taking turns.
All the marksmen side by side
Saw such a spectacle.
All their bullets whistled,
Flashed like stars,
And from the ochogor's mouth
Smoke billowed like a chimney.
The marksmen craned forward,
The bowmen stood on tiptoe.
Toshtuk entered and fired,
But the jamby did not bend.
They raced and shot;
Infidel and Muslim
Fired in alternation,
Struggling over
Kokotoy's jamby.
Infidel and Muslim
Brought strength to their khans;
It became a deed never forgotten
Until the end of the age.
Powder shook from their breastplates.
"This is an ill-starred game," they said,
And the khans despaired of life.
When the people had lost hope,
Saintly hero Bakay,
Follower of Khan Manas—
His sacred power was immense;
Surely dear Bakay was no common man.
He whipped his blue tulpar,
Rode up and fired once,
And cut through one tanap of cord.
Making Kartkurong prance,
Agybay of Argyn Khan
Rode up and fired once,
And cut through one tanap of cord.
He who turned from the black faith to the true,
Who fled the infidels and became an exile,
Almambet rode up and fired once,
And cut through one tanap of cord.
Wearing a blue rapys coat,
Mounted on a mottled blue horse,
Whom no wrestler could overpower
And no lion's tooth could pierce,
Chubak rode up and fired once,
And cut through one tanap of cord.
