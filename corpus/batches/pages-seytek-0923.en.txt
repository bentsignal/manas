And while all living beings stood thus,
Kuyaly of Kaiypchal
Also began her holy war.
Then Seytek spoke,
And these were the words he said:
“When he rages, roaring,
And summons his people and army,
Sarybai at their head
Roars and stirs up conflict.
The sin and the merit alike,
If he turns to evil, are his own.
Amid the four sacred duties,
Kaiypchal's champion maiden,
That surpassing, impetuous warrior—
This infidel Sarybai,
Who carries men away,
This is the mouth that has uttered so much,
This is the army standing ready to fight,
This is the surging, frantic people.
Like a grievous affliction,
This is the moment long awaited.
The tormented, desperate people
Cannot walk for their pain.
That swine who calls himself a warrior—
I will crush the monster;
If he fights, I will soak him in blood,
Hack him apart, and still his breath.
Talas is no easy land;
Kuyaly, who tests warriors,
Is no feeble or ordinary hero.
This infidel, son of a demon,
Is no lion equal to her, if you consider it.
Let us strike a terrible blow;
If fortune favors us, let us take him.
From the grievous death that has come,
Let us shield the people.
Let us test the strength
Of this vicious, wrathful swine;
Let us grapple and join battle.
Until we have grappled and fought,
Until we have rescued his great nation
From its misery,
The early hope has grown late;
My two eyes have become four with watching.
If my frail soul may rejoice,
My warrior heart pounds;
Oh, joyful hour!
Sarybai, whom I have sought,
Has appeared before my face.
What was broken in me is restored;
Thus my soul rejoices.
I lower my spear and face him,
And seize the fighting arm.
My poor soul is so overjoyed
That I can scarcely stay upon the earth.
Though I fight another six days,
My mounting fury will change;
If he rages, shall I flee
From that swine who calls himself a warrior?
Let us find a way to settle his account.
My name is Kuyaly;
I do not boast idly.
The wings beneath me
Have fought in desert and amid armies.
This infidel Sarybai—
Boasting is his habit.
Do not grieve, asking whether your vengeance died
While he still raged before his death;
Call him a doomed, overbearing wretch,
And let not his people remain forever unreformed.
Split apart his arrow-proof coat;
The calamity that has come from him—
Try warding it off with your own hands.
Ride out in single combat,
And clash with him once more.
These are the words I speak:
I shall cast my challenge to the moon;
The misery he brought upon the people
I shall meet and turn aside.
If I die, dear people,
