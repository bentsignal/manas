Did Bakai not lead the way
To Kakanchin's Beijing
At least seven times?!
Has old age not arrived
And taken up its place beside Bakai?!
Has this accursed, barren world
Not made sport of Bakai
And gone on deceiving him?!
Here he goes on holy war,
And in Khan Bakai's hand
Is the red banner with a golden finial.
When fortune was granted in my youth,
My Kula horse pranced beneath me,
With twelve companions at my side.
Truly, your uncle Bakai
Had Yraman's son Yrchy
Singing beside him,
And my dark diviner Tölö
Casting lots before him.
My jester Chalybai,
Who found the hidden cause of deeds gone astray;
Those unrivaled heroes
Were the generous hero's retinue.
When the Forty Companions traveled in splendor,
My wondrous games and revels—
This accursed, filthy world,
When I look back, never turned its face away!
Grievous hardship comes upon me
When I count the days of old!”
Kökcholok beneath him
Moved as though he might stop,
Rolling across the feather-grass plain,
While Khan Bakai upon him
Shuddered as he told his sorrows!
Your mighty uncle Bakai
Swayed and groaned;
He traveled on grieving,
His old wound stirred anew!
At a divided pass and a white ridge,
Unable to spur onward and cross,
Külchoro and Kanchoro
Could not travel steadily, those two.
The plump, evenly fat youths
Wandered around in circles.
Look here if you would see hardship:
There was no room to take them double,
And when driven on foot, they would not walk.
Refusing to walk, they lingered,
And stopped at the edge of Ak-Chiy.
Unable to contain his anger, roaring,
Your illustrious prince cursed them:
“By God, you two scoundrels,
When I drive you on foot, you will not walk,
And when I take you double, you will not fit.
Nor, you scoundrels, safe and sound,
Would you stay and guard the horses!
You do not heed the words spoken,
And will not go where you are driven!
You do not heed a man's words,
Nor would you remain behind in Talas!
I gathered argamaks, I gathered horses—
May Buurul be accepted in place of those thoroughbreds;
I gathered brave youths, I gathered men—
May Abyke and Köbösh be accepted in place of such kin!
Today I let you block my road to Beijing;
You have made many sorrows boil within me!
You two are left horseless and on foot,
And have brought upon my head
A marvel no one else could achieve!
Can there be men like you?
Is it right to call you men?
Did your hips not ache from lying down?
Was your sleep never sated?
By God, you two scoundrels,
You are no creatures the size of a fist
To be shut inside a saddlebag,
Nor are you six-year-old children
To be taken before me on the saddle!
By God, you two scoundrels,
Who wandered freely and grew up untended,
