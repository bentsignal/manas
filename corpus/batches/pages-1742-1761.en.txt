Kanykei lost her strength.
Coughing with thud upon thud,
He lifted his head from the bed.
When he rose and cried, “Oh!”
From the sovereign's mouth
Blood poured out by the bowlful.
Weeping in anguish and crying out,
Your sister-in-law lost her strength.
As the blue-maned khan grew sicker,
Unable to twist her reinlike hair,
Your sister-in-law could not stand for shrieking and weeping.
Your sovereign fell dreadfully ill.
The dark blood issuing from his mouth
Filled twelve whole pits.
Seeing the blood that flowed,
Your sister-in-law screamed and wept,
Unable to find even a fly's worth of help:
“The leopard's hour of death has come;
This unfortunate woman has no recourse.
My brow bone slopes away;
When my sovereign has passed away,
This unfortunate woman's fortune will be scant.
This poor head will drink mare's milk no more.
When the lord has passed away,
I shall yearn even for brackish water.
When the mighty one's eyes close,
My poor life will be abased.”
Then Manas said these words
(His illness now beyond all measure):
“Kanykei, its collar gold, its sleeves brass,
Its double mail with lark's-eye links,
Its brocade sash, its ample jacket,
Reinforced in places with fine iron wire,
Thwarting even Kelte's bullets,
Beyond the reach of arrows,
Impenetrable to steel points,
Though one fought six months without rest,
No bullet could pierce it.
I had Andijan and Kokand searched;
The cotton of the Six Cities and Kokand
I had brought in as tribute.
I had every part made strong,
Had it worked without stint.
Wearing it satisfied the heart;
No howling blizzard touched the body.
Akolpok, fit for battle—
Kanykei, lay it over me!
Let me turn my face to the True One;
When the angel of death draws near,
Let me close my eyes saying, ‘Allah!’
Until my dear soul gives its cry,
With Akolpok, worn in firefights,
Let me comfort myself.
Slung over the back, a mirror to the moonlike shoulders,
When life is sorely pressed,
A solace to the soul held in trust,
A matchlock when fired in play,
A Koichagyr gun when striking in earnest,
The living gun Akkelte—
