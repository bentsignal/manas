To keep them apart,
The fierce lion aided by the spirit,
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Whose father was a lion, that valiant hero,
Encountered the wrathful foe,
And fell into grievous calamity,
Fighting without rest for nine days,
Wounded in ninety places,
His strength exhausted, his power spent,
His keen steel greased with blood.
Kertelki and Kuyunboz,
With these two foremost,
Exactly twelve swift steeds were brought down.
The gray charger was felled by bullets,
The bulletproof armor was torn,
Kuyunboz was felled by bullets,
His broad trousers were ripped.
The sword shattered, its strap fell,
The spear broke, as though a mountain fell,
His borrowed life was sorely pressed,
The poor fierce lion’s father,
The warrior destined to fall,
Leaning against the ridge of Кең-Кол,
Was drenched in blood from the saber.
Sarybai grew stronger and raged,
The two peoples, Argyn and Kyrgyz,
Fled Talas in a mass,
Preparing to flee without stopping,
Loading their swift camels,
The wise ones grieving,
Saying, “So the heroes have departed,
Sarybai, the accursed swine,
Has come to finish us utterly.”
“He spared no life that pleaded,
He slaughtered everyone as he passed,
He destroyed all the falcons,
Today he has reached our very roots.”
They folded up their yurts,
And loaded them on gray camels,
Women wailed and maidens wept,
As the fierce battle began.
That precious life beloved by all,
He valued no more than a hair,
And left it bound by a thread.
They stood locked in single combat,
The earth was gouged and dust descended,
They fought without rest for seven days,
Until the field itself was ruined,
Wounded in seventy places,
His two mighty arms,
Hung powerless and numb,
His dear head, the lamp of life,
Was bloodied in many places.
The enraged infidel,
The hellish warrior of savage courage,
The fiend no living soul could withstand,
The monster who devoured those beside him,
Would split the ribs
Of any man who grappled with him.
He had hurled down many,
They called him the wrestler of doom,
And the punishment of those who opposed him,
The sentence of those already condemned,
He dealt with his own hands.
He was a giant surpassing champions,
Regarding others as less than a hair;
If you ask about that madman’s nature,
He was truly the master of strife.
Young Seytek, the fierce hero,
Steeling himself to valor,
Came forth, that fierce lion,
Leaning on his tempered-steel spear.
As brave Seytek stood there,
Ready to die if hostile death arrived,
To stand as a shield
For the descendants left to Seytek,
To challenge Sarybai,
And bring the battle to its appointed hour,
To save the surviving line
From the wrathful enemy’s hand,
