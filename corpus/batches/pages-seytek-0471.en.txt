I shall bring turmoil upon your enemy;
I tell you this with certainty:
If my mother Kanykei is alive and well,
I shall stand as your support.
But if Kanykei is gone,
Climb the ridge and plunge from it;
In your renowned princely uncle
Place no further hope.
I shall be no homeland awaiting settlement;
If my mother has passed from this world,
Külchoro, I shall be no people to you.”
Thus spoke the mighty bloodthirsty lord,
When he gave this news.
Then the trusty giant Kara said:
“I shall speed over hill and hollow;
Hold him firmly and do not let go,
While I bring my mother Kanykei.
I shall scour the land stallion-like,
And not be gone longer than a mare’s milking.”
Saying this, the giant Kara
Spoke these words to Külchoro,
Telling a wondrous and astonishing thing.
Then the giant Kara
Seized the black-thonged whip,
Swung it round in his grip,
Pressed the surpassing steed
Hard between his knees,
And raced off, raising dust,
Galloping through Küröңsai,
Until he brought him to Talas.
They dressed them in costly garments;
Among horses none could catch Jeljetpes,
And before Jeljetpes
No racing beast could keep pace.
They mounted them on Jeljetpes,
Seated Kanykei and Bakai upon him,
And took them all away, leaving none behind.
Boundless in his swiftness,
The brisk and clamorous giant Kara—
He was both warrior and lion.
Wandering in flight across the plains,
The flesh of her soles worn translucent,
My dear mother Kanykei—
Can my aged mother still be alive?
If that mother of mine still lives,
And I become young enough to seek her breast,
If I suckle at her breast and sleep,
If I breathe my poor mother’s scent
And smell it day and night,
My eyes and ears will open.
The pain within me will subside,
And the enchantments around my soul will scatter and flee.
I shall pitch my camp beside you,
My awe-inspiring hero Külchoro;
Then I shall hear your words,
Then I shall see your face.
If she is safe and alive,
Send a letter to my mother;
If you send it, send it swiftly.
My venerable white-haired mother,
My destiny and homeland before me—
If my destined homeland still exists,
Let me open my eyes and see her,
Let me suckle at her breast.
Win honor by rescuing me from the wilderness;
My dear Külchoro, ride now.
With six swift horses,
And a letter small as your palm,
Search across the face of the earth.
Set scouts and search the land,
Search the people seven days without rest,
And find my mother.
If my mother comes within my sight,
If she gives me her true breast,
If I suck and milk flows abundantly,
I shall appear before her eyes,
And pitch my camp beside you.
I shall be a binding for your waist;
Choro, heed my words.
I shall be the standard that bears the banner,
