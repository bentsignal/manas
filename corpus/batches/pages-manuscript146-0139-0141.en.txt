To the Mongols and the Manchus,
To all the great peoples,
Chongteñir issued an order.
Let us dwell upon the extent of his command,
Chongteñir acted thus,
What had been laid out was completed,
You march on, Chongteñir,
Calling it a bitter word against the Kyrgyz,
You lead the people, Chongteñir.
From the edge where the sun rises,
Chongteñir covered them with arrows,
From the north and from the south,
Chongteñir surrounded them with warriors.
Against the Chinese, the Kyrgyz were few,
The multitudes struggled, alas for those brave heads,
Before the Chinese, the people stood alone,
A grievous assault came upon them.
From every side he rained arrows,
The solitary people were hemmed in,
At that time Chongteñir,
Speaking firmly, clad them in armor,
His kingship became known,
Of that raging swine,
His giant prevailed and his challenge prevailed,
Entrusting survival to Almighty God,
Then turning upon the solitary one,
He was indeed that raging master.
Then there came a deep rumble,
Chinese and Chyban—the Kyrgyz were few,
Amid the shattered host,
Leaving neither whip nor storm behind,
They struck broadside in the struggle,
Send the champions into the attack,
All of you, halt and stand fast,
Warriors, form yourselves in ranks.
Then let this be proclaimed,
Arrange it in this manner.
If he is a warrior, if he is a lion,
If he is the bravest of the champions,
Let him come out in single combat, Kyrgyz-style,
Grant him the great turn,
Let us see the measure of his strength,
And what power he has against the Kyrgyz.
Having spoken thus, Chongteñir,
Issued a stern command.
The master of speech cried aloud,
And sent the battle toward the other side,
The red Kyrgyz, the great fighter,
They made ready at every drumbeat,
The war drum deafened the ears,
They had it beaten with a thunderous roar.
Ears deafened and heads grew numb,
It bound mind and senses,
Blood sprayed like sparks,
Across the broad battlefield,
At the place of destruction,
Ozu Khan rode out to single combat.
Like a black boar, like a fox,
The battle cry of Khan Joloy,
The champion stood apart from the others,
He mounted a horse and went against the foe,
Who had ever known such a challenge?
Do not ask what Ozu was like,
It was the day the warrior went forth,
What could a solitary lamb do?
It was a day fought for a half-share of blood,
A day when Jadigöy raised a flame,
In this faithless world.
