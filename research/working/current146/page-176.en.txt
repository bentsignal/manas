Saying, “Let that young ram be hung,”
After he had gone into their midst,
They struck him with an axe, calling him a hero.
They drove him against stone and crag.
At the end of the world,
Thinking, “I can strive onward and reach it,”
If he paid no heed to the Kalmyks,
So that even the aged would know,
The dark maral’s antlers branched apart,
For those who walked upon the earth,
For those who would possess the whole world,
Against immeasurable giants,
Against the multitude, old and full-grown,
Against the man who resisted,
His heart was young and treated the deed lightly.
When you see him, he is terrible to an enemy.
He threw the great host into turmoil,
Thinking, “I shall lay them down before his eyes.”
Against that one who would not obey,
Against the silent one who submitted,
Against the watchful, complete host,
Against the hero who resisted,
He made them endure true hardship,
And sent a rain of bullets over them.
He had them shot by Chengker,
Thus he settled their account.
Of the one who kept resisting,
He would dig out the dark spirit.
If an enemy rose within a handspan,
He plunged him into black blood.
If he was a hero who crossed the pass,
If he was a champion who grappled belt to belt,
He had him shot down again,
And made him taste close combat.
Blood by day, blood by night—
When he stood passing through wrath,
The marksman Chengker saw him.
