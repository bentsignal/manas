He truly came among them.
He stands above many
Of the stone-armored Chinese,
Above the strongest
Of the jacketed Manchus.
He is a mighty champion and a lion;
When fastened firmly at his waist,
He is a hero like an iron mace.
No such man is granted to mortals;
This surpassing falcon of a warrior
Cannot be found among mankind.
Spreading slaughter across the world,
He is a man fit to take the road;
Gripping his spear and crushing the foe,
He is a son fit to march with an army.
When fire boils like hail,
He is a son who can endure the embers.
Though an army of six hundred thousand comes,
Surging forward without sparing life,
He is a son who can withstand their bullets.
When spears are leveled and men cry out,
He is a son fit to face the massed host.
Smashing all before him and clearing a path,
He is a son upon whom one may rely.
Stopping it and not letting it flow onward,
He is a son fit to withstand a flood.
Among all round-headed mortals,
Could there be another champion like this?
He is a mighty champion and a lion.
Would it be right to speak of him otherwise?
His fierce courage is formidable indeed;
He is both a great lord and a lion.
He bowed eighty times before the idol;
The time has come for him to become Chinese with us.
The jeweled necklace of the warrior
Is one this son is worthy to wear;
The many-tribed Chinese nation—
Without bringing shame upon this people—
He is one who can govern them.
In strength and heroism,
He is a man complete in every way.
If we raise him as khan over the Khaganate,
How could Kongurbay compare with him?
He is like a shower of steel bullets;
There is none like him even in hell!”
So they raised an uproar,
And the judges put him to the test.
The multitude of Chinese khagans
Rejoiced greatly and clamored.
“This is the heir of khans,” they said,
“The master of Eselek,
The lord of this Beijing.”
Driven wild with joy,
They rang the bells with all their might;
The people of forty khanates seethed,
And closed in with mighty shouts.
Drinking spirits and holding a feast,
The heroes gathered in boundless numbers.
“A kinsman has come from the Kyrgyz,” they said,
“God has given him to us.”
They bound upright with ropes
An enormous idol as large as a house,
And staged an extraordinary spectacle
In the midst of the army.
They brought forth ninety kinds
Of parrot-tail feathers,
And had them splendidly set
Upon the crest of his armor.
They sent word to the Khagan,
Adorning it with magpie-tail feathers:
“Külchoro is needed as khan,”
And had these splendid things brought.
The standards dipped and clamor filled the air;
The people cried out and made their plans.
All beneath the vault of heaven
Held a feast to make Külchoro khan.
They purified him and robed him in ceremonial dress,
Then into a great pavilion with golden poles and copper ropes—
