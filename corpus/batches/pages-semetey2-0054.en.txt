Why did I not perish then?
Ah, my son Semetey,
May you be ruined if you call this taking horses!
Among the swarming Chinese,
One who slaughtered the people and took their herds—
Could he have an heir like you?
Can anyone prosper who calls you a living man?
My son, had you never been born, sink into darkness;
Rather than live, go and die!
Drink stones instead of liquor;
And if that is too little, my foal,
Mix in your elder Bakai’s blood and drink!
You boast without cause,
Saying you went to make war
Upon the innumerable Chinese,
Saying you raided the horses
Of a Kalda who lived out on the frontier.
There is no stallion and no riding horse,
Not one fit to mount in battle.
Be swept away together with your herd!
The devils you have driven here
Belong to a half-lunged Kalda;
Deceived in a wretched place,
Could a champion be like you?
Has anyone who calls you a hero
Ever prospered since ancient times?
You have driven here
The lice swarming at his collar;
They are all unbroken colts and fillies—
You have driven here his dogs.
From the sleeping Kalmak
You have driven here his cows;
From the Kalda lying in a hollow,
You have driven here his sheep.
Among the horses you brought,
There is no mount fit for a firefight,
Not one fit to ride on campaign!
Rather than raid horses of this sort,
Could you not have returned empty-handed,
Saying, “Fighting the black Kalmak,
My strength failed me,”
And come back ruined?
You rejoice, it seems,
Saying you have taken many horses—
Yet my noble lion Manas, companion of my youth,
Could have urinated upon stones at your age!
If only Manas had died before he was born!
My noble son, look upon
These horses you have driven here!
Does a hero not die of shame?
When fate casts it upon his head,
Does a doomed young man not see it?
Among all these horses,
There is not one fine beast
To ride with graceful bearing
At a memorial feast or celebration;
There is not one fit to gird on the Zhozhura sword
And circle the face of the earth,
Not one beast fit to ride into conflict.
Though you pull the saddle girth tight,
There is not one fit to ride into battle!
Kulchoro and Kanchoro—
Muradyl had raided away their horses,
And when we drove them on foot, they would not walk;
If we mounted them behind us, there was no room,
So the two were left on foot in the steppe.
They thought their elder would raid horses,
And drive them back toward us;
They hoped he would take
At least one thoroughbred racing horse, unless he were a dog.
The hopes of those two
Were dashed against stone on the steppe.
Semetey, in whom I placed my hope—
My son, despairing of you,
Bakai’s mouth has fallen closed.
He was your equal, your companion in age;
Parted from the companion of his own age,
This Bakai standing here—
