May his distinction resound among the people!
Cast into fire, may he not burn;
Cast into water, may he not sink.
May no mortal cause him harm;
May none who face him survive!
May no man's strength prevail against him;
May no lion's teeth pierce him!
Whoever confronts this child,
Whoever clashes with him, may he become black earth.
His uproar will never cease;
Let his name be Chubak the warrior!
I have named the child.
These words shall not be idle:
When he reaches twelve,
This son of yours, named Chubak,
Will be lion Manas's companion.
He will fight Kakan-Chyn;
He will be Bakburchun's enemy.
Let no mortal confront him;
May none who face him survive.
He is the champion, he is the lion;
The one who throws the whole world into turmoil
Is lion Chubak the warrior.
When he reaches twelve,
Let him go and settle in Talas;
Let him be a companion to Manas!”
So spoke the holy wanderer,
Stroking his forehead three times.
The people now thronged around;
Giving the child to Baltay,
The wanderer who had named him
Vanished from sight.
Akbaltay, granted a child,
Scattered all he had and grew resolute.
“Akbaltay's Chubak,” they said,
“The little one who upsets the world!”
His formidable name became renowned.
For Chubak, now given a name,
They hung an amulet around his neck.
His sixty stepmothers
Cherished, tended, and raised him.
When he reached six—
He emerged as a burning ember.
For unruliness, this Chubak
Had no equal among the Noygut people.
In fights he killed children,
And troubled Baltay.
“My Chubak is nothing but turmoil;
Someone will cast the evil eye on him,” he said.
Lamenting every day,
When he rose in the morning,
He duly performed the rites.
Six black bulls
He would have cut to pieces.
For Chubak's sake, Baltay's
Countless livestock were consumed.
Though his countless livestock were consumed,
“Keep my only son safe,”
His father Akbaltay
Would pray to God.
He who threw the world's people into turmoil—
Such was the origin
Of Chubak, Akbaltay's son.
That Chubak was no common fellow.
Of the little one who upset the world,
Baltay had seen the unruliness.
When he reached six,
His father Akbaltay,
Down in the city of Mecca,
Entrusted him to a mullah.
Those who saw his renown took fright.
Akbaltay's Chubak
Under the learned scholars
Studied for six years.
When he reached twelve,
He received a letter from the caliph;
Joining hands with all twelve,
He was named Illustrious Champion.
While he was in Mecca,
He witnessed a deed of precious worth.
As he slept, within his dream,
He found himself amid an uproar.
Into a vast and unending
Tumult he had entered.
As he looked into the tumult,
God put him to trial.
Before him appeared
A white-bearded holy wanderer.
Standing there, the wanderer said:
“God Most High created you,
And you were named Chubak;
He made you a lion to destroy the unbelievers.
Born with surpassing fortune,
The warrior Manas in Talas,
The youngest of the six lions,
God's beloved mortal—
He made you his equal companion.
The two of you will unite;
You will scatter the unbelievers' livestock,
And open the way for the faith of Islam.
You will quell your enemies' pride,
And ease your kinsfolk's grief.
Find him; that is the warrior Manas,
That is the lion born for good fortune.
When you come into his hands,
He will ride far and wide;
Throughout the whole world
He will journey, visiting every part.
No lion will remain untested against him;
No warrior will remain unchallenged with the spear.
He is the lion you should seek;
He is the warrior who will brighten your face.”
Having spoken of Manas, the wanderer
Vanished from sight.
When Chubak awoke with a start,
It was Khizir who had spoken;
It was God's doing;
It was a dream he had seen.
Chubak did not yet know Manas,
Had not yet ridden as his companion,
Had not yet charged the enemy with a shout;
The peoples did not yet know Chubak.
That Chubak grew wrathful,
And began an upheaval.
Now the warrior Chubak girded himself
And left the city of Mecca.
