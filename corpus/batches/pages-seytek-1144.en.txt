Cried, “Now I am left alone!
There were ninety men as great as mountains,
Though all ninety might die,
Kaisar alone was a true hero.
Among the coat-clad Chinese
He was the son of the strongest,
From the pure breed that girded itself with stone,
He surpassed even the great.
He met the enemy and was destroyed;
Our dream has turned ill.
Our khan died before even striking—
Has such a thing ever happened in all the ages?
Deprived of our renowned champion,
Our forces are thrown into distress.
Our splendid hero Kaisar is gone,
And now our backbone is broken.
Having let the Kyrgyz triumph
And surrendered mighty Kaisar
To those accursed Burut,
How can we retreat without shame?
What answer shall we give
To our great leader Kite?”
So saying, Chongbilgich
Roared out his grief,
And every mortal who heard him
Shuddered from head to foot.
Pale and ashen,
Wild with fury and scowling,
He loomed, scattering his wrath;
The wretched warrior from the khagan’s realm
Reared like a towering mountain.
The sorcerous warrior-hero
Truly brought on the end of the age;
His valor became plain
When he roared and charged—
Not only men but beasts,
Champions with strength upon strength,
Overwhelming, conquering monsters,
Devourers of all they encountered—
Every listening ear was stunned,
And at the sight of his dread aspect
Every living creature
Despaired utterly of life,
Eyelashes quivered as though singed,
Lips twitched and whispered,
When roused, his words boomed,
And the massed teeth in both jaws
Clattered like a mill.
They charged Seytek all together,
Loosing bows like pouring rain,
Shooting arrows like falling hail,
Thrusting spears with clamor.
In their midst, the hero Seytek
Turned Kulansur’s head;
There was no refuge but God,
Without so much as twitching brow or eyelid,
The poor hero now stood watching.
Raising his shield against arrows,
Beating spears aside,
Striking back and forth,
He made Kulansur dance,
And laid low everyone he met,
Waging war without a care.
Upon his great Kerik beneath him,
Bilgich laid the coarse whip,
Spurred in from the flank,
And drove his spear home.
His stratagem prevailed,
And the spear of the hero Bilgich
Struck Seytek cruelly.
God forbid the sight!
At that moment Chongbilgich
Shook off all grief and sorrow,
Recovered without pausing for breath,
And where he had thrust once, thrust four times,
Driving each straight without wavering,
With greater force than before,
Striking harder with every thrust,
Driving each straight without wavering,
Driving each hard without wavering.
