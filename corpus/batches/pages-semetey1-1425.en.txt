From the far bank to the near bank,
Even a courser cannot sport across it;
As for the depth of that dark water,
Forty ropes lowered in cannot sound it.
This is the lay of the land;
My orphan, my son, listen well!
Saying, ‘Capable Bakai told me,’
Go there and see it with your own eyes.
He used to be lord of the land;
No wild ass escaped before his face,
And no kinsman of Kongurbai
Ever stirred a step before me.
This year Kongurbai must have scouted the road,
Wearing the flanks of his horses lean,
That swine whose home burned
That the ear had heard of and the eye had seen,
Traveling every road.
Finding no enemy, he must have turned back;
His last instructions to Karagul
That shambling swine must have spoken,
Telling him to let them graze freely.
Kongurbai must have added Algara to the herd
And then returned,
The secret of his coming death, that doomed man
Must have told Karagul!
My hawk born of Manas,
Do not go by day—go by night;
Raise a battle cry and seize the horses!
Drive his herd and bring it back.
If Karagul, son of Katal,
Should happen to fall into your hands,
Slaughter him as a sacrifice;
Cut off his head, fasten it securely,
And bring it to your uncle!
The road to Beijing is over there;
My valiant orphan, reconnoiter the road!
Saying, ‘My valiant uncle told me,’
Store this old man’s words in your pack.
Through gray haze and burning dust,
Judge the way and reconnoiter the road.
My champion, whom I beheld late in life,
Your aged uncle, an old man resembling a prophet—
Heed his words!
Go straight into the dark mass of horses
And thrust out your spear;
Face the heedless slave’s herd
And raid and seize it!
Pour out your wrath, my only one,
And strike down his countless black livestock.
Of your grieving uncle Bakai,
Ease the anguish held in his breast!
Let the horses scatter at your attack,
And let the fat ones be slaughtered;
Let every beast and predator
Feast upon them with a blessing!
Amen, my child, amen!
May the affair with China run its course;
For the people until the end of days,
May it be a deed they will not forget!
Scatter Kongurbai’s livestock;
Of your mole-marked uncle Bakai,
Open his eyes wide as umach!
You have set out upon a perilous horse raid;
You have gone alone toward Beijing,
My only one, whom I beheld late in life—
I entrust you to the one God!
Of the teeming infidels,
Raid and seize their horses;
One who spares an enemy will be wounded—
Every one of the horse herders—
Kill them without a moment’s hesitation!
May God prosper your undertaking;
My champion, whom I beheld late in life,
May your lion father Manas support you!
To the one creator God
I have commended you;
Your khan and uncle, now grown old,
May he see you return alive!
