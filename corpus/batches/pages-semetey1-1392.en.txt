Two rode horseback and two went on foot—
The journeying adventure
Of the champion son of the gray wolf,
Who set his course toward Beijing,
The valiant man's bold exploit.
Along the road he himself had seen,
Beside valleys, springs, and streams,
Leading them on their way came
Their remarkable uncle Bakai.
Their uncle, elder Bakai,
Was an exceptionally knowing saint!
His words were clear while he lived;
Bakai, a judge of warriors,
Was clairvoyant in this world.
His speech was open and plain,
A resourceful saint,
Your uncle, elder Bakai!
Crossing lands of many kinds,
Passing three successive ridges,
How could your valiant man hold back?
Ögüz-Ashuu and Tai-Ashuu,
The six successive Chon-Chapky passes—
He reached the broad valley.
Kichi-Jyldyz and Chon-Jyldyz—
He crossed the two Jyldyz lands,
Into the hollow of Kichi-Jyldyz,
Along the Kara-Suu,
To Chatkal with its winding ford,
Where the former host had gathered
And marched in a winding column.
On the qibla side stood Boz-Döbö,
Where wretched Manas and his forty companions,
Reveling wildly and raising dust,
Used to play ordo.
Within that ordo ground
Were the khan's knucklebone and all the rest,
Everything left behind
By the bloodthirsty holy prince!
At the nape of Boz-Döbö,
At the place called Kejige, he halted;
In his grief, your uncle
Lamented the hero sultan.
He began with sorrowful words,
And began to voice his grief!
“With my paired sable cap
Swaying upon my head,
While we joined Manas in lament and song,
In the days when I lived in joy,
Ajybay and I were the strongest
Of the forty companions, and we were khans.
While noble Manas lived,
Even when only we two remained,
We were men like prophets!
Now my camp has shifted and the night has passed;
Alas, while Manas lived,
Those joyful days passed too.
The land remains, but he is gone;
When I reflect, such days have passed.
Leaving Khan Bakai to lament,
This cruel world has tormented me!”
Their uncle, elder Bakai,
His golden-cheeked battle-axe
Braced against his side, groaning,
Mighty Bakai in his ordo
Slumped down with a groan!
His former grief flared anew;
The breast of your mighty uncle Bakai
Rattled and heaved,
His beard trembling strand by strand;
Tears flowing from both eyes
Quivered down over his chest!
On this side lay the Kara-Suu,
