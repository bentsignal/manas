He brought them in as plunder.
To every son of a father, equal to a hair,
The steeds captured as spoils
He distributed, every one.
Some who found their opportunity
Took fine steeds as plunder;
Even the poorest of the Kyrgyz
Received red gold and jewels.
“Manas, born so precious—
May he be kept safe and sound!
Do not grieve over this, Jakyp.
Gray-maned Manas is that lion.
If he survives, watch and see:
He is the lion who will relieve your grief,
The resourceful, eloquent warrior.
He will join what has been broken,
Gather what has been scattered,
Rekindle your extinguished fire.
If this boy survives,
He will revive your deadened soul.
From the swarming Manchu infidels
He will demand an accounting and become khan.
If he survives, you will see:
Through him the downcast man
Will find peace.
For this people of forty Kyrgyz households,
He is the one who wishes them a life.
He will soothe the burning heart,
Open eyes clouded like porridge.
Set him in front: he brings good fortune.
Lead him along: he is like a countless army.
Enough, my wealthy Jakyp!
Your exposed back is covered now.
The Creator has given you Manas;
Honor has now been restored.
With blessings like a mountain ridge,
Is not this boy your support?
A true gray-maned one to gird your waist—
Is not this boy a lion?
If your Manas survives,
He will be the lion who finds your prosperity.
If your Manas survives,
He will be the lion who brings your laughter.
He will make your yurt's felt coverings rich with fat.
Do not stand against him, wealthy Jakyp.
God has given him fittingly;
He will make you khan on every side.
He will make your yurt's lattice rich with fat.
Do not grieve, wealthy Jakyp.
He will make you khan wherever you go.
He will bring your wishes to fulfillment.
Taking spoils wherever he fights,
All your chests
He will fill with yellow gold and treasure.
He will make things as your heart desires;
All your little coffers
He will fill with blue-silver treasure.
If this Manas survives,
When storms come, no wind will touch you:
He will be a sheltered wood, a dense forest.
When claimants come, no livestock need be paid:
He will be a dark, gray-maned warrior.
We were a people of forty Kyrgyz households,
Parted from our people in sorrow.
I look to him as my support;
Manas seems to me like all those Kyrgyz.
You grieve without cause,
Saying, ‘My boy has turned unruly.’
Can grief pay any debt?
Do not grieve, wealthy Jakyp.
Does a man afraid of death prosper?
The spoils Manas has won—
Let our forty hearths divide them.
Before death comes tomorrow,
Let us grow fat and regain our strength!
What the Creator has inscribed,
Let us undergo when it comes upon our heads.
For the one son Manas,
Before the teeming Khakan and Chinese,
Let us die as a sacrifice.
Do not fret and fuss, wealthy Jakyp.
Let us scatter all our livestock.
With Manas as our support,
Let us flee toward the Kyrgyz!
By staying here in Altai,
What inheritance will we attain?
With Manas as our support,
Come, let us go to the Kyrgyz!
We were a people of forty Kyrgyz households;
We were not living, but dead.
We were among the dead.
Amid the swarming infidels
We remain:
When have we ever been alive?”
Akbaltay spoke at length.
The forty Kyrgyz households gathered,
Taking Manas as their support.
They castrated their rams, making them wethers;
They fattened all their sheep;
They castrated their bulls, making them oxen;
They gelded their stallions, making them riding horses;
They castrated their male camels, making them geldings.
Thus the dear Kyrgyz people,
Planning their escape,
Pleading with the Almighty,
To find their cherished homeland,
The dear Kyrgyz made ready.
When the seven hundred champions and warrior Kochku
Had all been killed together,
When their great wealth had been scattered,
Tagylyk of the Chinese,
When he fled toward Esenkan that day,
A star was eclipsed in the sky;
Manas failed to see him.
Tagylyk escaped in flight,
Traveling in great haste,
Covering many a long stretch between,
To the tent with golden pillars,
To the heroic warrior Esenkan,
To the court with gates of iron,
To the heroic warrior Esenkan's
Royal fortress.
All around was a grove of plane trees;
In the midst of those plane trees
Was Esenkan's golden throne,
A sight to satisfy the heart.
Of many different kinds of carpets,
Sixty had been laid out.
The wonder of those carpets was this:
Soaked in water for forty years,
Not even an edge would fade.
Left in water for eighty years,
Not a scrap would rot away.
In his pleasure garden,
Esenkan was sitting
Upon his royal throne.
Beside Esenkan,
With coral plumes and precious beads,
The khan of Lung-Tung was among them.
With armored coats spreading wide,
The splendor of khans was there.
With jackets swelling out,
Mighty champions were there as well,
Men who cut off people's heads,
