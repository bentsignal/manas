The sovereign among women.
A bright sun fashioned by God,
A paradise among women, a sacred night—
My dear mother Kanykey,
Can she be a living soul who has not died?
My dear mother, this paradise,
Can she still be alive, not dead?
If only my mother would come quickly;
Facing toward the qibla,
With sharp steel for my pillow,
Let me open my mouth as wide as a cavern.
Let her bare her right breast,
Let her feed this ash-starved one,
Press my wretched self to her bosom,
And nurse me like a little child.
If my cavernous mouth opens,
If my poor mother is still alive,
And white breast milk is sprinkled in my mouth,
I shall be freed from this affliction.
My lion-born, heroic younger brother,
My brave brother guarded by the spirit,
Then I shall turn to you.
I shall look into your eyes.”
As he spoke thus,
Old Mother Kanykey wept,
Saying, “The bloodthirsty hero has come to this,”
And all the people watching wept.
No tiny soul could leave without crying out;
The sorrowful sobbed and wept.
His wretched mother Kanykey
Would have died without regret;
Not a fly-sized shred of life remained in her.
Her heart heaved and her body melted,
She swayed like a graceful gray pacer,
Rocked like molten lead,
Her body loosening and trembling;
All twelve sinews and eighty bones
Melted without remainder and trembled.
Her whole body shuddered,
And white milk tingled in her breasts.
“The sorcerers joined together
And carried me away.
I was led astray and passed on,
Parted from my people and gone.
In Jelpiniş beneath the earth,
I remained here for three years.
Of the pleasures of this world
I have often had my fill.
In the desert of Medina,
On the shore of Jan Baspas,
I remained there for three years.
Then I turned and came back;
I know that I arrived
At this black cave of the local sorcerer.
Eighty maidens are my soul-companions,
And I live in their company.
Once I ruled a people and maintained a nation,
But my greatness too was left behind;
Unable to hear or see,
I suffer here in misery.
Is my bloodthirsty head truly that of a khan?
To attain my heart’s desire,
To turn and return to my people,
To the city with its palace and the troubled nation,
To go back there again,
To find once more my brother, child, and wife—
To find and reclaim them,
My timely words have come too late,
And my two eyes have strained into four.
Hearing Külchoro’s voice clearly,
My old affliction stirred anew.
When I heard this, Külchoro,
Enemy of the infidel,
I halted and mourned an ancient grief.
If you truly are Külchoro,
And I am truly your gray-maned lion,
Then are my spoken words mere lament?
The finest wearer of a flowing dress,
The most gracious among women,
The purest of the long-haired,
