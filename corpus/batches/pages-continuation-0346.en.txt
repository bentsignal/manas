Even if a mountain came instead of a man,
They had strength enough to crush it.
He had a multitude of marksmen as companions;
The finest of those companions
Was Yengkor of the royal treasury,
The ablest of spearmen,
The finest deviser of stratagems.
None could match his horsemanship;
If he narrowed his eye and took aim,
No winged creature escaped alive.
He was calamity incarnate,
The trusted support
Of murderous Chyntengir.
He was now advancing,
Ready to cast his eye
Upon the gray-maned wolf, Er Kenen.
Er Kenen's Kertulpar
Trailed a tail like a parrot's.
If a ninety-day desert lay ahead,
He galloped without slowing.
He was a beast close to a demon,
Endowed with the nature of a tulpar.
Yengkor aimed at Kertulpar,
Taking the shoulder blade as his mark,
The edge of the thick neck,
The place where the spine would break,
The very center of his measure,
The dark pupil of death.
He measured carefully and fired.
Kertulpar beneath Kenen
Reared and crashed down.
Stricken with boundless grief,
Parted from Kertulpar,
Surrounded on every side by troops,
With abundant misery boiling,
With neither battle cry nor people,
Er Kenen stood alone in the fight.
He had no sheltering people around him,
No stallion to mount when wearied,
No Ebegey or Kogoy,
Those heroes who waged mighty battle
And came to rescue him.
He despaired of his dear life.
“Now I will capture this shameless man
Who has been left without a horse,”
Thought the boastful hero Yengkor.
Thinking himself a lion,
That madman believed himself alone
Equal to the whole world,
And came striding near.
The lion Kenen saw him.
His nar-kesken was in his hand;
He seized it firmly by the hilt
And drew the sword
From its sheath.
The hero and lion without a steed
Neither hurried nor faltered.
Thinking, “Here at last is death,”
He valued torment and death
No more than a trouser cuff,
For the Lord himself
Had bestowed upon him
Measureless courage.
He gripped and whirled the sword.
When the boar-like swine
Came within cutting distance,
He struck him across the neck.
The sword reached him with a whistle;
From artery and neck flesh
Black blood hissed.
His head, huge as a yurt,
Tumbled rattling to the ground;
Yengkor fell dead.
Fearsome Tengir saw
That Yengkor had died.
He threw the whole world into panic,
Spurring onward Kula-ker beneath him.
He shouted to his mace-men,
Calling his great champions:
“There is one Burut and a multitude of you;
What kind of people are the tribal Chinese?
Surge, Manchus, surge!
Knead him into the earth and trample him!”
Shouting for his spear of greatest havoc,
The huge spear made by Ebegey,
He bellowed and cried,
Bending and flattening birch and willow,
And summoned his countless champions.
At the horseless, weakened Kenen,
To deprive him of every escape,
Chyntengir strained and aimed,
Taking the place beneath the heart,
The measured edge of the lung,
The exact center of his aim:
“Even if he had a hundred thousand lives,
They would all fly out here.”
He turned his stallion's head,
And his eyes blazed like fire.
