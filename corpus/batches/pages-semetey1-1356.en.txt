I fitted it anew from the beginning.
I had ornament set upon its collar
And wound it in twelve layers.
For the clash with the teeming infidel,
With hero Khan Kongurbai,
I intended it!
So they might stand face-to-face,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
And throw themselves into the shooting;
I repaired what was chipped,
So the spear of that swine,
The khan of the Kechil, could not pass through.
I, unfortunate Kanykey,
Truly repaired the ak-olpok armor
Left by Manas!
The heroic khan's ak-olpok armor—
So my valiant orphan might wear it,
Might face and strike the enemy,
Drive the opposing Kalcha
As far as the khagan,
And drive the heroes he faced
Into Beijing;
So upon the teeming khagan
He might bring utter turmoil;
So my young son of the bloodthirsty hero
Might avenge himself upon the bloodthirsty foe;
If a Kalcha fell into his hands,
He might drag him along the road
And display him to the Dungans;
So if his doom came, he might die,
And campaign against
The teeming Chinese;
There were many wonders when I gave it,
So my valiant son might see them.
At that difficult time,
Your mighty mother Kanykey
Brought forth an article.
Your dear mother Kanykey
Made her motherhood known:
She clothed him in ak-olpok armor
That no bullet pierced in battle.
The blue saddlebag she brought—
The blue saddlebag bore no pattern;
Inside the white saddlebag,
What was there, and what was not!
Many champions lifted it;
Men marveled at the service
Of your dear, formidable mother.
It held coats of separate kinds;
Inside that saddlebag
Were wonders no one had seen.
Your exceptional mother Kanykey,
Bending her wisdom and pondering,
Knew he would reach Kakan-Chyn's Beijing
And grapple there;
If battle arose suddenly,
If death came by chance,
She thought a stray bullet might pass.
Showing her skill,
She clothed your lion uncle Bakai
In bulletproof armor.
“Long ago I knew
Of the costly campaign to Beijing,
And had prepared many pieces
Of bulletproof clothing.
So against the teeming infidel,
Against hero Khan Kongurbai,
He might fight without fleeing;
Taking no heed of death,
Never stopping to say, ‘I shall die,’
He might press the conflict to its limit;
Against the overbearing Kalcha
He might go sharp and resolute,
Roaring in wrath,
Raising the battle cry of his royal father Manas,
He might strike the foe without fleeing,
And enter battle without tiring.
