“If I do not shatter his throne and game,
If I do not slaughter this hulking slave,
If I do not hew clean through his neck,
If I do not catch, devour, and butcher him,
May I sink into darkness as though never born!
They used to say that the foe who flees
Will take for his wife
The finest of black-haired beauties,
The best among all women.
But I shall spill his blood at last;
I shall seize him by the trailing hem,
And cast that accursed, doomed Bilgich
To be torn apart in the gray-wolf game.
The people will clamor in uproar,
And despair of their wrathful champion;
They will say, ‘He was born of a noble man,’
And my lineage will become known.
All forty khanates of China
Will understand and despair of life!”
So spoke brave Seytek,
Consumed by fierce intent,
Brandishing and driving with his spear.
His eyes fixed on great Bilgich,
He followed directly behind,
While the black earth, though not split in two,
Endured the pounding through its depths.
Every rise was gouged away;
Wherever the hooves struck squarely,
Trenches were carved like canals.
It became tumult, calamity, and strife,
A terror no living soul could bear.
When they entered the downward slope,
Brave Bilgich’s great Kerik
Stretched his forelegs like a deer;
When they began climbing uphill,
Kulunsur swept on like a whirlwind—
Such was the ground of their pursuit.
Amid this tale of endless tumult,
They had now drawn close
To the place established as an ambush,
Where Bilgich’s army lay concealed.
Unable to bear his wounds,
Yet still the warrior Akjoltoy
Had ridden after Seytek.
Upon the matchless Kulunsur,
He whipped him right and left,
Spurred him on from either side,
And shouted in anger,
Calling out to Khan Seytek.
Kulunsur, whom no living creature could catch,
Shot whirring like an arrow;
When he passed over black stones,
They burst apart like charcoal.
Ah, wondrous beast Kulunsur,
He flew rumbling like a cloud,
His tail streaming over his haunches,
His matchless breeding plain to see.
From the black mountains of Kaspang,
From the mouth of Kapka-Tash,
From the heights of Dyngsha,
From the fortress no mortal had entered,
The stronghold firmly sealed,
Uncle Joltoy caught up
At the ridge of the Sentry Stone.
He tugged at his armor,
Shaking him about the head and eyes:
“You have courage but no sense;
You are a witless Seytek!
By refusing to heed advice,
Will you send Joltoy to the grave?
To a place where no man can pass,
Will you cast us upon the embers?
Without even binding our hands and feet,
Will you cast us into the fire?
Alas, Seytek, may ruin take you—
My child, what if the two of us are parted?
Did I not tell you before
That the accursed Khagan
Has paths narrower than a hair
And passes even I cannot cross?
