Not knowing the manner of how
One should approach the swan,
He came trotting with pounding hooves,
Saying, “Let the swan fly away,”
And drove it into full view.
Unable to contain his excitement,
Semetey came riding up.
Then Kulchoro spoke these words,
Uttering an uncanny thought no man could know:
“Understand my precious words;
Blessed elder, heed what I say!
Let us not set the bird upon the swan,
And not drink our fill of joy.
I have looked upon this swan;
When it calls with that resonant cry,
Its voice is unlike a swan’s.
When it shakes itself like drifting sand,
Its form changes a thousand ways.
It must be one of the spirits,
The very embodiment of a sacred power.
It has transformed itself into a swan;
My poor elder, hear my words:
It is the source of some great calamity,
A swan come from Mecca,
And it has five young.
Let us not bring it down for nothing,
And fall into its curse.
Bloodthirsty elder, listen:
If we fly a bird at this swan of omen,
It will bring shame upon us.
This swan is no ordinary creature;
It has a form resembling a human,
Or else it may transform
And cast its demon upon us.
It is a swan of wondrous power,
One of the miracles!”
While the boy spoke in this way,
The tiger swelled with daring,
And with a booming voice
Spoke to Kulchoro:
“How much sin can there be in a swan?
Boy, you have grown quite mad.
For no good reason you hunger
To say, ‘I shall fly the bird.’
Though still a boy yourself,
You stand there like a bakshy,
Reciting every omen;
You have become a fine one indeed.
Taking omens from things that do not exist,
You have frightened yourself,
Muttering so many words
That you bar my way.
If it is a swan come from Mecca,
If it has five young,
Let it face me and swallow me;
If it is a swan of wondrous power,
Let it kill me outright.
Is there sin in taking a swan?
Can one prosper by fearing a bird?
Can the sin of killing a swan
Be greater than that of killing a man?
When I had reached the age of twelve,
There in the fortress of Bukhara,
When my father Temir Khan
Agreed to what I asked,
And held the funeral feast for my father Manas,
I began my feats of destruction:
I tore one arm completely
From the camel-strong wrestler.
Where then was my death?
Where did sin overtake me?
When I migrated to Talas,
I loaded my cartridge pouch and fired,
And among the Kokand champions,
Beginning with Janaaly and Karach,
I devoured them all without remainder.
Where then was my death?
Where did sin overtake me?”
