Rolling her eyes like a pheasant,
The bold woman of singular nature
Lay whimpering as he made her submit.
The luckless woman with her companion,
Rolling her eyes like a rooster,
Lay crying, "Koykoy," as he made her submit.
His swaggering member, twelve handspans long—
Without judging its mouth or root,
When he thrust, it knotted within her;
The luckless woman, who had never seen such a thing,
Bent double like a trivet.
"Oh, alas, my back!" she cried;
"Wretched-day prince, you have consumed me!"
When Chürök bucked without ceasing,
She broke both lattice ties
Together with their fastenings;
When your prince thrust without stopping,
He tore both seals of her maidenhood.
The luckless woman with her companion
Satisfied the conqueror's desire.
Your ill-fated princely leopard
Left your dear sister-in-law Chürök
In a grievous state;
Without letting Chürök stir,
He lay upon her and pressed hard.
The leopard grew fierce
And sank down like an old male camel;
Without letting Chürök move,
He spilled his yellow fluid into her.
Holding her firmly and making her struggle,
He ravaged her private place.
When the sultan had finished his act,
Aychürök stood and said this:
"These are the words I plead to say:
Draw back again, my prince;
My eyes are coming out," she said.
Your prince made his stallion rear,
And drew out its tackle;
For as long as meat takes to cook,
Your Chürök lay unconscious.
Her strength vanished entirely;
She nearly died, then recovered,
And gathered her senses.
The quilt and white sheet
Were covered in dark blood.
Concealing that blood had flowed,
What could dear Chürök do?
She gathered and swept it all away.
Your hero's twelve-handspan
Great equipment—
With a white kerchief of pure silk,
She grasped it by hand and wiped it:
"Alas, prince, what shall I do?
All my strength is gone;
Hero, your instrument
Reached every bone in my body,
And passed beyond my navel," she said.
Unable to catch her breath, trembling,
Chürök sobbed and wept.
The steed of Khan Bakay,
Who began all that exists,
The falcon of your hero,
Who began the calamity.
Your dear sister-in-law Chürök,
In the morning, before the prince,
Opened the door and tottered;
The poor woman's senses gone,
She straddled like a louse-ridden horse.
In the morning, before the prince,
She now hurried about serving him.
She had endured the affliction,
Your poor, luckless sister-in-law,
Blood streaming down her calves.
Within the fortress of the white palace,
When two days had passed,
Again your sister-in-law contrived
To accommodate Semetey.
