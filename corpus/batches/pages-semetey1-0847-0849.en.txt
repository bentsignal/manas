These were the scoundrels' deeds:
They cast down all the thrones;
They demolished all the buildings.
They pulled out the pillars;
The great mosque was destroyed
And left level with the ground.
Its hollows filled up;
It became a mound like Tört-Kül.
My warrior lord, may you not die;

Seeing her land, she grew distraught;
Your mother wept in anguish.
Blood ran from her black eyes;
How bitterly she sobbed!
Those who stood watching
Had their hearts grievously stirred.
Your mother wept and shook;
What could poor Chiyirdi do?
Like a twig put in water,
She swayed, wailing and weeping.
“My orphan, whom I cherish,
If I tell you of the old days,
You will think I am boasting.
Sixty thousand horses in the hills,
Fine steeds and coursers mingled.
What power have I against God?
My support behind me, my son, was young.
Bereft of composure and support,
Our poor heads were troubled.
Then all our elder and younger kinsmen were foes!
From wounded honor I wished to die,
Yet I remained alive and did not die,
My dear child, because you were there.
From wounded honor I wished to depart,
Yet I did not depart then,
Because my needed orphan was there!
If I died of anguish,
My cherished orphan
Would be like an extinguished fire, I thought.
So I spared my life;
I did not spill my blood.
Here along the Talas,
Living among the Argyn,
With my young child beneath my arm,
I suffered humiliation and hardship;
Then my poor soul was thrown into turmoil.
Allah's sun in the sky grew overcast;
Sixty troubles came upon my head at once.
His father's countless treasures and great wealth—
Those dogs will plunder them, I thought.
When the orphan returns,
He will have no wealth at all
And be left powerless, I thought.
God may take me thus, I thought.
There is a place where I hid your treasure.
I, your unfortunate mother,
Have a tale that does not end.
Without letting a single person know,
Without a single person sensing it,
There at gray-mottled Kyzyl-Too,
I devised this arduous undertaking.
From the Argyn and the Kyrgyz
I took five strong men.
I gave horses as their wages.
Besides their wages,
I promptly gave gold and silver.
I let them crunch sugar like snow;
I let them drink their fill of black tea.
Spending my livestock,
I implored the five strong men.
I gave sharp tools of solid iron;
I gave steel mattocks as equipment.
I had them dig into the gray cliff face.
All the treasure
I entrusted to their keeping.
I had the opening carefully hollowed out,
So that a single person could fit.
I had the farther interior hollowed out,
Large enough for six thousand sheep to lie down.
So that the entrance would arouse no suspicion,
I carried out my scheme.
The sand from inside
I had carried away in bags.
I had this marvel done:
The sand from inside
I had poured into the great Talas River.
Countless treasures, yellow gold,
Precious stones and rubies mingled,
I had gathered in that cave.
I had this marvel done;
All the wealth
I concealed without a sign.
Thus the treasure
I had gathered in a single cave.
So that no suspicion would remain,
The dark sand above—
I gave sharp tools of solid iron;
So that the entrance would arouse no suspicion,
I had it brought down toward the cave.
Having hidden the treasure,
I contrived another plan.
At that time, unfortunate as I was,
I stood thinking.
When I left Talas,
They will destroy me utterly, I thought,
They might tell someone of my deed,
And inform on what I had done;
They might uncover my treasure, I thought,
And utterly destroy me, I thought.
Unable to feel secure in what I had done,
They might tell someone,
And thus God would strike me down, I thought,
And make such sport of me, I thought.
As twilight came and the sun set,
And the first people lay down to sleep,
I called out to the five strong men,
Calling, “Come here!”
I measured water in a ladle and gave it;
With a spoon I gave poison.
Thus I fed them their portions,
Making them all cry, “My liver!”
I took the cartridge pouch and loaded a bullet;
I slaughtered every one of them alike.
Doing away with the five strong men,
Of those gentle men who had done no wrong,
Five strong men I slaughtered.
Folding them at the waist,
Beside the hearth
I buried them and left the ground unmarked.
I have still more to say.
On the coming Day of Judgment,
For killing them though they had done no wrong,
Upon your mother's neck
Lies the blood of five innocent men.
What you see on this side
Is the treasure your mother hid.
What you see on this side—
When your leopard father was alive,
When my good fortune was intact,
Your mother Kanykei's white palace
Stood here as her court.
Its doors were iron and solid tin.
Though your mother was a woman,
Her prosperity was very great.
In the walled enclosure with its gate,
At Akylai's court,
The treasury stood alongside.
While your khan father was alive,
No visitor came to spy;
No cold-hearted stranger came.
No outsider approached to look;
No one came near.
No person without ritual ablutions entered;
No mortal opened its door.
In this palace enclosure,
None who encountered them left alive;
None who approached passed unharmed.
The guards were beyond counting;
The watchmen were more numerous still.
Human noses and ears
They wore as amulets—
Seventy executioners.
Such were your father's deeds,
The might he brought upon the world.
Good fortune attended him in everything;
He was a warrior protected by Shay.
The Almighty, in your father's likeness,
Will never create another soul.
Your father conquered the peoples of twelve realms.
At that time, against your father
No one remained to stand in resistance.
He was a warrior of surpassing fortune.
If you looked into his heart,
It was wider than the face of the earth.
He gathered the Muslim host.
“The bloodthirsty khan of the Burut!”
The khan of Beijing cried.
He rode a horse no missile could catch;
He wore a coat no missile could pierce.
He steeped his spear in blood.
When he heard your father's approach,
How could Konurbai sleep?!
He summered in this Talas;
Here he tethered Akkula.
When his wrath rose,
He ground his foes between his fangs.
When an enemy came out to fight,
He drove them before him.
If an enemy faced him,
He sent Almambet and Chubak into action.
Inflicting torment on the foe,
Your father laid many low.
Those who would heed his words,
He drove with his words.
Those who would not heed his words,
He drove in fury.
When he charged with hostile intent,
His enemy did not remain alive.
Those who faced him were destroyed;
No enemy came to confront him.
No mortal had seen his like.
Your father, the warrior Manas,
Raided Ombu and took booty;
He broke through its midst and made a road.
When the moment was ripe,
He laid waste to the Kyzylbash people
And made their girls and young wives weep.
The seven banners brought from Mecca
He planted in seven places.
Your accomplished father, the leopard,
Laid low the lands he reached.
Born and grown in Altai,
His fame was great among the people.
Whoever grappled with your father was humbled.
When he reached twelve,
He broke a royal court and took booty.
The lord who kept that court,

He scattered his wonders,
Seizing many of them.
All his treasure
He distributed to the Muslims.
When he came in wrath,
He made Akkula prance.
Alöökö with six thousand champions

When he reached Chong-Aksy,

Alooko's Konurbai—
There is a fitting time to tell the tale;
He has his father's slaughter to avenge.
