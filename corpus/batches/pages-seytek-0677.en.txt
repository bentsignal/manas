“When we gained countless riches,
When we raided the land of Künjüt,
And stretched the black-steel Zulfiqar
Toward the enemy,
When in every place where we traded blows
Our strength proved equal,
Why did I not die then?
Who did not jauntily wear
A blue-lynx coat and black fur cap?
While fortune crowned one’s head,
Who did not stride about roaring?
When they beheld our precious lives,
Did not the worlds tremble?
We brought misery upon the world;
Were we not lions, were we not khans?
Throughout the eighteen thousand worlds,
None remained who dared swing a lasso at us.
When we roared in anger,
The whole world plunged into turmoil.
When we scowled in wrath,
Your eighteen thousand worlds
Were utterly laid waste.
Now there is no valor, no heroism;
Against the infidel son of an ogre,
Our former courage is gone.
Has God stricken our strength?
Before the infidel son of an ogre,
Has God condemned us to disgrace?
Our heroism once surged and thundered,
Once it seems to have surpassed all bounds.
Perhaps mighty God and Kydyr decreed
That before this raging infidel
We should be humiliated.
My heart burns, my liver is aflame;
When glory flees from everyone,
Is this what the worthless world becomes?
When I was born,
You had ninety beasts slaughtered for my feast.
You bade them range across the whole world
And slaughter them as they went.
Yet now a warrior such as this
Is about to destroy my mullah
And, I fear, carry away as plunder
My golden-throned palace!
Long ago, the one God above
Made me a champion who shook the age.
Without making me equal to a single infidel,
Why have you diminished me so greatly?
Yesterday, on the plain of Jylgyndy,
Along the broad Talas,
When we shot deer if we grew bored,
And, when tireless in former days,
Played freely at our ease;
When the black-steel Zulfiqar
Was kept gleaming with oil;
When we drove
The black Chinese and Manchu before us;
Why did death not come then,
As we thrust our spears and broke their ranks?
Why did no bullet strike me dead?
When the Kyrgyz sprawled at ease
Across Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
We went and conquered Afghanistan—
Whoever met us there escaped unharmed?
With unsheathed swords in hand,
We slaughtered the Chinese,
Making them scream,
And drove them all the way to Beijing.
When we forced those mighty armored men
Back inside Beijing,
When there was more gold and silver
Than we could load and carry,
And more beautiful noble brides
Than we could seize as booty,
When we besieged Kara-Shaar and Kambil,
Holding them trapped,
And beat down
The black Khitans and Manchus,
Why did I not die then?
There were pounds of coin and yellow gold—
