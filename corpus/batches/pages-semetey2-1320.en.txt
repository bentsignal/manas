He stood unshaken, ringing like steel,
Counting the swarming Jediger
As no more than the hem of his robe.
The Greathearted Lion, left horseless,
Turned his face toward the qibla,
Girded on his sharp steel,
And wrapped himself in his white armor;
He did not draw back his outstretched foot,
Nor open his once-closed eyes.
Yesterday Khan Kanchoro and Khan Kyiaz
Charged and thrust their spears,
Fired guns and loosed arrows,
And besieged him where he stood.
I heard the noble lion, left horseless,
Was treacherously struck by a spear—
A shameful deed;
That he died and his bones were never found,
That the lion you yourselves had seen
Vanished without a trace.
Mist rose from the ravine,
And over the summit of Achkaikan-Tau
Black clouds gathered;
Mountains collapsed with a roar,
Floodwater ran and slopes slid,
Day and night became darkness,
And the ravine filled with turbulent water.
The trees on the shaded slope
Stood with their roots torn bare;
The Jediger's gaunt horses
Lay utterly dead and swollen.
I heard they reached
The hero's inheritance;
I heard my Greathearted One vanished
And departed from this world.
These were the hero's own words:
“In his old age, Khan Bakai,
And in her old age, Chyiyrdy—
May neither outlive me.”
He said my aged mother Kanykei,
Whose brow-bone arches high,
Had always been fated
To see few happy days;
Her hair at the nape was coarse,
Her lot was heavy sorrow.
“Do not let her shriek and tear her face,
Do not let her rage,
Do not let her take her tiny life.
If my dear mother does not die,
Though senseless hardship may strike her,
Let her live to see it,
The child six months formed
Within Aichurok's womb.
Slaughter ninety beasts for his feast;
If the child is a boy,
Give him the name Seitek.”
Those were the prince's necessary words;
That is exactly what he said.
“Make him like the morning star in heaven,
The one appointed by destiny;
If Kulchoro lives,
Make the boy his companion.
Against the enemy who comes head-on,
He will be his host of warriors;
If Kulchoro survives,
He will be his wing.
Growing strong and becoming a man,
He will stand equal to heroes' sons.
The child left in the womb will mature,
Meet my poor kinsman,
And find the lands of Jylgyndy,
Broad Keng-Kol, and wide Talas.
He will find the happy Kyrgyz
And the many Argyn people.”
The bloodstained warrior's last command
Was this, to be told to Aichurok:

Do not loose your black hair.
