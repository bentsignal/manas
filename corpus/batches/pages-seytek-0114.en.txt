Like a spring flowing from a ravine,
They stood face-to-face.
Overcome with love and yearning,
They melted into one another.
Any mortal who saw them rejoiced
As they swayed together like molten silver.
With a cry like a lion,
He pressed the boy to his breast, sobbing;
Külchoro’s head and limbs
Shook as he wailed.
“Are you the vision for which I prayed?
Are you the plane tree left by my elder?
You are all the vengeance left by the hero,
The mirror left by my lord.
Your elder has withered away like this,
Passing his days in servitude,
Drinking water through a reed.
I longed to die but could not die,
Could not take my own life,
Could not bring death upon myself.
When my hapless head endured
True misery and deepest humiliation,
I wandered long, burning and tormented.
My treasure left by my lord,
How long I have yearned to see you!
In my black, collared felt cloak,
When I went apart to gather firewood,
I met only suffering.
Abandoned to the enemy’s scorn,
The great ditch I dug became my lot;
Your elder grieved and was disgraced.
Though I sobbed, God would not take me;
This ruined soul cried aloud,
Unable even to walk by himself.
If I would enter the earth, the ground was hard;
If I would die, life was sweet.
Neither dying nor vanishing, I lived on,
Wondering what I should do.
I lived in hope of seeing
Even a glimpse of the boy.”
His whole being dissolved,
His flesh and sinews stretched with yearning;
His heart became burning embers,
As though his extinguished fire had kindled anew.
His dying soul revived,
As though God had fulfilled his wish,
As though the vanished Semetey
Had returned alive that very day.
The wretched Choro’s
Pale face softened;
Tears flowing from both eyes
Poured down his chest.
The sorrowful ones met,
Their hearts burning and dissolving;
The lost were found,
And the ill-fated Choro’s
Honor was restored that day.
When he saw the boy,
How could Külchoro not burn with feeling?
Tears streaming down both cheeks
Fell upon his chest.
He wailed with such a cry
That his voice reached God;
Overcome with anguish, Külchoro
Fell upon the boy and embraced him.
Holding him close, Külchoro
Stood weeping with piercing cries.
Pressing the orphan to his breast,
He stood sobbing bitterly.
As Külchoro held him,
The birch wept, the willow wept,
And every watching soul wept.
All those many people cried,
Consumed by their own grief;
They wept, saying that when misery befalls a hero,
This is how it must be.
Like elder and younger brothers reunited,
They stood mingled together;
Like the hair of a white gazelle,
They stood drawn close together.
