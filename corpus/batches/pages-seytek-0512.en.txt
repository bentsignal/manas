THE TALE OF SARYBAI, SON OF THE MONSTER
Beaten down by Sarybai,
They had no time to gather their wits,
No chance to fight with manly courage;
Many champions panicked.
Of the many vaunting warriors,
Some fled back again,
While those fated to die were slain.
Some champions whose death had not come
Were swallowed in swirling dust,
Their heads like buckets of blood;
Many champions panicked.
They went intending slaughter,
But met with bitter disaster,
And felt Sarybai’s might.
The boastful, swaggering men,
Those who called themselves heroes,
With their mottled bellies thumping,
Their blustering chests rumbling,
Those witless braggarts,
Failed to meet the foe they sought
And met the one they had not sought;
Many champions were destroyed.
All those who fell
Were hotheaded braggarts,
Empty-chested, broad-flanked men
Who had strutted in arrogance.
They cast away their lives;
Those who were not killed
Retreated and fled again.
The warriors who posed as champions,
Exalting themselves,
Those who said, “I am a champion,”
Threw away their lives.
While the army stood thus,
The earth heaved like lake and river;
While the people stood thus,
The whitening dawn broke,
Pouring light upon the earth.
As day began to dawn,
The cold wind before sunrise
Swept down the valley;
The reed tops shivered,
Young larks clamored,
All living creatures stirred,
Every beast awoke.
One by one the stars in heaven
Quivered toward the qibla;
The stars overhead glittered,
Hurrying toward the qibla.
The dawn opened bright,
The earth’s twilight withdrew;
A light breeze rose to a wind,
And the land appeared, filled with troops.
Shapes emerged from the darkness,
And the face of the world appeared.
When they gathered their baggage,
And the camp began to move,
The sentries on watch—
Those who scouted land and road,
And learned the enemy’s order—
Surveyed the ground and searched the way,
Often exchanging shots.
They would become entangled
In every peril of the foe.
