Will surely trample us underfoot, uncle?
Will they scatter all four kinds of livestock, uncle?
Will they dig out even the stumps and pass on, uncle?
Will they bind the Muslim heroes
And carry them off to Beijing, uncle?
Will they heap suffering on our heads, uncle?
Will they drive us to their city
And make us slaves, uncle?
Let us submit to what
The Black Kalmyks and Manchus demand, uncle.
So they do not slaughter the people,
They ask for Maaniker; let us give him, uncle.
Let us scatter our countless riches, uncle,
And flee toward Samarkand, uncle!
May your feast be damned—
Can anyone hold a feast as we have, uncle?
Can one prosper after falling
Into the hands of Chinese as numerous as ants, uncle?
He gathered every troubling word;
Maaniker was wealth left by my father,
And he demanded him, uncle.
The moon has turned into darkest night, uncle;
This has become a day of suffering, uncle!
Intoxicated by this wretched wealth,
We came saying we would hold a feast, uncle;
We have chosen torment instead, uncle,
And died before our destined deaths, uncle!
I did not heed my elder's words, uncle;
Now I have fallen into distress, uncle!
I did not heed the wise man's words, uncle;
Coming here saying I would give a feast,
I have fallen into a force beyond reckoning, uncle!
Will the Chinese enjoy
Khan Kokotoy's abundant riches, uncle?
This poor soul has fallen into peril;
Will they bury us underfoot, uncle,
And thus mete out our punishment, uncle?
Should we present Maaniker, uncle,
And scatter all our wealth, uncle!
As dusk falls and the sun sets,
When the foremost of the people lie down to sleep,
Should we flee toward the Muslims, uncle?"
Then Koshoy said this:
"He who gives a feast bears blame, my son;
He who gathers the people bears guilt, my son!
On the downward slope the grass is thick, my son;
The infidels' people are many, my son.
On the upland the grass is sparse, my son;
The Muslim people are few, my son!
The Black Kalmyk and Manchu peoples,
As we have heard since ancient times,
Are enemies of the Muslims, my son!
There is much to say of it, my son;
When Manas migrated from Altai, my son,
There was a place where he slaughtered
The finest of the stone-bedecked Chinese, my son.
You did not stay quietly in Tashkent;
You drank your fill of liquor and ran wild, my son!
Saying, 'I will hold my father's memorial feast,'
You came merely to see the spectacle, my son;
Now you have seen calamity too, my son!
Submit to what he demands;
Give him Maaniker!
