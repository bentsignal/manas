Chyng Tengir grew wrathful,
Calling upon the blue-gray hero,
Raging like a blue-gray boar,
His splendid form appeared like iron,
His eyes and heart were set apart,
He readied his tens of thousands,
He prepared his champions,
His warrior aspect appeared,
Forty-fold calamity made them tremble,
He moved and thundered,
Trembling without letting the day pass,
He issued a sweeping command.
When they came to the tower,
The tower of hell,
Do not ask of that tower,
Nine thousand fathoms high,
A war drum crafted by a master,
When they beat the war drum,
It split heads beneath the hellish sun,
Its striking could be heard afar.
The war drum stood firm,
Do not ask about the war drum’s voice.
It was the signal that the enemy had come,
The lamb cries out in hunger,
The wrathful thousand stood grim,
All the brave men who stood there,
The gathered lions,
Without moving or making a twitch,
A fiercely wrathful fire engulfed them,
Even under pressure they would not stir,
When he lunged at the stone-hearted,
He had no desire for the sand-hearted,
How many enduring ones were among them,
When he attacked, he spared no life,
He drove them on as a champion.
His mind unsated, he steeled his face,
All who faced him,
Death struck amid the uproar,
The people scattered and the land was ruined,
When grievous calamity remained,
The remaining people took up the whip,
Gathering what was done in an instant,
Saying, “Seek life beneath the moon,”
Sending each to his own place,
He brought measureless devastation.
From a thousand men, a hundred souls,
He selected and took them.
Saying that the khan’s command remained,
The sand caught fire and stone burned,
He set down a scorched cauldron.
Making the strongest suffer,
Making the last ones wail,
Making sparks fly from mottled eyes,
From mottled eyes into wind and fire,
Once enraged, he never retreated,
Destroying whoever confronted him,
In the task he faced head-on,
He did not return before dying,
From those assembled, he selected and took them.
Chyngteñir gathered an army,
He set forth what God had ordained.
Coming into sight before the Kyrgyz,
He acted harshly toward many,
Thinking, “My strength will suffice,”
Against the eighteen thousand worlds.
Turning him against a thousand men,
He launched a fine army.
One soul for every hundred men,
Was left in another army.
An uproar arose that split the earth,
A din arose that rent the sky,
Preparing the bellows and preparing the work,
The broad enclosure filled up.
The eighteen thousand worlds,
Heaped upon the Kyrgyz,
Its extent filled up like a mountain.
Ten thousand against a single man,
All of them gathered here.
A multitude that shook the earth,
Waving hands and mustering the army,
Driving his forces against the enemy,
Chyngteñir’s black banner,
A vast uproar that shattered the sky,
Beast-like, filthy and grim,
It came in surging masses,
Raging and growing venomous,
The black earth beneath the multitude,
Stopped under the violent force.
Warring against the whole world,
Laying the world waste,
This earth did not collapse,
Under his withering wrath,
It halted beneath the multitude.
There were champions and majesty,
There were countless kinds of multitudes.
