His shovel-broad teeth gleaming,
His mail coat glittering upon him,
Your sovereign lord began to speak:
"Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
My matched pair of companions!
My cliff that even geese cannot circle,
My deeds before God must have been pure
For my God to give me you two.
My matched pair of companions,
Keep these words in mind:
Today I have seen a wonder.
Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
You are the morning stars above my head,
The costly beaver at my collar.
When I send you ahead, you are my open road;
When I place you behind, you are my countless host.
You are those I fondle and love,
Those who wear my bulletproof coat;
When an army comes covering the earth,
When force threatens my fly-sized soul,
You enter battle without a start!
Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
When you were infant children,
So that you would become stout-hearted heroes,
I had you fed slices of kazy;
So that you would be my own brothers,
I had both of you nurse
At Kanykey's breast!
You and your companion
Kicked each other like yearling colts
And nursed in rivalry at her breast.
My courser fit for a six-month ride,
The sustaining shadow of a toiling soul,
My dear kinsmen, let me cherish you!
Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
You possess a noble nature that commands the gaze;
You are vigorous legacies left to me
By the khans who departed long ago!
When I climb, you are my handhold;
When I descend, you are my support.
My two equal heroes,
Now I shall seek your counsel;
Listen to me, my champions!
Did you see what I saw?
Did you perceive what I perceived?
If you did not see what I saw,
If you did not perceive what I perceived,
You two foolish-born wretches—
In the valley of Great Opol,
Beside Great Urgench,
At the edge of the river water,
Among the birches and poplars,
Smoke rises into the sky.
Heed my words, young men:
White tents can be seen.
Bear-fur saddle cushions and white saddlecloths,
Their girths fastened tight,
Their breast straps firmly drawn,
The horses of fine young men can be seen.
A caravan has camped at the ford;
Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
Your needed road has appeared.
Smoke rises from the ridge;
Red-patterned saddles with bird-shaped pommels,
And maidens' horses can be seen.
Their girths are fastened,
And blankets drawn over them;
Saddles of maidens that would satisfy desire
If one went there can be seen.
At the sight, your elder cannot keep still;
His heart is deeply stirred.
Smoke billows upward;
Thirty daughters-in-law and thirty maidens,
Fortuitously placed upon your road,
Can be seen at play, young men.
Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
Whichever of you goes will find them there;
Together, you two have the strength of one man,
