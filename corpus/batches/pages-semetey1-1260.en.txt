Baitailak, son of Bakai Khan,
Kalyk, son of Sary Khan,
Shaiymbet, son of Zhamgyrchy,
Kanchoro, son of Chubak,
Külchoro, son of Almambet—
All sons of khans,
Gathered around Semetey.
His knees bending to the ground,
To his uncle’s side
Külchoro came running.
Looking to either side,
Licking his lips like a hungry wolf,
The spear bending in his hand,
Arkeskene at his neck
Hung a full fathom long.
All distinguished heroes,
Gathered tight like deer,
All tiger-like heroes,
His attendant comrades beside him,
They all came together
To see Semetey.
The great Buurul—
The radiant Aichürök,
Her argali eyes shining,
Had six brides and six maidens,
Twelve in all, hold him.
Leather saddlecloth and white pad,
A saddle fitted to Buurul.
Akkangky, made by Almambet,
Its front bow of white gold,
Its rear bow of pure silver,
Both sides broad iron—
Aichürök saddled him carefully,
Making him strain as though his master had died.
The dear surviving Buurul
She now stood holding.
When the khans’ sons ran
And came before him,
The young orphan Semetey
Now spoke these words;
To the companions beside him
He said the following:
“With Khojo, who attacked us himself,
Let us measure ourselves.
With Khojo, craving battle,
With that raging swine!
Did we steal away his piebald mare?
We were kin from the root;
Did we scatter his white silver and sable?
Did we chase off his dun mare?
That Khojo has gone mad—
Did we scatter sable from his saddlebags?
Did we camp upon his village?
Did we commit some offense?!
Did we seize and slaughter his barren mare?
Did we reconnoiter his strong city?
Have we become guilty?
That Khojo, rising in force—
Did we reap his rice like hay?
Against thundering Toltoi,
Have we made ourselves guilty?
That he musters his army in fury,
That his spears quiver in readiness—
Were we rivals for that swine’s wife?
Did we earlier attack
And raid his village?!
He has longed for slaughter—
Have we fallen into his torment?!
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Was a servant of our common root;
Let us fight to the finish,
For we were sons of one father.
Mustering his host with a roar,
As though we had plundered his gold,
As though we had raided his village—
While my strong head remains,
How can I give up my wife?
The swine have risen in force;
