Let me take him as a gift,
And bring the gray wolf Semetey
Before you.
Agree to what he says,
And without quarrel or strife,
Lead him safely to the Khagan.
Pay the blood-price
For his lion father Manas, then return.
All the people have gathered;
We have come to face our reckoning.
After so many Chinese were slaughtered,
We have come to this pass.
Know that this is what I counsel;
Uncle Kalcha, heed my words.
Remain here and guard Beijing;
Make peace with the champion Semetey.
Negotiate without wrangling,
Do not quarrel and fight;
Return as a people reconciled.
Yesterday there was clamorous uproar,
And we began this grievous ordeal.
What horn of plenty ever sprang
From the hollow of Kaspan-Too,
From the banks of the Kara-Suu,
From the slopes of Kyr-Kayıng,
Or from the edge of Kyl-Irtysh?
We tore at one another’s beards and hair,
Fired guns and loosed arrows,
And fought in bloody struggle—
What benefit ever came of it?
From battle, turmoil, and war,
From this wretched business, when you look,
Tell me, Uncle Kongurbay,
Was your treasury filled?
Battle came and the people died;
Many different nations died.
The desolate land found no peace;
At the very word that battle was coming,
The people wailed and cried aloud.
Without quarreling or attacking,
Agree to these words;
My princely elder, pay the blood-price for Manas!
Now that your hair is gray with age,
Go safely to the Khagan;
My lord and uncle Kongurbay,
Return safely to Beijing.
That bloodthirsty champion and lion
Filled the Kara-Suu with blood,
Set the grassy lands ablaze,
And burned the valley.
He burned the valley and turned it to grease;
When his wrath was roused,
He even laid low his own father, Khan Jakyp.
That great gray wolf of violent birth,
After turning his back on the Kyrgyz,
After embracing the Chinese faith,
Speak words of conciliation to him.
Stand face-to-face,
Pour out spirits and serve food,
And offer them to one another.
Exchange words of reconciliation;
Meet and speak at length,
Bring your hearts together as one,
And return as friends.
At Karkyra, where men circle together,
Gather round and confer;
At Karkyra, where hosts assemble,
Hold council together.
Lay the matter before you,
And cast aside every grudge;
Lay your words before you,
And cast aside every wrong.
Make true peace and return.
Bid each other, ‘Farewell, and remain safe!’
Make peace once and return;
Like flour from white wheat,
Blend together, you two lions;
