Fashioned to suit you,
I have this fitting gift for you;
Recognize this service
Of your sworn father, the master smith!
And if I speak further, here is another tale:
While your father Manas lived,
Six sharp blades that fell from heaven—
Six accursed things raised among commoners—
Were handled shamefully,
Their cases split, trampled by horses,
Cast upon the ground to rust.
While bloodthirsty Manas lived,
Those six blades sated with blood—
I repaired what rust had eaten,
And fitted them even better
Than when sovereign Manas lived.
For the keen iron and steel,
On account of those six blades,
I endured and lost much.
If he swings one even at stone,
May my only one left by the bloodletter
Split it in two without stopping;
May he see the worth
Of his sworn father, the master smith.
Smashing the Chinese to pieces,
Its edge shortened when swung,
But when he swung it with a cry,
It lengthened to forty gez;
The six sharp blades never sated with blood—
When you battle Beijing,
My hero, my only one, I have these for you!
Against the Chinese you confront,
Let him hang one at his wrist;
Shouting the battle cry “Khan Manas!”
Let him strike the foe without tiring.
Let him reach the one he faces,
Drenching it in bright-red blood,
And pass on after wreaking slaughter;
Let the enemy who challenges him panic.
If Kongurbai should see him,
Let him lose his wits;
Saying, “The bloodletter’s true son!”
Let him flee inside Kakan.
I have prepared these gifts with care;
Take the sword with its sheath,
And enter among the Chinese without looking back,
Bringing alarm wherever you reach!
I fashioned Syrnaiza with full skill,
And, my only one, I have this too!
On the spur of the broken mountain,
At the point of the swelling mountain,
If they stand facing one another,
If they level their spears firmly,
And fight where they meet,
Let him thrust it straight and unwavering;
Let it bring mighty alarm.
If he comes face to face with the Kalcha,
Let him spear through his backbone;
Upon that notorious Kalcha swine,
Let him avenge the valiant hero,
And upon Beijing, lying vast and still,
Let him bring alarm in full measure.
This is the spear I fashioned;
My lord, my only prince, listen well!
The birch of stony ground—
I searched and had the finest cut;
To let it season in its bark,
I had it stacked in the shade.
I passed it from craftsman to craftsman,
Had careful judges test it,
And, to straighten every crooked place,
Had it pressed in a jig.
I had a woodworker shape it,
Plane away every knotted place,
And forty Kalchas finish it.
I surpassed my former craftsmanship;
Fearing it might suddenly break,
I had brass bound around it in four places.
