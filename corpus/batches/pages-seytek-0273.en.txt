Let me ride in, my whip trailing,
And learn how great his courage is.
I shall hurl harsh words at him
And touch his tender spot.
Glaring in wrath,
If I do not crush him from above
And scatter all his princely pride;
I shall bring misery upon his lord.
If I, Seytek, cannot do so,
May I sink away as though never born,
May I die rather than live.
Kanchoro is the one I seek—
He alone is my sworn enemy.
If he is a lion and a tiger,
And I do not rain shots upon him
Before the five Argali stars set—
My valiant mother Kanykei,
Who nursed me five times over,
From the torments of captivity
And the humiliation of slavery—
If I do not set her free!
If I do not level my flawless spear,
Raise a clamor that splits the earth,
And battle Kanchoro;
If I do not show the enemy
My courage and valor,
My strength and lionheartedness;
If I do not make Khan Bakai master
Of all his vast wealth and herds,
Lead my mother Kanykei
Into her noble palace,
Have myself called Semetey’s son,
And make my valor known to the people;
If I do not wage fierce battle,
And avenge the honor of not one father but seven,
Taking vengeance for my forebears,
May I die as though never created.
Let me see how strong
The fierce and wrathful Külchoro is.
If perfect honor grants me a day,
The Arгын and Kyrgyz, whose names accord,
Are the people my father left me.
Should a cherished khan’s son
Live as a fugitive?
Should a valiant khan’s only son
Live wandering in exile?
I shall bring my people home to Talas
And, unless I die, take vengeance
Upon the swaggering enemy.
I shall show the enemy
My savage, wrathful edge;
I shall go and strengthen
The fury I bear against my foe.
To the vile slave who gorges himself
On frozen goat fat and barren yearling horse,
Drunken with arrogance,
I shall go and send word:
‘Give us now Akolpok, worn on distant campaigns,
And Akkelte, fired in battle.’
I shall demand them
And ask for every possession.
I shall gather what was scattered
And reclaim all that was taken.
If he will not return what was taken,
If he will not heed my words,
If he will not give me the possessions
My father left in trust,
I shall tear open and scatter his bundles
And unleash a dreadful quarrel.
I shall raid his mares from the hills,
Tether his camels from the village,
And vent his rage.
If his savage fury seizes him,
I shall batter his brains to pieces,
And wage such strife.
That utter infidel,
That treacherous Choro warrior,
I shall crush from above
And scatter his full fortune.
Declaring, ‘I am the khan’s son,’
