Blood rushed into his dark face,
And the true son of the tiger
Now recovered his fury:
“Let go of my arm, woman;
Do not stand across my path.
While you lay there ill,
It seems you never truly recovered;
You have grown full of dreams and fancies,
And have become impossible.
Does a man not know his falcon,
And a woman not know her work?
Does she not know the head of the man,
Nor the order proper to a brave,
Nor even know herself as a woman?
I let you live freely as you pleased,
And pampered and cherished you so much
That you grew spoiled beyond measure,
And became witless, it appears!
You cooled and weakened my words,
And brought disgrace upon me;
Knowing so many muddled speeches,
You have turned out well indeed.
I went about thinking you were ill,
But you have turned out to be a bakshy!”
Semetey’s heavy whip,
Whose stroke could deafen the ear,
Made the defiant woman
Stagger beneath its blow.
Yesterday he had cured an ox hide,
Stretched it over a wooden core,
Cut the hide of a barren mare
Into narrow winding strips,
And bound it tightly around it.
He had an elm from Opol-Too’s foot
Torn out together with its root
And turned into the whip’s handle;
He had its waist wrapped in hide,
And had Bölekbay fit the shaft.
Its palm-piece was set with glass,
Its wrist-loop was of bargektüü leather;
Semetey seized the black-jawed whip,
Gripped it and bent it back,
Made the woman cry “Kank!”
And lashed her across the ribs.
May God spare one from such a sight:
Her billowing mottled dress
Split apart at the waist,
And her blood poured out in streams;
Her cherished patterned dress
Split open along the side,
And her blood flowed in waves.
Poor Chachykey the woman,
Like a mountain grouse upon a ridge,
Collapsed and crawled away;
From beneath her blue patterned silk dress
Her blood streamed down its hem.
The flesh of her side was torn,
And your poor, delicate sister-in-law
Fled inside screaming.
Her forefather was Tügöl Khan,
Blessed from his roots by Kydyr;
He had brought the woman to submission.
As for the blue-maned champion
Born after his father, the hero Manas,
He wore out horses and ranged the land,
Loosed his hounds and flew his birds,
And wandered about in revelry.
The delicate woman Chachykey,
From her softly gleaming eyes,
Shed tears in separate streams;
She screamed and wept and cried aloud,
Truly wept and sobbed,
And cried out in anguish.

Summoning both of them together;
