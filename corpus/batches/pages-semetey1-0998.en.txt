Would she not immerse herself
In wondrous games and revelry?
For the sake of this girl Churok,
Would she not suffer
The torments of the world?
Would she not behold
Every harsh decree of fate?
Would she not die
Locked in Churok's embrace?
Would she not immerse herself
In every wondrous delight?
Would she not lie down
Locked in Churok's embrace?
Damn that robber of your father's grave!
My destined path led away to Bukhara;
Had my mighty father lived,
This was the wife I should have taken first.
Accursed be God's taking
Of my father in his youth;
Accursed be the fate that left
A son without his father while still young!
This poor soul wandered far and long,
Unable to find
My Kyrgyz encampment in Talas.
I had failed to take
A wife like Aychurok.
Accursed be my wandering
To the city of Temir Khan;
Unable to find and take my equal,
I did not act as I should have!"
Your lord stood there grieving,
Transfixed by the sight of Churok.
Now let us tell
What Aychurok had done.
There are two traditions about her fortune,
A point disputed among the people.
Some people say:
"Churok is the daughter of a fairy,"
And say the renowned unfortunate woman
Threw her people into turmoil.
They say that from the pass of Mediyan,
From the place where the fairy played,
The noble hero Akun,
Taking eighty wrestlers as attendants,
While he was out hunting,
Found Churok there.
Her miraculous power was unlike all others,
Proud-standing Churok, the noble champion.
That discerning sister-in-law of yours had learned,
"My tiger lord, my Bloodthirsty One,
Must be coming," she had thought.
"Driving his train behind him,
Because of his longing for Akshunkar,
If the tiger's ardor rises,
He will reach Opol;
If the tiger's ardor rises,
He will pursue the matter to its very end."
From the beginning, everything she had done
Had been done in preparation;
Devastating Aychurok
Had gone out to await
Her rough lord and husband.
She heard reports from those who came,
And listened closely to knowledgeable people;
She heard them say, "Semetey,
Son of the Magnanimous Manas,
Would die for liquor and women."
When Aychurok heard this,
She gathered women and skilled craftswomen,
And exceptionally beautiful maidens
Who had brushed themselves with powder and rouge.
Within the city of Akun Khan,
Without letting a single person know,
Without letting a single person perceive it,
She chose maidens from among the Afghans.
Aychurok did not hold back
From the radiant, red-cheeked beauties,
