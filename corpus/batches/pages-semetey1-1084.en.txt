The places where the spears passed through
Remain even now,
Gushing though I do not move.
Having seen a most ill-omened dream,
I lay there, poor soul,
Suffering a most dreadful plight.
My dream in the night was an evil dream;
The edges of my ribs ache.
As though I had been stabbed while awake,
The very crown of my heart aches.
Going about to take Aychürök,
I will not remain in her torment.
Roaming among a mere handful of people,
I will not bear their sin.
This is grievous, disastrous work;
Do not give orders, Kozho, my dear friend.
There is a flaw in these words of yours:
Will he submit to your violence?
Will he give us his wife?
There is a son left by Manas.
Have you heard the news
Of the blue-maned lion Manas,
Who was born and grew mighty in Altai?
The lion Manas, the magnanimous lion,
When he reached the age of twelve,
Came saying, "I will fight,"
And the Kara Kalmyks and Manchu people,
So many Sibe and hosts of Solon,
Were slaughtered and all humiliated.
His name was heard through the ages;
Kyzyr blessed him, and he grew mighty.
Yesterday, heading toward the mountains of Altai,
Esen Khan's thousand troops,
And among those troops,
Beaded hat-buttons and coral stones,
With the lungtung khan among them,
To capture and bind Manas,
They received their khan's order.
So great a host
Had settled over the land.
His father Jakyp and Akbaltay
Had scattered all their wealth.
All the Kyrgyz of forty households
Had fled for refuge to the mountains.
Before the mighty Kara Khitai,
When they were about to overrun their villages,
Seize them all,
And slaughter them as sacrifices,
Girding a leather belt about his waist,
Against the seven divisions of Kara Khitai
He entered alone and laid hands upon them.
Poor Manas the lion,
Making his dapple-gray horse dance,
With Khan Zholoy at their head,
Drove them crawling all the way to Beijing.
The Chinese who attacked him fell;
Before the lion, Sultan Manas,
The foe he fixed his gaze upon fell.
Called "Manas the bloodthirsty Burut,"
His name became known throughout the world.
In the register of Beijing's Kökön Khan
He was inscribed.
When they heard this news,
All the countless Chinese,
Beginning with Esen Khan, grieved.
The daughter-in-law of that very Manas—
How will you take her?
If you carry on this way, Chynkozho,
You will fall into disaster.
If you seize her, Chynkozho,
Will God not strike you?
Will Semetey let her be taken
And stand watching without a quarrel?
Against the son of the Kyzyr-blessed lion,
Can anyone who attacks prosper?
Again, Kozho, hear my words!
That Manas was truly Manas,
It seems you have seen it, damn you!"
