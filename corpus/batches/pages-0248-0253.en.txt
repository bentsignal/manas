To serve the bloodthirsty hero as companions,
All forty mounted their horses.
Ahead of them was Manas the leopard,
Like a prince leading his people.
Raising cries and making a clamor,
All forty boys
Rushed as though slaughter had struck.
The wolf fled, trembling;
The forty boys encircled it,
Pursuing the wolf one after another.
Their shouting deafened the ears.
With generous Manas at their head,
The wolf before him—
Leaning down, he was about
To find a way to deal with it.
Now, just as he was reaching the wolf,
About to strike it,
He looked to his right:
Forty men descended from the crag.
They were the Forty Chiltens themselves.
The one transformed into a wolf
Was Aleikisalam himself by name.
Not he himself, but rather
His watching dark eye
Had taken the shape of a wolf
And was seeking a meeting with Manas.
Five men appeared upon the ridge,
Their white turbans like cauldrons,
Their raised voices like the call to prayer,
Their vastness half a mountain,
Their dread might enough to consume a soul.
There was my father Shay himself;
With your valiant, gray-maned Manas
The time had come for their meeting.
They stood clasping hands.
The dervish whose name was Khizir,
Saying to Manas, “I support you,”
Brought forth a close-fitting coat.
All the Forty Chiltens,
Their elders led by Father Shay,
Offered their hands in turn.
The blue jacket given as a gift
Was a strong jacket no shot could pierce,
Fit to wear against the enemy;
No shot could pass through it.
Burn meadowsweet: no ember could pass through.
Fire an ochogor: no shot could pass through.
Pull at the collar: no hand could pass through.
Its collar was gold, its sleeves copper,
Double mail with links like larks' eyes,
A collar embroidered with metal thread, a roomy coat,
Fit to wear in battle.
“Say, ‘My father Khizir gave it.’
Youngest of the six lions,
My lion, put on this coat.
Your strength as a champion is unlike others';
Allah Most High created you so.
Great indeed is your valor.
Thrown into fire, you will not burn;
Swept by a flood, you will not be carried away.
Your guardian spirit excels; your wrath is mighty.
All who clash with you,
Unless my Allah gives them sense,
You will shatter and level to the ground.
Of Allah Most High, the One,
You were born a servant.
Of the six lions,
You were born the peerless youngest.
Among the illustrious gray-maned ones,
You were born a champion.
A leopard ruling the people,
You were born a warrior.
You are a sultan, you are a khan,
Truly fashioned by the Creator.
Your guardian spirit is lofty, your name weighty.
Generous Manas, you yourself are mighty.
You are the support with which I gird my waist;
Created by Allah Most High,
You are my renowned, gray-maned lion.
Your guardian spirit is lofty, your name weighty:
No enemy can bring you low and conquer you;
No one can stand as your equal.
When you enter the clashing enemy ranks,
I will straighten and shorten your path.
Taking the bit, my foal,
I myself will lead you by the hand.
My one God, the Creator,
Sent me, saying, ‘Be his companion.’
The Creator, the one God,
To gray-maned Manas himself
Sent me, saying, ‘Be his companion.’
Barefoot and bareheaded,
I myself am called Khizir.
My foal Manas, open your eyes!
Those forty men beside you
Are the Forty Chiltens themselves.
To offer their hands, my gray-maned one,
They have come here now.
Befriend them each in turn:
They are the Forty Chiltens themselves.
Offering their hands in turn,
The Forty Chiltens came in with a clamor.
Those five men upon the ridge—
There is the renowned Shay himself.
‘I will be the warrior's companion,’ he said,
And has now come toward you.”
The five men dismounted
And embraced him.
Then Manas the leopard
Received the title “Lion.”
“You are a mighty khan, a pillar of the faith,
Truly created by God,
An exceptionally great man.
Do not remain in dark faith; become pure.
Know that God exists,
And quickly become a true Muslim.
My son, seen today, gone tomorrow:
One of seven days brings death.
If you do not fully know the faith,
That will be a shame upon you.
If troubles mountain-high fall upon your head,
Remain thankful.
The road to the hereafter is stony;
In the end, faith is close at hand.
He who does not know that faith
Is an infidel who knows no religion.
Let ‘Bismillah’ come to your tongue.
God is One, the Quran is true,
The Prophet is true, your faith is true.
Scatter what you have gathered;
Desire faith alone.
Know His existence and His oneness;
Know all the names of God.”
After Khizir had spoken thus,
Your gray-maned Manas, blessed by Khizir—
A star stirred in the heavens.
Khizir and Khan Manas
Pledged friendship forty-two times.
“You yourself are a great lion.”
With the Forty Chiltens and your Father Shay,
There they made their camp.
It was there that Khan Manas
Became a true Muslim.
Like the Night of Power he had desired,
Mighty Manas came to that night.
From dark faith becoming pure,
Mighty Manas entered the faith.
An illustrious, noble lion
Mighty Manas had become.
His breast with abundant faith
Mighty Manas had filled.
Oshpur's son was Chege.
Calling Chege to him,
“If you ask about his father,
He is the son of a Black Kalmyk.
Can he prosper with the name Chege?
This is a Muslim boy;
Can his name be Chege?
Let the name Chege be abolished;
Let him be given the name Kutubiy.
Listen well to my words, my son.
I have named you Kutubiy.
You have received a noble-born one's blessing.
My son, you are now called Kutubiy!
Listen well to my words, Manas.
I have said ‘Kutubiy’: this boy
Will bring you good fortune.
Set him in front: he is your fortunate path.
Let him follow behind: he is your countless army.
He is your brave and watchful warrior.
This Kutubiy standing here
Is the supporting kinsman granted by your fortune.
If you climb upward, he is your support.
If you descend, Kutubiy
Is the prop bound to your waist.
If he goes before you, he is your shelter.
When you grapple with and attack the enemy,
Kutubiy will be your protection.
Send him forth: he is the wing you long for.
This Kutubiy standing here
Will stand beneath your banner.
He is your good fortune, he is your support,
The warrior bound to your waist.
This Kutubiy standing here
Will gather with you what has been scattered;
In death, he will share your faith.
Your sheep will share one fold;
Your food and fellowship will be shared.
If you are truly a faithful companion,
Kutubiy will stand behind you.”
As he was saying this,
Kutubiy attended and learned the written word.
He was a champion and a warrior;
He knew God's name by heart.
Faith filled his breast.
To be the gray-maned one's companion,
He became a Muslim.
Abandoning his idol,
Kutubiy now came forward,
Upholding the Muslim faith.
From dark faith he became pure;
Befriending Manas,
He became a stranger to the Kalmyk people.
Kutubiy became a source of strength,
Staying beside Manas the lion.
He pledged himself to twelve spiritual guides;
He received learning from the wise.
Upon that path he was called illustrious.
They made camp along the river.
Manas and Kutubiy
Became brothers for the world beyond.
Together they chewed white wheat flour;
Together they grasped the Holy Word.
Taking an oath binding unto death,
Making their promise firm,
Riding steeds and grasping banners,
Performing brave deeds and fighting enemies,
Fully trusting his word,
That Kutubiy standing there
Became a source of strength for him.
His companions were reckoned by the stars:
Forty boys, with Kutubiy at their head,
Were your warrior's companions.
Of the forty households in Altai,
The wise man Akbaltay,
Broad of jaw and wide of tongue,
A great orator who never faltered,
A warrior rich in wisdom,
The Kyrgyz champion Baltay—
The forty Kyrgyz households gathered
And held a splendid feast.
Calling out like a young goose,
His white beard shining,
My elder Baltay began to speak,
Speaking slowly, setting his words firmly:
“When the head of our people died,
When, making sport of us,
The forty Chinese tribes divided us,
One of us went to Eren;
One of us went to Khangay;
One of us went to the Russians,
Unable to find a place to settle;
One of us went to the Kazakhs.
Thus we fell into suffering.
The eight sons of Karakan—
When they brought them all to ruin then,
Humiliating every one,
The Black Kalmyks and the many Manchus,
When they brought them all to ruin,
The youngest of the eight
Escaped like a vanished spirit, they say.
If we follow the handed-down accounts,
If we ask those who know,
Making Ala-Too his homeland,
That poor Ulak Khan is alive, they say.
Hearing with our ears, seeing with our eyes,
Since we came to Altai,
We have not walked out freely.
Of the other kinsmen,
We have heard of none at all.
Have they all been slaughtered and died?
Have they suffered grievous oppression?
Have their heads been trapped in a yoke,
Their heads entangled in calamity?
That they have all suffered humiliation,
That all were sold like livestock,
That they suffered grievous oppression,
That not one of them remained alive,
That all perished and died—
These are the things I fear.
My words to Allah were a lament.
Wealthy Jakyp's brother's son—
There was one called Üsön.
Driving Üsön away,
The Black Kalmyks took him.
On the far side of Beijing,
In the place called Sazangshang,
Among the Black Kalmyk people,
They set him to tending sheep, they say.
They abolished the name Üsön, they say,
And named him Köz Kaman, they say.
He entered the Kalmyk religion, they say.
The idol in the corner by the piled belongings
He calls ‘my God,’ they say.
He became a thorough Kalmyk, they say,
Brought calamity upon his head, they say,
And took a Kalmyk wife, they say.
His wife bore him children,
