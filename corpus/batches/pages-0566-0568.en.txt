When the year's twelve months were full,
Upon Karacha's people
A famine fell.
Karacha's numerous Noygut,
Lamenting that they could find no grain,
Scattered their four kinds of livestock.
Their strength exhausted, their vigor gone,
The hapless Noygut then
Fled in every direction.
Their people were left weeping:
Locusts had overrun and ravaged their land.
All the people gathered;
When such a calamity came,
All their livestock perished.
Their great people could find no grain
And were sorely distressed, I heard.
What one asks, the Almighty
Will grant, I heard.
The dun colt approaching its fifth year
Has now reached five, they say.
For Karacha, khan of the Noygut,
The time to part with it has come, they say.
Listen to my words:
Give all your wheat
And take the dun colt.
If you gain possession of the dun,
Your hopes will flourish and unfold,
And all misfortune will be cut away.
Khizir will visit and bring you fortune;
The true God will grant you a horse.
Ride it for six months: it will not weaken.
Until it reaches sixty years of maturity,
It will not grow old with worn-out teeth.
Ride it scouting: it will not tire.
Ride it to the rear camp: it will not sicken.
Ride it into gunfire: it will not weaken.
That is the peerless steed.
God Most High, the Almighty,
For the truly fierce-maned Khan Manas
To find, choose, and ride—
That is the horse He has ordained.
A horse descended from the unseen,
Truly surging into the holy war,
It was brought forth for your good fortune.
Such is that wondrous steed:
From the east to the west,
It has strength to run untiringly.
That is the horse to find and ride.
When it treads upon black stone,
The stone crumbles like millet.
Simply give it its head,
And it flies like a cloud with a thunderous rush.
It receives a saint's blessing
And will be called Akkula.
Its swiftness makes it restless.
My champion visited by Khizir,
All the mighty ones will meet you.
You are the champion; you are the hero.
You are a lion surpassing mortals;
Your title is the Illustrious Champion.
For the human children of the Creator,
You are the lion born into this world.
Your destiny is open; you have good fortune.
Against the eighteen thousand worlds,
You have strength enough to stand;
You have stature enough to contend.
I have recognized and favored your courage.
Akkula, the noble lion's horse—
Waiting six months for you,
I have sought to find it for you.
I, Babadyikan, have come;
My son Manas, know this:
I am the farmers' companion.
I recognize and favor your courage.
Fattening the wasted horse,
Filling the hungry belly,
Bringing abundance upon the earth—
Of God, Allah Most High,
I bear that charge.
I am the mighty saint's eye.
My champion, remain mindful:
I am abundance itself.
Of the renowned Babadyikan,
I am the watching dark eye.
More than his eye, I myself
Am his very watchful self.
Having met you in the open steppe,
My son Manas, listen:
You have received Babadyikan's blessing.
Among the people upon this earth,
You are named the Illustrious Champion.
Oh, my Manas, amen!
May no mortal advance against you;
My champion, against you yourself
May none who comes to face you survive!
May you wear a coat no bullet pierces;
May you have a horse no bullet reaches!
Bring slaughter upon those who covet your possessions.
If an enemy girds himself for battle,
Bend his back and take your revenge!
Your companions are eighty-four champions.
May those you face fall dead,
And may a red conflagration rise!
May no one who grapples with you prosper;
May no one who attacks you survive!
May your road lie smooth;
May the hero Khizir be your companion!
May Akkula be fat and fit;
You alone, my fierce-maned one,
May you be the equal of forty!”
So, meeting Manas,
Babadyikan
Bestowed his blessing.
Before a word could be said,
In the blink of an eye,
The white-bearded elder beside him
Vanished from sight.
Manas stood bewildered:
“He vanished from sight!” he said,
Staring, dumbfounded with wonder.
Then Manas, that dear lion,
Put his hands behind his back.
The dear hero moved awkwardly;
Beneath him was swift Aybanboz.
Taking off the saddle, leaving its back bare,
Onto the moonlike yellow steppe—
