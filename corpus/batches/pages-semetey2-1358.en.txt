They plundered everything and settled in ease.
The bloodthirsty khan’s treasury—
They could not finish dividing it.
Gold and silver
They could not load it all.
Noble brides and beautiful girls
They could not finish carrying off.
The great host of the Kara-Zhediger,
With Khan Kanchoro grown rich,
Glutted with all the world’s wealth,
Made for humankind until Judgment Day
An unforgettable example,
Flooding in and seizing the land,
Slaughtering and destroying many,
They overcame the infidels guarding the gate,
And entered to plunder the women in the palace.
They pulled out the roof poles and laid them flat,
They knocked down the main pillars,
And Aychyrok’s White Palace
Was what brave Kyiaz meant to take.
When he came and surrounded it,
When he tried to force Chyrok,
Inside the palace Aychyrok
Sat there in proud composure,
Too weary even to speak.
This woman Aychyrok,
This radiant beauty,
Her mail coat shimmered,
She shone like the rising moon;
The hapless fairy maiden’s
Manner was like this.
Pearl and ruby buttons,
Each as large as a fist, lay on her breast;
She spoke sadly, yet still shone.
Her sixty-keyed amulet
Hung, it seemed, upon the breast
Of that ill-fated celestial maiden, jingling.
With a thimble-small mouth and camel-calf eyes,
A sheath-slender waist and a snow-white face—
Look well upon her marks of beauty.
This woman Aychyrok,
This graceful beauty,
Had a gentle bearing and honeyed speech;
No such soul is born among humankind.
She swayed like a poppy,
In a fitted vest and silk gown.
The pampered, hapless woman’s
Hem streamed out like a spring.
When Aychyrok looked straight at him,
To brave Kyiaz’s eyes
She seemed like the rising moon.
When he saw Aychyrok,
Brave Kyiaz, son of Agysh,
Nearly lost his reason,
And stared, consumed with grief.
The hapless woman, faintly smiling,
Had a face wounded though bright as the sun,
And sorrow shadowed her smiling head.
Her dear hair, long as meadow grass,
Was spread across her breast.
Into her white face, smooth as a mirror,
Five fingernails had dug deep.
When brave Kyiaz came and surrounded her,
Aychyrok despaired of life,
Her color drained and her face went pale.
Blood flowing from both cheeks
Poured down over her breast.
Then Chyrok formed a plan:
If Kyiaz came close,
“I will draw Zulpukor from its sheath
And bring a final, crushing disaster
Upon this rabid dog.
I will somehow find my chance;
I will strike off this unclean slave’s head,
And settle the calamity once and for all.
The black blood from inside him
I will gulp down and swallow.”
Such was her fierce and terrible wrath.
