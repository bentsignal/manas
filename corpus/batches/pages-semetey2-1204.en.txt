At the face of his right shoulder blade,
At the edge of his spinal bone,
At the region of his vital windpipe—
“This is the place where Kyyaz,
The avenger of blood, must die,” he said,
And drove the spear home.
Sary Khan unleashed calamity:
Kyyaz doubled over on Tootoru,
And the huge, mountain-like warrior
Nearly went flying from the saddle.
He slipped backward from the saddle,
His feet came free of the stirrups,
The two fronts of his robe hung loose,
And his two eyes rolled wildly.
Straining in anguish,
Kyyaz, hero of the Jediger,
Despaired now even of his fly-like life.
He sprawled helplessly over Tootoru;
Just as Kyyaz was about to fall,
As Kyyaz, hero of the Jediger,
Clearly reached the hour of his death,
The vigor of youth left him,
His world contracted around him,
And he cried out in fury.
His protector was Kanchoro,
And such was the madman’s nature—
“My comrade!” he cried.
Kanchoro, son of Chubak,
Rode beneath him
Köktelki, the horse of Khan Köbösh.
He wheeled Köktelki around—
God had struck the slave with madness.
Thinking Kyyaz would tumble
From Tootoru and hit the earth,
And that Sary the Bald, who had done so much,
Would needlessly reach his side,
Cut off his head,
And bring about his death,
The raging slave seized sharp steel
And came charging up.
The scoundrel struck Sary Khan’s spear
Across the middle of its shaft
And chopped it clean in two.
Then Sary Khan realized
That Kanchoro was the enemy.
Your uncle’s heart constricted;
Looking neither up nor down,
With a roar that split stone,
And sparing not his life from death,
He began the joyous carnage,
Binding his miserable, mangy life
To a single hair and casting it away.
Looking at nothing around him,
Sary Khan proved steadfast,
Never sparing his life.
Damn you, treacherous slave,
Who forgot every honor and custom,
Wicked son who pisses in the cauldron!
This Sultan Semetey
Kept you beside him and made you a hero,
And made you equal to the sons of warriors.
He raised you from childhood,
And set your wretched life aright.
He hung upon your arm
A keen sword, sharp when drawn;
He gave you command over the Argyn and Kyrgyz,
So none would stir without your knowing;
He let you ride
Over the foes who faced you;
He had ninety beasts slaughtered for your feast.
Hoping you would become a father to the people,
Would slay the enemy who fought you,
Would display true valor,
And shield the Kyrgyz
From every calamity and malicious word,
He deliberately gave you a name fit for a khan
And called you Kanchoro.
Swaying, a hero no enemy could break—
