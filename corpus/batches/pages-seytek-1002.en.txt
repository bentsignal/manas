My mighty, far-seeing uncle,
Bestowed his great blessing.
A blessing to unite the people,
A blessing that would take effect,
A blessing that would stand unwavering
Until Judgment Day.
Ready as a sparrowhawk,
Into Khan Bakai’s mouth
Wisdom came from the unseen.
Such was King Bakai,
Fruit of the warm earth,
Bearing meaning upon his brow,
The citadel of a sovereign nation.
You became the gateway of the city,
The worthiest among men.
Speaking softly, yet striking firmly,
Noble old Bakai
Opened a mine of countless riches:
His speech was sugar, his words had power,
Yet such a soul now falters.
Bakai gave his blessing:
“May you keep a royal court and become khan,
And be a saintly soul,
Splitting a black hair with perfect judgment,
A man of utter fairness.
May your enemy’s path grow narrow;
When you grapple with the foe,
May the spirit of your father Manas
Be your companion and protector!
May the Forty Saints accompany you against the foe,
And may Khidr come at your cry.
May Bak-Baabedin strike in wrath;
May unity, abundance, and sacred grace,
My son, descend upon you!
Oh, amen, my son, amen!
May the milk of every yielding udder flow;
When you reach for your desire,
May the enemy you face be subdued,
And may your brow open brightly like a flower!
When you journey far,
When you enter battle,
May Zhol-Baabedin accompany you!
Making your road smooth,
May my father Shay, Zhol-Baabedin,
Protect you, my son!
May the hostile foe who confronts you
Find no fortune.
May mountains of black stone rise
Across your enemy’s road.
May your name spread throughout the world,
And may your lionheartedness be known, my son!
May no chill misfortune overtake you,
And may no rival defeat you.
Split the black hair with perfect judgment;
Be just toward my people!
May the people never turn against you!
My foals Kuyaly and Seytek,
If your hearts are wicked,
Your faults will bring forth evil.
Be fruit for the nation;
Heed the words I have spoken—
They will be wisdom for you.
If you become khan and maintain a court,
If you become a saintly soul,
If your fortune is complete,
May your name be heard by all who behold you;
Be a lion who shakes the world!
You, Seytek, be the gray-maned champion!
Your first forefather was a khan,
A wealthy man with teeming herds;
Every enemy he fought was laid low.
From among all souls came every hero,
The forty sons, all forty lions,
Who journeyed to Talas
And met the one who inherited the legacy,
Our forefather, the hero Manas.
He mastered the fattening of horses,
And though one man, he was worth forty.
Walking alone, he slew a thousand;
Take after your father and become a lion!
