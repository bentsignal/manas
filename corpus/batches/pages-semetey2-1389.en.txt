The wrestlers who received the command—
May God have cursed them long before—
Did to poor Akbala
Exactly as Chachykey
Had ordered:
They slit her crown and put worms in it,
Slit her soles and rubbed in salt,
Mounted her on a lanky mare,
And clothed her in a coarse felt coat.
The shepherd violated her by force,
The herdsman beat and violated her;
Not once, but a thousand times,
They committed horrors dreadful to know.
Upon Akbala’s head
They heaped not one torment but a thousand,
And brought her terrible misery;
Her noble head was enslaved.
Though a virtuous woman, she was beaten
And reviled by a scabby woman.
To think of it, those were dreadful days:
Kanykey and Akbala
Became slaves to Khan Kanchoro.
Day followed day,
Each passing in torment;
They sent Akbala barefoot
To follow behind the cows.
And poor Kanykey
They made tend the calves.
Let us leave this matter here
And begin again from another side:
Chürök, the moonlike woman—
Upon Chürök they heaped turmoil.
In the havoc inflicted upon Chürök,
Who showed regard for ancestral spirits?
Every one of the bloodthirsty hero’s countless treasures
They went and plundered, leaving nothing.
They seized all her silks,
And brought grievous misery;
They plundered all the gold
And inflicted bitter suffering.
Chürök became war-spoil—
Such was the havoc
The Jediger brought upon her.
In the mountains were ninety thousand horses,
But no swift courser or peerless steed;
After the mountain-like bloodthirsty hero,
No son remained!
On the hills grazed piebald horses,
With coursers and thoroughbreds among them;
There were fleet stallions, but no mares,
And to guard those horses
No heir of the bloodthirsty hero remained—
Such filth is this world!
Countless treasures and myriad possessions
Were seized by fools, who rose to power;
Alas, the precious world had gone awry,
And his poor mother was humiliated.
All the champions were beaten,
All the young wives were plundered,
While Kanchoro ruled the people,
Established in the palace.
Chürök, the moonlike woman,
The Jediger warrior Kyyaz
Had taken as war-spoil.
The myriad herds left by the bloodthirsty hero
He drove away in their entirety,
And every treasure of the lionhearted warrior
He plundered and carried off.
The Jediger raided,
Felling the plane trees,
Breaking down and toppling the palace;
Sixty women and widowed sisters-in-law
They noisily carried off as spoil.
They unleashed terrible misery,
And many households of orphans and widows
Beneath the wrath of warrior Kyyaz
Were slaughtered and left dead.
They cut down and felled the poplars,
