Such was the revelry she staged.
The games of maidens and young men—
Let us leave them here like this.
Of your dear Chürök and Semetey,
Let us now begin the tale.
Sixty pillows and forty quilts,
She had lavishly spread as bedding;
It was a wondrous spectacle,
Filling the human heart with affection.
She had a curtain made of pure silk
Stretched broadly across the wall;
Aychürök, daughter of Akun,
Increased her splendor in this way.
Her incomparable conqueror,
Her princely leopard and sultan—
Swiftly she removed his boots,
And nimbly removed his clothes;
She laid the prince and sultan down
Upon the great bed for delight.
After the sultan had lain down,
Aychürök, daughter of Akun—
Know her purity from this—
Now put her hands into water
And performed the hand ablution;
She poured the water of ghusl over herself,
Purified herself and prepared,
And prayed to the Almighty.
Preparing to lie in the embrace
Of the poor sultan,
"If only I reach my gray-maned wolf," she thought;
Had she not endured in longing,
Never approaching any person,
And lived unharmed?
The gray wolf lay huddled,
His thoughts sorely distracted;
Aychürök, daughter of Akun,
Appeared like the whole world to him.
Fragrant as musk, gleaming like copper,
She appeared like a fairy maiden.
Your prince's thoughts were swept away;
Until Aychürök came near,
The great-hearted princely leopard
Went limp and nearly fainted.
Until Chürök came beside him,
His tiny soul cried out;
He waited in frightened haste,
Every bone in his body trembling.
Early time became late;
"When will Chürök come?" he thought.
Your gray-maned wolf waited impatiently,
His two eyes becoming four.
Aychürök, daughter of Akun,
Pulled off and removed
Her gown made of fine broadcloth.
Swaying softly like dressed leather,
Whimpering like a puppy,
Her face shining like the sun,
She lay down embracing
Her sultan's breast.
They immersed themselves in delight,
And lay upon one bed;
Like two graceful wild ducks,
They now came together.
Like an argali ewe and ram,
They now entwined.
They lay upon one bed,
Immersed in pleasure like this,
And mingled like
Flour made from white wheat;
They slid against one another
Like the hair of a white gazelle.
They joined by their own will,
Embracing and kissing,
Speaking in whispers,
And laughing with ringing voices.
As the lovers lay embraced,
When the fierce conqueror stirred,
Chürök sprang up with a whimper.
