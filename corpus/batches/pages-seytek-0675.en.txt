When he charged shouting, no spear pierced him;
Those who grappled could not reach him.
None proved strong enough to trade blows;
No man defeated him in combat.
Could any man bend down and seize him?
Could anyone who faced him remain alive?
The infidel son of an ogre
Would not sink if cast into a river,
Nor be swept away by a flood.
He did not burn when engulfed by fire,
Nor die beneath a hail of bullets.
Even a slashing sword could not cut him;
The fearsome infidel’s body
Could not be pierced by showers of bullets.
He was one who had been scorched by fire,
With the eyes of a black demon.
Was he a foe created
To inflict suffering upon mankind?
A mountain with broad slopes,
Which no mattock could ever wear away.
He was a dreadful mountain pass
Beyond the strength of man.
He was a wind from Mount Kap
That leveled the whole world.
Everyone who observed him
Called Sarybay a swine.
Born where no people saw him,
He cast the world into turmoil.
His valor was unlike all others;
The sly wretch brought dreadful calamity.
All the people grieved,
Despairing of their precious lives;
The whole nation mourned,
Despairing of its very existence.
If the moon grew dark and veiled,
Though no clouds covered the world,
“What would become of the sun?” they said.
“How will the encampment
Of the poor, few Kyrgyz settle?
What recourse will they have?” they said.
Külchoro’s winged steed—
From time immemorial,
Whenever Almambet’s son Külchoro
Rode forth into battle,
It sighted the foe from afar.
A beast that had campaigned against the khagan,
And brought down mighty khans,
It had grown accustomed to war.
It had a flowing tail and mane,
A magnificent creature indeed.
It leaped like a flea,
And grew sleek as a gun barrel.
It never panicked at the battle cry;
Even on a moonless watch,
It never misplaced a single step.
Its ears shone like lighted lamps,
Its hooves were broad as bowls.
If an enemy deliberately thrust a spear,
It darted aside to right or left.
From every mortal danger
It whisked the warrior away.
It was the winged steed beneath him.
Entering battle, it brought good fortune—
Such was Külchoro’s way.
Would he retreat before death?
His opponent was Sarybay;
Would he ever turn back from combat?
For the infidel son of an ogre
The end of time had come.
His spirit seethed beyond restraint,
And he staked everything upon fate.
His adversary was Sarybay,
A fearsome calamity such as this.
He had seen heroes of countless kinds,
And champions throughout the world,
Yet before his fated death
He had never seen upon the earth
Such a dreadful battle,
Nor a wrestler like this.
Against the infidel son of an ogre—
