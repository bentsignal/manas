All the warriors grew excited:
Their finest drew the bow,
Their most skillful swung the club,
Heroes of powerful build,
Champions who shattered formations,
Bull-necked, with stallion thighs,
Whose grappling crushed the life from men;
They did not retreat even before death,
Nor flee back from a drawn brow.
The loud, deep-chested men
Were at the front of the raid;
Whoever they met, they would chew to pieces,
Tirelessly striking the foe.
Without saying, “I am dead, I give up,”
They advanced straight into the enemy—
Those dear men were at the fore.
The Jediger flooded in,
Their bravest around the edges.
The white palace had lost its standing;
In the hands of the Jediger,
What was there, and what was not?
Their hard men at the edges
Did not flinch when struck by swords;
Their fortunate men behind them
Did not cry out when pierced by spears.
Facing the red blaze,
They shouted and thrust their spears.
They stood face to face,
Clashing and fighting.
There were bold fellows too
Who surged forward when ordered;
Before one could say this or that,
In the time it took to blink,
Before one could utter another word,
They thundered over everything.
Once roused, they pressed to the end;
They cast a fearsome shadow,
Subdued all who faced them,
And plundered without leaving a thing.
Their renowned men were in front:
They could drink and never fill themselves with blood;
Facing a man, they did not leave him alive.
Their courage was great, their venom strong;
Whoever challenged them lost his life.
Broad-chested, wide-breasted,
They had many strapping men.
When a fierce gun cracked,
Many indeed started and fled.
There were also many clumsy men
Who joined the ranks and seized livestock,
Saying, “I too am a son of man,”
When the host was hemmed in.
Though they had tongues, they understood no speech,
And would not simply go where they were driven;
There were calves like those as well.
There were cowards too
Who never stepped outside at night.
An earth-splitting host of ten thousand
Committed dreadful deeds before one’s eyes;
Old women and young brides—
Against all those within the stronghold
They poured in by force.
There was no strength to exchange blows,
No means to grapple with them,
No power left to resist.
God’s sun in the sky was overcast;
Upon widow, orphan, and the weak,
Upon the wretched subjected to torment,
Sixty afflictions came at once.
Their insides burned to embers;
The Jediger surrounded them completely.
The dear Kyrgyz who had settled broad Talas,
Who would not yield a companion’s life,
Were gone.
The Jediger flooded in,
Showing no pity for those dear lives,
Paying no heed
That they were widows and orphans.
