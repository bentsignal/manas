His bearing was proud, his eyes deep black,
Tall in stature and broad of shoulder,
Both strongman and lionhearted hero,
A luckless hero capable of making
The whole world submit.
He looked from side to side,
Licking his lips,
Roaring in anger,
Shouting fearsomely.
His redness was like dun-colored fur,
And if you heard his cry,
It was like a lion's roar.
His eyes were like embers of saxaul,
When his gray mane crackled,
It was like a bolt of lightning,
Such was the sight of his power.
His giant frame was like a lion,
No soul could withstand his terror,
But panicked, unable to find a way out.
He seemed to swoop upon whomever he met,
And bear them off like a giant bird,
Fearing nothing beneath the moon and sky;
Allah Most High indeed creates
His servants in every form,
And such was Külchoro's form.
The space between his shoulders
Was like a field sown with grain;
Would anyone who challenged
That wrathful gray-maned hero remain alive?
The corners of his eyes were like flint,
As though this gray-maned hero could swallow the world
At the mere sound of his name.
If you looked at his ears,
The poor man bore the mark of a fortress,
As though he sucked out men's souls in combat.
His wildness was like a boar's,
His crackling like dry straw,
And a rash enemy who fought him
Could neither measure nor master his strength.
His charge was like a whirlwind.
I am dead, not alive,
Yet instead of dying I became an ember,
Living on as I waited for you.
Appearing alive, you made me rejoice,
Only to burn and scorch me utterly.
Vanishing beyond sight,
You abandoned livestock and household without a guardian.
When I catch hold of you, you say nothing;
Why did you begin acting like a helpless wretch?
For pity's sake, uncle, speak—
What my eyes saw from the ridge!
Would it not have been far better
Had you died instead of living?
Exalting your lionhearted pride,
You conceal your words without speaking,
You have knocked the wind from me,
You have quenched and chilled my breath.
Tell me your words, uncle;
If you stand there suddenly
Without speaking or uttering a sound,
And my wrath rises till I stand rigid,
A mighty quarrel will suddenly erupt.
The mystic's cave will turn to dust,
The face of the earth will run red with blood.
If I roar out in anger,
Those I encounter will lie dead,
So take care, lest I spring suddenly.
I may pounce upon you without warning!”
So spoke Külchoro,
His lionlike nature showing,
His furious rage pouring out.
His beard was like a quiver case,
His upright mustache
Like the haft of a greasy axe;
Such was Choro when you saw him.
He looked every bit the hero one could desire;
When wrath made him stand rigid,
He seemed ready to devour whoever challenged him,
Such was Külchoro's nature.
