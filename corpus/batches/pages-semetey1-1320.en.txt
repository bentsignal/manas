Let us reckon the years,
And choose an auspicious year
To go to mourning Beijing!
Let us reckon the days,
Set the day down in writing,
And choose an auspicious day
To go to lowering Beijing!
The Kakanchin have their people
And giants of many kinds;
By custom they drink the blood
Of the mortal who challenges them.
Chin-Machin has its people,
And the true Kalcha has eyes;
Its people swarm in multitudes,
And by custom drink the blood
Of those who go against them.
Yesterday, when Saint Ali the lion
Went there intending to fight,
When he brought splendid slaughter,
When he went intending to destroy them
And unleashed great turmoil,
When he was about to convert
The defeated Chinese to Islam,
A voice sounded from heaven
And appeared to Ali the lion:
"There is abundance and no hunger;
This Beijing surges with people.
In heaven Chalyiar's voice rang out;
It has no faith, but it has number.
Carpets lie spread across the land;
Great Beijing is bathed in radiance.
Turn back without troubling yourself."
When he heard this voice,
Saint Ali turned away;
Without fighting Beijing,
He turned back midway.
That is a land from which none return;
Those Chinese are a prolific people,
Smoldering anew whenever they are depleted,
Rooting themselves and growing ever stronger—
A people upon whom a prayer remains!
They multiply whenever they die;
Those are the ruling Chinese people.
The Almighty himself chose it;
That is a land blessed with abundance!
Do not behave recklessly,
Saying you will set your face
Toward fortified Beijing!
Yesterday, in Kakanchin's Beijing,
Its Kalmaks swarmed;
When God scattered their seed,
They covered the face of the earth.
From sunrise to sunset,
They are a multitude made of men,
A teeming Chinese people.
Kara Khan, father of the people,
Was of Kyrgyz stock.
He met his death and died;
When Kara Khan's eight sons
Were left young and crawling,
The Chinese seized them
And divided them by the lash;
Those fated to die perished.
They drove one away to Eren
And cast him into the deep.
They drove one to Altai,
And another to Kangai.
The eight were scattered,
Some of them twins born of one mother;
Unable to find them by asking everywhere,
How many souls withered away.
That Yakyp was driven away,
And Manas was born in Altai.
When he heard of his kinsmen's fate,
Your falcon father, the leopard,
Born there in Altai,
Threw China into turmoil.
Your bloodthirsty father, the leopard,
Unleashed the upheaval;
Esen Khan was a great lord,
