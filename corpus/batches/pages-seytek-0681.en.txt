Behold, your choro stood thus,
Seeming like a sheer blue mountain.
His Surkon beneath him,
He wheeled about, leveling his spear;
The steed he rode against the foe
He gripped tightly with his thighs.
Its shaft was steel and its point was keen,
A decorated spear with a tasseled tip,
Shining there in his hand.
Such was the nature of that ornate spear.
From a cluster of barberry on Opol Mountain,
It had been torn out by the roots.
The accursed, ill-fated wood
He had strongmen twist;
So that a sword could not cut through it,
He had it wound with black wire,
And bound with sinew.
He had it covered in mare’s hide,
And had Bölökbai fit the haft;
So it would not slip from the hand,
He had its handgrip grooved.
So it might last for many years,
He had craftsmen decorate it.
He had it leveled at the opposing foe,
And had a tassel fixed to its point.
A master beyond all masters,
He had befriended the prophet David:
The lame smith Bölökbai—
The hero Bölökbai had made it.
They strove and strove,
He and David taking counsel together.
When it came to working the bellows,
A levy was laid upon the Kyrgyz,
And bull-calves were slaughtered.
When it came to making charcoal,
Whole forests were destroyed.
He forged and drew out its point,
And, so that everyone it struck
Might writhe and die,
He tempered it in human tears.
He was a masterful man against the foe.
Subduing the whole wide world—
Was he not the choro accustomed to that?
Punishing the whole world—
Was he not the choro who had done so?
Born into this world—
Was he not the choro who had come?
The keenest of sages,
His eyes could see afar.
He had slain multitudes in many conflicts,
And emerged alone from the contest.
His strength was extraordinary,
And he was famed as the sharp-witted child.
That Külchoro was a lion;
How many giants who faced him
Were leveled with the black earth!
While Sarybai stood speaking thus,
The man adept and masterful with the spear,
Full of cunning and fierce in wrath,
The mighty man who spared no one,
Who never drew back from his youth,
And from hardship, conflict, and suffering
Had never once retreated—
He was that dark-gray-maned lion,
Among all souls ever born,
He was that vigilant hero.
Your noble uncle Külchoro,
When the warrior was distressed,
The black-maned leopard,
The spirit of Manas himself,
Made ready before him in truth;
The short-maned gray lion
Seemed poised to tear him open,
Preparing himself face-to-face.
His lashes burned like embers;
His adversary Sarybai
Coiled to strike like a dragon.
His broad front teeth, large as a gate,
Were shattered and scattered like flour;
At the sight, the heart was troubled,
