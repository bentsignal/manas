I shall plunder your fatty meat, Burut!
I shall stride far—make room, Burut;
Prepare now what I have demanded, Burut!
If you will not prepare it, speak your words, Burut;
Name the place where you will fight, Burut!"
Speaking wrathful words, Ezkara
Turned aside and raised a cloud of pale dust;
Playing a pipe made of pale brass,
He galloped away on Chongküröng.
To Kongurbay and Zholoy,
He came for counsel.
"Hey, Kongurbay, great Zholoy,
I shall settle accounts
With these foolish Kyrgyz Burut.
Let us not stand watching their feast;
Let us test how many giants they have.
If their giants have no strength,
Let us thrust spears into their feast
And slaughter every one without remainder.
Let us seize their racehorses;
Here in the midst of this feast,
Let us firmly unleash disorder.
Let us plunder their cooking hearths
And beat those who have come.
Let us trample the place of the feast
And swarm upon the servants
Who sit cooking the meat.
Speaking taunting, chilling words,
Calling them bastard Burut,
Wearing shameless coats,
Let us jostle and attack them.
Let us utterly plunder
The spectacle-loving Burut.
Let us stop the feast they have given,
Push aside their leaders,
And take possession of all their livestock.
Whoever resists,
Let us slaughter utterly, leaving none;
The meek who know no words,
Let us bind them alive
And make them denekul
In the land of Kakan-Chyn."
When their khan spoke thus,
Would the mass of black commoners
Merely stand and watch?
Would God strike the people
For standing still and watching?
The Chinese and Kalmyk champions
Fell upon Kökötöy's meat-cooking hearth
And began plundering it.
Not allowing the plunder to pass,
They beat the servants.
The black Kalmyks and Manchu people—
Their multitude was ten thousand black worms.
They gathered together,
Fighting over uncooked meat.
The stronger among them
Snatched it thundering from the weak;
Unable to understand one another's speech,
They stuffed it into sacks.
Those teeming Chinese had grown up indoors,
A people who had never seen the outside.
The snarling infidels
Never before, at any time,
Had been a people who saw meat.
From the middle of some meat,
Dark blood glistened;
At the edge of some meat,
Bloody foam seethed.
The black Kalmyks snarled,
Not knowing it was blood.
The foolish Chinese fumed,
Not knowing it was blood;
They did not know it was raw.
Fighting over bloody meat,
The smiling wretches
Beat one another with bones.
