Külchoro and Kanchoro,
You two grew up as equals.
You are confidants raised side by side;
Hero, you are comrades of the same age.
Spear him as you see fit;
Hero, bind your enemy.
These teeming black-clad Jediger—
I will not contest them with you;
Take their khanship for yourself.
Hero, do whatever the occasion demands.
Hero, Kanchoro and Külchoro,
You are confidants who grew up together;
United when you were no bigger than lice,
Joined when you were no bigger than thumbs.
Your sheep shared the same fold,
Your heads shared the same fellowship;
Whatever you found, you gathered together.
I know the hero’s secret—
Spear him yourself!”
So spoke brave Kyyaz;
Terrified by the hero’s venom,
Brave Kyyaz stood in anguish,
Pleading with Kanchoro.
When Kanchoro heard this,
He bristled in wrath,
Swelling boldly with resolve;
His eyes gaped like an opened pit,
His face gleamed like oiled wheat.
His two cheeks hung loose,
Enough to feast two giants;
When the savage grew enraged,
He seemed ready to slaughter anyone he fought.
With a steel haft and a keen edge,
He strapped on his battle-axe,
Put on his arrow-proof coat,
And his hostile intent became plain.
That broad-jawed battle-axe,
Its steel edge honed sharp,
Its handle wound with gold,
Destined for the enemy he would fight,
Was fashioned in every splendid form
By the master of masters, the great smith,
By Bölökbay himself.
When it came time to temper it,
The waters of the Black Spring were drained;
The smith perfected its edge.
Unable to supply enough charcoal,
A forest of plane trees was destroyed;
No creature could endure the bellows’ demands,
So a blue bull was slaughtered.
Such was the battle-axe.
He slipped it over his bare forearm;
Look closely, and Kanchoro
Raged like a white tiger,
Growling and working himself into fury.
The wretch strode forth
And addressed Külchoro.
But poor Külchoro
Had no strength left to exchange blows,
No means left there to wrestle,
No power left to grapple.
All strength had deserted the hero;
He stood there in angry anguish,
The poor man,
Pleading with Kanchoro:
“Oh, my dear Kanchoro,
You have known me from our earliest days—
My warrior who took the socketed spear
And shattered armies beyond number!
Today, elder brother and khan,
I am a wretch fallen into your hands.
Like grass swept into a wildfire,
Robbed of every protector,
I bow my neck to death.
We were foster brothers from the beginning;
My lord, I stand in your hands.
Hero, if you stiffen in wrath,
I will not resist—slaughter me.
