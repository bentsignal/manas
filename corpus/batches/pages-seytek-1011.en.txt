O my dear people with your royal seat,
Faithless indeed is this world;
Alas, we are leaving it behind.
Kara-Kyr, where the people summered,
Alas, we are leaving the land behind.
Great people with your sheltering fortress,
Alas, we are leaving the nation behind.
The people and their royal fortress remain,
Children and kin alike remain,
While cruel death has seized my back.
We mounted and set out upon the road,
To the land from which none return.
Now sight will fade and life depart.
O hapless people, your strength will leave you;
The sable cap upon my head—
Who has not worn it with a flourish?
When fortune stood at its height,
All who saw it raised a clamor.
Girding my waist for sacrifice,
Where my cord was cut and my newborn body washed,
My land, like Ala-Too,
From wrathful enemies
I guarded my fortunate Kyrgyz people.
I gave you pacing colts to ride,
Made you laugh and set your hearts at ease,
And gave you fine silk clothes to wear;
Each stream in every valley
I allotted, making each of you rich,
And found many ways to serve you.
O hapless people, for your sake,
Not letting even a breath of wind touch you,
I kept witless enemies
From coming near your lands;
Against every enemy who confronted me,
I turned white deer into cattle,
Driving and claiming them as booty.
O dear people, for your sake,
I turned blue-gray deer into cattle
And claimed every land I sought.
O Kyrgyz, for your sake,
I loaded the cannon,
And destroyed all who opposed me.
Whenever they met me face-to-face,
I drove them all away and destroyed them.
From among the five heroes,
The warrior Semetey began to speak:
“O Kyrgyz of Ala-Too,
I built houses for you,
Planted and tended gardens for you.
All your treasure chests
I filled with wealth of yellow gold.
I fulfilled your every wish,
Made your pastures spacious,
And covered your hillsides with livestock.
Your brave men and nobles—
I made every one of you a khan.
While this bloodthirsty soul still lived,
I made your homeland broad.
To blood-spilling enemies
I never surrendered
Even your barren yearling colt.
I became the khan who ruled the royal camp;
But now, a traveler and stranger,
I am taking leave of this world.
Death has settled upon my heart;
I am departing—farewell—
Along the road from which none return.
The angel has stretched out five fingers;
The decree of the Creator
Has issued its good command.
When the Almighty gives His command,
Does any mortal fail to submit?
If the true God so desires,
Will He not grant a soul
Even seven days of borrowed life?
The fate that has been decreed—
Must not every mortal behold it?
The deep black earth below—
Does it not bury the whole world alike?
Can the earth devour all but leave the soul?
