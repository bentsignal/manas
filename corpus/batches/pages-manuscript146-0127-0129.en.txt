Alas, he did not turn back,
Knowing the news, bitter with rage,
The Chinese deed was a hell of venom,
Saying they were Kyrgyz,
All the Chinese fell into turmoil,
He gave his blessing without stirring.
He asked a blessing from the people,
Their ordered ranks were shattered,
He hurled them about like a ball,
Fate’s command was this great,
To see how their designs would turn,
Ebegei stood at the edge of the people,
To Kogoi, the blue-maned elder,
Second among the true Kyrgyz,
To his friends Ebegei and Kogoi,
To consider the broad matter,
For Ebegei and Kogoi to go,
To serve as support and visible strength,
Let Kenen mount without delay.
The earth stirred without breaking,
His journey grew this long,
It was abundant, not slight,
He crossed the dark river valley,
He skirted the black-ridged mountain,
The steeds filled the ravines,
Countless gold adorned them,
Divided into thousands and hundreds,
Their dust struck against dust,
Countless ranks gathered,
They made a host that shook the earth,
My hero Kenen, the blue-maned one,
Making the mountain level beneath him,
Rising toward the sky and halting,
The army surged in alarm and dust,
Spears appeared on every side,
All the heroes divided into ranks,
Their champions rejoiced.
A wrathful man with no tethering post,
A lone sharp-maned hero,
If booty came by chance,
Saying it would strengthen the people,
O brother in the moonless darkness,
Saying it would become night for the people.
The old yellow one came with news,
What happened rank after rank,
To all the hundred thousand servants,
He appeared measuring himself against repentance,
Chagatai mounted the pale-gray horse,
Becoming one with the gray-mounted men,
Girded with black steel,
Gathering courage, he struck firmly,
Binding it firmly in the name of repentance,
Bringing a dream no ninety could take,
Tall of stature and broad of shoulder,
Both a champion and a hero,
The heart of his whole region,
Mounting him at the true place,
Chagatai was to fight,
They were to contend and dig for the people,
A spear of steel would be made,
Thrusting the spear and striking the host,
They would take the road from across the world,
They would mount their tulpar steeds,
Mounting tulpars, they would seize the banner,
Rejoicing in the one who was born,
Allowing no enemy to approach,
Setting the fortress defenders’ hearts aflame,
Mounting him at the true place,
Dressing him in a shield for truth,
Becoming like a great fortress,
Becoming one who had stood there before,
This struggle would be wondrous,
He took a spear as large as a pestle.
He bound them so none could move,
He again threw the enemy into disorder,
When wrath was granted, he wheeled about.
When the one he met became venomous,
The matter wove itself,
Putting on a black coat,
From the hardship that watched the ravine,
He struck against the enemy,
Driving his champion elsewhere,
One who had thrust forward before,
Who had ever seen such a crimson figure?
A red sun from the ring-bearing lord,
His manner and gait were unlike others,
Gripping his spear, he tensed,
His blue trousers were wrapped close,
At the cry of conflict he sprang up,
Showing his warrior nature,
Swaying like a contrary tempest,
If a thousand fell in the night,
When his brow entered trouble,
Even if all stopped mounting,
They dressed him in an iron coat,
They mounted him on Sai-Kuman,
One who had pushed forward before,
Who had ever seen such a crimson figure?
When the blue eagle observed,
He mounted the tulpar granted by Tengir,
Binding the condition that allowed no delay,
His charge flew like a cloud,
Seeing the enemy, the people turned again,
As though the Creator had made it so,
His stallion moved as if plucking strings.
The dark-red colt was a living force,
He alone rode the camel.
Toward the snare cast across the mountain,
The gathered peoples split apart.
