Would you have waited for me, my princely khan?
He was a broad ridge beyond six passes,
Who dwelt in the Ala-Too,
Whom no man could defeat in combat,
A lion born among the Kyrgyz.
Though women bear sons to men,
They said that among mankind
His like would never be born again.
You were the bridge laid across the river;
My bridge has broken and fallen.
It is this that I lament.”
So she cried out in anguish,
Your fierce but noble sister-in-law,
Wailing as she invoked the great warrior.
While Aichürök lamented thus,
The birches wept and the willows wept,
Every living soul that watched her wept,
The bristling lions wept,
And the black earth groaned and wept.
All who wept
Wept consumed by their own grief.
When a hero leaves a widowed wife,
This vain world lies desolate;
When a son remains without seeing his father,
When the river dries and leaves only its bed,
When darkness covers human eyes—
“This is calamity,” they cried.
The multitude wailed in an uproar;
Black Giant and Kyzylsart mourned
As though the leopard-like hero had died that very day.
It was as though they had dug the earth with mattocks,
Recited his funeral prayer with due honour,
And buried him with the congregation that day;
As though they had laid him in his coffin,
The nation groaned and the people clamoured,
As though carrying him to the grave.
Black Giant and Kyzylsart
Grieved, the two khans weeping;
The khan and all the people wept,
Their hearts deeply stirred
For the woman Aichürök,
As countless hardships loomed ahead.
Her black hair untouched by a comb—
Aichürök wept until she was spent
And spread it strand by strand;
Her precious hair, long as meadow grass,
She spread across her breast.
Your kinsman Kyzylsart—
When she saw Kyzylsart,
It seemed her precious sultan had died.
The people cried out and the nation stirred,
As though burying their great warrior that day.
She raised a piercing cry,
And her white face, lovely as a plane tree—
Alas for the frailty of woman—
She clawed and tore again and again.
Dark blood flowed where she tore,
And Aichürök’s strength departed;
Her pale complexion faded,
And the blood flowing from both cheeks
Poured down over her breast.
Blood streamed like water through a ditch,
And Aichürök’s strength departed.
Hear how she raised her wailing voice,
Lamenting for her vanished lord:
“A charger that would not tire in six months’ riding
You were, my princely khan.
A falcon that took the wolf when properly flown
You were, my princely khan.
If ten thousand rivers joined,
You were their confluence, my princely khan.
If ten thousand cliffs joined,
You were their mighty collision, my princely khan.
Of the heavens and the blue sky
You were the supporting pillar, my princely khan.
Among ravenous lion-warriors
You alone were supreme, my princely khan.
Those upon whom you fixed your gaze
You would subdue, my princely khan.
Those who faced you eye to eye you conquered—
