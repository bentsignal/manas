When they had slept their fill,
The swift steeds tugged and plunged
As they cropped the feather grass.
When dawn began to swell,
And light touched the earth,
The stars scattered from overhead,
As daybreak opened wide.
The stars vanished one by one from the sky,
And the cold wind before sunrise shivered;
The stars streamed from the heavens,
And every living creature
Rose from its place, calling out.
The willow branches bent,
Lines were traced across the sand,
The reed tops trembled,
And young larks chirped over the steppe.
The yellow dawn broke,
And Libra sank from the sky.
Mist thinned from mountain after mountain,
And countless springs poured through the ravines.
Ninety kinds of birds sang plaintively,
And the nightingale’s voice was heard;
The cuckoo called melodiously,
And all kinds of woodland birds
Began to speak.
Wild goats cried from the heights,
And stags called from the knolls.
The fog lifted everywhere,
Light spread over the earth,
And when the land came into view,
The horse beneath him
Was Köbösh Khan’s Kökborchuk.
Upon that racer Kökborchuk,
Striking right and left with his whip,
Your hero Kanchoro
Rode on in feverish haste,
The consummate, accursed villain,
Madly searching for Külchoro.
When he looked toward Kara-Üngkür,
Eager to find Külchoro,
He saw the cave mouth had been covered
And that across its entrance
Rotten grass had been scattered.
The hero Külchoro was hidden there,
But the heel mark of his horseshoe-soled boot
Stood out plainly in the open.
Kanchoro halted and shouted,
Calling to hero Kyyaz
Of the Jediger:
“Fortunate hero Kyyaz,
Your campaign has prospered!
What you lost has come onto your road;
Though delayed, your luck has turned—
Your fugitive has come onto your road.
Hero Kyyaz of the Jediger,
Your old good fortune has returned.
Let your wrestlers and lions,
Your heroes who boast of their strength,
Your able men skilled with the spear,
And all your strongest men come without mercy
To seize Külchoro!
Come, you wolves never sated with blood;
Let your most capable men
Charge the warrior head-on
And capture him alive.
Plunge Külchoro
Into misery so deep he cannot escape;
Subject the wretch
To merciless torment.
Bind both his hands
And hamstring him.
Do not kill him or let him vanish;
Take vengeance as heavy as a mattock.
Hollow out his right shoulder,
And flay his left shoulder!”
Make him the lowly slave
Of some sixty herdsmen,
Fetching their firewood and water.
