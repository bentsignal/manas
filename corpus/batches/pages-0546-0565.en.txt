If the dun yearling lives,
All your coffers
It will fill with abundant gold.
A true peerless steed, the dun yearling
Has the speed of a whirlwind.
It will not stop for any man;
Such is its formidable nature.
Its desire, the dun yearling
Shows the promise of attaining.
Its master, in the end,
It shows the promise of finding and reaching.
This wondrous dun yearling's
Master was born along with it.
If the dun yearling finds its master,
It will bring tumult to the world.
This dun yearling, may it be slaughtered—
Belongs to a back one can lean upon,
To one born above other mortals,
To a lion who will be a support.
Heed the words we speak;
Without grieving over the steed,
Find its rightful master.
You have happened to rear it;
The dun yearling is not your property.
This renowned, wondrous steed
Is no horse for an ordinary mortal to ride.
It has a giant to ride it into battle.
The wretched dun yearling
Has a master it awaits.”
Saying this to Khan Karacha,
The judge returned to his village.
