With twelve Manchus escorting him.
Beside this uncle of yours
Stand six divisions of armed troops,
And the peerless hero Kongurbay.
The great blue-gray-bearded Manchu,
Whose jeweled button is as large as a fist—
This is how your uncle planned to deceive him.
The mediator between them was a blazing fire:
Külchoro, son of Almambet.
‘I have become Kyrgyz again after living among the Chinese;
This swine you see with your own eyes
I have made my kinsman,’ he said.
He spoke with Khan Kalcha,
Binding their promise firmly;
They swore a covenant unto death,
Exchanged many solemn words,
And licked the edge of the sword.
The cunning man, Almambet’s son,
Had drawn the line
And devised the spectacle.
The time had come for him
To begin treachery against Prince Kong.
Boundless was the resourcefulness
Of Külchoro, son of Almambet,
Who subdued the whole wide world,
A lion-warrior who took after his father.
When he was only twelve years old,
He smashed a royal camp and carried off plunder;
Hardened and sated with blood,
He was hailed as a lion cub.
He proved himself a gray wolf;
He destroyed Chynkojo
And sent that gray wolf Toltoy
Into the grave.
Yesterday he spread turmoil across many lands—
Throughout Alty-Shaar and Kokon,
As far as Margalang,
He raided and conquered them all.
Ah, a lion-warrior upheld by the saint Shay,
A bold and radiant hero—
A hero born of a noble father,
Who subdued the whole wide world.
He won Kongurbay over with his words,
Revealing what a profound champion he was;
He was about to draw the fatal line.
Such is a true hero when you behold him—
Your gray wolf was about to stage his feat.
Moving between them, that crafty man
Had marked out the city of Beijing;
He was about to bring down upon it
A calamity no mortal had ever seen.
Setting the caravan in motion and leading the migration,
He would cunningly avenge himself on your Prince Kalcha;
Such was the falcon’s intention:
To pursue him to the bitter end
And finish Kalcha by cutting off his head.
He meant to inflict surpassing torment
And avenge the noble
Prince Almambet.
If his strength sufficed, he would attack
And cast Alööke Khan’s Kongurbay
Flat upon his back.
The nimble-born lion-warrior,
A blazing fire wherever he turned,
Would make whole armies give way when he struck,
And burn every enemy he faced.
Could the valor of Almambet’s warrior son
Truly be so great?
He was both a hero and a lion,
One who subdued the whole wide world,
And they called that champion Külchoro.
Resourceful by birth, Külchoro
Displayed his cunning;
He won over the Manchu khan Ezkara
And Alööke’s Kongurbay,
Persuading Alööke with his words,
And displaying his lionhearted courage—
The warrior among spear-wielders—
