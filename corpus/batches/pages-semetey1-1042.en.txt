"My dear princely leopard,
Before he has had time to rest,
Will be left with regret," she thought.
Aychürök stood thinking this,
Conceiving a scheme no one knew,
Displaying her great wisdom,
And guided the heroic prince and sultan
Toward Afghanistan.
They entered Kara-Shaar;
It was Khan Akun's city,
A settlement of the populous Afghans.
Aychürök, who led them,
Was the master among women,
And the daughter of Khan Akun.
When they reached great Afghanistan,
Its mastiffs barked thunderously,
Its bitches yelped and barked,
Its donkeys brayed on the rooftops,
And roosters crowed from the city.
Broad Afghanistan lay there,
Divided into guarded quarters,
With dark smoke visible.
Broad Afghanistan lay there;
Having traveled far upon the road,
The lions filed into
The palace prepared for them.
Aychürök's white palace
Had iron gates and solid tin;
Though the poor woman was only a maiden,
Her retinue was exceedingly great.
At the mouth of the doorway,
The champions posted as guards,
Holding clubs as large as houses,
All eighty rose to their feet.
Passing among those eighty,
Your sister-in-law went on.
She murmured to some,
And whispered to others;
When they heard this news,
The guards flew like arrows
And took the prince's horse.
At a pillar whose post was gold,
They tethered the exhausted Buurul
High and secure.
Aychürök's forty young women
Tied up horses in a bustle;
Their quickness was beyond measure,
As they tied them fast in a flash.
Within the white palace,
In the great ceremonial pavilion,
Akylay flew like an arrow,
And his sister-in-law opened the door.
Led by the prince who had brought them,
Twenty-five people entered now.
The prince now mounted the place of honor,
An enormous golden throne like a camel.
Around it stood a grove of plane trees,
And in its center a golden throne;
This was a garden granted by the Creator.
The princely leopard Semetey
At this time had gone as a bridegroom
To Khan Akun's city.
Your renowned sister-in-law Aychürök—
Ask not about the hero's estate;
She had known plainly beforehand
That the heroic princely conqueror would come.
"The descendant of the formidable lion
Will surely come," she had thought,
And your sister-in-law stood preparing.
For the fourteen horses she made coverings
Fashioned in the likeness of tigers.
She had their insides lined with fine velvet,
And maidens and young women bite down the seams;
She had golden beads set along their edges,
And their faces embroidered wholly in silk,
Had the pile twisted thick and shaggy,
So that to anyone who saw their color
They bore the very likeness of a tiger,
