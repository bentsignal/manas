All had become slaughter;
The common people gathered,
And in the clashing battle
How many people were killed.
Meek men and cruel men died;
In the upheaval that arose,
How many people died together.
Those who survived without dying
Staggered about wounded,
The two fronts of their coats flapping;
All their robes were torn,
All the heroes hard-pressed,
And all the people thrown into disorder.
They fought a monstrous battle,
Standing face-to-face,
And fought amid peril.
Let us leave that there;
His white turban was cauldron-sized,
And when he shouted his voice rang like the azan—
Let us begin the tale of Akun:
With his beard grown white,
While strength for the journey remained,
Khan Akun longed for the spear-fight.
He summoned the elders of the people
And gathered them all.
Crying like a young goose,
Hero Akun said:
"Let us elders gather;
For hero Manas the lion,
Let all of us be slain!
He was a leopard, he was a lion;
Your Manas who passed away
Was a black-gray-wolf hero.
The Almighty who creates life
Will never soon grant
Another gray wolf like Manas.
The handful of Muslims—
Fighting the teeming Black infidels,
He was the hero
Who set them free.
In Manas's absence,
The swarming descendants
Of Kakanchin's Beijing
Occupied the face of the earth.
Alöökö from Margalang
Targeted the common people in his hand,
The Muslims.
The proof that he targeted them was this:
In the days of Khan Alöökö,
He exacted tribute in girls;
Whoever resisted
He seized then
And slaughtered as a sacrifice.
He humiliated many,
Dragged them along the road,
And had the Dangyts tear them apart.
He collected tribute in gold;
The handful of Muslims
Were counted neither among the living
Nor found among the dead.
Unable to find any recourse,
The people remained wailing;
He brought turmoil
Upon the sons of the Muslims.
No one dared answer him;
In Manas's absence,
The people remained crying out.
We saw Alöökö—
Of the wrestlers he gathered,
Whoever provoked his anger
Was subjected to surpassing torment.
He established a chamber of wonders,
Where a dragon coiled.
Unable to withstand his wrath,
Sleeping children awoke.
He demanded Margalang;
Coming then from Beijing,
A khan named Alöökö emerged,
