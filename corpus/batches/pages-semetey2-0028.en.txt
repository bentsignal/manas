There is no forest into which I can flee,
And no Kyrgyz to come to my aid.
I would not be deceived by a deceiver—
Can my strength have fallen this far?
Or have I no waist left to gird,
No renowned companion and lion
Upon whom I relied?
I must have come to behold
This vast, impassable river;
Deceived by the frenzied slave,
I must have come here to die.
Rather than remain alive in the world,
What did I mean to do?
I must have meant to depart
This world all the sooner!
Will there be a day when I wear
Akolpok, left by my father Manas?
Will there be a day when I mount
The ever-ready Buurul?
Will there be a day when I drive
The frenzied slave toward Beijing?
The one who leapt far beyond the chasm,
Whom I guarded more dearly than my father Manas—
May he never age, may Taibuurul never age!”
Speaking thus, Semetey,
With life barely remaining,
Felt anguish smoke fiercely within him;
He lost his strength in grief,
And black night descended upon him.
Red-roan, with a low mane,
The ever-ready Arbuurul
Had come beside him.
He had no tongue with which to speak,
Yet the creature was wiser than a man.
When he heard Semetey’s voice,
Buurul came out from among the horses;
Within the herd, that thoroughbred
Had heard Semetey’s voice.
Neighing and snorting,
Pounding the black earth,
Tossing his forelock toward the sky,
Driving his hooves into the ground,
Drawing in his belly as he neighed,
Taibuurul stood there.
Arching his neck like a duck,
Arbuurul bent down.
The horse Buurul knew
That Semetey was in the water.
When he saw Semetey,
As though seeing his own foal,
Into the flowing river,
Neighing six or seven times,
Buurul the horse entered.
Great Buurul, bobbing his head,
Now reached
Hero Semetey.
He held out his great tail,
Thick as an armful.
“Buurul has come to me;
This is the place where I will not die,” he thought,
And the orphan’s eyes lit up.
In that woeful hour he grasped
The great tail
Of the prancing, ever-ready Buurul.
Once his lord had taken hold of the tail,
Before one could say this or that,
In the blink of an eye,
Buurul began to drag him.
The great lord did not die from his rashness,
But emerged from the water—
The great lord emerged
From the vast, impassable torrent.
The hero had lost consciousness,
The heedless-born lion,
A hero born after
His father Manas, the gray wolf,
And left by his noble father Manas
The Akolpok he had inherited.
