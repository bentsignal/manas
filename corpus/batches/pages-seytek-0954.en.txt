Their eyes dimmed and wandered,
The heroic host that served them
Leaned back, unable to advance,
And began to retreat,
While the two fought without respite.
Their noses were filled with steam,
Their backs and waists ran with sweat,
Their loins bent rigid,
And their dim eyes bulged.
They drove each other onward,
And whenever they collided,
They mauled each other like bull camels.
Such was the nature of the strife.
By day they never rested,
By night they never slept,
Never pausing even when they lay on their sides,
Never lying down long enough to satisfy sleep;
All rest was gone as they fought.
Almighty God alone knew the great cause—
Who had ever seen such a deed?
The people cried out in grief,
And as the two grappled, the black earth
Was torn from its hardened crust.
Seytek and Sarybai
Revealed the full measure of their valor.
Their arrow-proof armor and coats
Were torn apart layer by layer.
So fierce was the mighty battle,
So terrible a sight to behold.
Tookyzyl and Kulansur
Were drenched in crimson blood;
Their entire bodies
Stood motionless and numb,
While every arm and leg
Hung powerless, unable to move.
Such was the contest now.
As the struggle continued,
Tookyzyl turned his head aside.
He was a warrior formidable in cunning,
Sarybai, son of the ogre.
He asked Seytek
For a respite of seven days:
“Since becoming Sarybai,
I have never seen such combat.
I have seen men of immense stature,
And countless mighty men
Who multiplied like seed across the earth.
Against many warriors and many foes
I have fought in combat,
But nowhere on earth, warrior,
Have I seen a youth as valiant as you.
Your heroism is manifest,
Your gray-wolf courage plain;
You are a wolf, brave Seytek!
You are a dread, relentless champion,
A lion, brave Seytek!
The one who grapples and spills blood
Is you, brave Seytek!
To the heroic Kyrgyz people
You are like iron firmly fixed,
A fortress, brave Seytek!
This is while you still bear the name of a boy;
When the flesh fills out upon your calves,
You will drive the whole wide world
Before you and behold it all—
For that were you born, brave Seytek!
In fighting with you,
My courage failed and my strength departed;
The clash of battle has worn me down.
I who never tired am tired,
I who was a lion am exhausted,
I am wearied from fighting.
I began the struggle and have been shaken;
Today I have despaired of life.
Heed the words I speak:
Though you are far greater than I,
Grant me a respite of seven days.
If you truly are a khan,
Stand by your promise!
Swear an oath upon death,
