Having lost the warrior, steadfast Syrgak,
Unable to cease grieving,
The noble khan raged;
Instead of dying, he became a blaze,
His wretched soul beyond restraint.
In the mud of the dark spring water,
With Aykojo as their imam,
While they were saying their prayers,
Argyn Khan’s Ajybai,
Who knew the tongues of seventy tribes,
A man of consummate skill,
When they bowed their heads in prayer,
Was standing guard at that time.
Argyn Khan’s son Ajybai
Gripped his spear and leaned upon it;
Your warrior fell into a light doze.
That Ajybai had fallen asleep,
Alooke’s Kongurbay,
Your crafty hero lying in wait,
Watched and noted carefully.
Then, his great boots jutting out,
His neck held high like a rooster’s,
With his painted pipe bearing a golden moon,
That fierce man, great as a hut,
His saddle flaps fluttering,
A coral stone big as a leather flask
Shook upon his head.
Jerking his golden reins,
Driving Algara forward,
With a face like oiled wheat,
Alooke’s Kongurbay,
His eyes like twilight mist,
When the fierce man looked from afar,
They had dipped their hands in water;
All the Muslims
Had fallen in rows upon the ground,
And were bending head down.
The fierce warrior, who knew the enemy’s ways,
Had seen them from afar.
Bright dawn had broken,
Light had fallen on the earth.
That the warrior was lying thus,
The wretched fierce man had understood.
The black-cheeked battle-axe
Alooke’s Kongurbay
Had hanging at his wrist.
With its point forged of steel,
Tempered in dragon’s venom
In sixty separate immersions,
Leaving none unharmed when thrust,
Letting none it met escape alive,
The painted spear in his hand
The fierce warrior gripped and swung round.
Algara beneath him—
The wind could not catch his trot.
Khan Manas had not raised his head from prostration;
Kongurbay did not cease riding forward.
While Manas remained bent over,
“The surface of his spine,” he said,
“The place beside his lung,” he said.
Alooke’s Kongurbay,
Sparing nothing, with both hands,
Thrust with exceptional force.
Then Manas the leopard,
While Kongurbay was thrusting,
Untroubled by the ringing blows,
Kongurbay’s spear
The warrior counted as nothing at all.
Courage and lionlike prowess
Had been bestowed upon the hero without measure.
“If the obligatory prayer is broken,
That will be worst of all,” he said.
Reaching his right hand back,
The warrior seized the spear.
Noble Manas, the precious lion,
When he jerked the spear,
The painted spear that was stabbing him
Shattered into eighty pieces.
Alooke’s Kongurbay
Turned back, urging his horse on,
And fled in a clattering rush.
Then Argyn Khan’s Ajybai
Burst out from the flank.
The accomplished lord Ajybai
Struck Kartkurong with his whip,
And reached him sooner than an arrow.
As soon as he reached the fierce man,
He thrust into his side as he passed.
Kongurbay was wounded;
Then that swine was in a bad way.
When he groaned, “Oh!” from his mouth
Blood came gushing again and again,
And Kongurbay’s strength ebbed away.
That bastard’s Black Horse,
His Black Horse had
Wings upon its sides.
Whenever he met trouble anywhere,
It was always like this—
The horse would flee, as was its habit.
The forty or so lords beside him
Did not come near to aid their comrade.
Cloud gathered in the sky,
And the formidable fierce man, that noble lion,
With life hanging between death and survival,
Escaped in full sight of them.
His breast could not contain his many cares.
“God has plainly struck me!” he said.
Ajybai’s tears streamed in ten directions.
“While standing guard upon the rise,
I relaxed and fell asleep,” he said.
“I have earned the warrior’s displeasure,
I have disgraced myself,” he said.
“Will these Muslims standing here
Not learn of my doglike conduct?
‘You have done it twice, not once!’ they will say.
Will they not ride me like a black donkey?”
Not a single soul knew
How the Chinese man had stabbed him;
No one had found out.
By sleeping, he had let Manas be stabbed,
Yet Ajybai had no cause for regret.
Then Argyn Khan’s Ajybai,
Before your generous lord,
Dismounted in agitation,
The great lead-rope tied with rounded knots
He wound about his neck,
