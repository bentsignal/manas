One alone would attack them.
Told to take a cap, they cut off a head:
Khan Manas's forty companions,
Like white-headed winged steeds among horses,
Almambet and Chubak among them,
At the people who had come to Talas
All alike were astonished.
The steel weapons in their hands
They gripped, their straps wound round their wrists.
The champions, wolves of war,
Bound battle-axes to their waists.
All their weapons of war
They prepared and stood ready.
The sovereign prophet's faith was pure;
A golden throne remained in Beijing.
Almambet of Azizkan
Now turned Sarala's head,
And to stately Chubak
Spoke his counsel:
“If the enemy has come from Mecca,
You attack toward Mecca.
If the enemy has come from Beijing,
Let me mount the horse Sarala
And attack toward Beijing.
Holding a spear, breaking their ranks,
If death comes this year, falling to a shot,
Until the swaying dry stalks ripen,
Until the autumn gossamer flies,
Fighting six months without rest,
Let me die if my hour has come.
If I have the means, this enemy's
Midst I shall dye with blood.”
Sarala with the cowlike tailbone
Almambet wheeled round;
He turned Sarala's head.
Almambet, accustomed to exchanging shots,
Against the many foes who had arrived
Now stood prepared.
No man's strength could overcome him in a grapple,
No lion's tooth could pierce him;
With piercing roars and ringing battle cries,
Chubak of Akbaltay,
Who threw the whole world into confusion,
Who wore a broad blue coat,
Who rode a horse like Kögala,
That dark, fierce-tempered rogue,
Chubak of Khan Baltay,
Against the many foes openly approaching
Now stood prepared.
Ajybay of Khan Argyn,
Who knew sixty different tongues,
Went out to meet them
And began speaking gently:
“You handful of Muslims,
You have driven your men here;
Your skilled spear fighters
You have sent ahead!
Is there a mountain you cannot cross?
With you, Muslims,
Is there an enemy who has come to exchange shots?
Is there a pit dug in your path?
You have made ready;
Are there foot soldiers challenging you?
You have gathered in great strength,
You have prepared yourselves.
Is there a mountain lying unawares?
Is there an enemy from Beijing?”
Then they said:
“Reckless Manas,
If our strength suffices this time,
We have come to seize Manas.
'We shall raid,' we said, and came
For his mares in Keng-Kol.
From Manas we shall exact
Vengeance for the distant past.
Do not block my road;
Let my troops pass.
We have come to show
Something truly extraordinary!”
Ajybay, Argyn's son,
Turned back with a shout,
Calling to Chubak of Khan Baltay,
'Where are you?'
And beating the drum,
He gave word to the companions,
Striking the war drum.
Almambet of Azizkan
Whipped Sarala beneath him
On the right and on the left,
Spurring him on either side.
Syrgak of Khan Ulak,
With a horse like Kök Chebich,
Wearing a blue leather coat,
Charged close with a roar.
Calling 'God!' as his battle cry,
Hero Syrgak rode into the fray.
Veering with Kögala,
Starting a clash the moment he saw them,
A polished spear thundering in his hand,
At Chubak's looming form
The earth reeled and thundered.
Behind him the forty companions
Rode together with a thunderous roar.
'The forty leopards will reach them,' he thought;
'If the forty companions take the notion,
These people of mine who have come
They will sweep through and slaughter.'
“My beloved forty companions,
It is their khans who are fools;
What fault lies with the common people?
If you rashly attack and kill them,
We too shall die one day.
On the coming Day of Resurrection,
When the Prophet sits as judge,
What answer shall we give?
Those skilled among the spear fighters,
Before leopard Manas
Let us seize and bring,
The mighty men who led them.
Forty companions, your prowess is beyond comparison;
For you to destroy and slaughter,
There are no Chinese people here!”
They heeded Bakay's words.
Almambet of Azizkan
And Chubak of Akbaltay
The companions who had charged
Barely managed to restrain.
The Muslims who had come of their own accord,
Rashly coming to pick a fight,
Now fell into hardship.
Almambet of Azizkan
Drove off a thousand;
Chubak of Akbaltay
Took a thousand under guard.
All the remainder
Dismounted in a flurry,
Threw down their battle-axes,
And stood drawing a line.
The Muslims who had come themselves,
From Khan Manas's companions,
Were about to see a spectacle;
Khan Manas's companions
Were about to bring them trouble.
Riding a horse like Sarala,
Wearing yellow leather trousers,
Holding a spear without a hair tassel,
Girded with a bare sword,
Golden-crested, with a fiery sash,
Azizkan's only son
Rode into their midst.
Chubak of Khan Akbaltay,
Did you see him drive them off?
The soldiers of the twelve khans,
Making them all dismount,
Khan Manas's forty companions,
Before hero Manas,
Did you see them bring them bound?
Then Khan Bakay, the rich man's son—
The ground before the court was dusty;
There were some twelve thousand souls.
All went submissively,
Driven like livestock!
When they reached Khan Manas,
Did you see their lives grow wretched?
All alike gazing helplessly,
Did you see them lose their speech?
