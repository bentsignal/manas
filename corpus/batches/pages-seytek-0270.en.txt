He guarded me like father and mother,
A beast as dear as one’s own father.
He never grew saddle-sore when ridden on campaign,
For he was a faithful companion.
Through the spreading enemy like a ship,
As though beholding Semetey,
His face grew pale and wan,
And blood flowing from both eyes
Poured down over his chest.
His joints gave way and he sobbed,
His courage fled and he wailed;
All twelve limbs and forty bones
Seemed shattered into pieces.
The horse remained, but its master was gone;
May such treacherous misery be damned!
Is this what you are, filthy world?
My soul is embittered, my heart is aflame.
I could mount Buurul and make him surge,
But my stallion-riding elder brother is not here.
His horse remains, but he himself is gone,
And a fiery ember has fallen into my heart.
My cheerful, lion-hearted brother is gone;
Is this what the filthy world is?
Grieving and lamenting, utterly distraught,
He wished to die but could not die,
Unable to take his own life.
Consumed by sorrow, his heart burned,
And he fell writhing into a wasting sickness.
As though his elder brother had returned that day,
As though God had granted his prayer,
The warrior stood weeping.
While Külchoro stood weeping,
The hard black stones melted and wept,
The broad-breasted earth wept,
And the sealed blue heavens above wept.
The watching multitudes wept,
The dark-hearted lion wept;
Everyone who wept
Wept while consuming his own grief.
If grief leaves a soul alive after death,
A beast has no tongue with which to speak,
Though it stands grieving like this:
The birches wept, the willows wept,
And every living witness wept.
The divinely favored lion wept,
The dark-hearted hero wept;
Everyone who wept
Wept while consuming his own grief.
When a helpless beast survives its master,
It must suffer so, they wept.
When poor Külchoro
Beheld Taybuurul,
It was as though his gray-maned elder brother had revived
And returned to reach his horse.
It was as though the dead gray-maned hero had revived
And his eyes beheld him today.
His face went pale and wan,
And the tears flowing down both cheeks
Poured over his chest.
Unable to hold back his tears,
Unable to take his tiny life,
He burned and baked to ashes,
Then faded from his embers into nothing.
When he beheld Taybuurul,
How could Külchoro, upheld by divine favor,
Possibly remain composed?
The reason he could not be still
Was that while his noble elder brother lived,
Over rugged hills and sheer cliffs,
This was the beast he rode along the ridges.
In battles between Chinese and Kyrgyz,
This was the beast he rode into strife.
With his fame spread throughout the world,
And his many conflicts ever increasing,
This was the beast he rode into battle.
He rekindled extinguished fires,
Restored those doomed to die,
Rejoined many who had been severed,
Regathered many who had been scattered,
And carried him safely far beyond the ravine.
