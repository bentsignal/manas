Let Zhelzhetpes go and take their measure.
Tell them to submit
To my decree and to my word;
Tell them to give their treasure and their daughter
As tribute to Lord Tengir.
If they will not heed my word,
If they do not gather their herds and people
And offer them before me as tribute,
If they refuse to declare submission
And do not bow their heads,
Let them gird themselves for battle,
And come to the ground of slaughter;
Let none of those Kyrgyz remain behind,
To the field where we shall grind one another down!
They say the Kyrgyz have a Kenen,
That his myriad troops stand ready,
That he is a hero surpassing all the world.
Kermekash is the loveliest of maidens;
Tell him to surrender Kermekash.
And, living on undying and undimmed,
There is the peerless courser Kertulpar—
To sovereign Chyngtengir
Tell him to present both of them!
If he will not heed these words,
If that whoreson Kenen
Will give neither horse nor daughter,
I shall set fire to his farthest bounds;
If he draws my wrath,
I shall crush him and take his head!
If my rage truly seizes me,
If he will not obey what I have said,
Will not submit in truth and bow his head,
Will not come as my subject,
I shall not let him turn his plow,
Nor turn his hand to earning a living!
I shall not let him tread the face of the earth,
Nor scatter any seed for grain;
I shall allow him no livelihood at all!
If my full rage comes upon me,
I shall slaughter his countless myriads,
Send them wandering like the yellow plague,
And visit them with an incurable affliction!
Firing upon them from every ridge,
I shall wipe out every last one!
Tell him to submit to these words
And pay tribute to Lord Khan!
Go, Zhelzhetpes, go;
Go to those swaggering Kyrgyz,
Whose every weapon stands ready,
From Kenen, the giant of the Kyrgyz,
Go and bring back word.
Travel without tiring on your journey;
Return five days from now,
Late by no more than a mare's milking.
Sharpen your tongue and speak without fear.
The tribute and spoil I have named—
The radiant girl with gazelle eyes,
The beauty I desire,
The tempest with reins of hair,
The one who truly sets the heart aflame—
Drive back that prize without fail!
Complete the mission on which I send you;
Prepare and bring everything I have named!
So saying, Chyngtengir
Named fortunate Zholkerchik
And gave him to Zhetpes as a companion.
One who devoured all he encountered,
Zholbors, he also gave to Zhetpes.
To travel among the people
And learn the people's condition,
He took six pack mules,
Put on a coat no bullet could pierce,
And took twelve mighty champions.
Readied like a lion,
He set out toward Ala-Too;
Zhelzhetpes took to the road.
Ask no more about Zhelzhetpes:
White and pure within, corrupt without,
No other such soul has lived in an age.
They called him a son come from the Kyrgyz,
Half Kyrgyz and half Chinese,
A go-between for the peoples of two tribes,
A man of two divided minds.
He was the goldsmith of the Chinese,
The one who loaded gold upon mules,
The one who drew out men's secrets,
A diviner who observed and discerned,
Then laughed without speaking openly.
Unable to know whether he was Kyrgyz,
Unable to recognize his Chineseness,
He went about muttering to himself.
When Zhelzhetpes heard this, he thought:
— Every wish I had has come true;
I have been given all I desired.
My failing enterprise has turned aright,
My flown bird has come home to perch.
Swiftly invoking Baabedin,
He rode out on his embassy,
