Around the places left exposed,
The forty Kalchas, heeding my words,
Wrapped it with sinew;
When glue was poured within it,
Much of Bölökbai’s treasure
Was consumed.
Fearing it might suddenly break,
I had it covered with elephant hide;
Fearing blood might make it rot,
Below the spear’s tuft
I had it faced with leather.
So it would not slip from the hand,
And would not bring disaster,
I had the handgrip roughened,
And had the handgrip painted.
Its tuft was tipped like a sword;
The value of this single spear
Was fixed at eighty argymaks.
Kongurbai’s blue coat of mail,
That strong armor no bullet pierces—
Let one thrust pierce it;
When driven with the fist, let it sink softly
All the way to its base.
I made its wolf-tongue point barbed,
And set the tip jutting out.
To make it surpassingly keen,
I tempered it with Albaras steel.
So the child might know my worth,
If he drives it against black stone,
Let it enter like mud.
If the two sides take their stands,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
And fight without fleeing,
Let the splendid hero, the bloodletter’s son,
Enter battle without tiring;
Let him truly level the spear,
And, if the Black Chinese stand firm,
Pursue them screaming.
When he faces a determined foe,
At the sight of the gray wolf’s son,
Let them fail to endure and be ruined.
I made Syrnaiza as a gift;
When you bring turmoil to Beijing,
When you go to the Kakanchin,
Accept it as a gift.
When you strike the foe you face,
My only one, the valiant man’s son,
Drink your fill of joy;
Thinking of your poor sworn father,
Keep us within your heart!
The Kakanchin is the foe you will fight;
Splendid hero, son of the bloodletter,
Go and unleash havoc!
Let me give you the weapons of war;
Splendid hero, son of the bloodletter,
Take them without haste or confusion!
There is no end to my treasury;
In the gathered treasury
Of your sworn father, the master smith,
What is there, and what is not!
I knew the Black Chinese Kakanchin
Would be the foe you fought;
I long believed that the orphaned hero,
The bloodletter’s son,
Would avenge himself upon the teeming Black Chinese.
I give Syrnaiza as a gift;
My hero, take it gladly.
Meet them and fight,
And bring turmoil to Beijing!
Strike that swine, Lord Kongurbai,
And crush him like chaff;
Cut off his head, bind it on,
And bring it specially as a gift
To your sworn father, the master smith!
My orphan, son of the keen warrior,
Gird your waist with perfect skill!
Against the Chinese who cover the earth,
Enter alone and attack.
