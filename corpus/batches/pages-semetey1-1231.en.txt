Let me plunge into the spectacle;
Let me strike with Akkelte.
Young marksman Kulchoro
Poured the powder from its box
With a rumble,
And rammed in lead
The size of a sheep's head.
He drove it down with the ramrod,
Pulled back the quick-fire mechanism,
And set the cliff-like stock
Against the brow of Akkangky,
Making it ring and clank.
Leaning back in the saddle,
He scraped with his nail
The powder spilled from the touchhole.
He struck Chinese flint against firestone,
Set a glowing ember,
And lit the matchcord.
Upon Surkoen with a roar,
Toltoy of the Jediger
Came flying toward him.
Towering like a great cauldron,
Shrieking above him,
Külchoro held it firm as a bullet
And leveled Akkelte.
Toltoy came charging with a roar;
Saying, "He is within measured range,"
Lion-born Kulchoro
Fired Akkelte once.
Its bullet thundered with a crack;
When it drew close to Toltoy,
Your generous lord's Akkelte
Roared like a camel with its calf.
Toltoy's whole body
Trembled when he heard it.
Flame hissed from the muzzle;
The leopard's Akkelte
Nearly swept him away.
The bullet reached him shrieking,
But Akkelte struck too high
And its bullet flew far away.
At the gray hillock's ridge,
Akkelte's bullet struck,
And black turf the size of a millstone
Turned over and flew away.
Dear Lord Toltoy's
Ribs and heart jolted,
And his filthy guts quivered.
Hero Toltoy stood amazed:
Had thunder suddenly crashed?
What strange mystery was this?
Had the earth broken apart?
Was there a black-ridged mountain?
What age and what day was this?
Had an enemy come from the Khagan?
The earth broke and made a sound;
Was there an enemy coming to devour him?
Was there a bullet wrapped in haze,
Coming in an unbroken, roaring mass
Toward them?
Hero Toltoy took courage.
If an eager enemy had come,
He would slaughter them with his sword;
Let forty thousand enemies appear,
He would bring them all to their end.
He raged and made ready,
And turned Surkoen's head.
Ready to grapple,
Dear Toltoy now stood.
Good heavens—when he looked toward the pass,
A youth mounted on a red roan,
Its hair tail tightly knotted,
A ruddy young boy,
Came shouting from the pass,
Calling the battle cry "Manas!"
