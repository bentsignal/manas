Of all that ears had heard and eyes had seen,
Every soul who heard came.
His white turban was large as a cauldron;
When he cried out, his voice was like the call to prayer.
The khoja's heart found contentment;
Every pauper and dervish who had lacked means
Received seven head of livestock,
And his satisfied heart rejoiced.
At Kökötöy's death,
Even the utterly poor became rich.
Kökötöy's Bokmurun
Took instruction from the learned
And questioned the wise.
Hearing counsel from the wise,
He had a shroud made of fine cloth;
Hearing that it would be splendid,
He dressed him in a loose silk robe,
Mounted him upon Zhelmayan,
Gave a letter inscribed with a verse,
And gave provisions to the scholars.
He had Egypt and Baghdad
Called upon side by side,
And had Kökötöy's shroud
Brought from great Egypt.
He wrapped him in the shroud,
And in this way consumed
Kökötöy's countless riches.
He had him washed with Zamzam water,
Performed the rites from head to foot,
And had him bound in a robe of honor.
He had the mourning standard drawn straight,
And had Külaiym voice
Kökötöy's lament.
He had all the riches
Prepared and counted,
And to give to those who washed the body,
Had exactly five hundred
Armored robes made ready.
The treasury doors opened,
And Kökötöy's countless riches
Were squandered and scattered
At his mourning feast.
Khan Kökötöy's resting place,
His grave, was dug apart.
From champions who wielded spades
And stalwarts who wielded mattocks,
He summoned them all without exception
From Kokon and Samarkan,
And brought grave-digging champions
From among them.
They dug his resting chamber evenly,
Dug it broad in proper measure,
Dug it perfectly balanced,
Leveled it and dug it all large.
He had its burial niche hollowed,
And had it extended from head to foot.
So that it would remain bright
Until the end of time,
He had garnet and ruby
Split open,
And had lights set
At his feet and head.
"Khan Kökötöy has died," they said,
Making it known to the people.
Saying, "These are the khan's wives,"
They dressed all seven wives
In billowing black garments.
When this Kökötöy died,
People gathered in a mass;
All the countless giants
Heaped the earth upon him.
"Khan Kökötöy has died,"
"The dear man has seen
The true abode from which none return."
