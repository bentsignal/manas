“Let me die,” they said, but could not die;
The poor people stood there wailing,
Unable to take their own lives.
While the people stood like this,
Karakan's eight sons,
Led by Jakyp and Ulak Khan,
All began to speak in turn:
“Rather than live on like this,
Let us sink as though never born,
Let us die instead of living;
These Chinese humiliations,
How can we endure them until death?
We too were Karakan's sons;
Unless we are worthless, every one,
We were princes of his line.
We are dead while still alive;
These Chinese humiliations
We have suffered till Judgment Day!
If we go on living, without dying,
What inheritance will we gain?
These Chinese have grown powerful;
Let us clash with them in thunder!
Karakan, who has passed away,
Our father's greatness
Was at least as high as a mountain ridge.
Let no one say Karakan's eight sons
All failed to take after their father
And turned out fools!
Let us head toward Kara-Too
And settle in a sheltered hollow;
With dear Baltay at our head,
These fucking Chinese—
Let us steal their children and slaughter them,
Without a single person knowing,
Without one human being sensing it;
Let us draw them aside and slaughter them.
We helpless orphans,
Let us steal and slaughter them unseen.”
They prepared to mount their horses,
Pleading, “Holy lord!”
They made their weapons ready,
Girded on their battle swords,
Chose their mounts from racing steeds,
Received their Kyrgyz kinsfolk's blessing,
Saddled their choice steeds,
Hastily said their prayers,
And readied themselves like lions,
With Jakyp and Akbaltay at their head.
Sixty champions mounted,
Skirting the lower reaches of broad Kashkar;
Every one of their choice coursers
They shod anew.
On karabayyr horses, on noble warhorses,
With sieve-like lungs and copper limbs,
They rode their winged steeds.
At the slope where the mountain ended,
Beside that river with its sandy channels,
From the jutting mountain spur,
From the looming promontory,
When the true dawn broke,
Back then, when the dawn swelled forth,
Dust came surging, surging up.
They looked toward the surging dust:
Laden with gold, rubies, golden coins,
Resting by day, traveling by night,
Red gold coins and ruby stones,
Precious things loaded on camels,
Eighty champions from the kalday
Were on their way to Beijing.
There on that road they met
Ninety Chinese champions;
The lions Jakyp and Akbaltay
Caught them in the open plain,
Smashed them to pieces,
And killed them to the last man.
Ninety-five laden camels,
Black single-humped camels with red tails,
They took as booty.
Seizing the camels in their raid,
The lads returned to the Kyrgyz;
Agreeing to what was said,
To every household, one apiece,
They gave a fist-sized piece of gold.
Thoroughly sated with their spoils,
Having wreaked great devastation,
As dawn broke clear,
They lay there making merry.
While the caravan drivers, on their journey,
Were making camp,
While all the champions
Were being slaughtered,
While merciless havoc
Was being wreaked,
While the champions
Were being offered up as sacrifice,
One who served Alöökö,
A champion called Jöö Jeldet,
That fool escaped,
And reached his khan alive.
He went and told his khan,
Before Alöökö
Telling horrors no one knew:
“My khan, I have seen wonders;
I fled here from the rebels.
When dawn broke clear,
When light fell on the earth,
I saw a gray, mottled banner;
Piercing cries, shrill battle shouts,
I saw a tumult of mighty yelling.
They began the slaughter;
To Esenkan,
Bearing loads of gold,
All your champions,
They killed them all, to the last man.
On karabayyr horses, on noble warhorses,
With sieve-like lungs and copper limbs,
They rode mounts swift as vanished spirits;
Though enemies rose in ilek upon ilek,
They charged them without flinching.
With sashes wound as turbans,
As they leveled their spears in ranks,
They called upon the Almighty.
It turned out they were Burut themselves,
And all your treasury
They were out to plunder!
They trampled over our heads;
The loads of coins, the heaps of gold,
They took as booty and scattered.
I saw they were Burut themselves,
I looked: their eyes were black.
They dug my pit,
They boiled my misery,
They scattered my possessions,
They slaughtered my people,
They broke my back,
They would not hear my words,
They took my livestock as booty,
They spilled my blood like water.
So, my lord khan,
Gather up your army;
The gold that was plundered,
Gather every bit of it back!”
Once the messenger arrived,
A light was lit upon the tower,
The great war drum was beaten hard,
All the spears were lowered,
All the people gathered.
When their khan's command arrived,
All the Chinese grew excited,
All the mighty men grew wild,
All the heroes flared with rage,
All the champions made ready:
“I will finish them all together,” they said,
“I will slaughter them, leave nothing,” they said.
They had their armored coats prepared,
Had their coursers readied.
What Alöökö wore
Had diamond buttons big as fists;
Such was the sight, if you could see him.
Raging and blustering,
With the war drum pounding hard,
Alöökö gave his orders,
Upon the Black Kalmyks and Manchus
Again unleashing terror.
Beside Alöökö,
With their jackets puffed out,
Were a thousand mighty khans;
With broad armored coats spreading wide,
Were a thousand kalday khans.
His eyes blazed fierce as fire;
When told, “They took your gold,”
His thirst for blood seized him:
“Come, you men who cultivate wisdom,
Look upon this affliction!”
Wise was that mighty man;
Against the whole world
He had the power to stand untouched.
At Alöökö's doorway
His tigress had borne fifteen cubs;
No human dared come near.
Beneath him was a golden throne;
Who would die six months hence,
Who could discern that now:
Six diviners he had as well.
Their very fame made hearers shudder;
The sixty branches of sacred learning
They had studied to mastery.
Taking up their written books,
The diviners and sorcerers
Laid out their divinations.
The six diviners of the infidel,
Of that hero Alöökö,
Addressed their khan at length;
All the diviners crowded round:
“Your gold has been found!
Karakan's eight sons
Have grown to manhood and become warriors,
The equals of heroic sons.
Gathering an army of capable men,
Taking their warriors together,
They have plundered your goods;
Those lads have caused great trouble.
They seized your servants;
Of your champions they spared none,
They killed every one of them.
The gold on ninety-five camels
They plundered and took as booty;
Every one of the champions
They fell upon and slaughtered in the plain.
Without anyone touching a champion,
The common people go about;
But Karakan's eight sons,
The Chinese who venture outside,
They rob and plunder and devour.
Unless you slaughter their leaders,
Smash them to pieces,
And scatter them completely this year,
They will never let us prosper;
They will never leave us safe.
Send champions, gather troops,
Gather the whole army in strength;
Let us descend upon their homes,
These Burut—may their homes burn!—
Let us scatter their spirit far and wide.
Let us search their bundles,
Draw our line across their place of honor,
And give these fucking Burut
Something to remember!
Let us speak grim words;
If they do not give up the gold,
Let us seize their beautiful women.”
When the diviners
Had spoken thus and gone,
Concerning Alöökö's war drum,
When he issued his command,
His champion readied himself and beat it.
When that war drum sounded,
It shattered the ears.
The war drum was beaten hard;
When they heard the war drum's voice,
Every one of his champions
Now came crowding in.
Of the jacket-wearing Chinese,
The strongest men grew frenzied;
Of the stone-adorned Chinese,
Their bald heads gathered.
The Torgut assembled to a man,
Blowing their trumpets noisily,
Firing cannon here and there.
Of the Black Kalmyks and Manchus,
The champions assembled;
To recover the gold,
Again laying a grievous claim,
They imposed a levy.
Seething like ants,
The Chinese swarmed over everything.
Their champions held on leashes
Upon the heads of the eight sons
Laid a terrible affliction.
Their leader was Baltay Khan;
Beside Akbaltay
Were eight orphaned boys.
When they came upon those boys,
Their valor high, their menace strong,
They wiped out whoever opposed them;
Now abandon hope of these infidels.
Eighty-four fully armed champions,
Red fire wherever they fought;
Unable to understand human speech,
Their champions were a breed apart.
All surged forward, yelling,
Bringing calamity upon defenseless heads.
At the sight of those champions,
The people of the world gathered:
“They will destroy us now,” they said;
Unable to withstand their wrath,
Many Kyrgyz were slaughtered.
Seen today, gone tomorrow:
Such is this shitty world!
“They will finish us off,” they said,
“If this hero grows enraged,
He will slaughter us and spare nothing.”
The people grieved;
All who had their senses
Before the hero Alöökö
Bowed like newly married brides.
Wise Akbaltay
Dismounted and ran forward,
Before Alöökö,
Bending his knees to the earth,
Akbaltay stood bowing.
Akbaltay, eloquent in speech,
Sharpened his plain tongue;
Before Khan Alöökö,
Dear Baltay spoke in full flow:
“Hero, if you truly are a khan,
Here is a fine land for you to rule;
Here are the Kyrgyz common folk, if you would slaughter them;
Hero, here I am, if you would kill me!
I have no strength to fight,
No means to wrestle with you,
No tongue with which to answer back,
No chance to clash in battle.
Here are herds: take your fill;
Here is blood, if you would spill it;
If you would keep us as your subjects,
Here are the Argyn and Kyrgyz people.
If you would slaughter us as ‘rebels,’
Here I am, Baltay, their leader!”
When Baltay had said this,
“What you say is true,”
Said Molto Khan, looking on;
To Alöökö himself
He issued a weighty command:
“The gold that was plundered,
There is no trace of where it went,
Nor have we caught the man who holds it.
There are innocent people, peaceable people,
Among the many are so many good ones;
Can we slaughter them all?
Among this people are robbers and bandits,
But the people at large are innocent;
Someone in particular is doing this.
Let us pursue him, chase him down,
And lay it squarely on his neck.
Saying, ‘The wretched Burut did it,’
How can we slaughter this whole people?
We need land to sustain life;
To keep collecting tribute,
To gather our treasury,
We need a great mass of common people!
Let us find the guilty one,
These fucking Burut—
Let us beat their spirit far away.”
Then Alöökö said this:
“These fucking Burut!
The gold on ninety-five camels,
These very Burut squandered it.
These eight sons of Karakan
Have herds amassed in Chatkal;
If they had the strength, these Burut
Have the will to fight with us.
Something else is in their words,
Something different in their eyes;
If they had the strength, these Burut
Would stand to fight me myself.
Unless we take precautions now,
Strike them and break up their herd,
They will never leave us safe,
Unless we scatter them in every direction!
Put out the fires they have lit;
These fucking Burut,
Move them all away together.
Give them no land to settle;
Drive one of them to the Russians,
Drive one of them to Iyren,
Cast them down into the depths!
Drive one to the Kazakhs;
Plunge them into torment like this.
Drive one to ridge-flanked Kanggay,
Drive one to the Black Kalmyks of Altay;
Level their ancestral stronghold,
Drive every one away and make them disappear!”
At this, Molto Khan
Obeyed Alöökö's word.
His extraordinary champions
Descended and worked their havoc;
Without killing or destroying them,
They bound them alive.
A khan's command is not spoken twice:
Dear Baltay and Jakyp,
At the head of forty Kyrgyz households,
They drove toward Altay.
One was driven to Iyren,
One was cast into the depths,
One was driven to the Russians,
Finding no place to settle.
Their former grief was roused again;
Their whole community was scattered to dust.
Their hands tied behind their backs,
Driven away in humiliation,
As they were being divided,
The people wept, the old ones wept,
All the people wept:
“Ah, Karakan, do not die,” they said,
“Let this people suffer no torment,” they said,
“Such humiliation as this,
Do not visit on Your servants,” they said.
Too many sorrows to fit within their breasts,
The dear people who had suffered plunder
Shed tears in ten streams.
Now grievous trouble was laid on them;
Karakan's eight sons
Were bound, not one left free.
They hung their cauldrons in the place of honor,
Scattered all four kinds of livestock,
Raided the mares on the hills;
If anyone came near, like this,
They struck and splattered his brains,
And brought out of these people
Every last hidden cry of anguish.
They bound those without strength;
The strong ones who resisted,
They seized and killed.
The children left behind
They made slaves for life,
To the kakan's guard khan
They assigned them as servants.
They beat those who had courage;
The women and the children
They pillaged and took as spoils.
Nine thousand horses on the mountain
They swept away without stopping.
The camels on the grazing grounds,
Alöökö slaughtered,
And the giants who claimed to be heroes.
From among the women he took the skilled;
Alöökö, cursed be your disposition,
Took the girls with five locks of hair.
They dug deep trenches,
Brought every misery to the boil,
Bound their hands, letting no one free,
And slaughtered every one
Of the leaders who might resist them.
They raised a mottled banner,
Raised a din of piercing cries.
Low lay the downward slope;
The Kyrgyz army was small.
Grass sprang upward;
The Chinese people were many.
They had giants of every kind,
Assembled from far back in their lands,
The mighty ones had come.
A person who fell into their hands
They beat relentlessly and smashed;
To tear him apart was their practice.
When someone crossed them,
To hack his head off with a sword
Was the custom of these Chinese.
They readied their little drums
And plundered everything, leaving nothing.
They made the old women weep,
Tormented the old men,
And brought unpitying strife.
They dug up the hearths,
Smashed them in humiliation,
And scattered the Kyrgyz people.
Acting with tyrannical power,
They did whatever they pleased.
They were the people of the kakan's realm;
With anyone who opposed them, these Chinese
Had the habit of drinking his blood.
Molto, the great Chinese lord,
To slaughter the Kyrgyz,
That gray-maned warrior,
Had come prowling from Kakan.
His champions were extraordinary;
The infidel full of wrath
Had many riders mounted on elephants.
His giants who went horseless,
Swarming like ants,
His foot soldiers came surrounding them.
With collars about their necks,
His champions were of many kinds;
If those champions attacked,
They tore a person to shreds.
At Alöökö and Molto,
The mind reeled; their menace was strong.
The raging infidels
Leveled anyone who opposed them.
Their champions, terrible in their fury,
Smashed the stone mausoleums
And utterly conquered the Kyrgyz.
The hero Alöökö and Molto Khan
Sent their mighty army surging;
As they pillaged,
Those whose hour had come died,
Many Kyrgyz children died,
And great Döngö, khan of the Sibe,
Ylama from among the Kalmyks—
Of the clamoring infidel,
The hero Khan Alöökö,
Do not ask about this tyranny!
They took the cauldrons as booty,
Sated themselves with plunder,
Seethed like black spiders,
Swarmed till the earth was misted over.
The sky was clear, the earth overcast;
The Chinese arrived in full force.
“Alöökö's ninety camels,
You fucking Burut took them!” they said,
And the world's Last Judgment
They brought down upon their heads.
To the infidel Khan Alöökö,
To the great lord, the hero Molto,
Many Kyrgyz fell as spoils.
With not a soul to answer back,
Many Kyrgyz became obedient amblers.
They burned and smashed the yurt crowns,
Brought hardship and made them weep,
Brought death upon them for certain,
Drained all their strength,
Made slaves of the men,
Made widows of all the women.
Against the infidels like black spiders,
Not a soul could summon courage;
Against the redoubtable hero Döngö,
There was no champion, no support.
When they faced one another,
There was no hero who could stand firm.
Seen today, gone tomorrow:
Damn your father's grave, treacherous world!
Those slaughtered here were beyond counting;
To rescue them,
That Karakan was not here!
“Never filled by food obtained by begging,
Never abandoning our fierce appetite,
If driven out in the dark,
Prowling like leopards,
We were the dear Kyrgyz people.
We camped along the river;
When the hero Karakan died,
We became like an extinguished fire.”
First of all their khan had died;
No one remained to find a way,
And all the people were dying.
They had camped along the river,
With not a soul to lift them up,
They were like an extinguished fire.
The heroes lay under blows,
The livestock stood penned in.
“The moon is dark, nothing can be seen;
Such is the night,” they said.
“Treacherous world, be damned!
When the Chinese fury came,
Such was the day for us,” they said.
While the throng wept in anguish
And spoke of their sorrow,
Before they could open and close their mouths,
Before they could say this or that,
Alöökö from among the Chinese,
Muradyl of Kyrmysha,
Great Molto from among the Manchus,
The stone-adorned Chinese,
Their bald heads gathered;
Of the jacket-wearing Kalmyks,
Their strongest men gathered;
Of the sash-wearing Chinese,
Their warriors gathered.
The wise and the resourceful,
Alöökö and mighty Molto,
Their best men, who knew the enemy's stratagems,
Those with the sharpest spears,
Those with the keenest swords,
Champions who wielded iron-headed maces,
Their great wise men,
Sorcerers who opened written books,
All assembled together.
Dust curled up into the sky,
The Torgut gathered to a man,
The swordsmen made ready,
Their ochogor guns and battle-axes
They strapped on with a threatening air.
They called the kakan's seat Beijing;
When your Chinese grew enraged,
They were a people who swept over everything.
When their commander proclaimed an order,
They were a people who came to the last man.
The handful of Kyrgyz,
They had utterly harried
And overcome.
Whatever they themselves pleased, the Chinese
Were doing now.
Taking women and girls as booty,
They were sated now.
