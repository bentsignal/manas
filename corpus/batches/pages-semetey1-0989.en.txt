She changed herself into a white swan,
And that was when she carried it away.
Hearing the truth, he shouted aloud;
Lion-born Kulchoro
Called his elder, Khan Bakay:
"Akun, it seems, is her father,
And Aychurok is his wife.
Chynkojo, son of Shygay,
Must have stirred up bitter strife.
Unable to endure it, my sister-in-law
Wore her horse's shoulders raw,
Roamed across the whole world,
And must have lost her senses;
She must have carried off Akshunkar,
And so attained her desire.
Saying, 'If he is a man, let him come,'
She must have taken it to Afghanistan.
I heard the plain truth from my sister-in-law;
I heard that Aychurok took it."
Then tiger Bakay,
The saint whose lineage Saint Shay sustained, said:
"What you have said is true.
Akun Khan, the khan of Afghanistan,
And the Bloodthirsty Manas, those two,
Shared the pale star of the heavens
And became friends unto the next world.
They drove livestock as in-laws,
Bowed in homage and tasted fat,
Opened all their hearts to each other,
And had abundant treasure—yellow gold—
Poured down through the roof ring.
They exchanged words of promise
And bound themselves as in-laws."
Tell him to heed the words
Of his elder, old Bakay, without slight;
Tell him to muster Kyrgyz and Argyn,
A mighty, densely gathered host,
A multitude beneath which the earth bends,
And only then set out.
Many there bear claims that breed strife,
And many foes will meet him in battle;
If he will not listen, many enemies await.
Let my only son heed my words:
Many foes lie watching one another.
If my only son has sense,
Let him take his resourceful, wise
Elder Bakay with him;
Whose banner makes men surge,
Who destroys all he encounters;
Let him take the mighty hero Sarykan.
Jamgyrchy will find a way;
Let him take that resourceful hero.
When Manas the Destroyer lived,
We raided Afghanistan and returned once,
And drove them back in disgrace.
On the road leading to Afghanistan
There stands Kambyl-Bel,
And there is the pass of Belendi,
And a desert two days wide,
And a people wild beyond measure.
For slaughtering the giants
Janaaly and Karach side by side,
They harbor vengeance along that road."
When Kulchoro heard this,
He spoke to Kanchoro,
Uttering an astonishing thought no man could know:
"Ah, uncle Kanchoro!
Does a mortal man not die?
Must this borrowed, fleeting soul not see
What God has laid upon one's head?
Khan Bakay has grown old,
Jamgyrchy is aged,
Sarykan's time has passed,
And Bolokbay will be burdened.
If they were goods for the bride-price,
