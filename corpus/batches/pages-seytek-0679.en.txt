He became the hero Külchoro,
Külchoro mounted his horse,
He climbed towering into the saddle,
Subduing the whole wide world,
Driving and ruling wherever he went.
He crossed many a formidable pass,
He struck the enemy in many lands.
Unless his appointed hour had come, he did not die,
An infidel such as this
He had never seen in all the world.
He girded himself for the encounter,
Swaying as though struck by a peri.
He readied his Jazayil sword,
And prayed to the Creator;
It was too late to turn uphill,
And, eager to face his foe,
His two eyes became four.
Then the lionhearted choro
Roared straight ahead,
And raised the battle cry of Manas;
He thrust his sword into its sheath,
And appealed to the spirits
Of the heroes who had passed into eternity.
His adversary was Sarybai,
A foe as mighty as a mountain.
Sarybai mounted Jooruker,
Riding jauntily without a care;
He counted neither your choro nor any other,
And did not value the eighteen thousand worlds
As much as the hem of his robe—
Such was the madman's nature.
“I am Sarybai,” he said,
“The eighteen thousand worlds
I shall gulp down like a dragon,
Driving them before me and devouring them.”
He grew furious and worked himself up,
And spoke to Külchoro,
Beginning his savage rage.
Gulping like a dragon,
He had overwhelmed all the world;
None who opposed him remained alive,
And he had laid waste the valleys.
Whenever he came in bitter enmity,
He even destroyed his own kin.
He prepared himself and sharpened his steel,
And crushed the people of Kanghai.
He had ridden on campaign to Altai,
And received the old lion’s blessing.
Wherever he had camped in passing,
A sacred shrine arose,
And hosts of holy men supported him.
Wherever he had spent the night,
A deep-rooted plane tree grew,
And God raised a protecting cliff at its roots.
He sprang from a lineage of saints;
The one confronting him was Sarybai—
Could he stand without facing him?
Would God strike him if he held back?
The one he grappled with was Sarybai—
Could he stand against such a struggle?
He was the heir of the lamented Lion;
Would God strike him if he fled?
On the promontory where armies gathered,
Upon the rounded hillock,
On the saddle of the forked pass,
On the flat ground broad as a palm,
Upon the ridge where armies massed,
Sarybai, son of a monster,
At the place where they faced each other—
By the echoing Jar-Kyya,
Along the slopes of Jan-Moinok—
Against the infidel huge as a cliff,
Your hero choro was about to contend,
Against the warrior Sarybai.
He met Sarybai, skilled and deft with the spear,
Mighty in wielding the sword,
He met Sarybai,
The infidel who had roamed there.
The hero choro was about to contend
Against the warrior Sarybai.
