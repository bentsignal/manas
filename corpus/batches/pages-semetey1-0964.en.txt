My precious sister, heed my words!
Listen to this reasoning of mine:
I came searching from Afghanistan
For the husband destined to me long ago!”
After Chürök had spoken thus,
At that moment Chachykey
Prepared herself with shrill resolve,
Swaying as though struck by a hero.
She spoke harshly, snarling,
And assailed Chürök to her face:
“An old maid like you, who never took a husband,
Has caused trouble many times;
A sharp-tongued woman like you from ancient days!
There is no place you have not worshiped,
No man you have not wandered after;
Do not grow excited without cause!
In the city of Khan Akun
You wandered long in yearning.
Hearing you called shameless,
Your gray-wolf husband took offense.
In the place of his father, Khan Akun,
His words filled with venom,
You sat until you grew old.
Spit, spit upon your face, woman!
I will not consent to what you say;
I will not surrender Ayköl alive.
Do not take him from my hands without right.
The bloodthirsty, unfortunate lord
Has no father-in-law named Khan Akun,
And no wife named Aychürök!
Listen, woman, to my words;
I shall cool your ardor!
I will not agree to these words of yours.
Before your husband does so,
I shall give you a letter of divorce.
Gird your roaming waist,
Wander and find for yourself
The husband you are to marry!
I will not agree to what you say;
While my dear life remains,
I will not give away my bloodthirsty one!
Upon your girded waist,
Go away without shrieking
To the husband you have been seeking!
A steep cliff rises by the running spring;
Do not wail, wretched woman!
You are the eye of one who travels by night;
Your appearance seems cold,
You are the very devil and demon!
At the words you speak,
I stand amazed at you.
You appeared like an albasty
Before these eyes of mine.
I am Semetey's wife;
Keep away and do not approach.
Do not cast sorcery, woman.
Minced about here and there,
You appear to have the ways of a mare;
When I test you from every side,
You appear to have the ways of Satan!
Are you an evil spirit risen from the water,
Or a sly creature roaming to lead men astray?”
Speaking cold words,
The pampered wife Chachykey—
God now struck the foolish woman—
Spoke words colder than all:
“I shall bind up the hair down my back;
If I let my lord be taken,
May I marry my father Shaatemir!”
Humiliated, crying aloud,
Unable to find her bearings,
Aychürök, daughter of Akun,
Now wept with heaving sobs.
Her heart broke apart,
And tears poured from her eyes:
