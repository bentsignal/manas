Upon mankind.
With wide trouser cuffs and bare broad chest,
The imposing warrior Kyyaz
Wore a black hat as large as a cauldron,
And possessed the will
To rule the whole wide world.
When grim, he looked ready to pass judgment;
When he moved, his turmoil
Seemed able to engulf all humankind.
Such was Kyyaz’s appearance.
Like a fat stag in spring,
He was a swaggering fellow;
Like a wild ram on the summer pasture,
He was a strutting fellow.
The Jediger of the Seven Valleys
Were assembling and advancing,
While behind them warrior Kyyaz
Drove them onward.
Anyone who turned and fled,
Not leaving a single soul behind,
Warrior Kyyaz struck down in wrath
As he advanced.
The Jediger, afraid of Kyyaz,
Came surging onward.
They whispered among themselves,
And the helpless people
Clicked their tongues in dismay.
One to another
They spoke their last farewells:
“If we attack the heaven-favored grim hero,
We are dead.
How shall we ever see alive again
Our poor wives and young children?”
The people lamented amid clamor and grief:
“Whenever it may come, our dear lives
Will end beneath oppression.
Ah, behold this merciless slaughter—
Sit and watch it, father!
We have suffered it through Külchoro.”
All the bearded elders
Of the Jediger lamented without cease,
Amid uproar and grief:
“From our leaders we have always
Suffered grievous humiliation.
Driven forth in disgrace,
Today we shall die by the grim hero’s hand.
From time immemorial until now,
You have attacked the heaven-favored great hero
As though he were an enemy;
Your raising a force against the grim hero—
This deed of yours was wrong.
This Semetey standing here,
The stalwart descended from Manas,
Was a father to us Kyrgyz.
He was our father because
He guarded Ala-Too and made it our homeland;
His father, the gray-maned hero Manas,
Gathered the scattered, wandering Kyrgyz,
Led them and made them a nation;
He waged war against China
And won for the Kyrgyz
Keng-Kol and Talas as their homeland.
How have you Kyrgyz forgotten this?
Can anyone who confronts
The descendant of the native gray-maned Manas,
A grim hero such as this, ever prosper?
Think on this, my wretched people:
Can one who attacks
The lion hero’s son survive?
No man dared cross weapons with
The honored Sultan Semetey.
It was not merely the Kyrgyz people;
I heard that slaughter befell
The Chinese realm, and none escaped.
The warrior-sultan, son of Manas,
Was a lofty, towering mountain pass;
Not only the Kyrgyz, but the whole world
Was guarded by that hero.
