He was a true hero as well.
This one too was a blood-drinking warrior,
A champion of the Kyrgyz.
He was a lion by creation,
Whose like did not exist in all the world.
When the storm came, he let no wind pass,
But stood as a walled and fortified stronghold;
The champion of the mighty Kyrgyz
Was a banner planted on the heights.
This lion-born, valiant companion,
When unleashed against a challenging foe,
Became calamity and turmoil itself.
Among round-headed mortals
Another such could never be born.
Broad-chested and firm as stone,
Such was the comely companion;
Let us hear the answer
That fulfilled bloodthirsty Semetey’s wish.
The bridle fastened to his horse,
He who remained six months in the womb
After the death of his father, the khan’s posthumous hope.
Across passes, valleys, and springs,
Never sated by drinking blood,
Never sparing a life once he attacked—
He too was one of the fearsome ones.
Hardened by constantly spilling blood,
To every mortal who had heard of him
The poor man was known as the subduer of women.
His fortune was complete;
He was a lion upheld by providence.
What matter if bloodthirsty was his name?
Save that he was the son of Manas,
Külchoro was a spear-cast
More valiant than Semetey.
Powerful in cunning and mighty in stature,
Both champion wrestler and lion,
He was an exceptional hero who knew
Every stratagem of his enemy.
His valor was extraordinary,
And this furious companion’s
Strength was beyond measure.
He spurred the gray steed that flew like a cloud,
Making it bound in excitement;
Arbuurul, greatest of beasts,
Grew wildly restive again and again.
Riding Buurul, he made him plunge,
Driving the peerless racehorse
Into a frenzy of rearing.
He wore golden greaves reaching the ridge of his boots,
With embroidered trouser cuffs.
The riding breeches Kanykey had made
Shimmered at the hems.
Akkelte, left by your champion Manas,
Flashed across his broad back.
Ah, the thundering madman
Girded on a silver-mounted belt,
Smiling as he spoke and struck fear.
Taking a steel axe in his hand,
He mounted and rode between the two sides.
On the farther side stood Semetey;
The companion spoke these words to them,
Then made Buurul prance proudly
And rode up, that fierce man.
Semetey, Bakay, and Sarykan,
With his heroic elder Kanchoro,
Came and offered counsel.
“My two elders, Sarykan and Bakay,
You old men are standing here.
My bloodthirsty elder Semetey,
With brave Kanchoro at your head,
All of you are standing here.
In wisdom and strategy
I have never been found wanting.
I deceived Alööke’s son Kongurbay,
The Manchu khan of the Kalmaks,
Calling him my kinsman,
And led him onto the road.
