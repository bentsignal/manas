May your gray wolf’s road be blessed,
His waist-girdle fastened firm;
Your young champion had set out
To bring Kongurbai down.
To die, if death should reach him,
Or, if his strength sufficed, the countless foe
To raid them all and return—
Eagerly braced for the deed,
The valiant prince then
Swayed as though struck by a blow.
His thoughts, it seemed, were divided;
He loomed like Mount Opol.
The young man turned and spoke,
To his uncle, valiant Bakai,
Words of a dread, irrevocable fate:
“Uncle Bakai, leopard,
Thinking, ‘My son has ridden out on patrol,’
Do not grieve, my holy sage!
I am going to meet
The decree the Creator has written!
If death reaches me in Beijing,
I am going there to die!
Gripping my spear, I shall break their ranks;
If death reaches me, I shall fall to a bullet!
On the path of my poor father
I shall die as a sacrifice.
Kongurbai, khan of the Chinese,
I shall meet in a fierce contest
And return after wreaking slaughter!
Kongurbai is the one I shall fight;
Upon that swine, the khan of the Kechil,
I shall lay bitter grief!
If my lion father supports me,
The lion of the lower Black Chinese
I shall split in two;
With that slave, the khan of the Kechil,
I shall meet and then return!
Do not grieve, my uncle Bakai;
Go on living without sorrow,
And for your cherished young son
Mourn for six days!
If I have not returned in six days,
I shall have been left afoot in the rear—
Take your two sons away,
And sweep onward to your mirror-bright city;
Say, ‘My beloved son has died,’
And give up all hope of me!
I am going to open up
One whole face of the open steppe,
And with clamorous cries, their countless herds
I am going to scatter!
I go to confront
Great Kongurbai the Kalmak;
If death arrives and my days are done,
I am going to meet it.
My uncle Bakai, brave gray wolf,
Against the mighty khan of the Kechil,
How could I remain without going?
How could I fail to raise
Red slaughter and roaring battle cries?
Kongurbai is the one I shall face;
If my strength comes, the Chinese
I shall offer up as sacrifice.
With the infidel whose surge is mountain-high
I shall grapple in combat!
Against the heroic khan of Kalday
I shall unleash havoc;
With that thundering dog
I shall fight to the finish.
If fate reaches me, I shall die;
If my heroic father supports me,
Kongurbai’s countless horses
I shall raid and drive off, every one!
I shall bring them mighty grief;
From Kürpüldök and Keng-Shor
I shall seize the horses without delay!
Turn Kokcholok’s head, uncle,
And wait, thinking, ‘When will my gray wolf return?’
