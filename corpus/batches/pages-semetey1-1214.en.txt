He had trained it on slopes;
On descending ground Kongurbai
Dragged it like tumbleweed.
When one stallion urged another,
It ran without turning, shimmering like a mirage;
Behind the leader, Achbuudan
Was now drawing close.
Kulatai, tiny as a young tick,
Was racing with all his longing.
Whipping Maaniker,
Young Aidar raised the cheering cry.
The stones beneath his tread burst apart;
Kulatai and Maaniker
Ran side by side with a roar,
The earth circling in a mirage,
Drawing close to Achbuudan.
Splitting the dense host in two,
Kulatai came charging in,
Nearly moving to the front.
Day had gone and night was ending;
The boy riding the kulan
Found a marvelous tongue:
“Do not cheer me, Aidar, do not cheer;
If I fall, will my teeth not break?
If I die, will God not strike me!
A colt light as tumbleweed—
Will it not fly off wildly?
If I tumble to the ground,
Will my death not truly arrive?
Will my bones not shatter small?
Will my tormented life not end?
He raced the colt and won a prize;
God did not strike Kenei.
Let him race it when full-grown,
And win the prize here then.
Instead of prize meat, eat your fat;
Eat the blood of your mother.
Eat the age of prizes that has passed;
Eat the liver of your seven forefathers.
What shameless man races a colt
Alongside grown horses?
Do not cheer, Aidar, do not cheer;
Will not our strength fail,
Will not our blood be spilled?”
Hero Zholoi’s Achbuudan—
The many Chinese standing tall
Cheered it on fiercely.
Kulatai proved a tulpar;
Khan Zholoi’s Achbuudan
He seized by the tail with his teeth.
Behind Kulatai,
Hero Agysh’s Surkiyik,
Flattening its ears like a kulan,
Sweeping the earth like a whirlwind,
Had passed the other racers.
The Black Kalmak and Manchu people
Blocked it in sixty places.
Behind Surkiyik,
Hero Töshtük’s Chalkuiruk—
There was no remedy for Chalkuiruk:
It had descended beneath the earth
For nearly seven years,
And had been above the earth again
For barely seven days.
Unable to lower its head,
The wondrous beast, weakened,
Chalkuiruk came exhausted.
Behind it,
Muzburchak’s Telkyzyl
Also ran shimmering like a mirage;
Kökö Khan’s Kökala
Pressed close behind.
