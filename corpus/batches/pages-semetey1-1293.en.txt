He was about to strike me down.
There was malice in his words,
Crookedness in his eyes;
If I came within his reach,
He intended to do me harm.
No iron is keen unless it is tempered,
No accursed people thrive unless raised apart;
No iron is keen unless it is forged,
No people are like raging Khojo’s.
An uproar arose ahead;
That Khojo, not knowing what he said,
Prepared as though to seize him.
Whipping his black horse,
Roaring in fury,
Khojo came then,
Shouting the battle cry “Shygai!”
Furiously he thundered;
At Khojo’s terrible aspect,
The whole free body
Of anyone watching shuddered.
Semetey stood without concern:
“Chynkojo was my kinsman—
Let him level his spear straight at me;
If he truly has a grievance,
Let him thrust and strike!
Let him unleash his full wrath;
I shall stand and grant his turn,
And let him avenge whatever ancient wrong he bears.
Let him level his spear and strike me,
Truly inflicting open humiliation;
Will he not then look upon Buudan?
If I do not strike back,
Will he not regard me as kin?”
Without flinching, Semetey
Now stood looking ahead.
Chynkojo, son of Shygai,
Tensed and thrust his spear.
In fury Chynkojo
Charged and drove the spear.
His waist bent far backward,
His eyes bulged out,
His beard bristled strand by strand;
Roaring in anger,
Saying, “This side of the ornamented belt,
Right at the edge of the lung,”
He charged and drove his spear.
Semetey did not flinch.
Akolpok, inherited
From his father, gray-wolf Manas,
Yielded to no such foe,
Armor fitted to your lion.
Saying, “It has grown very old,”
Your poor mother had repaired it,
Mending every pierced place.
When it reached Akolpok,
Khojo’s spear
Shattered into eighty pieces,
And Khojo’s stratagem failed.
Had he not seen many lands,
That accomplished Khojo?
Did he not know the enemy’s stratagems,
That experienced Khojo?
A great club as large as a house—
That road-hardened Khojo
Seized and swung in his hand;
Gripping it with both hands,
At the prince, mighty as Khoja,
He brought it down upon the crown.
When the club struck his head,
Chynkojo’s cast-iron mace-head—
Its shaft burst apart,
Its head flew rattling away.
The helmet worn upon his head,
At the very place the blow struck,
Had stretched and bent;
But Charaina, fashioned
By your dear mother Kanykei,
Had remained and stopped the blow.
