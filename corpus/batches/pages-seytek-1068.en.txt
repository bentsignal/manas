May destiny bring before you
The panther, the hero Seytek.
If, while sleeping in your dream,
You behold a place akin to paradise,
Holding the sun in your left hand
And seeing the moon in your right,
Your wish has been granted,
And the one you watched for has come.
Your abundant fortune has opened,
And the wonder in which you were immersed,
The lion Seytek himself,
Was the moon you lay embracing.
If the sun was in your left hand,
Your prosperity and fortune are on their way.
One who does not burn when cast into fire,
Whom a sword cannot cut,
Who does not sink when cast into water,
Who yields his wisdom to no mortal,
Whom no man can defeat in combat,
The solid core of tempered steel—
That panther, the hero Seytek,
Will be, dear sister,
The father of your male offspring.
If you saw a true eagle in your dream,
The one who subdues all fanged beasts,
The one who throws the world into tumult,
Is the lion Seytek himself.
If you rode a chestnut horse,
Your treasure was Seytek’s possession.
You will be immersed in delight;
He has come to meet your destiny,
And you will play without growing weary.
If you went hunting and flew your bird,
And took both lions and tigers,
It is a manifest gift from God;
Is it not your gray-maned Seytek?
All the fanged beasts,
Startled from their noble pride,
Unable to bear his awesome might,
Fled in every direction;
This means the hero Seytek
Is your lion, like his father.
With the spirit of Manas supporting him,
Every one of his enemies
The young panther Seytek
Will trample beneath his feet.
Against the Black Chinese and the Manchus,
He will march and attack the khan;
In time, his deeds will become an epic.”
Perizada Külayım
Thus interpreted the dream.
Saying, “May your dream come true,”
She brought her words to an end.
When Akbermet heard this,
She swiftly cried, “Baabedin!”
To the spirit of Manas
She dedicated a moon-hoofed horse,
Slaughtered a double-humped camel,
And a mare whose udder was a span wide.
In a copper cauldron with golden handles,
She boiled and cooked the meat,
Spreading out all her wealth.
Her heart surged and her blood boiled within;
Bermet was so excited she could not keep still.
From the city of the fairies,
From the settlement she had brought along,
Among the companions of her own age,
Among radiant, exceedingly lovely women,
Who walked and played together,
Confidantes skilled in conversation,
Had she not brought with her
Eighty young wives and eighty maidens,
Companions who dispelled her sorrow?
Their waists were handspans, their faces like the moon,
They were serene beauties,
Radiant faces without a single mole.
The heart turned toward them at a glance;
Their beauty was fashioned unlike any other.
Half were wives and half were maidens,
With thimble mouths and camel-calf eyes,
