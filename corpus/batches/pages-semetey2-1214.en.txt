The mighty-born Külchoro
Seized both ears
Together with the forelock.
He made the stallion snort
And felled him where he stood.
Ah, that strong-born, mighty lion
Then cut off his head.
Until Sary Khan was buried,
Until the gray stallion was slaughtered—
Leave this matter here.
Now begin the tale of Kanchoro,
Whose soul departed amid matchlock fire
And the drawing of bows.
He gathered the Jediger and led the host,
So vast it made the earth sag.
The vanguard roared,
And when one looked upon the rising tumult,
They were shouting, “Kanchoro!”
He gathered the strong and led the host,
Leading no small force, but a multitude,
Driving the entire army together.
Firing matchlocks and drawing bows,
They surged forward
To surround the sultan upon the ridge,
And close around him,
To wage battle unto death,
To exact their vengeful grudge,
To bring a day of bitter grief,
And offer the gray-wolf hero Semetey
As a sacrifice.
The great dark host of Jediger,
Its ranks upon the rise roaring,
By the might of almighty God,
Charged to the last man.
The Jediger covered the earth,
Shouting “Kyyaz!” as their battle cry.
The army advanced and the people clamored,
Their renowned men at the front,
Their heroes along the flanks,
Ordered to seize and crush him,
While that fool Kanchoro stayed behind.
The host surged with a deafening uproar,
Striking the great war drum with a crash,
The trumpets deafening the ears,
The drums pounding loudly,
As they drew near the hero.
The clamorous army came onward.
Külchoro, son of Almambet,
When he saw the onrushing host,
Felt his ribs torn apart;
Tears poured from his eyes,
And his heart was deeply troubled.
He tightened his belt in earnest,
And, unable to endure it,
His whole heart caught fire.
He gripped his spear and made ready,
And buckled on his scabbardless sword.
His sword would spare no life;
His heart was torn asunder.
Death’s grim, tumultuous work
Seemed very near
To the gray-wolf Choro’s eyes.
“Now my silver-eyed, sun-faced,
Thunder-voiced uncle,
The figure who gave me shelter,
My sovereign and sultan—
If the great dark Jediger horde
Overruns him while I give no aid,
What inheritance could satisfy me?
Before my uncle, towering like a rugged mountain,
I shall die first
And go as a sacrifice.
If I turn away in battle,
Though his shelter is worth a hundred armies,
And lose the one who shields me,
What inheritance could satisfy me?
He carved me meat and gave me fat,
And fed me butter when I hungered.
