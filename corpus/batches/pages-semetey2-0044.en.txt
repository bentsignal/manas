You have been thrown into confusion,
Living day and night as you pleased,
Eating the barren yearlings of the herd,
You smooth-tongued swine,
You have let yourself be defeated.
Your words come out faltering,
Your strength is gone, Karagul,
And your eyes look weary.
Your words come out crooked,
You have lost your former bearing,
And your eyes show the torment endured.
Another self stands in your eyes,
A stranger stands within your body.
Is there a road through the gray mist?
Deprived of your beard,
Karagul, have you come here wailing?
Did an army come from the Kyrgyz,
Raising a gray-white banner?
Was there a moon-crested standard?
Tell me, Karagul:
Was there an uproar splitting the sky?
Looking over every side of you,
You have the look of one escaped from slaughter.
Your composure has departed;
What words have you brought?
Did they draw bows until arrows fell like rain,
And shoot their strange bullets like hail?
Have you come out of that tumult?
From an outcry that deafened the ears,
Have you come out of that tumult?
You look as though you escaped a net
From which no living soul gets free.
You look as if you came from a road
Traveled without rest for six months.
You look as if you emerged from water
No scout could ever cross.
Without evasion, speak of your sheath.
Karagul, son of the Chinese,
Tell the truth and speak no lie.
If a foe has come from the Burut,
If an unhealable grievance threatens
Beijing of the Kakanchin,
I shall ready Algara and mount.
I shall ride out and slaughter them all,
Taking the army that goes with me,
Kill old Bakai,
And drive them to their own land.
I shall plunder their possessions,
And wreak destruction in this way.
If the enemy is from elsewhere,
I shall drive them to their land.
Karagul, willingly tell the truth.
If a people has come of its own accord,
If an overbearing lion has come,
He came to behold a terrible spectacle;
Without leaving so much as a hair,
The bastard shall die by my hand.
The great sorcerer guarding Beijing
Has bestowed mighty power.
Who has clashed with me
And returned to see his people?
When wrathfully I make ready,
Can anyone come and contend with me?
While the precious head
Of me, hero Kongurbai, remains,
Can anyone grapple with me and win?
While my noble head still stands,
You are foolish, Karagul;
Can any man force it to bow?
While your noble lord watches,
Can one who clashes remain alive?
When I command and exert my strength,
While your mountain-like Kalcha remains,
Can a mountain withstand me?
Do not hang your head, Karagul,
Like one who has suffered hell.
While your mountain-like Kalcha remains,
Can assembled enemies prevail?
