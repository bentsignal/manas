Finding no host to challenge,
With my tufted spear and patterned sword
Brandished in my hand,
When I subdued the whole world
And lived in joy,
While my dear uncle was alive,
I would take up the mighty bow,
And this poor, grinning soul
Would put his strength to the test.
While my magnanimous uncle lived,
We plunged eagerly into every sport,
Testing our power and measuring our might,
Setting eighty mattocks upright,
And shooting an arrow through them.
While my mighty uncle lived,
We boisterous, valiant souls
Would test our strength.
Such was my former strength;
It was in the time of my uncle,
The leopard, gray wolf, and bloodthirsty lion.
When I held the wrathful khan of Beijing
At bay,
When I subdued the whole world
And set it ablaze;
When my trouser legs
Could scarcely contain my limbs,
When all the strength within my body
Could find no rest,
We shot through mattocks to test our strength,
Clashed together and fully tried our power.
Such was the nature
Of your once-vigorous uncle.
Unbroken and undivided,
He remained whole like this.
Such was the strength
Of your uncle who departed long ago:
For the prize, forty barren mares
Would be caught and slaughtered from the herd,
And we would test our strength.
Ninety barren mares would be slaughtered
For the swift and peerless champion,
The man who breathed more strongly than mortals,
Who, when chaos fell and enemies came,
When dust flew and the earth trembled,
When disaster befell a hero
And a hellish ordeal arrived,
Would go where none could survive,
Stake this precious life,
And grapple with the enemy.
From those who fought the fiercest,
Those consummately skilled with the spear,
The fortunate masters of stratagem,
Whose majestic forms seemed a myriad troops,
Whose eyes were round as hillocks,
Who seemed ready to devour any foe they saw,
Whose wrestling strength was unmatched,
Whose heroism was beyond measure,
From imposing warriors,
From broad-breasted champions,
From those who devoured lives in combat,
He would turn the mouth of a swift steed
With a camel’s forelock and a low mane.
Gathering many such men,
My lion uncle, fierce with wrath—
Men mighty and strong,
My heroic uncle Semetey—
Would test their strength.
In the marshlands of broad Talas,
My dear uncle, my lion uncle,
While the great Kyrgyz people lived in peace,
We dwelt in broad Talas.
When one day my destined fate
Would not let me rest,
When my trouser legs
Could scarcely contain me,
Finding no cliff from which to fly to my death,
No enemy against whom to die fighting,
No mountain high enough to cross,
No bullet with which to shoot,
And no road along which to ride,
