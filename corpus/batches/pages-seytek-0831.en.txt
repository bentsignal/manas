Deprived of my protector,
And there my cherished hopes froze.
May no believer die
As I suffered humiliation;
If my God creates a living soul,
May He spare His servant such torment.
They scorched my breast over fire,
And sliced my hide like leather.
They ignored my pleas and words,
And robbed me thus of all my strength.
They dug a pit as my prison,
And stripped off my robe as plunder.
They had my hands bound behind my back,
While my eyes blazed like a lion’s.
My own brother Kanchoro
Thus made my misery boil.
He had holes pierced through my elbows,
As though I had killed
His father, Khan Chubak.
He gave me to ninety fearsome champions
To crush and pulp my breast,
Imprisoned me in the raging waters,
And gave me to ninety fearsome champions
To club and beat me.
Though we were kin raised together,
He burned me with smokeless fire,
Tormenting me,
And would not hear me when I pleaded,
Leaving my eyes imploring,
Swelling my flesh grotesquely,
Thus making my misery boil.
Not from a stranger or an outsider,
But from my own kin I suffered this wonder.
“Kill him if he so much as stirs,”
He ordered guards stationed beside me.
My right shoulder and my left,
Where my two arms drew their strength,
He had gouged through from behind.
My two eyes blazed,
So did my misery boil.
When I suffered such torment,
Had sorrow weighed upon my bones?
Having faced fresh calamity here,
Had my courage deserted me?
Had my joints weakened and strength departed,
Had the smiting God struck me?
They gouged my shoulder and placed a writ there,
Saying, “Let him be the slave of slaves,”
And named me Denekul.
My two hands were bound,
My two eyes were blazing,
My tiny life cried out,
Every bone in me cracked.
When my lion’s head burned,
For nine years under Kyyaz
I lived as a woodcutter.
A deep pit seems to have been dug;
Our misery truly came to the boil.
My days passed in slavery
While I served brave Kyyaz.
Whatever I bit became a bullet,
Whatever I grasped became an ember.
I regarded a lamb’s fat as frozen treasure,
Carrying firewood for nine years,
And found hell terribly vast.
Only a spoonful of blood remained in me,
My flesh was but a blue, raw scrap.
Carrying firewood on my back,
My collarbones were rubbed raw.
In this living hell,
My heart ached so.
My heart chilled into ice,
My tiny life throbbed with pain.
Torn apart by anxious thoughts,
The tears flowing from both eyes
Spilled down my chest.
Facing hell itself,
Carrying firewood for nine years,
My lion spirit was reduced to this,
Enslaved for so many years,
