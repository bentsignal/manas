Then the hero Seytek,
His heart straying and troubled,
Saw countless disasters and afflictions
Looming close before his eyes.
Calling upon the Creator for aid,
Sparing not his life in the struggle,
Ready to die if true death had come,
But if he remained alive,
To stand as a shelter
For his uncle Külchoro.
He resolved to wage fierce battle,
And rescue Külchoro, his guardian,
From the infidel’s venomous bullet.
He would deliver him
From the iron-rope net,
From the deep-dug pit,
From the blazing bed of coals,
From the monster’s hands.
“If the moon is veiled in darkness,
What will become of the night?
If my valiant uncle Külchoro
Falls into the infidel’s hands,
What will become of his days?
May I be accursed if I do not act!
Let me be a sheltering ridge,
A visible source of strength and courage,
A support around his waist,
And become his true companion.
From the wrathful dog’s hands,
My valiant uncle Choro
I shall rescue.
He never watched idly while others fought;
He guarded me like a father,
And cared for me like a mother.
He was a towering black mountain,
A lofty crag of such height
That no surging host could cross it.
Will he break through my crag?
Will he destroy us to the root?
Will he divert the river from its valley?
Will there not be black-veiled widows?
Leaving not a tuft of hair,
Will he truly take us as plunder?
Will he not pour out dreadful wrath,
Driving away those who remain
And breaking their ribs?
You have acted with purpose;
Make this merely an age of trial fallen upon us.
God, if You created him as a living soul,
He was a lion born of a leopard—
God, keep my uncle safe!”
So saying, the hero Seytek
Had his breast filled with heavy care,
And tears streamed from his eyes in ten directions.
The prince’s descendant
Was seized by terrible fury.
To confront Sarybai,
He utterly renounced
His precious, wonderful life.
He readied himself like a hawk,
And strained forward like a lion.
Khojosan came to his aid,
And charged in beside him.
Behold, such was the boy:
His eyes burned like embers,
And he surged like a flood.
Gathering courage and preparing himself,
Heaven-sent wisdom came to him.
Throwing the whole world into alarm,
He urged onward
Kerkula beneath him in bounding strides.
The holy wanderer named Kydyr
Came straight on, working the bit,
While twelve champions led
His prancing bay horse.
At the wrath of that sacred falcon,
Khans who saw it died;
Souls who grappled with him died;
Surveyors who tried to measure him died;
Envoys who came stripping for battle died.
