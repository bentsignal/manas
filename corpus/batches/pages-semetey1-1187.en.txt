Thus setting them upon the road,
The racehorses streamed out in splendor.
All the chosen tulpar steeds,
While infidel and Muslim
Stood clashing together,
Were driven out
By young Aydar of the flowing forelock.
Two hundred youths were beside him.
After young Aydar drove them away,
After they reached the starting place,
Let us leave that matter there
And begin from this side:
Kokotoy's Bokmurun
Sent out another announcement.
Kokotoy's Bokmurun
Proclaimed before the multitude:
"Sons of Adam have gathered;
You old men have come.
All you heroes and noble champions
Have come.
The leopard supported by the Shah,
Your lion, has come.
The hero who knows well the enemy's devices
Has come.
He who says, 'I trust in God above,'
Has come.
The tireless noble champion,
Your valiant man, has come.
This is the foremost of great gatherings;
This is my khan father's memorial feast.
The horses must reach their ground;
Until the horses return,
Will the people not grow weary?
I stake a camel with its load,
And exactly sixty servants.
Let one champion come from the infidels
And one champion from the Muslims.
Let their lances bend;
Let the heroes come out and joust.
Let those whose death has come die,
And spill a lion's blood.
Let them charge and pierce
The ridge of the ribs;
Let them make a spectacle for the people
And fight lance against lance.
Let them come out to joust;
Let the one who strikes win the prize,
And the one struck remain empty-handed."
When Bokmurun said this,
Kongurbay had failed
To gain much profit as plunder.
Algara beneath him,
The prancing black horse,
He had not been able to race.
He was destined to joust with Manas
And cause turmoil
At Kokotoy's feast.
Kongurbay knew
That Akkula would race.
He had long desired,
"May he lose Akkula
And stand gawking on foot;
May I take revenge for my father."
"Before he can pull aside,
I will thrust a spear into the muscle
Of stallion-like Manas;
I will split the raging Manas
Before the assembled people;
From bloodletter Manas the Burut,
I will take my revenge alone.
