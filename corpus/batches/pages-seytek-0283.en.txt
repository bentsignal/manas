I drew it from its sheath.
When they pressed hard upon me,
When those accursed Jediger
Came surging furiously,
Shattering all my fortune,
Setting all I had aflame,
I took the keen Albar sword in hand.
Because of all that had befallen,
Because of your ill-fated mother,
A hundred Jediger champions
I struck down wounded.
The black-hearted Kanchoro,
That wicked black devil—
Curse my womanhood!
I failed and struck awry.
My bright eyes dimmed and wandered,
And from the mother who cherished me
I was left alive,
Torn away in disgrace.
The flesh of my right side
Was split apart by a bullet,
Twelve torments and a thousand cruelties
Loomed close above my head.
Sobbing and weeping in grief,
Through unheard-of shame and turmoil,
I was parted from my mother.
I did not spill your blood;
I still bore an uncertain child,
And so I could not bring myself
To take your accursed life.
Wailing shrilly in sorrow,
I was parted from the mother
Who sat cherishing me.
My heart burned and my blood drained,
And all strength left wretched me.
Curse my womanhood!
I have no mountain-high stronghold,
I suffered bitterly—the world is filth.
Curse my womanhood!
I have no strength left to fight.
When I screamed, none heard my voice;
When my true hero was slain,
When my blessed one wrought slaughter,
When they destroyed my gray-maned lord,
They tore away the noble woman,
From the hands of your wretched mother—
My mother, sacred as Mecca.
Though I pleaded, they heeded no word.
The days had shriveled me to a handful;
I embraced and kissed my darling,
Breathed once the scent of her breast—
Your venerable white-haired mother.
I tore my hair until it thinned;
Blood streaming from both my eyes
Flowed down over my breast.
I shrieked aloud in grief,
Parted from my own true mother.
As the vile ones beat me,
And subjected me to this disgrace,
I wished to sink into the earth, but it was hard;
I wished to die, but life was sweet.
They caused my darling bitter pain,
They ground me utterly to dust,
And dragged my mother away—
My generous venerable mother,
My broad-hearted dear one.
‘How can I remain alive?’ I cried.
‘From this day on, oh Mother,
Whom shall I hold sacred?’ I cried.
I turned my face toward death
And tore out my disheveled hair.
Because of all that had befallen,
Because of my dear mother,
I raised a piercing cry,
Not sparing my fly-sized soul.
‘Let me depart for the true world;
For my venerable white-haired mother,
I would gladly die contending.’

The keen Albar sword that was commissioned—
