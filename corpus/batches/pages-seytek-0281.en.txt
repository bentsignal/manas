He gathered his young warriors
And named them the Forty Companions.
Finding no land left to raid,
On this side of Kakan,
In the open country of Kaspan-Tоо,
Along the ridge of Altyn-Tоо,
In the lifeless, barren desert,
He encountered it by providence:
Zulpukor, descended from the heavens,
Was found there by chance
For your gray-maned heroic forefather.
Knowing it would bring great slaughter,
He adorned its hilt with gold,
And the saintly elder, brave Bakai,
Gave it the name Zulpukor.
Zulpukor is a keen, fiery sword;
When wielded against a facing foe,
When swung against a vaunting enemy,
It reddens and bursts into flame,
And when struck at an encountered foe,
It lengthens to forty fathoms.
Whomever its edge touches is gone;
When it is swung, life departs.
Its edge does not catch as it cuts,
Its touch is scarcely felt,
Until the victim falls and blood flows.
When it strikes stone, dust erupts;
When it strikes sand, fire leaps forth.
No enemy dared face it,
And if some boastful foe
Chanced to attack it head-on,
Not one of his lineage survived.
When drawn screaming from its sheath,
It yearned to drink blood;
No living soul who clashed with it
Returned safely to his family.
If you would strike, that is the keen blade;
Passing down since your forefather’s time,
That is the treasured heirloom,
The sharp blade handed down from old.
When they pressed down upon my head,
When they drove off all my livestock,
When they seized my gold and left me grieving,
And entered to plunder the royal camp,
Only a blue dress remained upon my back.
I had no son behind me,
And no strength to fight them.
The world turned to filth and emptiness,
And my Akkelte remained
In the hands of the dog Kanchoro.
He ruined all my days;
Listen, my son, to my sorrow.
Go to that rabid slave
And ask him to return as your inheritance
The possessions of your gray-maned father.
If he refuses to give them as your inheritance,
Return without quarreling,
And come tell wretched me,
“Kanchoro refused to give them.”
There is a wondrous matter I must tell;
Listen without grieving, my son.
That single precious object
Is worth all the livestock beneath the sky;
Such is this honored treasure—
My orphaned darling, heed me well.
Your earliest forefather, your great grandsire,
Grew up and was born in Altai,
Then stirred the whole world.
Red and green flames burned,
And his worth became known among the people.
The prowess of that gray-maned hero
Was inscribed among all nations.
At twelve he conquered a royal camp
And rode against a realm with its own court;
His fame spread across the world
And was entered in the chronicle
Of the khan of Kakan city.
He tended white stallions and gathered steeds,
Swiftly assembled valiant men,
And slaughtered ninety beasts for his feast.
