The poor creature mourned,
And the pale mare wept with him;
The khan wept upon hearing the news,
And all the people wept.
When he stiffened beside the mare,
They knew that he was Choro.
His swiftness was beyond measure;
He bounded forward like a sheep.
Then Külchoro,
Traveling onward without sparing himself,
Crossed rugged hills and passes,
And traversed the steep mountain crossing.
Along the Seven Valleys,
To the fortified city with its gates,
To Khan Kyyaz’s palace,
At last he arrived.
At brave Kyyaz’s hitching post,
He tethered the pale mare.
The lion Choro, favored by fortune,
The hero Choro who never fled death,
Looked all around him—
Behold what Choro was like:
He licked his lips like a blue-maned lion,
Beat upon his chest,
And struck his willow-silk tail
Against his shanks.
Snorting and neighing,
He pounded the black earth,
Drawing in his belly and heaving,
Striking the ground until it shook.
He neighed in mourning,
And, like a creature beholding its foal,
Stretched out his neck and cried aloud.
When he saw the pale mare,
He stood like a prince guarding his people;
What could poor Choro do?
He seemed a child who had seen his mother.
He had once been a peerless steed;
The pale mare came first,
Stretching her neck toward Külchoro
And neighing with all her might.
Not a single hoofed colt remained;
All had bolted in terror.
At that moment Külchoro
Was overcome with despair;
Countless hardships rose before his eyes.
He stopped at the stone gate
And lamented his long-borne sorrow.
Choro yearned in shame to die,
And his poor head longed to behold
The true abode from which none return.
The wide world narrowed around him,
And he grieved for want of even one mare.
“My uncle Kül, khan of the world,
Has suffered hardship and disgrace.”
Though he said, “I shall die,” he could not,
Nor could he take his own life.
The flesh of his calves throbbed,
And all the flesh of his soles
Burst and bled upon the stones.
He limped onward in pain,
Leaning against the gray lattice gate;
Külchoro walked with a faltering step,
His power spent and his strength gone.
At twin embankments shaped like a saddle,
He bent forward and stood.
From amid the vast herd,
The bloodthirsty warrior’s gray mare,
That mare taken as plunder,
On seeing Külchoro,
Flung her forelock toward the sky
And stamped her hoof into the ground.
She was a beast once owned by his bloodthirsty uncle;
The pale mare neighed
And circled around him.
The birches wept and the willows wept,
And every living soul who watched them wept.
She had no tongue with which to speak;
As the mare stood there like this,
Your hero Choro wept with her.
