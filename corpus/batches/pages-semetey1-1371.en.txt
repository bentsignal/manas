Did your valiant uncle Bakai
Not gird himself for such deeds?!"
Roused upon Kökcholok,
Your uncle Bakai the gray wolf
He took a blue spear,
Praying to the Creator
Put on a blue lynx-fur cap,
And bore the blue-mottled shield and banner.
In Bakai's hand
Was a golden-cheeked battle-axe;
At Bakai's left side
Was a pouch made of gold.
His foe was the Kakanchin;
Bakai separated from Talas,
Your uncle Bakai then
Loomed like Ala-Too.
Across the broad back
Of your lion Bakai
The firearm Kök Chybuk gleamed;
In the scabbard of your dear uncle Bakai,
The sword clanged in its scabbard.
The sight arrested the heart;
Your lion uncle Bakai's
Form and majesty,
His bearing on horseback,
Appeared equal to a thousand troops.
Then Bakai's white beard
Spread apart,
Divided to the right and left,
And your uncle Bakai
Appeared in the form of a saint;
Renowned as a true saint—
Was that not Bakai,
Who, while lion Manas was alive,
Learned the ways of fighting Kakan—
Was that not Bakai?
Your uncle Bakai led the way;
The sight arrested the heart,
And though your uncle was old,
His gray-wolf nature showed.
Old Bakai was one,
Kulchoro and Kanchoro
The younger kinsmen of lion Semetey.
