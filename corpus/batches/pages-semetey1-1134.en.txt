Khan Manas's forty companions—
The most cunning of spearmen.
Among those companions,
Azhibai and Chubak were the strongest.
Khan Manas's forty companions—
The sight of them divides one's attention;
Most looked like naked blades.
Told to take a hat, they cut off a head;
Told to drink water, they drank poison.
Each was a gray-wolf peerless champion,
Forty distinguished men
Who came from forty different places.
Each of the forty had a different way,
With barbed spears and red caps,
Like blazing red fire.
The forty companions, wolves of war:
Tölömön's diviner
Cast omens at their head;
Yrchyuul, son of Yraman,
Sang beside them.
Their wrath turned outward;
Khan Manas's forty companions
Bound on swords without scabbards.
Beside Khan Manas,
They stood raging,
Finding no mountain to strike,
Finding no enemy to fight,
Gathering themselves with a growl.
The forty companions wore
Blue-lynx and sable hats;
The way they faced was red fire.
Young Aydar walked
Before valiant Manas,
Giving him six swift horses
And a palm-sized letter:
“Koshoy and Töshtük are your khans;
Our souls are sorely afflicted.
At Khan Kökötöy's memorial feast,
A great gathering assembled,
And we are all about to be slaughtered.
Gray-wolf Manas, go
To Kökötöy's memorial feast.
The Muslims lie in humiliation;
Rescue them
From the Black Chinese and Manchus!
If you do not rescue them,
The fearsome Black Chinese
Will bring hardship upon their heads
And strike them down to the last.
Koshoy and Töshtük may die;
The Black Chinese and Manchus
May divide all the Muslims
Into shares measured by the whip.
They may slaughter every one, leaving none;
Kalcha, Zholoy, and Ezkara—
These three have joined together
And may do whatever they please.
At our very hearth we suffered humiliation;
Among the Black Chinese and Manchus,
We saw a tyrant named Kongurbai.
Zholoy used his force,
Demanding that Maaniker be given him;
Ezkara inflicted humiliation.
They were ready to make us taste salt,
And ready to plunder the girls.
They seized and ate the meat;
They were ready to destroy every face.
If you, the burdened one, do not go,
They may cast a cold catching-pole
Upon the racehorse that would not be given when asked.
They may leave all the Muslim giants
Entirely on foot
And seize their horses.
Before all the other peoples, those Chinese,
If allowed to have their way,
May use force at the feast.
I saw it with my own eyes.
They besieged the Muslims,
