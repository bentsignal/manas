Before one could say this or that,
In the blink of an eye,
At the reedy hollow in the gorge,
Your accomplished uncle Bakai
Suddenly appeared before him.
When the fox saw Khan Bakai,
The poor creature darted aside
And flashed toward the top
Of the black cliff above the gorge.
Bakai stood shouting,
Pleading with the one God;
Kumayyk came racing,
His ears laid flat to the ground.
As the fox was about to leap
Over a black rock as large as a house,
Kumayyk raced up and caught
The fox doomed to an unclean death.
He passed over the ridge,
And, gripping the fox in his teeth,
Flung it over a black rock
As tall as a camel.
When it struck, the fox split apart.
Ah, Kumayyk, wondrous creature—
His prowess was revealed!
Bakai came racing over,
Offering praises to Kumayyk!
“My beloved Kumayyk,
My champion left by the lion!
Today all my pent-up grief has opened,
And all my sorrow has scattered!
Joined with me when you were no bigger than a louse,
My beloved Kumayyk,
United with me when you were no bigger than a thumb.
On the plain where you chased the fox,
May the entrusted life of your uncle,
Lion Bakai, be your sacrifice!
Your capable uncle Bakai
His white saber with a mottled neck.
Hurriedly then,
Took it from his side.
Bakai cut its windpipe,
And performed a wondrous rite.
The expert leopard Bakai
Skinned the fox into a whole pelt;
His anger settled and he grew calm.
Without speaking a word or saying a thing,
Your aged uncle became absorbed in his work.
“Oh, do not be offended, my child, do not be offended!
There are watchmen farther on;
I will accept what you have said!
If I return to Talas,
I will have it made into a hat
For my wretched son Baitailak!”
Having skinned the fox and carried out his design,
Poor Bakai made a feast
For the ravens and magpies.
He swiftly invoked Babedin,
And, for the sake of lion Manas,
Named and dedicated a horse, beginning with Aituyak.
Semetey, whom he had led with him,
Prepared to begin the reconnaissance;
He pleaded with Semetey,
The sole support he had:
“Ah, my beloved Semetey,
The omen is auspicious, the undertaking good.
We shall tread upon the head
Of that lordly Kongurbai;
If mighty Allah supports us,
We shall make the infidels howl
And scatter all four kinds of their livestock!
We shall bring them boundless sorrow;
We shall raid and seize the herds at Keng-Shor
From that swine,
The khan of the Kechil, and make him grieve!
We shall make slaughter into a spectacle;
Ah, my beloved champion,
We shall avenge many wrongs!
