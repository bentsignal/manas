It never yielded in the least;
It bore every mark of a thoroughbred.
When seen among the people,
Sarala with its stake-straight neck
Was made to arch proudly by Almambet;
Sarala with its slender tail
Was made to quiver by Almambet;
Sarala with its cow-like croup
Was made to whirl by Almambet.
Kongurbay of the infidels
Was then standing there watching.
Almambet, son of Khan Azozyl,
Was about to ask Manas for the joust
And meet his death there.
"Panther Manas, my soul's friend,
Please consent to what I ask.
The Kalmyk has come out to joust;
Give this joust to me.
He too was a khan's son,
And I too was a khan's son.
Let us joust with spears
And satisfy our hearts' longing.
Please consent to what I ask;
The joust against Kongurbay,
My friend Manas, give it to me.
Please agree to these words of mine.
He was a fellow who wore
The Chinese robe, broad and red-flowered,
Alongside me;
From childhood we stood opposed
And constantly provoked each other.
Since we were sons of elder and younger brothers,
He was a fellow always beside me.
Kongurbay, son of Alooke—
Our forefather was Chylaba;
Over Chylaba's tea mine
He was a fellow who quarreled with me.
I have a grievance against that pig:
My land of Beijing that I had won,
Kongurbay took away,
Along with my gold-bearing mine.
He scattered, divided, and destroyed
The people who looked to me.
If you truly are my friend,
Hear my words, Manas.
From that day until this one
I have been unable to take my revenge.
I have been unable to see Beijing,
Chinmachin, or Kakan.
My true friend Manas, grant this to me;
When else shall I see your support?"
When Almambet had spoken so,
Great-hearted Manas replied:
"Ah, Alake, my gray wolf,
Would the black Chinese and Manchu people
Not consume us with their slander?
Would they not say, 'When Khan Azozyl's only son
Fled and came to the Buruts,
They mercilessly handed him over
To be put to death'?
Would the Chinese not mock us,
Would God not strike down my land?
Would the fortune of this gray wolf Almambet
Not be broken by this deed?
The Chinese would turn it into a legend;
Would God not strike us for it?
Would they not gnaw at our very core,
Saying, 'That wandering Chinese man
They handed over to death'?
Would they not say, 'The patterned spear in his hand
At last failed him'?
Would they not say that Manas
And all the assembled Buruts
Spared him from Kongurbay?
Would they not say that to Kokotoy's feast
Almambet came as a fugitive,
Fleeing and wandering?
Would they not say that all the Buruts gathered
And Almambet's punishment
Was carried out by them?"
