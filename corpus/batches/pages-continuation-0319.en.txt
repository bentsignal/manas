Let me strike with honeyed words,
If he bristles and refuses to talk,
I shall seize him by the collar.
I shall forestall the quarrel,
I shall block the violent man’s path.
Wisdom’s husbandman, Akjoltoy,
With the majesty of a vast host,
Whose every utterance was sweet,
A fluent, free-speaking servant,
A servant well prepared in speech,
In great and restless haste,
His surpassing resourcefulness revealed,
Kulunsur, huge as a bare mountain,
Sprang and ran like an arrow,
And loaded his gold upon a camel.
Kulunsur and the red camel,
To those two raging men,
To the giants Ebegey and Kögöy,
He led them forth as gifts.
Warriors, you have come
From your homeland,
You have set out from your people;
Here is the khan, and here am I,
If you advance, here lies Talas,
Here am I, the eloquent Akjoltoy!
If you truly are khans,
You will pass for lions;
If you are witless, worthless men,
If you are fools who understand no words,
You will cut off the offered head.
The red camel is the gift, and I am the sacrifice—
So your Akjoltoy spoke eloquently.
Ebegey and Kögöy, two giants,
If you two will come to your senses,
You are the ones to whom I speak,
You are my shining dark eyes.
You have stood for deeds of renown,
We are kinsmen of a single root;
If you will not heed reason or words,
You are wretches who ruin the cause.
No Chinese foe with a red-tasseled spear
Is here, shouting and thrusting;
No fearsome enemy is here
Who comes raging against us,
With a feud from ages past.
Consider where this deed will lead;
I bow my neck before what is right,
I offer all my worldly wealth.
Our roots are one, so let us unite;
Our pastures adjoin, so let us stand together.
Our faith is shared, our lives are one—
I say that we must unite.
Though we wage slaughter for forty years,
Though we raid horses with battle cries,
We are steeds that never tire.
Though slaughter lasts for forty years,
Though we clash in combat,
Though we don blood-soaked caps,
Destroying property and taking peace away,
Piercing lungs and taking heads,
Though we become bitter enemies,
We are kin who cannot endure separation.
Ebegey and Kögöy, two lions,
If you are heroes, you will understand:
No one can break the strength
Of a small people firmly united,
But everyone commands the sons
Of a numerous people without unity.
If we stand firm by our pledged word,
If we face the foe that faces us,
Can any cold-hearted invader come
Boasting and playing the hero,
And defeat us in battle?!
If divided, shall we not be overcome,
And the heroes’ backs be broken?
Will not the many enemies who hate us
Overrun and slaughter us,
Quenching the fire we have kindled,
Plundering and laying waste,
Until our battling foe is satisfied
And the heroes’ fortune is broken!
If heads are cut off, the people will be thrown into turmoil,
And the triumphant enemy will rejoice.
Ebegey and Kögöy, two lions,
Accept the livestock offered to you.
As for the weighty words just spoken,
Whether profound or shallow,
Look deeply into their meaning!—
So Akjoltoy spoke,
Folding his hands and presenting the gift,
Sharpening his precious tongue;
Your elder, the venerable Akjoltoy,
Spoke on unwaveringly.
When they witnessed this deed,
Ebegey and Kögöy, the two lions,
Lowered their banners to the ground,
And from their twin thoroughbred steeds
Leapt down and ran forward.
