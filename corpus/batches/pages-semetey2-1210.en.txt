Turning pale as a gray horse from a white herd.
The stars stirred in the heavens;
Let us be friends even in the next world,
Our sheep sharing one fold.
If wealth is found, let us gather it together;
Shall we not remain comrades?
Take the task upon yourself;
Find some way to accomplish it.
Jeti-Özön was our land,
And Jediger was our people.
Hero, take both reins and the single lead-rope
Firmly into your own hands;
Become lord yourself, hero,
Over the Jediger in Jeti-Suu.
Whether it brings sin or merit,
Take command of us.
Against this champion Sary Khan,
If you are a champion and a lion,
Find some way to settle the score.”
After he had spoken thus,
Finding the counsel fitting,
Your hero Choro
Strapped on his heavy musket
And readied his weapons.
At that moment Kanchoro—
Among spearmen, Choro
Had skill and cunning;
As an expert marksman, Choro
Had matchless prowess;
As a deviser of stratagems, Choro
Had skill and cunning—
Blocked the ridge of Talas
As far as the eye could see,
And lay in wait at the place
Where Sary Khan would come over the rise.
At that moment Sary Khan,
Before one could cry, “Look out!”
In the blink of an eye,
Was overwhelmed by fury;
Your uncle the khan, distraught,
Could not find Semetey.
At that moment Sary Khan,
His heroic heart constricted,
Not knowing whom he approached,
Rode up onto the ridge.
Kanchoro lay in ambush;
Sary Khan came onto open ground
And drew near to the hero Kanchoro,
That treacherous infidel.
Sary Khan came close.
As he crossed the saddle-shaped pass,
Over the rough white rising ground,
With a broad flat below,
And went farther on,
At Echoing Jar-Kyya,
Along a track wide enough for one horse,
In that narrow winding defile,
Where a black rock as large as a yurt
Forced riders to squeeze past,
The hero Kanchoro,
That treacherous infidel,
Displayed the perfection of his marksmanship;
His deadly, formidable gun
Destroyed whatever it was aimed at.
My uncle Sary rode straight in,
My uncle Sary, defender of the people;
Brave Kyyaz confronted him,
And fought with Kyyaz.
Kanchoro’s steel bullet
Struck Sary Khan in the forehead;
The bullet of death fastened upon him.
His white beard, like mountain wormwood,
Spread across his chest;
My mighty khan, Uncle Sary,
Fell upon the pass with his head split open.
His blood gushed out,
And from your uncle Khan Sary
His life fled with a cry.
