Manas raged without stopping;
The forty Kyrgyz boys
Gathered to stand beside him.
Of the eighty Kalmyk boys,
He crushed each one he met,
Heaped disgrace upon them like sheep.
Whoever exchanged blows with him,
Manas tore out his throat.
As he was about to finish them all,
Pursuing the boys,
About to tear all their coats,
About to swallow their blood if he caught them,
About to wreak a terrible slaughter,
Those Kalmyk boys,
Scattering every one,
As he drove them toward their village,
Scattering every one,
As he was about to finish them all,
Leaving none he caught unharmed,
Punching them sprawling as he went,
As the Kalmyks were about to learn,
As grave turmoil was about to arise,
His father, rich Jakyp,
Was surveying the grazing grounds.
Puffed up upon his gray stallion,
Ill-disposed rich Jakyp
Happened upon them.
Driving the forty boys back,
He seized Manas:
“Ah, my son, you have ruined me!” he said,
And poured forth sorrowful lament.
“I brought my herds here and raised them;
My Altay was a fine land.
Its people were good and generous;
My Kalmyks were a fine people.
If you go on like this, my son,
You will dig my pit;
You will boil, my little foal,
My misery from far away.
You will drain my strength;
Unless you cease this wildness,
You will have my herds plundered.
Your wildness knows no measure;
To meet them and strike with clubs,
The sea of Kyrgyz is not here.
My son, we wandered as exiles to Altay;
Your wildness reaches far.
Instead of behaving, you piss
Into my churn filled with kumys!
Wretched son, you will bring me
To utter ruin!
Unless you cease this wildness,
It will stir turmoil in my sleep.
Tomorrow will they not raid
My horses at pasture?
There is no forest to shelter in,
No Ogoy to seek refuge with.
Will the Kalmyks not do violence,
Unless you cease this wildness,
To the wealth I have gathered?
At last you will bring me,
Going on like this, to ruin.
Will they not draw a line through us all,
Unless you cease this wildness?
The Black Kalmyks, the many Manchus,
Will they not put me through wonders?
You will scatter, wretched son,
My gathered san of cattle.
You will have shed, wretched son,
My blood at these Kalmyks' hands.
Stop! You will cut off my life.
Unless you cease these doings,
You will set disaster going!”
Shouting at all the boys,
Jakyp drove them back and stopped them.

“Asking God for a child,
Truly a calamity
Is what I received!
You have not yet reached nine;
Why have you brought so much hell?
You will dig my pit.

The mischief you are doing,

A wild child like you!
You will drain my strength,
You will have my herds plundered,

Karakan's eight sons,

One of us they drove away

The wretched herds you gathered in vain,

If you heed the boys' cries,
They raised the battle cry “Manas!”
On this side was a hut;
If you looked at that hut,
A leader of the people and ten thousand souls
Might fit there with room to spare.
On one side the forty boys,
With Manas leading them, inside it
Could freely play ordo.
A black-spotted leopard
Beside that very child Manas,
Raged at his flank.
A short-tailed, gray-maned lion,
As though to tear Oshpur apart,
Readied itself before him.
His seki side numbered eighty-four;
Wherever he swept was red fire.
Seeing this, Oshpur's
Rib bones trembled,
His shit-filled intestines shook.
He could not help losing his senses;
The handle fell from his wrist.
When he saw Manas's bearing,
The casing fell from his heart.
“This mad son of Jakyp
Will ruin whoever provokes him,
Will leave no opponent unharmed.
Thick-armed, stone-hearted,
An elephant-limbed, dauntless hero,
He has reached his full strength.
Now he will bring great turmoil to the Kalmyks.
One form he has is a lion;
When I look again, a leopard.
He is blood-drinking itself.
Their eyes clouded, the many Kalmyks
Have failed to see him at this time.
He will bring strife to Chin-Machin.
He will ruin whoever he seizes.
This ill-omened Burut
Will leave no opponent unharmed.
He has planted in the ground
His spear with a thickened socket.
It will bring strife to the world,
This child's resourcefulness.
Ah, this son of a wretch,
When he rages and his madness seizes him,
He is a lion who will destroy the Oirats.
Whatever he pleased upon the steppe,
He has gone and done it.
Rich Jakyp's lambs,
Every one alike, leaving none,
He has gone and slaughtered them!
‘Why did you slaughter the lambs?’
If I should happen to ask,
If he catches my hem, he will ruin me,
He surely will not leave me unharmed.
Why should I stay here?
‘Restrain your child,’ I shall say;
Let me reach rich Jakyp!
Unless I bring his father,
And then see what happens,
He will hasten my death,
He will strip me of my means this instant.”
Saying this, warrior Oshpur
Dashed off toward Jakyp,
Charged without sparing himself.
The stallion beneath him,
He whipped it right and left,
Spurred it this way and that,
Breaking the steppe and plain beneath him,
Galloping upon his stallion,
Hurrying and flustering without stopping,
Of those accursed many sheep,
Not counting how many had died.
What could dear Oshpur do?
Hurrying and flustering without stopping,
As though an evil spirit had seized his wife,
As though fleeing with pursuers near,
As though his pursuers chased close behind,
As though his wife had borne a son,
At rich Jakyp's court
Rich Oshpur arrived and dismounted.
Words of calamity and horror
Rich Oshpur poured out as he dismounted:
“Your son Manas has become a warrior.
When I spoke, his ears did not listen.
All those who went to him,
He drove to crouch like hares on the ground.
Forty boys as companions,
Your intrepid child has gathered.
As though wolves had slaughtered them,
He has slaughtered sheep upon the steppe.
There is no soul like your child.
The companion at his side
Is a gray tiger licking its lips.
It is pointless to speak:
A black-spotted leopard
Is your son's retainer.
On the open steppe, the forty boys
Have built huts.
In their midst, your mad son
Has become a sultan.
The forty boys, full of meat,
Race on foot as young horses and shout.
The boys who come in front
Raise the battle cry ‘Manas!’
Your son's name is Chong Jindi;
Who knew his name Manas?
Your son has slaughtered lambs.
All forty boys gathered,
And bestowed the name Manas on him.
You bore no mere child, Chyyyrdy;
You have borne a hero.
One who cannot drink his fill of blood,
You have borne an infidel.
He will not let you turn your plow.
If this son of yours goes on like this,
He will not let us stay in this Altay.
This son of yours will cause trouble;
Spearheads will clash.
He has reached twelve years;
With the Black Kalmyk people
He will fight this very day.
Had you heard of it yesterday?
Esenkan's envoy,
The sorcerer had known:
‘Manas will arise from the Burut,’
And had long been searching.
He was born here in Altay;
He is the very Manas foretold.
Beyond Manas himself,
These are his very watching black eyes.
You, Jakyp, have blazed up,
Turned your thoughts to deceit.
‘My son's name is Jindi,’ you said,
And did not name your child Manas.
Cut your river willows down;
He is going to burn Altay.
Restrain that child of yours!
When he sees a Kalmyk, he corners him;
When he sees a Manchu, he beats him.
I, Oshpur, who tend the sheep,
Am tired of your child's wildness.
There are rumors among the people:
The chief horse herder, great Kanjar,
Even him, your troublesome son Manas
Seems to have killed.
Unless you restrain this son,
The Black Kalmyk and Manchu peoples,
Look at their numbers, like black insects.
The saying is like this:
Seven wolves, one sheep:
If they attack it, will they leave its carcass?
