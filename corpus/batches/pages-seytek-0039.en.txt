“A falcon with talons of steel!”
Overjoyed, Aichürök
Throbbed with gratitude,
And trembled with delight.
Wrapped in pure silk,
Drenched entirely in blood,
From the moment of birth this child
Looked unlike any other:
A black mane down his back
Parted in two and streamed,
And he looked exactly like
His mighty gray-wolf lion of a father.
“His lashes are flame, his eyes live coals;
No child like him was ever born.
Broad-browed and wide-shouldered,
With tender lips and overhanging brows—
Look closely at him from every side!
His nose is so like his father’s nose,
As though one had been molded from the other;
His ears are like his father’s ears—
The very image of that gray-wolf hero.
His black eyes gaze
Exactly like the true prince’s own;
Indeed, they are the measured black eyes
Of the hero himself,
The very eyes of the leopard.
It is as though one likeness were taken from another;
Souls are born in many forms,
But his stare seems to set the heart ablaze,
And seeing him quenches every longing.
He seems to have fallen straight
From the mouth of the leopard-gray-wolf hero.
If my daily grief should vanish,
If he survives and grows,
If he matures and becomes a warrior,
Standing equal with warriors’ sons,
If, though orphaned, he comes of age
And somehow rejoins his people,
Will he find his homeland—
Reedy Кең-Köl and broad Talas?”
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Lay there sunk in worry—
Do you see what happened?
When the constellation Taraza sank
And dawn broke,
The fierce woman’s labor ended;
With the Morning Star overhead,
She gave birth beneath a sign.
When her child was born,
Look at the omen that appeared:
The Sun rose from his right brow,
The Moon rose from his left,
And the two came into conjunction,
Becoming his sign.
Moonlike Aichürök
Had given birth to a son.
See how the child surpassed
Even his father!
If this child lives,
They will call him God’s beloved servant,
The son of the noble gray wolf,
A brave youth left by the champion
Of the famed mountain springs,
A source of strength left by the dear departed,
A talisman left by the hero,
A plane tree sprung from the place
Of the mighty gray-wolf warrior.
Souls will seek refuge beneath that plane tree,
If only he lives to stand.
To all who behold him,
He will be a banner planted before them,
A gift left by the hero,
The hoofprint left by a noble steed,
A brave heir to the ancestral treasure,
My golden palace on the uplands,
A fortress left to me as a gift
By the lord who died far from home,
A golden-hoofed steed
Who races untiring across the desert,
Who plunges through the lake to seize the swan—
