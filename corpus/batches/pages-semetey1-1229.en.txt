As though about to wreak destruction,
His voice deafening the ears.
Külchoro lay sleeping
As Toltoi approached;
He stood erect and untroubled.
Though the boy was a servant,
Almighty God had multiplied in him
Heroism and leonine courage,
Granting them without measure.
He had no tongue with which to speak;
Arbuudan beneath him
Was sibling to Algara
Of Maiden Saikal.
He had raced with wild asses
And contended with thoroughbreds.
Külchoro remained asleep;
Hearing the sound, Taibuurul
Flattened his right ear.
As though he had seen a wolf,
The animal groaned
And flung the crust of black earth
Upon Külchoro.
Still Choro did not wake;
Snorting and choking,
He pounded the black earth's crust
With his feet.
Still Choro did not wake.
He had begun snoring;
Fearless Külchoro
Slept without stirring.
The child-finding monster
Had rested his head upon a stone
And fallen asleep exhausted.
Taibuurul stood alarmed, thinking,
"If hero Toltoi suddenly arrives,
What will he do with his spear?
He will put the twelve-year-old boy
To death."
"Choro knows wickedness;
At tomorrow's gathering day
He will ride me like a black donkey,"
The horse thought, and like one seeing a wolf,
He snorted.
He turned his staglike neck;
When he struck and scattered the earth,
Still the boy did not wake.
The earth split in a mirage;
Toltoi of the Zhediger
Came drawing near.
The matchless horse Buurul
Bit down upon his bit;
The spiritlike Taibuurul
Spun around the spear
That had been thrust into the ground.
Külchoro upon him
Nearly fell from Taibuurul,
Then righted himself.
"Oh, oh, what is the swine doing?"
The boy cried as he awoke;
His small heart pounded,
And all his insides and intestines
Heaved without stopping.
His waist slackened and he hunched;
The young prince Külchoro
Opened his eyes wide.
Sleep scattered and fled;
His eyes opened wide as umach,
And his brow drew tight,
