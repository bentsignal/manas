In Talas, the choicest of lands,
All these delights abound.
When one looks upon a plane tree,
Seeking out the creatures there,
And examines one branch,
A long-tailed shrike
Appears upon one branch;
Looking at another, one finds a hooded crow,
And looking upon another—
A ring-necked jackdaw.
When one looks at one branch,
A great shaggy owl is perched there;
Looking upon another,
A compact sparrowhawk appears.
Upon a single plane tree
Countless kinds of creatures have gathered.
Our wondrous, spacious Talas
Is surely the richest of lands,
And the Kyrgyz and the few Argyn who inhabit it
Are surely the flower of all peoples.
Was it a land where the great Kyrgyz would not
Camp for months on end,
With revelry by day and games by night?
Could such pleasures fail to flourish there?
Wearing costly hats of blue lynx,
Could they fail to sway in dance there?
Playing many games together,
Could they fail to rejoice there?
“This is what life is for,”
Could they fail to think there?
With budlike mouths and camel-calf eyes,
With teasing, alluring eyes,
In crimson dresses, slender-waisted as the crescent moon—
With such graceful beauties,
Could men fail to lie there?
Could they fail to lose themselves
In splendid games and laughter?
Upon the wide, level mountain plains,
Could they fail to make camp?
By day young wives, by night maidens—
It has many a surging stronghold.
There is entertainment in abundance
And every kind of pleasure.
It has oleaster, birch, and willow;
Pines and birches reach toward the sky,
And every kind of beauty is there.
Within those dense woods
There are tawny lions,
And striped-backed, ring-tailed
Tigers as well.
Every creature endowed with life,
Everything the ear has heard and the eye has seen,
Everything people have spoken of, is there.
The world seethes, filled to overflowing;
In our wondrous Talas
All things have come into being in abundance.
Is it not the choicest of lands?
Do not the Kyrgyz who dwell there prosper!
Spacious Talas, our ancestral homeland—
Was it not won by our forefathers!
Ah, what a world this is—
Would any man living here not prosper?
Upon its terraced hills
Wild goats and argali stand;
Within its level forests
Piglets and wild boars graze.
Every creature possessing life,
In our wondrous Talas—
Every living thing is there.
When you look toward the mountains,
There is snow that never departs;
When you look within the forest,
There are goitered gazelles with high throats.
Every creature ever created—
Every kind of living being is there.
Goshawks and other keen-eyed birds
Beat their wings in flight.
Kites and sharp-eyed birds,
Birds poised and ready to hunt.
Its plane trees are as large as houses,
