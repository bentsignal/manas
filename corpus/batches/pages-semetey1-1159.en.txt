Would the Kalmyks not take them and return?
Would the Muslims' pride not be broken?
Would the Black Kalmyks and Manchus not say,
“We have thrown down their khan,”
And would God not utterly strike us?
Before the lumbering Joloy,
I have no trousers on my backside;
How can I go to him with them bristling bare?
Before that hulking Black Kalmyk,
How can I grapple body to body?”
Your dear elder, Khan Koshoi,
Made his meaning known in all these words,
And then your elder Khan Koshoi stood there.
Koshoi of the Katagan
Had been matched against the wrestler:
If he threw him, he would win the prize;
If he fell, he would be left with nothing.
Your elder's lament
The lion Manas heard.
As soon as the leopard heard it,
From atop his saddle,
From beneath his saddle cushion,
Bloodthirsty Manas, that noble lion,
Said, “Let my elder wear these when he grapples,”
The splendid, finely made kandagai
He took out there.
Manas's splendid wife Kanykei,
A strong maid almost like a saint,
Had lined its inside with brocade;
Sixty young wives gathered
And worked on it through the nights.
She had the whole earth searched,
And had mountain goats from the high mountains
Brought at her word.
Fitting it for the hero,
So the mighty man could wear it when wrestling,
She had made it in earnest.
Kanykei, wondrous in every way,
For fully six months
Had it steeped in tanning solution.
She finished all its seams
And wrapped them with silk.
So he could wear it when strength was needed,
She had intended it for your elder.
Young wives cut it and maidens sewed it;
The finest needlewomen sewed it.
The fairy-like Aruuke,
Who had tested five hundred young wives,
And capable kinswoman Kanykei
Had the defective parts unstitched;
They filed down steel
And poured it in, plate after plate.
It wearied the hand that held it;
A musket ball could not penetrate it,
But lodged in its midst.
It bent when grasped;
When its wearer thrashed in fury,
All the wrestlers
Lost their holds, one after another.
Embers could not pass through it when firewood burned;
The magnanimous one's kandagai
Could not be pierced when a cannon fired.
Its waistband was tied securely;
The auspicious kandagai
Had embroidered cuffs.
Its inside was brocade, patterned like lark's eyes.
There among the people,
Prince Manas the leopard
Was now about to give it to Koshoi.
Taking the kandagai in his hand,
Making his dun horse prance,
The noble hero
Now went up to Koshoi.
He turned Akkula's head.
Your elder, old Koshoi,
Your magnanimous blue-maned Manas,
Now stood voicing his sorrows.
