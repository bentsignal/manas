Let me pray for glad tidings;
With no brother-in-law to fight for me,
I have been unable to avenge my dead.
If the Almighty created him with a living soul,
Hear my lament well!
My gift left by my companion,
My treasure left by the dear departed—
If he grows up safe and sound,
How shall I pursue my blood-debt?
When the homeland’s wing has grown strong,
I shall overtake and halt my wandering caravan;
May God let me take revenge
Upon the witless villains!
My blood-price taken as plunder—
If He lets this sorrow of mine live,
If the Almighty preserves
My brother-in-law among the herds and my son,
If those two, safe and sound,
One day mingle together
Like fine white-wheat flour,
And sweep forth
Like hairs from a white gazelle,
Advancing together
Like the Morning Star in the sky;
If the pale youth, white as a horse,
Becomes his friend for the world beyond,
And, setting their unity in one place,
The two should join together;
If they confront
Kanchoro and Kyyaz,
Shouting as they level their spears,
Firing muskets and drawing bows,
And wage a mighty battle!
Look at the wonder
Inherited from his father and forefathers.
Why should I, like some wretched woman,
Keep wailing and shrieking,
Keep sobbing aloud,
Reveal the fire within me to Kyyaz,
And act so rashly?
Will he find his people,
The prosperous Argyn and the many Kyrgyz?
Will he gather the scattered caravans
That shelter beyond every ridge?
If this child of mine survives,
Will he take revenge
On Kanchoro and Kyyaz?
They destroyed my royal camp in Кең-Köl.
The accursed wretches
Mounted my pacing horse at the tether.
They ruined all I had in Talas;
My poor head was shattered,
And I could not redress my grief.
They burned my wealth in Talas—
A curse upon my womanhood!
My poor soul howled and moaned,
Yet I could not redress my anguish.
Today God has shown to my eyes
The son who will avenge me.
If no son had been in this womb,
Who would pursue my blood-debt?
If I cry and keen aloud,
Who would hear my sorrow?
They plundered my livestock
And made my blood flow like water.
Over the yellow-ridged passes
They scattered my Kyrgyz people.
They ground all I had to dust;
Those two who perished in the flames
Seared my very soul.
They braided my hair in five locks
And carried me off as booty.
They dishonored my dear mother
And destroyed the fruit of my plane tree.
They bent my spear at the shaft,
Laying waste and crushing all,
And reduced me to this helpless state.
If Providence blesses those who come after me—
A curse upon my womanhood!—
From the villains I could not redress
My grief.
