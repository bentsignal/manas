Even those renowned throughout the world—
He shattered countless lives,
Leaving them strewn one by one.
The ill-fated brute
Had raised a great storm of battle.
Sarybay was such a lion,
Meeting assembled champions
And standing amid their war cries,
Where heads were hewn off and blood was shed;
He was the lion who strode onto the field.
Striking like a young tiger,
Such was the host that fought and shattered.
The mighty battle raised a tremendous din,
A tumult of ringing war cries.
Against the ranks, vast as Mount Kap,
Against the warrior Khan Sarybay,
He crashed like a thundering mountain,
Rushing to destroy the people
And overrun the whole wide world.
This descendant of a noble lion,
Who never shrank from death,
Was not Sarybay but one man against countless hosts;
Your forefather never retreated.
He rejoiced when told of joy
And burned when told of death;
Told to drink water, he drank poison;
Told to take a cap, he cut off a head;
Told to spill blood, he poured out lives.
He was the offspring of a renowned lion—
Such, I see, was Seytek.
His first forefather had been a khan,
And the forty Chinese tribes had dwelt at ease.
He began the shooting and stirred conflict,
Never pausing to heed any man;
Unable to withstand his courage,
The peoples of the whole world were laid waste—
So the impassioned warrior Seytek
Waited and took his turn,
While his bitter foes fell still.
The noble lion’s descendant
Turned the head of his gray kulan steed
And leveled Manas the lion’s ornamented spear,
Making it thunder as he thrust.
Sarybay was his opponent,
Who had wrought such calamity;
Pouring out his wrath, Seytek shouted
And invoked the sacred spirit
Of Manas, his royal forefather.
He broke through forest and mountain to clear a road
And set his hand to a mountain-sized deed;
His battle cry deafened the ears.
He invoked the spirits of his ancestors
And of the many heroes of former cities,
Calling upon their sacred power,
And Seytek leveled his spear.
Sarybay, son of the ogre,
Knew well his own great strength
And now smiled beneath his mustache:
“I shall snuff out his burning lamp
And send him from this world;
I shall beat the fierce spirit from him.
Now I shall settle his account,
Make the boy scream,
And cut short his fly-sized soul.
I shall wreak boundless ruin
And take as spoil Kulansur,
The steed no bullet can catch.
The brute has run mad;
I shall soak his body in blood
And lay him down to grease the earth.
When he comes near, I shall cut off his head
And crush his liver like sausage.
This swine seeking blood
Has come to me himself,” he said.
“The raging champion’s time
To be slain here
Has now arrived,” he said.
“This young fool doomed to death,
This rabid cur,
Has come here himself,” he said.
