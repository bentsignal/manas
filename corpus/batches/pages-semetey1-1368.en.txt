You have chosen to ride on campaign,
And scattered your inexhaustible treasure
Across the open steppe!
Toward Beijing, the land of no return,
You have ridden out to begin reconnaissance!
You had need of wretched me,
Yet have abandoned your wife in desolation!”
Speaking thus, Aichürök
Screamed and cried aloud,
Sobbing in true grief.
The bones of her ribs seemed to split,
Her heart was grievously torn,
And tears poured from her eyes;
Unable to find her bearings,
The hair down her back grew thin.
Tears streaming from both cheeks
Poured down over her breast.
When she spoke, her words tasted of sugar;
Her black eyes shone like the morning star,
Yet tears flowed from both together.
From her dear eyes, like black currants,
Tears fell a whip’s length.
Her back seemed broken,
And in the wretched woman, radiant beyond mortals,
The form of a holy seer appeared.
The peerless Lady Chürök,
Of that infidel’s surpassing wisdom,
Stood barring the prince’s way.
Her waist a handful, her face like the moon,
What a renowned beauty,
The wretched daughter of the peri!
She turned Buurul’s head.
Twining herself in the reins,
She stood screaming and weeping;
Wrapping herself in the stirrup-leather,
She stood weeping inconsolably.
The splendid hero, born to surpass
His father, gray-wolf Manas—
Could he remain without going to Beijing?
The leopard’s very image—
Could he rest without going to Kakan?
The peerless, broad-chested warrior,
Great in courage and heedless,
Gave no thought at all
To whether he might die today.
Your gray wolf swelled with spirit,
He thundered aloud,
And spoke to Aichürök:
“One of the seven days is death;
Does one prosper by fearing death?
Are there people who never die?!
When the Almighty issues the decree,
When Azrael, who draws out souls,
Stretches forth his five fingers,
The cunning tongue that spoke is stilled;
In this deceitful world,
When death truly comes,
Who can flee and escape?!
Will the dug grave not take us in?
Will the black earth not swallow us!
To know every art,
You have become a saint, poor woman!
You have taken to weeping mightily,
Since you find weeping so delightful;
May your father Akun Khan die,
And your brothers Azhybai and Tümönbai
Die along with him!
May your princely father Akun Khan die;
If you delight so much in weeping,
May all your kin die!
Hear the ceaseless words
Of this ill-fated, fierce woman!
The road of holy war is a great road—
Is it right for a wife to bar it?
The Kakanchin is the foe I shall meet;
When I set out upon the holy campaign,
Can a wife bar my way?!
If this weeping is so fine,
