Showing her distinction,
Led the great-hearted hero
Into the gilded tent.
They spoke in whispers,
And laughed with ringing voices;
They offered arak for thirst,
And gave a handsome feast.
They quenched his thirst with kumis,
And slaughtered in a row forty
Unbred colts brought in the train;
They had fat lambs and unbred colts
Slaughtered together,
And had sweet food and choice meat
Boiled until tender;
They had them gnaw sugar like snow
And sip black tea.
"Let our sultan, who arrived exhausted,
Recover his strength," they said;
Each applied himself eagerly
And set out food of countless kinds.
Glen with springs and sheer cliff—
Herself wise, the luckless one,
Akun's daughter, the bold woman,
Beside the great Ürgönch,
As dusk fell and the sun set,
Had the tents folded
And loaded on every camel.
"Let my king, my princely leopard,
Enter and travel through Afghanistan,
And be immersed in every delight;
The white palace prepared for him—
Let him lie in that white palace;
Let my hero take his fill of affection
And be immersed in great delight," she said.
Fourteen horses bred in Ogoy,
And at the head of the fourteen horses,
A white shaft tied in a ring,
A shaft fit for fourteen horses—
Your formidable sister-in-law Chürök,
Showing her extravagance,
Had swaggering Buurul
Mounted by her attendant.
Your accomplished sister-in-law Chürök
Wore a loose silk robe,
And rode a pacing ambler.
Unnoticed by a single person,
Unseen by anyone,
As dusk fell and the sun set,
When the people were first settling to sleep,
She set out toward Afghanistan.
She arranged a splendid spectacle,
And had fourteen swaggering horses
Led along riderless;
Ahead of the fourteen horses
She arrayed all the maidens,
And to make the bride and young man
Disappear without leaving a trail,
Behind the fourteen horses
She strung them out in a festive procession.
The luckless daughter of Akun
Set her affairs in order like this,
And erased every trace.
The reason she erased the tracks was this:
The fourteen horses were known as racers,
And their tracks might be recognized;
If Chynkozho, son of Shygay,
Should learn of it by chance,
He would begin a fierce shoot-out, she thought,
