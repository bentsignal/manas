He went onward, leaning low;
His forelock tangled and matted,
His splendid head, broad as a basin,
Was covered in red dust.
They lowered their heads toward the earth
And ran in an extraordinary way;
They stretched their necks toward the ground
And took strides beyond measure.
Algara and noble Buurul—
No wind could match their gallop,
No beast could match their tread.
When they reached certain places,
A ground-hugging fog rose like a mirage.
Such was the nature of the racer:
The Bloodthirsty One’s Buurul
Drew near to Algara.
Look well upon the plight of Karagul,
This doomed man here!
His speech was clear when he spoke;
Keen-eyed, he was a judge of horses.
When he saw the speed
Of Buurul beneath Semetey,
He feared a messenger
Of Kakanchin’s Beijing might hear,
Or, if not that dog, a scout
Might come out before him.
He feared they would begin a fierce spear-fight
And separate him
From the champion son of the Bloodthirsty One.
Shouting in fury,
Raising the battle cry, “Kakan!”
He crossed and went beyond
A pass shaped like a saddle-back.
“If my wretched life falls into his hands,
What dreadful thing will happen?”
Thinking this, Karagul
Fled onward in haste—
Karagul, chief of the horse herders,
This one doomed to vanish.
When he saw Buurul’s bearing,
Fearing the raging Burut would catch him,
Fearing he would cut off his head,
He fled onward.
“Will I catch him?” Buurul wondered,
And raced in haste.
Lengthening his stride like an argali,
His eyes reddening like a lion’s,
The land streamed by like a mirage;
The creature ran like a gazelle,
Drew near to Karagul,
And came close upon his heels.
At that time Karagul—
Such was the plight of Karagul—
Longed desperately to reach
The city of the Kakanchin.
Out on the plain on this side,
His heart became deeply troubled;
Karagul floundered along,
Looking as though he would die.
Algara ran sluggishly,
And Karagul suffered bitterly.
His eyes darted wildly,
And his legs began to dangle.
He struck Algara beneath him
From side to side with his whip,
Spurring him on both flanks.
“What has happened to this dog?” he thought;
Afraid for his life and frantic,
Karagul hurried desperately
And spoke to Algara:
“Algara of the accursed khan,
You were the khan’s own horse.
Your condition is not that of a worn-out old beast;
My fly-sized life is under strain,
And this is no time for you to cease running.
Algara, whom I mounted in trust,
You have brought moonless night
Down upon Karagul’s head—
