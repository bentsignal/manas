Does one destined for death not die?
The albasty took a hundred forms,
Does he not somehow live out his day?
The mark of a lionlike warrior,
While life remained, he did not yield,
Never abandoning a single desire,
Onto the broad field bright as the moon,
As if destined to stand so,
Their thrusting spears clashed,
Without closing their thirsty eyes,
They fought face to face,
The warriors would not retreat from the foe,
The heroes would not leave this world easily,
Would the people’s khan bow his head?
Before this furious Chinese Ozu,
Under this crushing humiliation,
Would a hero consent?
Trusting in his honor and fate,
He drew himself up and stood ready,
Speaking many fateful words,
The land became broad for the dawn riders,
He stood and asked for a blessing,
His coming before Ozu Khan,
Against the Chinese standing in their fortress,
He considered what the enemy would yield,
O noble people of the white-capped Kyrgyz,
Hero Kenen stood in the midst,
A wise man in every hard hour,
Of all wise, plane-tree-like warriors,
He opened his palm and gave his hand,
He gave the firm armor no bullet could pierce,
Forged from blue iron,
For the many battles he had fought,
Worthy of a splendid soul,
They clad him in layer upon layer of iron,
All rose in fury and defiance.
Born as a true thoroughbred,
The term of my splendor ended,
They mounted hero Köböy upon it,
Their hands met and clasped,
The spirits of the forefathers,
Bowed before the Creator,
They covered Köböy in soot,
Köböy rode out to single combat,
Riding toward the furious foe,
The dear heroes received a blessing,
He leveled his battle spear,
A soul born with strength drew near,
To sacrifice himself for the dear people,
All that was within his heart,
He inscribed the writing in blood,
The enraged Ozu Khan,
Dear Köböy was courageous,
Facing humiliation in single combat,
Elder Köböy gave counsel,
The spear champion Köböy,
Had three hundred thousand men,
Köböy’s wisdom was legendary,
He had the finest men,
His champions were exceptional,
His warriors were many,
To the fork-necked Akylska,
To Tarkys, who urged on his horse,
From a haze beyond the reach of sight,
From beyond the distant mirage,
The hero came out onto the battlefield,
Upon the face of the yellow steppe,
Köböy rode forth.
His loud cry became a battle shout,
The hero’s courage rose like spring,
Köböy rode forth,
And Ozu Khan saw him.
And surged forward like a rising flood,
Gripped by fury, he swept in,
Scattering the venom of his rage,
Opening his mouth as if to drive off hunger,
He was a warrior with a gray wolf’s mane,
Wondering what would become of Kögöy,
They prayed that God would prosper his undertaking,
To the Creator, the sole sovereign,
They prayed, “God, protect the warrior,”
The watching people trembled and wailed,
They cried out to him in alarm,
As he plunged among the enemy,
“God, preserve the warrior,” they prayed,
The white-kalpak Kyrgyz cried out together,
“God, grant victory; do not disgrace him,”
The helmet that covered his sight,
Was scraped and battered apart,
