His palm was still not filled.
Aychürök, daughter of Akun,
Disparaging the two men,
She now spoke plainly:
"You have courage but no sense;
What can I say, my sultan?
Leave off your foolishness, my prince.
This place is called Opol;
It is no land like Talas.
Your elder in-law is not your own people.
Your village is in Talas;
These Afghans are your wife's kin.
If you trust your elder in-law,
My prince, all news of you will vanish!
When it comes to speaking reason,
Does your elder in-law have words?
When it comes to carrying a matchmaker's gift,
Does your elder in-law have skill?
His tongue is eloquent, but he has no wisdom;
There is no infidel like your elder in-law.
To be given to a prepared bridegroom,
Your elder in-law keeps arranging it;
Every day your elder in-law drives me on,
Saying, 'Marry Toltoy!'
'A blue dress patterned with flowers—
Put it on,' your elder in-law says!
'Toltoy of the Zhediger—
Marry him,' your elder in-law says!
When he goes between sides and makes strife,
Does your elder in-law have sense?
'Toltoy of the Zhediger—
Marry him every day,' he says,
And your elder in-law leads me on!
If I do not go to the Zhediger,
Your elder in-law means to stretch me on the rack;
He means to lead me to Toltoy
And hand me over."
After she had spoken thus,
Azhybay stood and began to speak;
Azhybay, eloquent beyond measure,
Spoke softly but struck hard:
"Chürök, slander has entered your mind;
Do not disgrace me
Before the husband beside you.
You noisy, wretched woman,
What you said is indeed true;
All that you said is true!
To give you to brave Toltoy—
It is true that I arranged it;
Driving you on until breathless—
It is true that I did it!
Because of what has happened,
Because of you, Chürök,
Chynkozho, son of Shygay,
The Kozho forceful in his deeds,
In springtime would not let us
Mount a mare with a flowing mane;
In wintertime he would not let us
Eat a fat-tailed yearling sheep.
He brought turmoil upon us
And came contending for Chürök.
When they laid a foul siege,
And Toltoy of the Zhediger
Slaughtered every person
Who ventured outside,
We stood and raced our horses;
Because of you, Aychürök,
We fired guns and drew bows,
And stood fighting without fleeing!
We held our ground for nine months;
For nine days we fought without rest.
With teeth jutting out,
Horse corpses became a mountain;
With mustaches drooping,
Men's corpses became bundled heaps.
Many formidable beks died;
Many innocent people died.
