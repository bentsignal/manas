Listen to these words you have heard,
Heed me, my son:
May this child come to nothing—
Alas, he appears an enemy to my eyes.’
Those were my mother’s words.
As though your father Manas had sired him,
Your dear mother Kanykei
Carried him without rest for nine months,
As though she herself had borne him.
Had you heeded my words before,
Külchoro, who drank the right breast,
Would have been your inheritance,
A loyal follower of your good counsel;
But your Kanchoro, who drank the left breast,
Is in his heart your enemy—
He appears a foe to my eyes.
Take care, my child, and hear my words:
If you continue raising Kanchoro,
He will become your calamity.
His enmity is revealed,
For from my breast, my foal,
Black blood flows and pours;
At heart he appears an enemy.
If sent against a foe, he will not support you;
Let the wretch go his way—
At heart he will never be your ally.
He is an unripe cucumber and will never grow sharp;
The first omen has appeared:
He is a calamity and will never form a loyal people.
If he lives and grows,
He will mature into a warrior,
Become the equal of heroic sons,
And answer your words with defiance;
Claiming to be your brother, he will fire his gun
And become your enemy.
He will sow discord among your people;
When you confront a battling foe,
He will blacken your face with shame,
Make hollows of your open eyes,
And bring your death upon you.
If this Choro survives,
He will strip you of your honor.
His faith is alien,
And at heart he is your enemy.
Your radiant father had the habit
Of treating his ancestral foes like brothers,
Pitying them as kin,
And so he would die
At the hands of those closest to him.
Find a brother from another place
And make him your support, she said;
This child is not your brother—
Finish him off, she said.
But I would not heed my mother;
Thinking, ‘What can one lone son do?’
I did not keep him apart from my equals.
And now, at the final hour,
This bastard Kanchoro
Has proved an enemy at heart.
My enemy became my brother,
My foe dwelt at my very heart.
Did I go about calling Kanchoro and Külchoro
My brothers?
Has my poor soul flared and burned,
Consumed by one close to me?
Those were the words my mother planted;
She said he had always been an enemy.
When my blessed mother Kanykei
Spoke with care, I disobeyed,
Unable to foresee my death—
And now I am doomed to die miserably.
My own Kanchoro has become my foe;
