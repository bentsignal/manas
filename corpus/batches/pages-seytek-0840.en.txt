He seemed to trample the eighteen thousand worlds
Underfoot like kneaded dough.
When he appeared in all his menace,
He resembled a deep-dug grave.
The infidel’s roaring
Was like a flood bursting from a mountainside.
He rumbled and worked himself into a fury,
Muttering to himself.
His shape shifted strangely;
He seemed ancient-rooted and immovable.
Wits were confounded and hands went numb,
All order overturned and broke apart.
When he charged in wrath,
Setting his threatening gaze and lunging,
He was like a craggy mountain ridge.
The eighteen thousand worlds—
Not merely the world, but every beast,
Every living creature—
He seemed born to drive and crush.
The lower realm was thick and vast,
Yet when the infidel showed his menace,
He seemed able to burst through its very depths.
Every creature that moved
He seemed ready to slaughter and devour,
As though in this wretched world
He alone were destined to hold dominion.
Such was Sarybai and his host.
Whenever he saw an enemy confronting him,
As though their women were seized by nightmare,
His charging foes turned and fled.
When he fixed his gaze upon the world,
He seemed ready to bring slaughter everywhere.
Impulsive in thought and narrow of heart,
Sarybai was a grievous, relentless foe;
Many souls had died in clashes with him—
Such was the strife you would have seen.
He drove the army forward in a mass,
Keeping the ranks together;
Dragging bronze-tipped spears,
He taught the host the art of war.
They marched along the ridge without a murmur,
Struck obliquely without stirring,
And he drove the host without letting it waver,
Keeping it united and unscattered.
He roared like a lion.
The army that heard his fearsome fury
Found no road by which to flee,
No refuge from the misery pursuing them,
No place where life might find ease,
And no greater champion than he.
Sarybai oppressed them.
The accursed, faithless world
Narrowed until it seemed less than a handspan.
Golden heads of singular destiny
Grew old beneath oppression.
We burned, we flared, we became embers;
When quenched like embers, we became ash.
We lived our days beneath oppression,
And thus we came to nothing.
Zhelpinish was the land where I dwelt;
The finest land remained behind.
The finest people became a wretched nation;
If you wish, lament—the people remain behind.
Sarybai, son of Zhelmoguz,
Has he bound my fortune?
Has he broken my spirit?
Has he stripped off my coat?
Has he shackled my hands?
Has he shown me his marvel?
Did he heed the words of the warriors who pleaded?
Driving onward and slaughtering the people,
Has he shown us his marvel?
The lion who devastated all four quarters,
Who, in raging combat, took men’s lives—
True Lord God who created us,
Did You write this swine’s humiliation
Upon your brow as fate?
Many a soul lamented,
Yellowing like grass in the wind;
Many a mortal was lost in thought.
