Chasing the white doe,
And truly had turned her into a cow.
My lion father had indeed
Brought the whole world under his command.
My gray-maned father stirred up strife,
Chased the gray doe and turned her into a cow,
And wherever he appeared, imposed his will.
He shattered the Chinese and opened a road,
Struck down the reluctant and seized booty,
Attacked mighty nations,
Scoured the passes and captured the heights,
And, splendidly arrayed, took many ridges.
He became a banner to his kinsmen;
When he faced a watchful foe,
He became a blazing conflagration.
He was the fortress of the Ala-Too;
Of my poor gray-maned father,
The spiteful enemy who plotted against him
Would ask, “From where did he arise?”
Whoever remembered him was amazed;
He was the people’s great champion.
He slipped an arrow from his quiver,
And whoever came out to exchange shots
With my peerless lion father was crushed.
None who challenged him survived.
His weapons numbered eighty-four,
His stroke was a crimson fire;
None who faced him remained alive.
Whoever confronted him fled like the wind;
Whoever exchanged shots with him was ruined.
The enemies who beheld his majesty
Despaired of their borrowed lives.
When dust spread across the wide plain,
It became the ground for bitter combat.
My devastating gray-maned father
Settled in the heart of Talas
And tethered his fourteen steeds.
Enemies who came out shooting
He drove before him.
When they provoked his anger,
From six ravines he hauled up
Sixty pairs of black camels.
A fearsome tiger was his guardian;
Broad-chested, himself a lion,
No living soul could equal him.
No mortal could approach him head-on,
No enemy could prevail against him;
He readied himself like a tiger,
And none he charged survived.
His supreme courage was like a mountain ridge;
My lion father beyond all reckoning
Seemed able to devour a man in wrestling.
His silhouette was like a fortress,
As though it would swallow any attacker.
His beard was like a grove of plane trees;
When he descended, all seemed shattered—
So exceedingly was he made.”
Thus the warrior Seytek reflected,
His father appearing before his eyes,
His mind bewildered and divided,
And now he stood content.
His first forefather was Tügöl Khan;
Kara Khan never leaves his descendants.
The sharp steel sword truly has a sheath.
The people clamored and raised a tumult,
The nation went about in uproar—
It was no lie but the truth.
May God never take away a father;
May no child be left behind
Without knowing his father alive.
If the Creator is indeed true,
May He never inflict such humiliation
Upon one of His servants.
My heart was troubled with grief;
This time my eyes have seen
My gray-maned lion father,
So I bear no grievance against God.
He is both bloodthirsty and lionhearted;
All twelve limbs of my dear
Gray-maned father are whole.
