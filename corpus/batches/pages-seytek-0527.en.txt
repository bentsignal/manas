Lest it be said that he surrendered him to the enemy.
Shall I settle hidden away,
And rather than go on living thus,
Become prey for the blue vulture?
Without coming to my uncle’s aid,
Shall I turn my tulpar’s head away
And stand there drooping,
Like a man struck by palsy?
This raging Sarybay—
I shall reach him from the flank.
My dear uncle,
Let me become your support!”
He lashed Telkyzyl,
And if you ask about the state of that sea-like hero,
Tears streamed from his two eyes
And dripped upon his chest.
His waist arched firmly backward,
And his boiling eyes bulged.
Your ready-born falcon
Whipped Telkyzyl,
And where the mountain vanished at the pass,
He wheeled Telkyzyl’s head.
The sharp steel spear—
Then the hero Seytek
Gripped it crosswise in his hand
And came on, making the earth tremble.
When he reached the warrior,
The infidel hero Sarybay,
He marked the face of the right kidney,
The place beside the backbone,
Thinking, “Here is the surest place to kill,”
And reached the dreadful, venomous infidel,
The warrior Sarybay,
From the flank.
He thrust hard into his kidney as he passed.
But when his spear struck,
The infidel son of the Monster
Proved surpassingly cunning.
Fixing his eye firmly upon the mortal spot,
Seytek unleashed his fiercest fury.
“He has a powerful arm and deadly venom;
He may suddenly thrust straight through.
It would be shameful if I gave no aid.
If my uncle Külchoro closes his eyes,
We shall retreat along the valley.
We shall become a feast for foreign foes,
Then be driven about like sheep
And become the enemy’s banquet.
If my dear uncle perishes,
Who will brandish the black-edged battle-axe
And wield it without faltering?
Who will lead the many Kyrgyz and Argyn
Without plunging them into grief?
Who will whirl the hawthorn-handled battle-axe
And swing it straight and true?
Who will lead the teeming Kyrgyz and Nogoy
Without casting them into turmoil?
If we lose my lion uncle Kül,
We shall retreat along the valley
And be like a fire gone out.
We shall settle along the hillsides
And be like the moon that has set.
If we lose our mane and tail,
We shall be left maimed.
If the head remains but a horn is broken,
We shall be left hornless.
If one of the horns breaks,
We shall be left powerless.
If one breast is severed,
We shall be left without milk.
If death has come, I shall die;
But if fortune favors me, Sarybay,
I shall slash you and spill your blood.
I shall wage a mighty struggle
And rescue my resourceful uncle
From the hands of the wrathful one;
I shall deliver him!”
From birth, the hero Seytek
Was as mighty as half a mountain.
