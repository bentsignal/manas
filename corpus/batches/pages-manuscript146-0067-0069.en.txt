Again came the wise man’s words,
Thinking with cunning and working your scheme,
Making your false words seem fitting,
It seems you remained defending them,
The stock of the Kara Kyrgyz,
It seems you remained praising them.
You went about with this lie in China,
It seems you intended to give it away,
When the people of China heard it,
You yourself, at the root of it all,
It seems you called it their land,
The force of your words made it overflow.
You are this roving eye of the Kyrgyz.
The work you carried on from its root,
You saw it whole from its appearance.
Weighing your wits concerning the Kyrgyz,
It seems you called it their foundation,
For these Kyrgyz—not merely China—
It seems you called it their land.
You went about ruining the people,
This envoy of the Kakanchi,
It seems you intended to leave.
Your manner is that of one who ruins a people,
Your form is that of a black scourge,
You are one of the thousands beyond counting,
If you stand there sprawling and abandon it,
Your blame will settle upon the Kyrgyz.
If you make Beijing ring out,
Speaking your truth without reserve,
Finding its inside with your hand,
If you make your hand a servant,
From Kyrgyz to Kyrgyz,
The envoy of the Kyrgyz.
Outwardly, your enemy is black,
Your ruined secret is what you destroyed,
You have thrown my people into ruin.
Jeljetpes stood there in anguish,
Lowering his face and yielding,
This vain world is faithless,
Its falseness became known,
Taking truth itself for falsehood,
After the khan poured out his wrath,
Was there any khan who could answer back?
From Tengir’s battle cry and venom,
My thought that has no equal,
Returning again to the true matter,
Grief-stricken and sorrowful,
With no other thought left to pursue,
There stood Jeljetpes.
Lamenting, his eyes shone,
His care divided into countless cares,
Chyng Temir, whose wrath was hell,
Appeared in a fearsome aspect,
Though one begged, no life would be spared,
Though one pleaded, none would remain.
Born of a father and mother,
What created mortal does not die?
