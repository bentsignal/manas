Mist rises from the ravine;
When misfortune is truly driven to fury,
It finds a thousand true devices.
She reads Arabic spells and blinds the eyes,
Turns into a white swan and alters the weather.
Do not call Chürök ordinary,
With all her wondrous powers;
Do not think Chürök insignificant,
So extraordinary is her nature.
The beauty of that unfortunate woman,
Her lovely form, was found in no mortal,
Nor was the precious grace of her heart.
By day she pleased Kyyaz,
Delighting and beguiling his soul;
When night fell, she deceived Kyyaz
By casting a spell upon his eyes.
Thus not the slightest suspicion
Remained in Kyyaz’s mind.
The renowned Aichürök
Stayed beside Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
Flirting with him
Throughout the day.
At night she blinded
The hero Kyyaz’s eyes
And deceived him.
She would lie with her back turned to him,
And every day, disguising
One of the attendants herself,
She would give Kyyaz
One of the young women beside her.
A rivered spring beneath a sheer cliff—
Will it yield its heart when it dies,
Such a wretched creature as that?
The days waned and the months passed;
Since brave Kyyaz had taken Chürök,
Nine months were completed,
And nearly ten months had passed.
Her womb grew ripe and her term was full,
The appointed time and day arrived.
Beside moonlike Chürök
Were her attendants,
Sixty young wives and eighty maidens;
Disguising them as herself,
She sent one each day
Into Khan Kyyaz’s embrace.
Keeping her chaste self pure,
Preserving herself untouched by anything,
Dear Aichürök
Protected herself in this way.
The unfortunate woman possessed sacred powers
And many miracles;
Among women in flowing gowns,
She was a soul who surpassed them all.
The tormented, ill-fated woman
Had a seal upon her tongue.
She could sweep along at a horse’s speed
And fly throughout
The whole wide world;
Such was the magic she possessed.
Only Friday knew of her flight,
Only the sacred night knew of her travels;
When she transformed and flew,
Who could know of that single flight?
She made the open plains her wondrous summer pasture,
Chanting incantations like a sorcerer
And casting blindness over human eyes.
Of all the wisdom granted humankind,
Not a hair’s breadth was lacking in her.
Her curse was dreadful, her venom strong;
Whomever she cursed, she laid low.
Those who saw her majesty trembled;
She knew with perfect mastery
The magic that blinded eyes.
When she transformed into a white swan,
Only the darkening night knew of her flight;
Who else could know that she had gone?
When she unleashed her powers fully,
She turned a summer day to winter
And froze every summer pasture.
