In frantic haste I traveled,
And came through tumult and turmoil.
My beloved hero Sultan—
I have seen the bloodthirsty lion!
When sent ahead, he brought good fortune;
When riding behind, he equaled countless troops.
He wore a blue lynx-fur cap,
And wielded a mottled-blue shield;
He spared no soul who faced him.
His eyes saw from afar;
Amid a great assembly,
He emerged alone from the race.
The mightiest of saints,
The fortress of the blessed lion.
Precious elder Bakai,
A saint who knew the ages,
Your fortunate uncle Bakai—
Behold how great his dignity.
His blessing enriched the soul,
His worth brought radiance to life.
His word was never spoken twice,
And he watched over the whole universe.
He received letters from many lands,
And forty blessings from Kydyr.
He became known as precious Bakai,
And received blessings from men of wisdom.
He taught the ignorant to know,
And set a thousand upon the right path.
My forefather, elder Bakai,
Before the magnanimous lion
Gathered ninety holy men,
And had them solemnly perform the marriage.
Elder Bakai the peacemaker,
After giving the poor daughter of Kaiypchal
In marriage to the bloodthirsty hero,
Like stars in the heavens
They stirred together,
And the two lions embraced,
Lioness and lion together;
Once they had united,
Like fine white-wheat flour
They blended into one,
Like the soft down of a white gazelle
They slipped together.
They sank into delight,
And lay upon one bed;
They settled in a fitting place,
And joined as husband and wife.
Because of a calamitous affair,
Sarybai, son of the ogre—
For all that Sarybai possessed,
Against that infidel son of the ogre
A swung sword could not cut,
A lion’s teeth could not pierce,
And human strength could not prevail.
Because of Sarybai’s wrath,
And the venom he poured upon the people,
Brave Semeñ, son of Manas,
Took Kuyaly, daughter of Kaiypchal,
The champion maiden, as his wife;
Then that champion maiden
Began the fierce combat.
The great epic is a mighty wonder;
Many times they fought.
Of savage infidel Sarybai,
She told the whole tale.
Kaiypchal and the Ogre
Shared the land where grain was sown,
Shared the people in splendid prosperity,
And shared the ground of fold and dwelling.
Speaking of humiliation and hardship,
Kuyaly, daughter of Kaiypchal,
Entered the great dispute.
Into a vast calamity with no end,
Into the turmoil she plunged.
She told of the ridge
Of a pass no mortal could cross.
Of the clash with Sarybai,
Of countless ordeals and great commotion,
She told the whole conflict.
