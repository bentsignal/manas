I am Sarybay!
When I charge in fury,
I shall richly grease my sword,
I shall chew up the whole world, sparing none,
Yesterday you sorely provoked my rage,
I shall lay waste to all, leaving not a hair,
My wrath has risen; I stand rigid,
If you come out to clash, I transform,
If you enter the field, I go mad,
I mangle whoever crosses me,
I am the black scourge itself,
Gnawing whoever I encounter,
I am the black eye of the scourge,
I embody every kind of horror,
When I devour, blood cannot sate me,
When I grapple, I spare no life,
I am the black scourge itself,
You cannot go on swaggering about,
Stirring up quarrels for sport,
Why do you not restrain your courage?
How many troops have you killed?
Why have you roused this madness?
I shall overturn your burden,
My raging frenzy has seized me,
I shall not spare one of you,
I shall enslave your people,
Such is the nature of battle,
I shall destroy your lives,
I shall exhaust every stratagem,
I shall turn aside
Your flowing river,
I shall break your backs,
I shall chill your breath,
If you are a hero, be one like me—
I shall knock the wind from you,
I shall drench your banner in blood,
I shall silence your clamor at one stroke,
If I toy with you, you will not recover,
Your lumbering frenzy will not subside,
I shall master your clever one,
I shall defeat your champion,
The sly Kyrgyz have grown insolent—
I shall settle all their accounts together,
I shall slaughter them, sparing not a hair,
You have provoked my rage, you wretches,
I shall kill many of your lions!”
Thus spoke Sarybay,
Taking the form of a night-devouring fiend,
The swine spoke with a roar,
Shouting menacingly,
“Come here to confront me!” he cried,
Calling out the heroes,
His eyes like the hollows of a lake,
As though he would suck dry any soul he saw,
Such was his splendid appearance,
Whichever way you looked at him,
He seemed like a dragon lying coiled,
Ready to reduce the whole wide world to dust,
Driving it before him and swallowing it,
Tall of stature and broad of shoulder,
A monstrous wrestler, a great affliction,
Dark and formidable, fit to face a hero,
He taunted the army and called them out,
Standing there and speaking these words,
When you looked at Sarybay,
He seemed to constrict the age itself,
And make precious heads grow old,
As though in this wretched world
He alone would become its master,
As though he would drive and devour
The entire wide world,
Crushing every feeble soul,
The madman fancied himself
Ready to slaughter them and unleash conflict,
As though, with none left to oppose him,
He would become the master
Of all eighteen thousand worlds,
Such was the madman's state,
As though he would shatter the whole world,
Driving all before him and slaughtering them.
