Your headstrong wildness is worst of all;
Unable to settle your quarrel,
You have come here.
Leopard Manas, the bloodthirsty lion,
When he reached the age of twelve,
Moved here
And found a home in bushy Talas,
And found the forty Kyrgyz tribes as his people.
When he came and settled in Talas,
The whole populace gave everything
To Manas, Jakyp's only son.
When Manas came to Talas,
He kept a son as a hostage.
From the khan of the Kakan people, from Beijing,
A khan named Alööke arose.
Not a soul remained to answer him back;
People died from his wrath.
Sparing none of the people from tribute,
Alööke kept exacting it.
Whoever incurred his wrath,
Alööke seized and, like a kid goat,
Cut him down.
Whoever answered him back,
He had dragged along the road,
Made a feast of him for the Dangyt,
And had him ripped apart.
In a pitch-black dungeon
Alööke kept him imprisoned.
Whoever defied him,
He cut straps from his back
And kept flogging him.
Such were Alööke's deeds:
When anyone went to Alööke,
Every person who went
Paid homage and bowed.
Alööke's deeds
Became clearly known
To magnanimous Sultan Manas.
With Khan Bakai and Manas at their head,
When they launched an assault
Against Alööke,
And heard reports from those who came,
Brave Manas went to meet him
And, they heard, burned everything down.
All his possessions were scattered;
Scorched by the fire, Alööke
Fled toward Beijing.
From where he settled, Alööke
Overran broad Kashgar.
When he reached Great Aksy
And crossed Kum-Aryk,
At the edge of broad Ak-Chiy,
Alööke posted his sentries
And made camp.
Brave Manas bore down on him
From above
And scattered all four kinds of his livestock.
He caught up with him and seized him,
And slaughtered him as a sacrifice.
Saying, “Manas slaughtered my father,”
Alööke's son Kongurbai
Came to bear a grudge
Against noble, magnanimous Manas.
Manas was such a hero:
For the mere handful of Muslims,
Magnanimous Manas the leopard
Was a fortress that protected them.
What twelve ages had never wrought,
Ruining Sultan Manas did;
In that bygone age,
Not a stick for a horse-catching pole remained.
Manas was such a hero;
His fortune was complete.
When one looked within him,
He was broader than the face of the earth.
A hero like Manas,
For the Muslim people—
