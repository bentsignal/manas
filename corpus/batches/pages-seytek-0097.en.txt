I shall go before the khan,
I shall ask him for clothing,
Whenever I think of the brave hero,
I burn in fire and flame.
For you herdsmen,
I carry firewood,
I am your captive brought as spoil,
I am your slave who gathers wood.
Do not torment me; heed my words,
I beg you, give me a horse,
Accept these sorrowful words I speak.
If you deem my plea wicked,
Drag me along the road
And hand me over to the Dangyts.
If you deem my plea unjust,
Then put me to death.
If you pity a slave,
Heed what I have said;
If you count me as a human being,
Lend me the strength of one horse.
I am your captive held in bonds,
I am a slave within your hands.
I am yours to do with as you will,
I am a man in your own hands.
I am nothing but a captive slave,
Black Khan's dark-eyed son.”
When he heard these tidings,
Karagul, chief of the herdsmen—
There stood the sly wretch,
The forceful Karagul,
Who from his khan, brave Kyyaz,
Had taken his example.
Exceedingly wrathful Karagul
Grew furious and made ready;
The accursed madman
Confronted him in a rage:
“Look how this rabid, cunning slave
Has run mad!
His armor once weighed eighty-four,
Wherever he struck, red fire flared.
While the doomed man still had strength,
He was a mass of glowing embers,
He was a bullet forged of steel.
He was the Kyrgyz people's crimson banner,
And uproar among their enemies.
When he drew breath, he could burst the gall,
A silent, deadly crimson poison.
Now his poison is spent and his strength gone,
His power is exhausted and his blood drained;
All strength has left me, Külchoro.
The armored coat with golden collar and copper sleeves
Has been stripped from my back.
The host that wrought slaughter at my cry
Has been taken from Külchoro.
My flesh is but a blue scrap,
My blood no more than a spoonful;
I have no voice with which to shout,
No fistful of hair left to bristle,
No tongue with which to speak.
Accursed world, ever stalking me with disaster!
I have no waist on which to bind my belt,
No refuge where I may stand firm,
No dark, rugged mountain,
No black cliff to circle,
No mountain on which to lean,
No strength with which to strike,
No power left to fight.
Once we brought the whole world to heel
And dealt doom to all we met—
In former days we were heroes.
Now cut off from the living,
Our shoulders gouged on either side,
We gather firewood as slaves,
Unslain, we live as captives,
Poor wretches wandering astray.
Let the lord show mercy—
That is why we beg for a mount.
If your valor is true,
You will heed these words
And lend me the strength of one horse.
