Karagul separated the stallions
And took them from the herd;
Karagul had performed
A merciless deed of daring.
Within that herd,
Mares were left without stallions,
And yearlings were left without their sires.
He settled the coursers
On the slopes of Kyr-Kayyn,
At the edge of Suuk-Tör,
And drove the horses away separately.
Taking them where they could not be seen,
To a spur of the distant mountain,
Into the hollow of Kyr-Kayyn,
Karagul brought and left them,
Then halted in that place.
“I had forty horse herders;
He burst in and destroyed them!” he said,
Lamenting what had happened before.
“Semetey, son of the Bloodthirsty One,
If I do not dig your deep grave,
If I do not make your misery boil,
If I do not club you in the water
And show some marvel
To the orphan son of Sultan Manas,
I will take with my sword
The head of that bastard champion,
Drag him from the water,
And make him fit for the Danggits.
I will cut off his head, tie it securely,
And go to hero Kongurbai
To claim the glad tidings.
If I do not perform such a feat,
May I sink away as though never born;
May I die rather than live!”
Saying this, he worked himself up,
Turned wrathful and transformed,
Girded on a sword without a sheath,
And like a cloud about to rain again,
His face turned wrathful.
He sped down into the valley
And raced along the water.
Let us leave him this way
And begin the tale of the rash Semetey,
Who was being swept down the river.
When he had emerged from the water before,
His strength was spent and his breath gone;
When he opened his mouth to say “Ah,”
Water poured out by the wineskin-full.
His power was exhausted and his strength gone,
And the beast like Arbuurul had gone from beneath him.
Deceived by Karagul,
The rash youth had been left in this state.
At the mound by the circling water,
He halted at the mound.
When he had gathered his senses,
He cried out like his mother
And lamented Taibuurul:
“Ah, this accursed, treacherous world!
Will I die in the water?
Will I, this wretched bastard, remain bereft—
Will I ever see Taibuurul?!
Ah, my beloved Taibuurul,
Joined with me when I was no bigger than a louse,
United with me when I was no bigger than a thumb,
Met when I was no bigger than a tick,
My thoroughbred ridden into battle!
Külchoro and Kanchoro
Are my two brothers from the same nest;
They joined what had been severed
And gathered what had been scattered.
Alas, my kinsman Bakai, in this woeful hour!
There is no thoroughbred like Taibuurul
For me to catch and ride here;
There is no kinsman Külchoro or Bakai
To come to my aid.
One seen today is gone tomorrow—
Is this what you are, filthy world!
