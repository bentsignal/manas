As they humiliated her,
They inflicted a terrible ordeal
Too dreadful for human eyes;
They committed such violence
As no living person could bear to witness.
Your poor, fierce-hearted mother,
Once upon a time,
In a time long past,
Had a proudly arching brow;
But when the hero’s eyes closed,
Her hope of seeing daylight grew faint.
The hair at her nape was like grass;
The ill fortune amid all her joys
Was too heavy for a camel to bear.
The hair at her nape stood stiff;
The ill fortune amid all her joys
Towered a spear’s height.
Kanykey moaned,
Sobbing like a camel for its calf.
“This is the khan’s decree,” they said,
And the forty champions who received the command
Rushed upon her like players fighting for the goat.
Her ribs and her poor waist,
In the champions’ hands,
Were crushed and shattered.
She had no strength left to cry “Ah,”
No sense left to answer “Yes”;
Your mother shrieked and wept.
Seeing it, the common folk—
The dear Argyn and Kyrgyz people—
Lamented and cried aloud.
When they saw your mother Kanykey,
The birches wept and the willows wept;
Every living soul who looked upon her wept,
Strong warriors wept,
And the murmuring multitude wept.
All who were weeping
Wept as though consumed by their own grief:
“Kanykey was his mother,
And Kanchoro was her son!
The ominous dream has come true;
Never in all the ages
Has a man so disgraced his mother!
What a black-hearted creature!
The people cannot endure such a khan.
He had his own mother dragged like the goat in kökbörü;
Now our strength is gone.
My dear, a villain who does this to his mother
Can never mount the throne again.
He brandishes and wheels his spear—
That black-hearted slave
Will utterly ruin the people.
He will surely unleash turmoil;
At the slightest move, he will
Slaughter all these people.
Sparing no one who stirs,
My dear, he will revel in massacre!
When that savage dog unleashes his assault,
He will slaughter
Everyone who so much as stirs.
He is a witless brute beyond measure;
A villain who treats his mother this way
Will spare no khan.
He will sow rebellion and begin strife,
And if he quarrels with
All these people, young and old,
He will surely destroy them.
And when madness seizes him again,
He will leave not one person alive
But slaughter them all.
He will scatter all the Kyrgyz;
My dear, if a dreadful enemy attacks with a war cry,
He will expose the Kyrgyz to ruin.
This accursed Kanchoro
Is wickedness itself,
The black eye of all strife.
He is a living torment;
Unable to find a worthy challenge, he ravages the land,
