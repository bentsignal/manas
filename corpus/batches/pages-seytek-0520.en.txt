Marking out the place of death,
The wonderfully vigilant hero
Külchoro stood waiting,
And against the infidel Ogre’s son
Thrust his spear upon reaching him.
As though he had punched a stone,
The noble man brought all his strength;
Its steel nature became evident:
The ornamented spear did not break,
But bent like a tent prop.
The wretched son of Almambet
Revealed himself a true hero,
Supremely vigilant and blessed with fortune,
His courage like a fortress of steel;
None who clashed with him could survive.
The ornamented spear had a steel core;
Thus he kept thrusting it.
If you ask about that ornamented spear,
It was designed and tempered for war.
Bölökbai, planning for battle,
Had fashioned it, poor man.
He hardened and twisted its shaft;
The master craftsman Bölökbai
Fitted and finished it beautifully.
He had iron drawn through its core,
And firmly increased its measure.
He edged it like a wolf’s tongue,
Lest by some chance in close combat
It slip from the hand,
And adorned it with lacquer.
Every bard sang of it,
And every discerning man listened.
If you ask about that ornamented spear,
When its charcoal was prepared,
A whole forest on the green plain was felled.
When its forge was fired,
A tribute bull of the Kyrgyz,
A blue young bull, was slaughtered.
He tempered its steel core,
Fashioning it expressly for battle,
And Külchoro drove it with tremendous force.
Raging and straining,
He again took in hand
The cast-iron head as large as a house,
And struck with that iron head.
Seizing the ornamented broad-mouthed arrow,
Sarybai shot a hundred shafts,
Summoning all his courage.
He made the age itself constrict,
As the madman stood thus.
Whipping the dun kulan-horse,
That capable servant, fitted for war,
That powerful servant who knew
All the stratagems of the foe,
Fortunate Külchoro—
Was he not the warrior who had struck down
Not only Sarybai, but the world?
Had he not subdued
And conquered the whole world!
His eyes saw from afar;
He was wiser than multitudes,
And stood unmatched in a solitary race.
A supreme hero, master of the spear,
The tiger-warrior and sage
Who truly knew the enemy’s stratagems.
He was a champion apart;
Behold, the gray wolf Choro bore
The weight of Mount Kap.
Sarybai faced him
And struck with wild abandon.
When Zhelmaian’s head was turned,
And Sarybai swung his mace,
Külüstön, upheld by the ancestral spirits,
Did not flinch at its ringing blows;
He valued Sarybai’s attack
No more than the hem of his robe.
He neither swayed nor stirred;
The poor hero upheld by the ancestral spirits
Did not move even when his gear was spent.
