In the desert of Medina,
On the shore of Jan Baspas,
I remained there for three years.
Then I turned and came back,
And was parted from the multitude
That had sat there as my protection.
In this black cave of the local sorcerer,
Eighty maidens are my soul-companions,
And I live in their company.
Once I ruled a people and maintained a nation,
But my greatness remained over there;
Unable to hear or see,
I suffer here in misery.
A dry tongue offers no remedy,
And there is no power by which to escape.
My ears are deaf and my eyes are blind;
I, a dear warrior, have nearly withered away.
My coat has become a bed of torment;
All my hands and feet
Are bound by a spell.
I am not dead but alive;
Captured by a sorcerer,
I lament, crying, ‘O Mother!’
But speaking is of no avail.
Unless my valiant mother comes,
There is no other remedy.”
Having said this, Semetey
Turned his gray-bearded mouth,
Faced toward the qibla,
And shook off his grief as he lay down.
Raging and convulsing,
“My dear mother Kanykey,”
He pleaded with Kanykey
And opened his mouth as wide as a cavern.
To escape from the sorcerer,
He stood asking to be made whole;
From his poor, fierce mother
He stood asking for her milk.
When Semetey opened his mouth,
“Of my dear, fierce mother,
Say, ‘My mountain-like elder brother has died;
The people have seen the true place
Where the nation cannot be destroyed;
Remain and mourn him thus once more.’
If milk flows from my mother,
Your moonlike brow will brighten,
Your pure heart will glow,
And your face will shine like the moon.
Crying, ‘My twin, my elder brother, has come,’
The fire within you will subside,
And your painful sorrow will ease.
If my valiant mother is alive,
Let her come and stand face-to-face with me.
Let her keep kindling the whetstone,
Let her perform the offering.
Let her roll up her sleeves,
And let my mother make her prayer.
Tell her to stand before me,
Her heart melting,
Her flesh and nails stretching toward me,
Her tiny soul crying out,
Pleading, ‘My only one,’
And sobbing as she makes her prayer.
If her breasts soften and milk flows,
Then God has granted you favor.
I shall clearly see with my eyes,
And truly know myself.
Like a star in the heavens,
I shall roll along like a plump sheep
And join you.
The reason I can join you is this:
I am not dead but alive,
One of those who have not died.
The sorcerers led me astray
And carried me away.
I was parted from my people and gone;
In Jelpiniş beneath the earth
I remained here for three years.
Of the pleasures of this world
I had my fill long ago.
