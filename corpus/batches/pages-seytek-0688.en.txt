Against the infidel huge as a mountain,
Against that hero Sarybai,
He went forth to provoke battle,
To fight doggedly for six full months,
And grapple in a bloody struggle;
Ready to die if true death had come,
And, if the true God so willed,
To surrender his fly-like soul.
He mounted a courser swift as water;
The wretched defender of his people
Knotted the gray horse’s tail tightly.
He prepared himself in fury
And began the bitter combat.
The lion Choro bound his precious life
As lightly as a single hair and cast it away.
The one charging was Sarybai;
Such was the field of battle.
He did not look around him,
And spared his life not at all.
He did not glance from side to side,
And cared little for his life.
Against the infidel, son of a monster,
Against the hero like a racing camel,
Whose final hour had come,
Against hero Khan Sarybai,
He rode out and made ready.
He drove Surat forward with his thighs,
And took in hand a fearsome spear
With a steel shaft and a keen point.
The one he would face was Sarybai;
Your valiant one spared not his life.
He shouted, “Baabedin!”
And raised the battle cry, “Manas!”
“May God set this deed aright;
My father, Manas the Destroyer,
Protect me upon this path!”
He shouted, “Manas! Manas!”
And called, “One God, Creator of all,
Protect me!”
Now your valiant one attacked.
The other had driven and devoured
The whole wide world.
He had taken heroes of countless kinds,
Taken innocent peoples,
And destroyed many mighty champions,
Yet even then that swine’s
Single belly had not been filled.
Of the mortals upon the earth,
He had left not one alive.
He had turned his face toward conflict;
Everyone who grappled with him
He gulped down like a dragon,
Devouring the whole world.
All four corners of the earth
Had grown feeble, unable to endure
The swine descending upon them.
He awaited whoever faced him;
When he roared in fury,
The black earth trembled
And smoldered through its depths.
In his rage, the mad brute
Would wrench his battle-gear’s mouth aside.
The creature was no son of man;
He would crush the enemy to dust.
Külchoro attacked the mountain-sized dog,
For he was a lion beyond compare.
The one he would fight was Sarybai;
Such was the foe he faced.
Sparing not his renowned life,
Your wretched, valiant kinsman
Faced the infidel huge as a mountain,
The hero in the fullness of his strength,
Who gnawed apart all he encountered
And swallowed every living soul.
No one who came to face him
Had escaped with life.
He came to wreak ruin in combat
And wrest his people
From the hands of a blessed nation.
