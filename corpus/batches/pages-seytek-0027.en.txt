Thus her labor came upon her.
On the sacred night of Friday,
During the day of Thursday,
Full labor came
Upon moonlike Chürök,
The wise and wondrous woman.
Her spirits failed and her flesh ached;
Labor came upon Chürök.
Blood came as the sign of the child;
Seeing the sign, she cried aloud,
And Aichürök’s strength deserted her.
Her back throbbed with terrible pain;
All her sixty joints and forty-one bones
Ached without a single exception.
Whenever Chürök sat down,
Both her dark thighs hurt;
The poor woman’s bladder also ached,
Leaving her unable to move.
Her whole body hurt,
Her most intimate parts were in pain;
Her labor became a terrible affliction.
She screamed and cried aloud,
Sobbing because she could not endure it.
Her ribs seemed to split apart
And tears poured from her eyes;
Cruel, accursed death
Seemed close before her eyes.
Sobbing, the woman Aichürök
Spent her frail, fly-like life,
Screaming and crying aloud:
“Show me the brave boy left by my khan,
The heir left by the bloodthirsty hero,
The banner fastened to the spear,
The gift bestowed by the betrothed,
The hoofprint left by the noble steed,
The brave boy left by the falcon—
O God, show him to me unharmed!
Make this grievous pain subside,
Brighten this mirror-like face!”
Her spirit was sorrow and her heart was pain;
“Let this burning anguish subside,” she prayed,
Seeking a remedy from Allah,
The unfortunate woman beseeching God
For the child within her womb.
“Grant him to me,” she pleaded;
“What will he endure
Among bitter enemies?
If my appointed days end and death comes,
What will become of him when I die?
How shall I ever see
The renowned young gray wolf,
Born of my blood and dark eyes,
The brave boy I have longed for,
The hero destined before me?
Alas, countless worries have torn apart my mind
For the child sprung from my loins.
Without letting the enemy suspect,
Can I entrust him to someone’s care?
If they use force without warning,
If they suddenly commit violence,
Will they fear the watching eye?
Will they act as enemies
To the brave boy left by the lion?
Will they kill him without delay,
Before he reaches maturity?”
Countless cares would not fit within her breast,
Her tears streamed in a thousand rivulets;
The poor woman wept all the more.
The reason she wept
Was that she had been taken as spoil;
The unfortunate woman wept in sorrow
Because she had been given an evil name.
The reason she wept was this:
She had no strong protector,
No secure place to shelter,
No forest into which she could flee,
No Nogoy people to defend her;
Her misery was beyond measure.
She had no renowned Kyrgyz people,
Mighty as mountains, to be her hope.
