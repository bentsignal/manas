The matchless champion—
The Creator who gives life
Had fashioned such a hero.
A black-mottled leopard,
He looked ready to rend flesh at once;
A compact, gray-maned lion,
With a fearsome tiger at his side—
No such being would soon appear again.
Behold his heroism:
If he charged straight ahead,
Sixty black camels
Could not match his hard courage and fierce wrath;
He looked able to wreak slaughter upon the Kalmyks
And take every one he met,
Letting not a soul escape.
Among mortal men,
All kinds are born.
Seen at his full height,
He was like a champion bearing a standard,
Like a commander ruling a nation.
His body was like a mountain ridge;
The boy’s mighty vigor
Was like the blast from a forge’s mouth.
The boy’s splendid form
Was like the crest of a high pass.
The sockets of his two eyes
Were like hollows in marshy water;
Whomever he charged, he looked ready
To seize, fold up, and swallow whole.
His broad, spacious forehead
Was like the sweeping slope of a mountain;
His stature seemed half a mountain—
Now Choro saw how splendidly he was formed.
When grieving Külchoro
Caught sight of the boy,
His whole body became glowing embers,
His eyes became burning fire;
Early words came only late,
And at the prince’s young son
He stared with eyes wide in wonder.
The boy was like the crest of a middle ridge;
If merely stirred,
He looked ready to devour whoever faced him.
He was a lion of heroic build,
A man who could subdue the whole world,
A warrior surpassing other men.
No one could measure himself against his strength;
In battle he looked ready to devour men.
He was hard as iron;
When he charged, he looked ready to devour a soul.
His body was like a planted plane tree;
The son of that peerless man
Could not rest without wreaking slaughter.
His surge was like the rush of a lake;
When he spoke at length, he flamed.
Fire poured from his mouth;
His sharpness showed
Like unyielding black steel.
He had the look of one who would advance
Against countless troops.
He had the look of one who would cast havoc
Upon all who grappled with him.
Subduing whoever challenged him,
Bringing disaster upon whoever fought him,
Throwing all who saw him into tumult—
Such havoc he seemed destined to wreak.
Not merely Kyyaz, but all the peoples,
The Chinese host and the hosts of Rum,
He looked destined to conquer.
Upon his enemies, he looked destined
To cast terror and turmoil.
He crouched like a lion;
If he faced those who came to blows with him,
He looked ready to slaughter them all.
His broad ears were like shields,
His bright eyes like morning stars—
Would any mortal dare face him?
Against the hero’s son,
Could anyone who faced him remain alive?
His body was straight as a plane tree.
