I shall break open its mouth with an axe.
My people, let me slaughter the cattle;
For valiant Lady Churok,
Let me hold her wedding today.
Gather livestock from every quarter;
Let the wealth from my hands be spent.
Today perform my marriage rite with Churok.
Today we shall eat meat from one dish
And face each other in one bed.
Today I shall scatter all my livestock;
For the noble, unfortunate woman,
Today I shall spend all my poor means.
Upon the broad plain,
For this unfortunate woman's wedding,
My people, know this today:
I shall slaughter a thousand dark mares,
I shall slaughter a thousand blaze-faced camels.
Let the poles bend today;
Let champion warriors joust today!
Let poles break and lashings fall;
At renowned Churok's wedding,
Let heroes fall and lashings drop.
Let the strongest wrestlers come forward,
Wearing yellow leather trousers;
Let them seize each other by the cuffs,
Let them wrestle on foot,
And let the victor become rich.
Let no tale remain that she was a woman taken as spoil;
Let no such talk remain among the people.
Let no resentment remain that she was a plundered woman;
Let no such grudge remain within.
Let all the peoples think,
'It was as though he took a maiden in marriage.'
Let no other thought arise.
Let them say, 'Khan Kiyaz was a leopard warrior;
Such was his intention.
When he took Aichurok,
Such was the wedding he held.'
To all the gathered souls,
To children chasing butterflies,
To old men coughing often,
To the senile who cannot come to the feast,
To old men remaining at home,
Let them say, 'This was hero Kiyaz's desire;
Such was his intention.
The dear ruler of the people—
Such was the feast he held.'
Set aside excessive talk;
Take whatever you desire.
Hold fierce games and great wrestling;
My people, this is what I desire!"
So spoke hero Kiyaz.
Because of what had happened,
Because of Aichurok,
He gathered the people behind him;
Driven by his longing for Aichurok,
The multitude of Zhediger
Heard that the khan's decree had come.
The poles bent,
And champion warriors jousted.
Broken poles piled like a mountain;
They straightened their poles in their grip,
Thrusting them out at full length,
Boasting to one another,
And clashing fiercely in the joust.
When their poles met,
They shattered at the middle with a crack.
At the great feast held by Lord Kiyaz,
The multitude of Zhediger
Rejoiced and bustled.
Wrestlers fell, propping themselves up;
Men were thrown from their horses.
Those thrown and those who threw them
Stripped one another bare,
Plundering each other completely.
They seized each other by the trouser cuffs;
Heroes and wrestlers grappled,
Putting forth all their former strength.
