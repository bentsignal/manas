"If only that dear young man would come,
So we might see him to our hearts' desire;
If only we could embrace the young man once,
And then afterward die," they say—
Thus your maidens go about wishing.
"There are many souls among our people,
And many boys of twelve;
They cannot manage to put on their clothes,
Nor cover their hems when they urinate.
Even now those boys
Feel no shame before their mothers.
The red flower has faded," they say;
"The tender young sapling
Has journeyed toward Judgment Day," they say.
The maidens, distraught, wailed,
Crying out as though a husband had died.
His white steel gathering rust,
The practiced horse Buurul
Would not yield to the Urgench,
But strained toward the heart of its current.
His blue steel gathering rust,
Having crossed many lakes and rivers,
The seasoned horse Buurul
Strained toward the place where the waters converged.
His front teeth flashed;
Without veering up or down,
The practiced horse Buurul
Swam straight across through the roar.
Over the champion, noble as a prince,
White foam streamed visibly;
That white foam thundered,
Surged over the young man's head,
And poured down.
When they saw that foam,
The many maidens watching cried,
"Now he truly is dead,"
And tears poured from their eyes.
Kulchoro, upheld by holy spirits,
Upon the red horse Buurul,
Came on with growing force before their eyes.
As he gained strength, the maidens rejoiced
And wailed, crying, "Baabedin!"
Just as he drew near,
Another marvel appeared:
Waves gathered as large as houses,
Colliding with one another,
And rushed down in fury.
When they were about to engulf him,
About to pass over him and bring death,
Your champion, Almambet's son,
Raised his spear;
The wave that came from ahead
He thrust away before him,
And the wave that came from behind
He swept away behind him.
Like one thoroughly skilled,
Thus Kulchoro came onward.
Nine spirits supported him;
It was the moment they held him up.
Nine spirits upheld him;
It was the hour they took him under their protection.
There in the water, the naked Khizr
And young Aydar took the young man
By his horse's reins.
The forty chilten and Khizr Ilyas
Loudly cheered and urged him on.
The giant black bird, the Simurgh,
As though to carry the young man
Away from within the water,
Stretched down its talons
And came swooping upon him.
The splendid horse Buurul
Swam unwaveringly through the Urgench.
I could make myself a sacrifice for that steed!
There is a circling white cliff and a deep pool;
If Arbuudan should drift downstream,
There is a bank where death is certain.
Buurul knows that as well.
