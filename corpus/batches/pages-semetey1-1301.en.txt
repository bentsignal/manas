Until the flower of divine law flies,
I shall fight firmly for six months.
While my able head remains,
How could I give up my sister-in-law?
With that earth-scorching dog,
Let me grapple neck to neck.
Able uncle, what are you saying?
Let a dog listen to these words!
My valiant mother Kanykei
Entrusted something carefully;
These were my mother’s words:
‘A single bullet left by Manas
I sewed into the collar
Of Akolpok,’ she said.
Binding it back and fastening it,
When we returned this way,
My dear mother Kanykei
Entrusted it to Akolpok.
As we came this way,
She grasped my hand and held me,
My beloved mother,
And, saying, ‘If grave trouble befalls you,
Remember your father,’ entrusted it,
Binding and securing it again;
My beloved Kanykei
Entrusted it to orphaned me.
When we lived in Talas,
We herded horses at Kanjailak,
And shot the soaring vulture
Before it could reach us.
Once I came blazing
Into this world;
With the gun left by my father,
I had the skill of a marksman.”
From the hard-pressed sultan,
Young prince Külchoro
Asked for Akkelte,
Striking him with words like these.
Your valiant prince and sultan,
Unable to say, “I will not give it,”
And not wishing to thwart the hero,
Dismounted and gave him Buurul,
Unfastened and gave him the treasured gear.
Semetey gave them to the hero.
Look at the form of that desperate man:
In his Kokon mottled coat,
He ran crouching along the road,
His knees bending to the ground;
The prince did not stop but ran,
Flaring in fury,
The prince fleeing in great strides,
Surpassing his former valor,
Running straight ahead,
And hiding in a black cave.
When he reached the black cave,
The dear hero born to surpass
His father, gray-wolf Manas,
Hid his whole body,
Sticking out his head as large as a hearth,
Hiding his dark body,
Sticking out his head like a crow.
As hail poured down,
Streaming from Chynkojo,
Despairing of his fly-like life,
True Manas’s only son
Took shelter in the black cave.
Hero Semey, son of Manas,
Was hidden from sight,
Concealed in the black cave,
His whereabouts unknown.
Having gone to hide in the cave—
Such was Semetey’s ordeal;
His poor life had come under strain,
Else would a hero act this way?
Now let us tell
The tale of Külchoro:
A servant perfectly skilled in judgment,
Strange and singular from childhood,
