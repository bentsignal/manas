Whatever anyone said,
He paid it no heed;
He took no notice of people's words.
“He truly is Chong Jindi,”
The Black Kalmyks and Manchu people said,
Making him their laughingstock.
He did not hurry about like other children;
Until he reached the age of five,
Your valiant Manas did not walk.
Chong Jindi was the name they called him;
Who knew his true name was Manas?
The Black Kalmyks and Manchu people
Called him “Jakyp's crippled son.”
Whenever the Kalmyks saw him,
They kept on mocking him.
Peerless Manas, wild and reckless,
When he reached the age of five,
Drew his feet beneath him and walked.
From seven years to eight,
When the boy had grown,
He became a burning ember;
Among the forty Kyrgyz households
There was no such unruly child.
He played with the other children;
Once that warrior began to play,
He would not stop his games.
Of the children who played with him,
Many were left crawling.
If he seized them in anger,
The children shrieked and screamed.
If he truly gripped them hard,
They crumbled like ground grain.
One day Manas the leopard,
Unable to restrain his excitement,
Gathered forty boys from forty households,
All those precious children together.
On the plain of Great Altai
They gathered and began to play.
These sons of forty Kyrgyz households
Played their boisterous games;
Valiant Manas was among them,
Distinguishing himself in play.
From the Black Kalmyks and Manchus,
Aged fifteen or sixteen,
Somewhere between those two ages,
Eighty boys arrived.
Among those boys
Was the Gray Kalmyk's eldest son,
Both headstrong and bold.
Those eighty boys behaved wickedly;
Calling them “fucking Buruts,”
They humiliated the children.
Then those boys cried out:
“Wandering Buruts who have come here!
These children of the Buruts—
Crush them and wipe them out!”
The eighty boys charged,
Setting upon the forty;
The forty children screamed,
With scarcely any life left in them.
All forty boys
Bent their knees to the ground
Before those other children;
The forty, fearing for their lives,
Before the eighty boys
Bowed like young brides.
“Good sirs, spare our lives!” they cried,
Despairing of their fly-sized souls.
Look at those forty boys:
The strength had left their arms,
The courage had left their hearts.
Screaming, running away,
Despairing of their fly-sized souls—
Such was the children's plight.
The eighty beat the forty,
Unmoved by their pleading.
Their ragged coats were torn apart,
Their noses streamed with blood.
The forty children wept and screamed;
The children cried out in pain.
Manas stood, telling the Kalmyks,
“Stop! Stop!”
His cries of “Stop!” did no good;
The Black Kalmyks would not relent.
As Manas kept saying “Stop,”
The frenzied boys rushed at him.
Seven cudgel blows and four punches
Struck Manas.
When those cudgels struck him,
Without looking from side to side,
Your warrior unleashed havoc.
Without sparing his slender life,
Without looking all around,
Without sparing his life at all,
His face like oiled wheat,
His eyes like twilight mist,
His eyes blazed fiercely like fire;
The thirst for blood truly seized him.
His broad front teeth, like a gate,
Teeth unlike those of other people,
Bared themselves white.
When the forty boys lay beaten,
That fellow touched by Khizir
Now began an astonishing fight:
“Black Kalmyks and Manchu people,
Let me slaughter your whole people!
Your eighty have gone mad;
Let me rest only when I have slain them!
The children of the forty households—
You Black Kalmyks with your topknots!
You would not leave peaceful people alone;
Your cruel weight has fallen on them,
As though they had seized your fine horse,
As though they had started the trouble.
Who could bear this suffering?
If they press me, I will finish them.
These Kalmyks have grown overbearing;
Let me have a brawl with them!”
So spoke the warrior Manas,
Roaring in his fury,
Raising the battle cry, “Kyrgyz!”
Just watch that extraordinary boy:
A black foalskin coat on his back,
A black cudgel in his hand.
Swinging the cudgel in his grip,
He rushed upon the boys;
He passed among them as though at play,
And twelve boys died at once.
The eighty boys scattered in a panic,
Scattered and broke apart,
Chattering in Kalmyk,
Babbling in Chinese.
So many boys were thrown into panic,
Crowding together in mortal fear;
So many boys fled.
Fearing Manas, those children
Tumbled and fell;
In their terror the children's
Cries reached God.
Your Manas, in his fury,
Now spread utter havoc.
Twelve of the Kalmyk children
Now lay sprawled and dead.
