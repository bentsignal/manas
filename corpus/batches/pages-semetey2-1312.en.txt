That scheming infidel—
That woman Aychürök—
Know this that I tell you;
You are a man, Kyyaz, heed my words!
When you take Aychürök
And carry her to your village,
One woman grasping at your collar
Must not destroy a whole cliff;
One woman raising a quarrel
Must not ruin all the livestock.
The two reins and single lead-rope—
Without treating Chürök merely as a woman,
Place them wholly in her hands.
Submit to whatever
That shimmering infidel demands;
Do the deeds she tells you.
For the fierce woman who overcame you,
Until seven full years have passed,
Keep doing whatever she says.
Set up a gilded throne,
Plant a garden of plane trees around it;
Let no willow fail to leaf.
Let nightingales and birds of every kind
Perch in the tower and sing expertly.
Let no resentment remain
Inside the ill-fated infidel,
The fierce woman Chürök.
From beneath every plane tree
Let a spring boil up and overflow;
Upon every plane tree
Let mournful creatures sing and trill,
And whoever hears them weep tears.
Build her a palace for promenades.
Brave Kyyaz, giant of the Jediger,
If you are a man, if you are a lion,
Take these words to heart.
Gather your heroes and hold games;
Hold the games of Kanchoro Khan.
Carry out the words of a wise hero;
Without discarding a single word,
Do exactly as I say.
I tell you ten thousand words—know them;
Accursed Kyyaz, heed my tongue!
Prepare all four kinds of livestock,
And deceive Chürök, who struck from above,
With all your wealth.
This woman Aychürök—
What does that shimmering infidel
Possess, and what does she lack?
She is good and radiantly beautiful,
And she also has humanity;
She is a true sorceress and witch,
And has an infidel’s nature too.
This is what I tell you, brave Kyyaz:
Do not raise your whip and strike her;
Do not take the daylight from beneath her;
Do not leave yourself in such torment.
Do not rage at her in manly pride;
Never curse or revile her.
That sorcerous infidel,
The enchanted woman Chürök—
If you should accidentally wound her heart,
If you should quarrel by mischance,
If you should fight by accident—
She knows on Friday where a bird has flown,
And on the holy night she knows its course.
She has skimmed the surface of the earth,
Flying as she roamed
The whole world under moon and sky.
That gracious, beautiful infidel,
That woman Aychürök,
Has no region she has not known intimately,
No man she has left untested,
No region over which she has not flown,
No man she has failed to visit.
She has roamed the whole world,
A serving woman who knows six spans,
And knows all the world
