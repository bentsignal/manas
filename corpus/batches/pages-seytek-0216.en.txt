Will he slaughter every living soul?
Will he seize and devour my child?
Will this wretched Kyyaz
Spare not a single soul,
But carry out a massacre?
Do not grant death to my household,
Do not make my sorrow boil.
Do not turn my words into lament,
Do not leave me bereft,
Do not break my back.”
Thus spoke Aichürök;
Her troubled heart would not be still,
And tears poured from her eyes.
From those dear blackcurrant eyes,
Tears streamed a whip’s length;
From those large black eyes,
Separate streams of tears flowed.
A dreadful deed came into her mind,
A dire and calamitous deed to contemplate.
Her black hair, thick as meadow grass,
Spilled across her breast,
While twelve afflictions and a thousand cares
Loomed close above her head.
Her mind reeled in bewilderment,
As every calamity seemed to gather upon her.
“O holy one, set this right,” she said,
“Set it right upon this very road.
My lionhearted father, gone to the other world,
Support me with your spirit!”
She put on her white headscarf,
And the poor woman, unable to endure,
Prayed to the true God,
Wailing and weeping in anguish.
He was a valiant lion,
And through his fathers and forefathers
He descended from surpassing gray wolves.
God, preserve the boy;
He himself is a panther, a gray wolf, a lion.
My illustrious Külchoro,
Press on and wage your battle.
The foe with whom he must clash
And fight until death
Is the hated Khan Kyyaz.
To test himself in combat,
Your warrior lies waiting on the road.
While the hero waited thus,
Aichürök cried out in anguish
For her long-suffering brother-in-law:
“If the moon is veiled in darkness,
What will become of his night?
If fierce Kyyaz attacks
My dear gray wolf Choro,
What will become of his day?
My brave brother-in-law was captured,
Until only a spoonful of blood remained,
His flesh bruised raw and blue.
He has neither strength nor power—
What will become of him?
If God unleashes calamity
And Kyyaz suddenly
Butchers Choro,
Kyyaz will be filled with savage fury,
And a day of terrible disaster will come.
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Will spare no life that seeks refuge;
Though they beg, he will not leave a spoonful of blood.
He will destroy them root and branch,
And pass on after slaughtering them all.
He will gouge out living eyes,
Leave not one soul alive,
And devour every living being.
Without the slightest pity for these souls,
He will carry out a massacre.”
Aichürök wailed and wept,
Her heart pierced and divided by grief.
She prayed for Choro,
And tears poured from her eyes.
Her heart was torn again and again:
“If Choro should suddenly be slain,
Will God strike us once more?”
