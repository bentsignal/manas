The Kyrgyz son of the people,
Saw something immeasurable,
A star stirred in the sky,
With the hero Kenen the destroyer,
He became a close companion in plunder,
Settling in Talas, he became a friend,
He became a hero to the white-kalpak people,
Keeping the horde, he became khan,
Honor the two tribes,
He was a truly wise soul.
For Kenen beyond measure,
A gray felt mat became a throne.
With wisdom, suffering, and hardship,
Joined together with the one who broke through,
Entwined with his kinsmen,
In the horde that stood aroused,
Though he was a Kyrgyz among strangers,
Without merely watching and failing to act,
This was a blessed deed and word,
Ebegey, the wise Chinese man,
The twins he led,
A boundless road remained,
Taking provisions and a flask,
Taking the two heroes in hand,
“Give us the people we seek.”
With a lion’s strike and tiger’s strength,
They came out on the hunt,
Driving Kuman and shooting,
As the sun sank red,
Drawing fire through their labor,
They went to the Kyrgyz heroes,
Let him reckon the people’s scattered condition,
Let him see and inspect with his own eyes,
The son of hero Kenen,
My Tengir who overturns the world,
The people’s grievance against their friends,
The binding at his waist.
His covering that would not freeze in the cold,
Your mighty protector in whom you took refuge,
The warrior, gray-maned hero Kenen,
From God the Almighty,
The protector you obtained through prayer,
Your two lion-like champions,
The two had arrived,
Ebegey and Kogoy, my two elders,
Before the two elders,
With formidable swords strapped on,
Like clouds about to bring hail,
Their faces turned fierce,
The dream I saw was a fortunate dream,
A dream in which anguish struck the heart,
A dream in which the people fell into distress,
Ebegey and Kogoy, my two elders,
A dream of going to the true world beyond,
The dear Kyrgyz people in their white kalpaks,
A dream in which they fell into calamity.”
Whatever fate may be, we shall see it,
If the famed rumor is true,
The command of my Creator,
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
We shall see what comes from the enemy’s wealth.
In the dream I was seeing,
That notch lay carved out,
As that harsh age passes over us,
The sunlit world passes from our hearts,
Those left among the many souls,
What will become of us?
Unable to advance freely into the struggle,
It seems we shall be made worthless.
Your strength will go to waste,
What will become of us?
With our outstretched legs unable to draw in,
It seems we shall become corpses.
Without looking back when our backs are galled,
Encountering an enemy,
It seems we shall be smothered.
Living on after being told to flee,
Drinking water in an unknown land,
Embracing and kissing the child,
Stretched out and halted upon the earth,
It seems we shall become mere game.
The torment of this world,
It seems we shall endure and see it.
