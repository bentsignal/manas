He became a saintly prince;
He received a letter from the caliph
And blessings from many lands.
His renown became known among the people,
And he was called Kenen the Hero.
He kept lionhearted companions,
And the heroes Ebegey and Kögöy beside him;
He turned his thoughts to countless concerns
And filled innumerable treasuries and myriad goods
With yellow gold.
He became like a surging lake,
With a nation to guard him.
The wicked foes who came
To seize the Kyrgyz of Ala-Too
Were leveled with the black earth.
He eased the hearts of his kin,
And drove off the cruel oppressors,
Breaking their backs.
The white-capped Kyrgyz became one nation,
And the leader of those Kyrgyz
Was gray-wolf hero Kenen.
His mount was Kertulpar,
Ebegey rode at his side,
And great Kögöy was his wise counselor,
His closest companion.
None remained to answer him defiantly,
And no force remained to overwhelm him.
Dear Kenen became khan,
And wherever he looked was at peace.
His honor was high and his wrath was mighty;
He split a black hair with perfect justice,
Never tearing apart or dividing
Kin from enemy unjustly.
He never favored the wicked;
He arose as a blessed lion,
Generous in nature and broad of heart,
With fortune perfectly fulfilled.
Splitting a black hair with perfect justice,
He arose as an impartial warrior-sultan.
The envious burned with resentment,
And his many watchful enemies,
Without even fighting, despaired of life.
His name became known throughout the world.
While gray-wolf Kenen reigned,
His chief wife Gulayym
Saw her affairs blossom like flowers,
And her fortune surged and overflowed.
She bore two sons, named Kulansaryk
And Alymsaryk.
When she had given birth to them,
She said, “I have borne two sons;
My wings now span a fathom, my tail fills an embrace,
And I have taken my place among the people.
My Alym and my Kulan,
My fine young sons whom I behold with joy,
Will remain behind me.”
She rushed about, placing her hopes in them,
And, gazing upon the children,
Prayed that they would be her legacy.
She had ninety animals slaughtered for their feast,
Had the babies lulled with cradle songs,
And gave them rhyming names.
Her prosperity surpassed all others;
The festive banners rose like misty mountains.
At the feast for those two,
The uproar split the sky.
At that very celebration,
Every soul whose ears could hear
And whose eyes could see gathered together.
The treasury doors were opened,
And abundant wealth was scattered
Among those who had none.
Playful young lads
Scrambled to seize the gold,
Then traded the riches they gained
For walnuts.
They overflowed with delight;
It was a splendid revel.
The ruler of Chin-Machin,
Descendant of Khan Aydung,
Chyngtengir, khan of Beijing,
Descendant of Khan Kongurbay,
Was a khan enthroned upon gold.
The countless people of the forty Chinese tribes
He held within his palm,
Never letting them slip from his grasp.
His dread was like a steel bullet;
Among all mortal sons ever born,
No such scourge as he had existed.
The wrathful hero Chyngtengir
Tempered his mind and pondered a plan.
Of his storehouse with forty doors,
He had one door opened,
And scattered provisions so vast
That eighty years could not exhaust them.
He requisitioned every riding beast,
And worked every mule until its back was raw.
