As though wearing your own coat,
Do not swagger without cause!
You ride over the rugs laid down;
This deed of yours is no small thing.
As though you and your elder brother
Might trample and ride away over them—
These are not Kyrgyz maidens.
Boy, what is it you have sent flying?
Tell me your circumstances fully:
Who is it you mean to fight?
What is it you have lost?
Who is it you wage war against?
What village have you approached in courtship?
Who are the in-laws joined to you by marriage?"
As Churok stood speaking thus,
The sea-like Choro began to answer:
"If taunting words are fine,
I shall test their strength;
Taunting you with proper skill,
I shall strike every bone in your body.
If you act beyond all propriety,
I shall bring you all to ruin;
I shall raid the village
Of your father, gray-maned Akun.
If the Afghans prove unruly,
I shall plunder their finest goods
And deal harshly with the rebels.
Thirty brides and thirty maidens,
You lie here making sport;
I understand the purpose of what you have done:
You have come to welcome splendidly
Your bridegroom named Semetey!
I understand your purpose well:
You have come out into the street to welcome
Your bridegroom, lion-like Semetey!
I know your measure.
When the time comes for me to die,
I am a slave worth no more than a dog's hide.
If you ask my true origin,
I am the son of the Chinese Almambet;
I tell you the truth as I stand here:
I am Semetey's servant!
Do not trouble me, sister-in-law;
Agree to what I say.
You should not mock
A man whose head is bound by duty;
Give me Akshumkar!
Let me strip the weariness from my horse,
And fly throughout
The whole wide world.
At the edge of Ala-Kol,
You changed into a white swan;
So that you might somehow find a husband,
Semetey's Akshumkar
You carried away!
What I sent flying was Akshumkar;
The man I will fight is brave Toltoi.
What I lost was Akshumkar;
The man I war against is Chynkojo.
If it is a matter of kinship, Khan Akun is our in-law;
I shall speak closely with Ajybai,
And cross weapons with Tumenbai.
Yesterday, at Kokotoy's memorial feast,
With gray-maned Manas at the head,
When multitudes gathered and the people clamored,
When infidel and Muslim
Swarmed like worms,
And the spectacle reached its height,
