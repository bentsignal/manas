From shimmering beauties,
Each day she chose one of that kind;
From a thousand maidens she chose one.
One cheerful and gifted in games,
Who paced with a swaying, ambling gait,
From those she chose one.
From those whose hair was braided splendidly,
Who moved proudly among young men
And had seen many kinds of pleasure,
From the accomplished ones she chose one.
From those whose hair was braided with precision,
Who had played often with young men,
Were accomplished in every affair,
And accustomed to answering every demand,
She carefully chose one.
With a thimble mouth and camel-calf eyes,
Too restless to remain in anyone's grasp,
Gracious and knowing how to show respect,
Unlike ordinary women,
One surpassingly cheerful and capable—
From those your sister-in-law chose one.
With a thimble mouth and desirous eyes,
Who, when she saw a young man,
Spoke all manner of yearning words,
From such women she chose one.
With a chest like a running hunting hound,
And eyes like a coursing hound,
With a graceful neck and camel-calf eyes,
From these she chose one.
One whose sight opened the heart,
A renowned beauty with a moon-bright face,
From those fine women she chose one.
Broad-lunged and breathing heavily,
With a hanging dewlap and a thick neck,
Able to load the tent securely,
And draw the ropes tight,
An outstanding woman among women,
A wrestler who moved with heavy thuds—
From these she chose one.
With breasts showing upon her chest,
Whose sight opened a person's heart,
From these she chose one.
From those who bound their hair with yellow gold
And braided it in wondrous fashion,
She chose one.
From accomplished women who had spent much time
In the company of young men,
Who, before one could say this or that,
In the time it took to open and close one's eyes,
Could finish any task,
From those she chose one.
From cheerful women of strong character,
From the accomplished and unashamed,
She chose one.
One who had matured like a doe,
Whose hair wound around her feet,
Who had matured like a red deer,
Whose hair wound around her head—
She took a sensible leader.
She took surpassingly beautiful young women,
And skilled, graceful women from among the brides;
She took a maiden more beautiful
Than all the other young women.
Into the valley of Opol Mountain,
Beside Great Urgench,
They gathered to play their games.
The poor woman longed deeply
To go out and welcome her lord.
So that not a single person could leave,
She had the mouth of the gate sealed.
When her mother came trembling,
And stood shrieking to bar her daughters,
Saying, "Do not go,"
Churok mocked her mother.
Broad river valley, spring, and sheer cliff—
The graceful, black-eyed beauty,
Proud Churok, poor woman.
