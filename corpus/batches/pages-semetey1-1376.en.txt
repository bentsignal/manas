That raging, death-dealing swine
Has, as you should know, such great renown!
Store an old man’s words in your pack;
Children, listen without making me cry out!
I received it from the transmitters of tradition;
I, your uncle, great Bakai,
Asked those who knew!
Yesterday, in the campaign where giants perished,
In the ordeal my noble lord brought upon them,
There were mighty men and lions,
And there was my champion Almam,
Who could read the Chinese tongue!
When they wreaked slaughter among the Chinese,
The Chinese Shypchaidar and
Giant Tiken—
When the leopard Manas, that bloodthirsty lion,
Seized them and ripped their bellies open,
The fiercest of the Chinese,
Kyrym of the red spear—
That strongman Kyrym
Was knocked from his gray mule,
Struck down by my dear elder brother,
And brave Syrgak of Khan Ulak
Cut off his head.
Deprived of his father,
Kyrym’s son Muradyl
Was left behind at that time,
An orphan while still young.
Living as an orphan, he grew to manhood,
Joined the people of the land,
And, I heard, emerged as a formidable man.
The Kara-Kalmak and the Manchus
Were struck with fear, I heard!
Muradyl learned surpassingly well
The sixty burku and the ilin,
And became learned, I heard!
Against those who had suffered humiliation,
He became an adversary, I heard;
Muradyl could bring down rain
And became a weather-maker, I heard!
Across the face of the sloping land,
At the edge where the enemy descends,
He prepared weapons and took up a spear,
And his face too turned wrathful;
He girded on a sword without a sheath,
And like a cloud about to rain again,
His face turned wrathful.
“If I reach the Burut whose homes burned,
I will wreak slaughter;
I will go and avenge
My father, who died,” he said.
He made his home at Kyzyl-Kyya.
When I asked many questions
Of those who had heard and seen,
They said Muradyl was a hero who bestrode the world!
They said he himself was a weather-maker.
They said his fortunes were complete.
They said he was a warrior who knew
The enemy’s stratagems openly and well!
The pine-covered ridge stretching crosswise there
Is that pass of Kyzyl-Kyya;
The hero named Muradyl
Is the one who dwells in that place.
From the flank of the great mountain
A whirlwind arose, I heard;
From the great Chinese people
An army arose, I heard.
Muradyl surpassed
His father in might, I heard!
He set out to bring turmoil
Upon the Burut, I heard;
If the need should arise,
He set out to seize
Half the khanate of Beijing, I heard.
He set out to bring distress
Upon the Burut, I heard!
A crosswise pass and clear water—
