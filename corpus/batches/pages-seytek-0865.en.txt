Many who once reveled now wander,
Many souls wander wailing,
And much livestock has been left ownerless.
“Alas, my dear brother,
He died innocently, for nothing,”
“On a road of torment with no turning back,
He met an infidel’s fate,”
Many people wander lamenting.
Yellow with wasting sickness,
Their hearts, minds, and thoughts
Are distracted—many among the people;
Yellow with wasting sickness,
Many mortals suffer anxiety,
And all their reason and thoughts
Are distracted as well;
Some have perished in battle,
And others too have died.
“Ah, Sarybai, we are done for,” they say,
“Through Choro, the blue-maned brother,
We have endured great calamity,” they say.
“I shall seize the lands
Of the consummate blue-maned lions,” he said,
“I, brother to a lion, a bloodthirsty tyrant,
Shall oppress the people,” he said.
“An army from the whole world gathered,
But in the end we were miserably slaughtered;
He slew so many of us,” they say.
“He heeds no guilt or pity,
But wrought a total massacre,” they say.
Their cruppers torn apart,
Many wander ruined among the host;
Their girths snapped,
Their saddles slipped beneath their bellies,
Many people rush about in panic.
Staggering senseless in confusion,
Their two legs dangling,
Every knee scraped raw,
Every rein torn apart,
Armor ripped and fluttering,
Spears shattered with a shriek,
“Ah, Sarybai has slain us,” they say,
“He killed until his fury was spent,” they say.
“The people are ruined in massacre;
Thus has God struck us,” they say.
“This tyrant’s offspring
Would humiliate and slaughter us,” they say.
“This monstrous slave Sarybai
Has made sport of countless men,” they say.
“The Lord Creator made Sarybai
Great and powerful,” they say.
“He inflicted misery and humiliation;
Sarybai is a vicious dog,
He has butchered us all,” they say.
“If death should overtake us suddenly,
If by mischance we perish,
We shall know him for the dog he is,
If the rumors told are true.
On the Judgment Morning at dawn,
That swaggering swine
We shall make ride a black donkey,” they said.
So the army clamored,
Until the host lost its senses,
And while they poured out their grief,
Kulansur, the steed he rode,
Flew rumbling like a cloud;
The stones struck by his hooves
Shattered with a ringing cry.
Rearing and surging forward,
The riderless Kulansur
Shot ahead like an arrow;
That splendid steed had a flowing mane,
And for the young hero Seytek
He had been born a perfect match.
When he glided, he was like a snake;
When he wheeled, he was like a whirlwind;
When he strode, he was like a wild ass—
Such was the nature of that horse.
The riderless fleet-footed steed
He made rear and plunge,
And seized the white reins knotted in a loop.
