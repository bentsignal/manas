Manas the lion, in his foolishness,
Had gone about thinking,
“I took a wife from among the people,”
But that towering great bloodthirsty man
Had truly been living as a bachelor.
She turned abundance into want;
Could there be a wife like this?
Is a woman of this sort
Fit to be called a wife?”
Speaking so, your elder
Let loose many kinds of cutting words
And said them openly.
They struck the swaggering bloodthirsty man
Like lead bullets.
Sultan Manas flew into a rage,
Raging there among the people.
He moved to seize and slaughter Kanykei,
To put her to death.
His eyes blazed fiercely like fire;
His thirst for blood truly seized him.
God now struck the bloodthirsty man:
“Beginning with Kanykei,
I will slaughter the women.
Rather than go on calling
All who wear white kerchiefs my wives,
I will put them all to the sword.”
His eyes blazed in fury;
His thirst for blood truly seized him.
His teeth, huge as gates, showed;
Venom poured from his mouth.
His anger was terrible, his venom strong;
He set straight anyone who crossed him.
The forty companions of Khan Manas said,
“God has struck the bloodthirsty man;
His thirst for blood has seized him.
All the women will be slaughtered,
And our honored sister-in-law Kanykei—
The bloodthirsty man will cut off her head.
He will put to death
Our resourceful treasure,
Our lionhearted, fierce sister-in-law.”
Agybay, son of Argyn Khan,
Stepped between them.
Brave Bakai, son of Oloyat,
Seized the bloodthirsty man
And held him back as he thrashed in fury.
Almambet, son of Khan Azozül,
Saying, “Hero, restrain your anger,”
Intervened and stopped him.
When Manas lunged at their sister-in-law,
All forty companions,
As if disaster had struck them,
The old and gray-haired among them
Staggered, understanding what had happened.
All forty companions said,
“Why should we look to
A bloodthirsty man who slaughters his wife?
All forty of us are sons of forty khans;
Let us all disperse
And return to our own lands.”
The forty companions bristled
And glowered at Manas:
“He obeyed at once
The fool whose trousers would not fit;
Preparing to kill
Kanykei, our true treasure,
God has taken leave of the bloodthirsty man.
We became Manas's companions;
Why should we stand here for nothing?
If he attacks Kanykei,
Let us grapple
With the bloodthirsty man, mighty as Kambar.
We forty companions came from many lands;
Kanykei became our refuge.
She joined what was severed
And gathered what was scattered.
By laying course upon course, she made a house;
By planting shoot upon shoot, she made a garden.
