They clashed with swords,
The swords shattered and splintered,
Leaving only bare hilts.
They stood face-to-face,
Striking each other with heavy blows,
All were drenched in blood,
Their arrow-proof coats and trousers
Were utterly torn to shreds,
Rent apart like saddle straps,
All their strength was spent,
Ribs were broken one upon another,
So fierce was the ordeal, so great the battle,
That no regret remained.
Unable to throw him from the saddle,
Unable to defeat brave Seytek.
Such was Seytek’s state.
His valor was extraordinary,
The young man’s bearing was exceptional,
He was the gray-maned champion of his line.
When Sarybai unleashed his venom,
Shouting as he thrust his spear,
And each time he swung his battle-axe,
The falcon favored by the spirits,
That dear lion, proud as a cock,
That steadfast hero, never stirred.
Behold how his fathers and forefathers
Came to his aid.
The wandering saint Khydyr himself,
If you mark it, stood beside him,
And nine guardian spirits aided him.
When Sarybai spilled blood,
They came and entered the fray:
A black-mottled leopard
Sprang at him from the flank,
A bobtailed gray-maned lion
Prepared to strike him head-on,
And before the people a fierce tiger—
No such being exists among my folk—
Fought at Seytek’s side.
They kept the warrior occupied,
And every mighty blow
That warrior Sarybai struck,
They intercepted before it reached him.
From his right side forty men
Against that infidel Sarybai
Leveled their spears in a row.
Khan Almambet and Chubak were there,
Their mighty spirits joining him,
Standing as support
For young warrior Seytek.
“Seize him, my son, seize him,” they cried,
“Finish him—do not leave him alive!
Crush the belly of that villain
Who boasts himself a warrior, like the belly of an ass!
Gouge out both his eyes!
Bring that bellowing wretch down
And dash him to the ground!
Smash his ribs and back
Until they crack and splinter!
Pour out his blood in torrents!
Take that shameless swine
And stretch him full-length!
Strip away the villain’s pride,
End that monster’s life,
Gouge out both his eyes!
If you are truly our beloved,
Catch him and slaughter him!
Strip away every last twitch,
Bring the khan to his death,
Bring the lord’s devices to nothing,
Beat out his raging madness,
And settle his account in full!
Drive him steadily—do not rush!
Pin him fast and let him not stir!”
He steeped his steel in blood,
Seeking a way to strike fiercely;
The cunning swine had gone berserk.
Almambet, Chubak, and the forty men
Raised their voices in a shout—
Men who had departed for the other world,
