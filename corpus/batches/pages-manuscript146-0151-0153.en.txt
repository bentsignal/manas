Overcome by his majesty,
Ozu burned his people,
Appearing beneath that banner,
He spoke many words of battle,
Truly separating for single combat,
One by one, man against man,
Know that he stretched his path wide as a house,
The head that turned them round,
The pointed head that spun them about,
Roaring loud enough to split ears,
It was a time when Azhy watched,
His fanged body trembled,
In fear his face turned pale,
A word to unite and cast among the people,
A word for all the gathered people,
In wrath he braced his mighty back,
Hero Kenen spoke weighty words,
Speaking of every fear and alarm,
Let me cast fortune upon another,
The battlefield had become harsh,
Let me take them piece by piece.
Having spoken thus, Ozu Khan,
Armored himself in steel beneath the sky,
Striking aside each stone he met,
He devastated the broad Kyrgyz host,
Wearing down the champions,
Burning the great Kyrgyz people,
Scattering sparks like a thrown stone,
Fire flew from his mace,
Venom issued from his storm,
Wrath poured from Ozu Khan,
Upon this unfortunate tribal people,
With the bottomless blue spear,
Devastating them from right and left,
Calling for the house-eagle to come,
He thundered like the sun.
The suffering Kyrgyz, the poor people,
Burned with grief, asking what had happened,
A Kyrgyz man had fallen as a sacrifice,
The dark-faced multitude had bred in abundance,
They said it had drained everything from the multitude,
Seeing the fury, they cried aloud,
“Let us receive it with palms outstretched,”
Let us seek it from the Creator,
Whatever fate may bring, behold it,
I grew accustomed to devastation,
Saying Köböy had taken my honor,
My wrathful, precious soul,
With Ala-Too rising behind you,
For the white-capped Kyrgyz people,
For honor and for the hero,
On the appointed day,
For the livestock that sustains life,
Saying he gave his needed life,
Once gone, he would never return,
Saying he was humbled in this world,
By day and by night,
Across the barren plain where horses ran,
Holding a feast and slaughtering feast-beasts,
Doing a deed that shed radiance,
Kenen stood behind you,
Each of you became a guide,
Calling the tribes Kayyñ, Saryk, and Alay Saryk,
Your people descended into the valley,
At the appointed time a hero came forth,
The two lions came out together,
They cast turmoil into the massed battle,
Does a warrior not die by the enemy?
If he remains mounted in this world,
He closes his eyes and the deed is done,
O enduring multitude of people,
Though spoken, the people here,
To true Köböy, the many people,
Grieved sincerely, saying you had died,
Your true self parted from life,
The closed eyes did not open,
The outstretched legs did not draw back,
Your true soul left your eyes,
Then Kenen truly died,
It became a day of scattering,
The heroes who remained were sorrowful.
