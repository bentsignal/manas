I beat his age-old fury from him.
Shielding them from storm and wind,
Sparing their livestock from hell,
I preserved the people of Zhelpinish.
The reason I preserved them was this:
The infidel, that monster’s son,
The warrior who threw the people into turmoil,
Brought a great calamity
And spat upon all the multitude.
My venerable father Kayypchal
Was richer than all the world;
Every kind of livestock he owned in abundance,
Spread across the whole wide world.
What words could the infidel,
The monster’s son, have left,
After unleashing such savage calamity and strife?
My father had settled and made his home
In Zherge-Tal, fair as Zhelpinish.
All four corners of the world
Were filled with my father’s herds.
If I did not recover the spoils
From that infidel, the monster’s son,
That destroyer of the land;
If I did not spend and scatter
His white-treasured wealth;
If I did not spread slaughter to his farthest bounds,
Despising my thread-thin life,
And bring violence down upon him—
He himself became entangled in torment,
And, lying in ambush at the feast,
When the moon was waning,
Because of Sarybay
I barely escaped death;
Then I began the slaughter in earnest.
Valuing my maiden head as a warrior’s,
For honor and for the people,
For the glory of my dear khan father,
For the mountain pass my people cherished,
I wrapped my head in blood and made it a banner.
We fought for some seventy days,
And when the fierce conflict raged,
His sword knew no restraint
As he slaughtered the blessed people.
When he stretched his cold hand
Toward wealth that would not be yielded on demand,
Thus did God strike
The swine who descended upon you.
When he slaughtered the poor, blameless people
Without leaving a hair behind,
Pouring his frenzy upon the multitude
And oppressing them,
That infidel, the monster’s son,
Made troughs from pine trees,
Heaped calamity into a black mountain,
And made a path no living soul had seen.
Breaking through the ridge, he opened a road
And began strife among the peaceful people;
Openly inflicting humiliation,
He threw the whole world into turmoil
And carried his tyranny so far.
He smashed and kicked away the bowls,
And flattened the plain smooth as oil beneath a blade.
He relentlessly inflicted humiliation
And laid oppression upon our heads;
Unable to endure his abuse,
I fought him without cease.
I rescued the multitude who came to the feast
From death’s flying arrow,
From the enemy’s iron net,
From hell’s burning embers,
And from the venomous dog’s hands.
Making my proud head a banner,
And my maiden soul a warrior,
I found a sure way to defeat
The enemy who had come girded for battle.
With my own hand I beat
The swine’s far-famed fury from him;
Thus did I settle his account.
We dueled without rest,
