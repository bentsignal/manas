Tölöbay and Kalcha seem able to swallow us.
Unless you yourself defend us,
They seem able to plunder our goods.
With Aychürök at their head,
Smashing stone to rubble,
They seem able to seize
Every wife and maiden as plunder.
The pit has been dug deep;
Unless you yourself are our noble one,
Our bitter brine has begun to boil.
Upon Chürök, whom my father raised,
They have brought great turmoil to her village.
A claimant comes to bring disaster;
An enemy comes to face us!”
When Ajybay said this,
The lion prince Semetey
Swelled like a lion.
His anger came and would not cease;
The former fury
Of the gray wolf, son of Manas, flared.
As Ajybay stood speaking thus,
His eyes blazed like fire;
Descendant of the accursed one,
Bloodlust seized him.
His broad white teeth like a gate—
Grinding them then,
He chewed them section by section.
“Ah, Ajybay, my brother-in-law!
I have oiled my white steel.
When they sorely provoked my anger,
I laid low the lions
Of Argyn and Kyrgyz.
Of battles and slaughters such as this,
I have seen many!”
Saying this then,
Semetey raged.
He had karnays and surnays sounded,
Loaded the army upon camels,
Had the tulpars tethered,
And the brave men made ready.
Running like a lion,
He prepared to exchange fire with Toltoy;
The lion boy Semetey
Donned his battle clothing:
“Let me lift my lower leg
Upon a charger like Taybuurul;
Let me test myself in combat
Against that one who boasts of his might,
Against my kinsman Chynkojo!”
From his royal father Manas,
When entering true slaughter,
There was a doolbas drum to be beaten;
When struck, the earth grew still
And cowards' faces changed.
When beaten, it deafened the ears
And could be heard from far away.
Made only from the hide of bil,
When covered it measured three fathoms;
When the enemy surged upon them,
His champion beat it for the omen—
Hear its voice and flee far away.
When the doolbas was beaten,
It deafened the ears when heard;
While your falcons lived,
It had brought turmoil upon many.
At this time Semetey
Was roused at the word “war,”
And surged at the word “slaughter.”
He raged at the word “spear fight”;
When your gray wolf charged,
He strained greedily like a wolf.
When had Khoja ever raged
And attacked the blessed hero?”
From Aychürök's palace,
The doolbas was beaten firmly;
Thirty brides and thirty maidens,
With Aychürök at their head,
The girls lost their senses.
