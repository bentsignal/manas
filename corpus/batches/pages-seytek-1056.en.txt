He split a black hair exactly in two,
And seized his prize in an unguarded moment.
Against a fearsome enemy in adversity,
He attacked without retreating.
He was a marksman by ancestral inheritance,
A seasoned warrior looming like a mountain.
He was the wind-sheltered plane tree
Of grey-maned Sultan Seytek,
A lion-like champion who, once unleashed,
Let no living soul escape.
He would not let a white swan leave the lake,
That white-blazed black steed of a man.
Should a sudden flood sweep down,
He would stand unmoved and unshaken,
Never flinching from fear of the foe,
Standing beneath the battle standard.
When calamitous hardship came,
They would die in the same grave.
The reason they would die together was this:
They had sworn a solemn pledge
And bound it with utmost firmness.
They chewed white wheat flour together,
And licked the edge of steel;
Thus they swore their oath
And sealed their promise firmly.
They became friends until Judgment Day;
Seytek and Akjoltoy
Dwelt together as village neighbors,
Let alone the many campaigns stirred up from China.
In bravery and lionhearted might,
The names Akjoltoy and Seytek rang alike;
Two lion twins, two heroes—
How could either be lacking?
Against the foe they were vigilant,
On campaigns they were master marksmen,
Seasoned warriors worth the whole world.
On journeys their lives were one,
At pasture their livestock was one;
They shared their gathered wealth and treasure,
And if one died, the other prayed over him.
They shared the same feasts and companions,
Their villages and enclosures were one.
The marksman, brave Akjoltoy,
The noble hero, hurrying greatly,
Readied all his weapons,
Strapped on his gleaming sword,
For when had that hero ever held back?
He took up his ornamented gun,
Murmuring as his spirit rose,
Assuming many fearsome forms.
Kulunsur stood tethered there,
And Akjoltoy untied him,
In feverish haste.
Without setting foot in the gold-soled stirrup,
He sprang up in a single bound
And mounted Kulunsur.
Facing toward the north,
Your kinsman Joltoy rode away.
Beneath him, Kulunsur
Tore up the ground wherever he galloped;
When given his head, the furious steed
Thundered as though about to fly.
His tail filled an embrace, and he had a flowing mane;
The creature flew like a whirlwind.
His flanks were straight as a ramrod,
His compact frame small as a flea.
His poll was broad as a ewer,
And upon his croup one might set
A six-span cauldron with three supports
And leave it hanging there.
Akjoltoy’s Kulunsur
Was unlike any other beast.
The breadth between his forelegs
Was like a valley filled with meadowsweet.
The white blaze upon his brow
Was like polished steel.
Keng-Özön, untouched by living feet,
Was a valley with a broad-flowing river;
For the saint called Mashayak
Had departed from this earth.
