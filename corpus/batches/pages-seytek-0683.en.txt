When the lamented man saw it,
Did he not unleash unsparing hardship
Upon the enemy?
Not revealing that its master was dead,
Allowing no alien hand to touch it,
Never yielding to a tyrant,
Never giving an inch to the foe,
Did he not unleash
An unsparing tumult?
When the realm was scattered,
When dogs overran the royal camp,
Did it not strike down
Both Ajybai and Kölbai?
Weeping bitterly to the one God,
And setting it aside for Külchoro,
Did not the venerable lady Kanykei,
Grieving and weeping, take it
Away from Talas?
From noble gray-maned Almambet,
Syrbarang remained as a gift.
“It is the inheritance left by my father;
How can I refrain from shooting
At the infidel son of a monster?”
The enraged choro
Was seized by his thirst for blood.
His opponent was Sarybai;
Not a spoonful of blood would remain.
From the noble-born choro’s
Majesty surged strength.
When you looked upon his fearsome face,
His hair stood five felt-boots high.
The noble, peerless choro
Made ready like a hawk;
To the enraged choro,
Wisdom came from the unseen.
Strength surged from his majesty,
And Sarybai, whom he confronted,
Came straight out to meet him.
When you looked upon the choro’s form,
His mustache swept upward.
When the fickle world was scattered,
He renounced his wife,
Abandoned his people and homeland,
And set out toward the Muslims;
Did he not bring
A great turmoil and calamity?
For the souls left behind,
For the children who would become descendants,
For those apart from his restless soul,
For those destined to remain,
A firm support for troubled souls,
For the children who would remain behind,
Thinking it might one day serve them,
Did not noble, peerless Almambet
Bring grievous hardship
Upon Beijing?
Thinking those here would need it,
Did not noble, peerless Almambet
Bring the blood-avenging Syrbarang
As he went forth lamenting,
And carry it here?
In sport it was Ochogor,
In earnest, a ravenous beast;
When fired with care, it shattered armies,
An attacking foe fell to its bullet,
And in fury it broke whole ranks.
When its report rang out in battle,
In the hour granted by God,
While ill-fated Almambet lived,
It thundered when fired at the foe.
The whole body of mortal man
Shuddered from head to toe.
Where its screaming bullet struck,
The enemy scattered in panic.
When the blessed man bore it,
It was the mirror upon his shoulder;
When it struck a bitterly contending foe,
It was the solace of his entrusted soul.
When noble Almambet died,
Its true place of no return,
