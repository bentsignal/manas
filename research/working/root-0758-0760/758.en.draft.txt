The Argyn are left weeping.
Powerless against Kobosh,
Many more go about loathing him.
‘Alas, Manas, do not die!’
Many more keep yearning:
‘While that Manas was alive,
When bored, we would shoot roe deer;
When not bored, we would lie at ease.
Riding horses in their third year, flying hawks,
We played in wild delight.
How could we have known such hell?
What could dear Kobosh do?
He made calves carry loads,
Made us endure such hell,
Had the butcher slaughter goats;
He meant to make us suffer.’
From time to time, growing violent,
A khan like Kobosh arises;
His voice drives the soul out.
As Kobosh lay in pomp,
Immersed in many pleasures,
As Akylai and that Kobosh
Lay entwined together,
Manas's son, the orphan slave,
Was sorely troubled in his thoughts.
The orphan who had wandered here
Shed tears from his eyes.
As though his father had died today,
His mind was sorely troubled.
At his pompous uncle Kobosh,
He seemed to grow angry:
‘Who pledged allegiance to twelve spiritual guides—
May my holy father never die!
May I never be left fatherless!
Such humiliation as this—
May no mortal be made to suffer it!’
Steeling himself, burning within,
The orphan could not stand watching.
The warrior born of Manas—
Here was where he would spread havoc.
The orphan now made a stir;
Of Manas, gone to the world to come,
He stood demanding the possessions:
‘Uncle Abyke, hear my words.
My father has died.
The countless riches left by my father—
When you plundered and divided them,
When you contrived matters thus,
My poor widowed mother—
When you struck and split her breast,
To Temir Khan's city,
When my mother went wandering,
My dear mother Kanykei,
When she fled toward Karakan,
All the great wealth,
When you ruined and scattered it,
In those days, when you plundered the valuables,
When the boar stirred up trouble,
Ten days after my father died,
Toward my unfortunate mother,
When you became a predator,
Every treasure, wealth and possession,
When you took it as booty,
While I was still in the cradle,
My mother Chiyirdi,
When she pulled me out and fled straight ahead,
My cradle standing empty,
My brave mother Kanykei,
The slave woman's child,
Taking him from his swaddling clothes,
When she bound him in the cradle,
My mother nursing him,
When she put her breast in his mouth,
Your shouting split stones.
Upon my wretched mother,
When you burst in raging,
You found just the right way!
Striking my mother with a battle-axe—
