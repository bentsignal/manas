Who has not worn it jauntily?
While luck and fortune remain,
Who has not walked with thunderous pride?
At the name “Brave Külchoro,”
Did the whole age not tremble in fear?
No mortal dared confront me,
No enemy willingly sought me.
At the mere word “enemy” or “foe,”
Blood burst from his flanks,
And life fled at my voice.
The critic who tried me died.
The land grieved without seeing me,
And the spy who grieved the land died.
Such a valiant choro was I,
A lion no pleading enemy could capture,
A warrior upheld by the spirits.
Laughter made the day set,
And merriment made the dawn break.
Both merry and lionhearted,
Both champion and hero—
Such a choro was I.
I was a glowing ember of red steel,
I too was an arrow
Shot forth from the Kyrgyz.
But now I have fallen into ruin;
When I reflect, no strength remains in my limbs.
I am no longer myself.
Again exhausted and gaunt,
I have grown thin.
My powers spent and strength gone,
I have grown old.
In the days when fortune was mine,
When true battle-fury seized me,
We wrought slaughter among the Oirats.
When we charged in wrath,
We raided and conquered the Chinese.
When the fever of battle came upon us,
We wrought utter ruin.
When we charged and poured forth our rage,
We slaughtered every last enemy.
Now my final reckoning has come,
And heroic courage, strength, and vigor
Have been struck from me and gone.
The reason I have been brought so low
Is that yesterday Sarybai, son of a monster,
Unleashed all his power,
Piling up torment mountain-high.
Roaring like a hungry lion,
He overran the plain and cleared a path,
Invaded and plundered Talas,
Seizing all the spoils,
And wreaking terrible destruction.
My courage surged and my heart burned.
If true death comes, I shall die;
If God truly wills it,
If true death comes, I shall die.
My words spoken early came too late,
And I watched with desperate longing.
My eyes flared with indignation,
And, still alive, my heart boiled.
Against that infidel, the monster’s son,
I stood first on one bank, then the other.
Shooting hard and drawing my bow,
I fought him in a bloody clash.
I lacked the strength to strike him down,
And could not defeat the vicious dog.
My stout ribs came apart,
And my frailty was revealed.
Tears poured from my eyes,
And both my spears shattered.
The tears flowing from both eyes
Fell upon my chest.
Twelve steel and a thousand spears
Were shattered in the jousting.
Twelve chargers and countless swift horses
Were killed and lay fallen in combat.
Thus my strength proved insufficient.
It seems I have fallen into ruin.
I have none of my former strength;
It seems I am no longer myself.
