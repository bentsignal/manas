Inside that fortress,
Beneath the golden pavilion,
In the imperial garden,
Kongurbai was lying
At the time appointed for his leisure.
A shrill cry rang out;
No other living soul dared approach.
One man stood there in anguish,
Calling Kongurbai by name.
When they heard Karagul's voice,
All the guards bent low,
And every messenger
Ran scattering in every direction.
When he heard the great uproar,
His snoring ceased;
Hero Kongurbai, the great Kalcha,
Opened his eyes wide as bowls.
When he wished to step outside,
And look upon the people's welfare,
At the gateway door,
At the opening where messengers passed,
By the towering watchtower,
At the foot of that tower,
The open field stretched broad,
And people thronged across the expanse.
When he looked upon them all,
There among the people stood Karagul—
Here was a most astonishing sight.
The reason it was astonishing:
He was covered in billowing dust,
All of his head and face
Were splattered with blood.
Even in sleep such a thing enters no dream;
That Karagul should come to this—
Had anyone ever heard of such a thing?
When he looked at Karagul,
He had no beard as before;
Algara had no tail.
He had a shadow but no living form;
His tulpar had no tail,
And his burning heart had become an ember.
"From his father, gray-wolf Manas,
I never suffered torment like this.
Instead of going off with the booty, you two,
Why have you come to me?
You have a shadow, but no countenance;
Were the two of you dragged along?
You yourself remain, but your beard is gone;
Were you pierced and left for dead?
Karagul, you have a tale to pour out;
My heart is torn with doubt.
You and Algara, Karagul,
Were closer than any living pair,
But your appearance is dreadful.
Where is the deep-dug trench?
Where is the army
Besieging this Beijing before us?
Karagul, may you burn and die,
Was it you, my elder, who would come
Having suffered and grieved,
Having lost every limb,
And was I the prince who would behold you?
Where is Red-Mist Mountain?
Where is the foe you fought in slaughter?
You have a sheath but no sword;
When I look over every side of you,
You bear the marks of coming from carnage.
You have a horse without a tail;
From what grievous calamity
Have you escaped, ill-fated man?
Is there a close road free of ambush?
Is there an army that came out to fight?
They have long been our enemies—
Has a foe come from the Burut?
When I see your state, Karagul,
May no human bear your appearance.
Your strength and bearing are gone;
You look like a man who has been struck down.
