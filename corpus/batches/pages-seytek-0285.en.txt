If one perishes, does not the black earth
Receive the whole world?
Do not grieve, Mother;
Will the descendant of a heroic khan
Rest before finding his people?
Would God strike down a warrior thus,
Leaving him unable to find his people?
The goshawk flies to its own land;
The forlorn hero returns to his people.
The Telik bird flies to its own land;
The wanderer returns to his people.
I shall go to the dear people
Who sit waiting to cherish me.
I am the khan’s sole descendant;
Why should I keep fleeing?
I shall go to the renowned people
Who wait to place their hopes in me.
I am the son of a noble prince;
Why should I shun my homeland?
There is silver on its sunny slopes,
Upon the rolling ridges of the yellow hills;
I long for my people, and I shall go.
There is gold upon its hills,
Upon the rugged ridges of its rocky mountains;
My thoughts are with my people, and I shall go.’
Saying this, the hero Seytek
Bade farewell to his mother with these words,
And spoke many other wondrous things.
He raised a gold-crescented banner,
Donned armor no bullet could pierce,
And mounted a swift horse no bullet could catch.
The tail of Kylkürөң
He knotted diagonally.
To free-flowing Keng-Kol and broad Talas,
To his homeland went the hero Seytek.
To the dear Argyn and Kyrgyz people,
To his nation went the hero Seytek.
He crossed the mottled rolling ridges,
Traversed mountains with arduous passes,
And journeyed along the road ahead.
Spurring himself on with courage,
He appealed to the spirit
Of his bloodthirsty father-in-law.
Her heart sank and broke apart—
A dreadful sight to behold—
And tears poured from her eyes.
From her quail-like eyes
Separate streams of tears flowed;
From her blackcurrant-dark eyes
Tears fell a whip’s length.
Unable to draw back the mucus from her nose,
Unable to wipe the tears from her eyes,
She stood weeping and shrieking,
Thinking thoughts she could not restrain,
Pondering the child’s fate without answer.
Tormented and burning with grief,
She wondered, ‘My only son has never seen this road;
What will become of him?’
His dear mother stood grieving.
As his mother stood thus,
The hero Seytek grew fierce,
Angrily making ready.
Wisdom came to him from the unseen;
Murmuring with gathering resolve,
This poor heroic son
Spoke to his mother:
‘Alas, Mother, what are you saying?
Does a mortal man not die?
Whatever command God ordains,
Must not a mortal servant endure it?
Does one whose time has come not die?
When the angel reaches out five fingers,
Saying, “We have need of this mortal,”
And the command has been given,
The nimble tongue is stilled from speaking.
When the naked blade of Gabriel
Comes rushing straight against him,
When five fingers reach out,
When the soul departs and a person dies,
Will the dug grave not receive him?
