Heard that Chürök had been taken as spoil
And subjected to humiliation,
Heard that in her old age Kanykey,
Your mother, had been stripped of dignity
And cruelly oppressed.
Wailing aloud, Akbala
Plunged into mortal peril;
The poor woman cast aside
Her fragile life, believing herself already dead.
Her knees bent toward the ground,
She ran on, wailing aloud,
As though her ribs were breaking apart;
Grievous torment and certain death
Appeared before the doomed woman.
Immeasurably swift,
The ill-fated woman sped on;
With no care for her fragile life,
Broad-rumped, with wide-swinging braids,
The graceful, ill-fated woman
Came upon the forty wrestlers
And saw her mother Kanykey
Suffering humiliation.
Unable to die though she burned with anguish,
Before the throne in the palace,
At the pit they had dug,
Amid the abuses devised by Kanchoro
And the violent uproar,
Akbala entered the fray.
Her lovely hair, thick as meadow grass,
She gathered and bound upon her crown;
Külchoro’s coat of mail
She drew down over her head.
A matchlessly swift gray three-year-old,
The foal of a young mare,
A horse born like the whirlwind—
Tucking up her flowing dress,
She sprang bareback upon it.
Wheeling the gray horse about,
She struck down seven
Of the wrestlers and sent them sprawling;
She made them swallow their fill of blows
And cry, “Mercy, daughter-in-law!”
Akbala drove them onward.
Every wrestler who came near,
She slashed and stabbed with her dagger;
Akbala laid them low.
Though they were mighty veteran wrestlers,
All fled from the woman,
As though slaughter had fallen upon them;
They fled for their lives.
Into the gated palace,
Into Chachykey’s stronghold,
Into the garden pitched for royalty,
Into the encampment of Khan Kanchoro,
Akbala drove the wrestlers,
Sending them staggering inside.
Dealing with each in turn,
Akbala laid them low.
“We have been disgraced by a young wife!
Some of us have been killed!
We have been disgraced by a woman!
Some of us are dead!”
The wrestlers fled inside;
And Kanchoro learned
That they had come stumbling in.
Once Kanchoro understood,
He leapt grimly from his throne
And ran out through the gate.
Poor Akbala came charging,
Driving the wrestlers
And penning them against the gate,
When from farther in, puffed with pride,
The accursed firebrand,
The scorched madman,
Kanchoro suddenly appeared.
When she saw Kanchoro,
Valiant Akbala
Unleashed her terrible fury.
