Whoever comes with the joyous tidings,
I will agree to what he asks.
My great herds of all four kinds,
I will give them in their entirety.
On the hills, my horses in golden san,
Argymaks and coursers mixed together,
You who bring the joyous tidings,
Choose and take my coursing steeds.
If those herds of mine are too little,
Pour my blood into a leather flask and take it!
If you wish, take camels as well.
Listen, people; listen, elders;
My whole multitude, listen, every one!”
To attain his heart's desire,
Having said this, rich Jakyp
Went away to his horses.
When he thought, his strength ebbed away:
“What kind of child will she bear?” he thought;
His dear life could scarcely be held in a fist.
His rib bones seemed to split;
Jakyp's heart was torn in many pieces.
Unable to stand the anxiety,
Jakyp went to his countless herds.
The black stallion of Kambar Ata,
The old stallion among the many horses,
Rich Jakyp caught it.
Hanging his kise belt about his neck,
Pleading to the Creator,
The stallion of Kambar Ata
He offered as an ay-tuyak sacrifice.
Jakyp slaughtered the stallion;
When he had eaten his fill of its meat,
He pillowed his head on a white saddle,
Turned his head toward the qibla,
And rich Jakyp went to sleep.
He covered himself with his felt cloak,
Pleaded to the one God.
Sleeping propped upon his elbow,
Taking a little nap and rest,
His eyes were caught by sleep.
“Creator, You have troubled me!”
Barefoot and bareheaded,
Into Khan Jakyp's dream
There came
A white-bearded wandering holy man:
“Turn your horse around, Jakyp;
Say ‘God!’ and rise, Jakyp!
I am Kydyr, peace be upon him.
I have come to your side:
The Creator has granted a child;
I have come to tell you that.
Banish every sorrow, Jakyp;
Today you will have a son.
Name him Manas, Jakyp!
This son of yours is born here in Altay;
If he lives and grows to manhood,
He will set the world in uproar.
Wherever this son of yours strikes
Will be red fire.
His armed companions will number eighty-four;
Wherever this son of yours strikes
Will be red fire.
When he reaches seven years,
He will enter before the caliph and receive a letter,
Receive a blessing from a prince.
He will give his hand to twelve spiritual masters;
The forty chilten will lead and open his way,
And he will be called Illustrious.
Drinking blood, he will grow hardened;
Destroying whoever faces him,
He will be called Bloodthirsty Manas.
He will be a fierce gray-maned lion,
His fortune will be complete.
The youngest of six lions,
He is God's beloved servant.
No enemy will afflict him,
No one who faces him will remain alive;
His fortune will be complete.
If you look within him,
He will be broader than the face of this earth.
Facing Manas,
No mortal can do him harm.
In the eighteen thousand worlds,
None will remain to raise a pole against him.
Upon Beijing of the kakan's realm,
He is the lion who will wreak havoc;
Upon Beijing, where no flood reaches,
He is the hero who will bring upheaval.
Your son, the leopard Manas, has come;
Now take heed, Jakyp, hear me!
Give him the name Manas,
Banish every sorrow;
Before the Black Kalmyks and Manchus,
Give him the name Chong Jindi!
Your child born this very day,
His protector is Kojosan,
His support the forty chilten.
When you see this child of yours,
Devise something no one can imagine.
Of your sleek herds of horses,
Slaughter many and hold a feast!
Today your barren wife will bear a falcon,
Today your dun mare will bear a racing steed.
This is the noble creature Kydyr speaks of today,
Your lion-son, the leopard Manas,
This is the horse he will ride on campaign.
Today the two were born on the same day;
They were joined when tiny as lice,
They met when tiny as ticks.
This is the mount he will ride in battle.
It will be a surpassing racing steed;
Ridden six months, it will not grow lean.
Until it reaches sixty asyy,
Its teeth will not wear and it will not grow old.
Its tail is sparse, its mane thin;
When it chases a kulan, it will not let it escape.
All its bones are blue stone.
The mount beneath him
Will die sooner, its life short.
This is what I say; heed my words;
Hero Jakyp, understand!
This is what I say, these are my words:
Your son Manas, your little foal,
Your goshawk born unlike other mortals,
It will take time for him to grow.
Among the seething infidels,
The Chinese people will be his enemies.
Until he reaches twelve years,
Grows to manhood and becomes a warrior,
Becomes the equal of other men's sons,
Until he takes a spear in his hand,
Until he leans to thrust his spear,
Until he mounts his horse no bullet can catch,
Until he wears his coat no bullet can pierce,
Until Manas becomes Manas,
Until he sits astride his horse,
Until he moves among the people,
For the Kyrgyz people with their court,
Until he becomes their seat of power,
Until he reaches twelve:
‘My son's name is Manas,’
Let not a single person know,
Let not a single Kalmyk sense it!
‘My son's name is Manas,’
If you blurt it out in boasting—
Manas's fame was already known;
Seven years before this,
It was entered in the true kakan's register.
When he heard the name of Manas,
He stirred trouble among the people;
The khan of Beijing was distressed.
That is this renowned son, Manas.
‘My son is named Manas,’
Never let the Chinese hear it!”
