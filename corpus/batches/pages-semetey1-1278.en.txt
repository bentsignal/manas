He drove them all together and crushed them.
The Jediger army gave way;
The raging Kulchoro
Drove and chased
The countless Jediger host
And packed them into Jar-Kyya.
The young prince Kulchoro—
As he drove the Jediger,
Their tusks jutting out,
The horse corpses formed a mountain.
Their mustaches drooping,
The warriors' corpses formed a heap.
Raging, he shouted;
The great black Jediger
Struck back and scattered.
From fear, the Jediger
Split into many different groups;
Those whose deaths had come
Spilled their blood upon the plain.
Let us leave that there
And begin from this side:
Kulchoro of Almambet,
Baitaylak of Khan Bakai,
Kalyk of Sary Khan,
Shayyymbet of Jamgyrchy—
All were khans' sons.
Raising the white standard as their banner,
Raising a clamorous battle cry,
The blue-maned lions now charged
Into brave Töshtük's army,
Onto the road where slaughter would come.
Standing face to face,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
They fought with snarling fury.
The heroes grappled in battle,
Driving birchwood spears
Into the very heart.
The living suffered humiliation;
Those whose deaths had come died.
The sword-bearing Jediger
Fought with scabbards and all;
The battle-ax-bearing Jediger
Fought with whatever they had.
Brave Toltoy of the Jediger
Began battle early.
What could dear Tölök do?
Raising the white standard as his banner,
Crying “Aziret!” and raising a clamor,
Arrows from bows shrieked;
Bullets from short guns clattered.
Spear points clashed;
They raised a raiding clamor;
The warriors' heads knocked together.
They fought face to face,
Standing pressed against one another.
They stared each other down and fought long,
Wrestling with battle cries;
Those fated to die died.
They confronted each other face to face,
Standing and straining hard,
Fighting beyond mortal measure.
Tölöbay, son of Tölök,
Charged with a shout from the flank,
Raising the battle cry of Khan Kozho.
The most skillful with the battle-ax—
Such was Tölöbay,
The finest of the gray youths.
Renowned for striking with the battle-ax,
Skillful at striking with the whip,
The finest among so many,
With dust billowing into the sky,
Crying Toltoy's battle cry,
Tölöbay charged shrieking.
Riding only dun-gray horses,
Wearing bulletproof coats,
Raging and seething,
Tölöbay's great army
Engulfed Afghanistan, shrieking,
