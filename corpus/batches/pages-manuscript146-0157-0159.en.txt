His steed came from the broad land,
The bay horse crossed five stretches,
Courageous by his very nature,
The bloody trial came a third time,
My elder Köböy gave counsel,
He parted from the bay horse,
The bay horse fell to a bullet,
He plunged into the mass of the enemy,
Then hero Köböy,
Went on foot, bent nearly double,
True strength came into his soul,
His strength was exhausted in the struggle,
It became a night that blinded the eyes,
If you saw it, it was a grievous day,
His armor hung loose upon him,
Not surrendering to hero Ozu,
The two of us fought,
Who had ever seen such hardship?
Blood flowed from his black eyes,
Dear Köböy stood his ground,
Both hands labored in battle,
A honeyed cry rose to the lips,
The dear youth had strength to stand,
The red vessel filled with blood,
They grappled, bracing with resolve,
They were left bearing regret,
Their strength departed as they struggled,
His dear eyes labored on,
Suffering cruel humiliation,
That accursed, cunning Ozu,
He appeared close upon the battlefield,
Ozu Khan stood in this manner,
One kind among his many forms,
His cunning aspect watched where they settled,
His marksman shot as swiftly as spring.
The majesty of your marksman,
He spread out his back,
Saying the Ayrat deed stood in the midst,
Saying his head and brain were shattered,
Calling the spoil his own,
Your bullet was as large as a sheep’s head,
They bound it in battle and passed on,
They had bound him to true death,
They forced his head to the ground,
Life left him before his face had changed,
Death’s bullet struck his temple,
Our dear elder Köböy,
Had gone to single combat with no other choice.
Seeing this, Ebegey,
Parted from his comrade in sacrifice,
Called the Kyrgyz into the midst,
Broken with grief and separation,
Left diminished and alone,
His soul seared by the loss,
Left solitary like an ascetic,
Without offspring, gathering his remaining strength,
None remained standing; they died,
The figure before them,
Hero Köböy, hard as a hammer,
A warrior-khan to the Kyrgyz,
A close companion beneath one coat,
The people embraced his spreading fame,
Ebegey and Köböy had been born,
Those shared twins,
If a fated bullet struck from above,
Giving a final blessing to the lips,
The enemy could not take the dear hero,
His banner-head stood in the stormy trap,
Nothing remained for those who saw,
Now let me die for his sake.
A lion-hearted soul, truly born,
Created for true honor,
Let me surrender this fly-like life,
Saying this, Ebegey,
Mounted the pale horse of pure milk-color,
Fastening on his coat of mail,
Spurring from right and left,
Casting himself into the countless ranks,
Crying out, “Comrades in sacrifice!”
He attacked hero Ozu,
They fought face to face,
The myriad mouths opened wide,
Frowning, they showed no mercy,
Without delay they thrust their spears,
The dear spears shattered in battle,
Their bodies were wounded and bent,
All their blood was washed away,
No bullet could halt their grappling,
They struggled with all their strength,
They waged a fierce conflict,
Each one was a mighty champion,
They fought from the first blow,
They committed a harsh and savage deed,
Each set his teeth against the other,
They covered the hilltop in blood,
They turned the whole struggle into dust,
Unable to overcome one another,
Their strength mounted,
They seized collars and wrestled,
They grappled like rival hordes,
They struck without restraint,
They fought with crushing blows,
They stood locked collar to collar,
They reached the horses’ flanks,
Against mounted China, the Kyrgyz were few.
