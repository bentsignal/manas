If they should fail to hear,
Or hear yet pay no heed,
And lie idle unexpectedly,
If they do not see this spectacle,
If they do not come to my feast,
Tell them to die, not remain alive!
Tell them to behold Kökötöy's red banner with its golden crescent
From atop their own houses!
Tell them to give us
All their dark-eyed daughters as booty.
When you travel farther,
There is the renowned city of Bukhara.
Ruling that Bukhara
Is a khan named Temir Khan;
They have round baked bread
And houses with four corners.
Wearing his blue skullcap high,
Give Temir Khan my greetings.
Say it is Kökötöy's memorial feast,
The foremost of great gatherings.
Say the father of the people, hero Koshoy,
Is the chief directing the feast.
To see this spectacle,
Let none of their elders remain at home;
Let them all come out upon the road.
If they pay no ear to the words,
If they do not heed my command,
Tell them they will see a fine quarrel;
Tell every one of them to die.
If my anger should rise,
This Bukhara standing here—
I will trample its head
And scatter all four kinds of livestock.
I will slaughter its master where he stands;
By force I will slaughter his mares.
When they draw near, I will
Beat out the brains of every one.
I will make Bukhara pay
For its deeds of long ago.
On the far side of Bukhara
There is a place called Zheti-Özön.
Dwelling in Zheti-Özön
Are the people called Zhediger.
Among those Zhediger
Is their khan called Bagysh.
Let him tether his tulpar;
Let not one warrior remain.
Leveling their spears,
Urging on their thoroughbreds,
To Kökötöy's memorial feast,
Tell blue-maned Bagysh to come.
If he does not come to my feast,
If he does not see this spectacle,
I will simply wake him from sleep
And raid away his horses!
Tell him to know of this feast,
To cross here with his host,
And submit to what I say!
When you turn back from there,
There are reeds in the black desert;
He plays with palace maidens,
A fiddler in a house with daughters,
A cupbearer in a house with kumys;
There is maiden-adorned Agysh.
His strength is immense,
A peerless man born a hero.
Beneath him his chestnut tulpar
Fits him like a sleeve;
He perches without a wrist-rest
The white-shouldered eagle of Orolmo,
Which watches wolves and foxes.
Hero Agysh, no taller than an elbow,
Is sinew from head to foot,
A renowned and formidable man;
When he faces the Kara Kalmyks,
He is a man whose back reaches them.
