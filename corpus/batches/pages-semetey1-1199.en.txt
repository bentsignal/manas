As though ready to come to his aid,
They held out their spears
And stood as his support.
The finest of the forty companions,
The most skillful of spearmen,
The very strongest heroes,
The blaze-headed thoroughbreds among horses—
Almambet and Chubak among them,
With Khan Agybay and Bakai there—
They stood in support then;
All the falcons were there.
If Manas dismounted,
The leopards Almambet and Chubak
Would reach him before he could open his eyes.
The lions Agybay and Bakai
Would make a massacre
Of the fucking Chinese as they passed.
Hero Manas the leopard,
His nose bulging like a leather flask,
Was truly seized by thirst for blood.
Manas had forty companions;
Kongurbay had forty princes.
Behind Kongurbay
Were bellowing Manchus,
Swaying Shibe,
Their cuirasses hanging loose,
And all the kalday governors.
Among Kongurbay's supporters
Were skillful spearmen
And excellent bowmen.
There were warriors no horse could bear;
If they happened to dismount,
They would reach a man before an arrow,
And upon reaching the fucking wretch,
Lift him and mount him on a horse.
There were footmen skilled in mounted games;
There were giants unlike human beings.
Each had made ready to fight;
Those fine fellows had come.
Were it not for Manas the leopard,
The many kinds of Chinese
Would all have engulfed them
And brought them together to their end.
The reason they could engulf them was this:
They came as a flood covering the land,
As one with the face of the earth—
The infidels had come.
If you looked at Kongurbay,
He too was a blue-maned lion.
Jerking his golden reins,
Making Algara rear and lunge,
He turned the black horse's head.
The carefree Kalcha khan
Stood facing the single combat.
Then Manas said this:
“This one was the khan of the Chinese,
And I was the khan of the Muslims.
If I do not grant him his turn,
It will seem I am mocking him;
If I do not give him the first course,
It will seem I am mistreating him.
Take your turn, Chinese!” he said.
Khan Manas stood upon the bank.
Kongurbay charged and thrust;
The leopard born of a lion
Did not flinch at the ringing blow.
Kongurbay's bronze spear
Shattered and flew apart;
He did not count it worth his trouser cuff.
“Take your turn, Burut,” he said.
Kongurbay stood stretching tall.
The Chinese warrior stood on the height;
Hero Manas stretched and thrust.
The bronze spear broke apart completely;
Kongurbay did not start.
Khan Agybay came
Galloping on Kartküröng.
Into your bloodthirsty hero's hand
He gave another spear.
