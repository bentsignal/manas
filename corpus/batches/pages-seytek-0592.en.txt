The Kyrgyz pondered and rose in turmoil;
The mighty kinsman, the valiant lion,
Donned his armor impervious to bullets,
Separated himself from the people and rode out alone.
Behold such a gray-wolf warrior,
Appearing like a lofty blue crag;
Before him he was a grim tiger—
When he set his will, no living soul could match him,
And no foe who faced him prospered.
Behold such a gray-wolf warrior,
Appearing like a hill
Upon a boundless plain,
Like a link in the chain of true steel
That encircled
The entire world.
He appeared like the fruit
Of the tree of life
That nourishes the world.
He went to confront Sarybai,
To meet him face-to-face,
To die if hostile death had come;
At all times he was the people’s wing,
A fortress before the nation—
It was his custom to ride out in single combat.
Whoever beheld him lost all composure;
He appeared to be the pillar
Supporting the one created world,
The roost
Of the giant black zymyr bird.
When he charged, his wrath poured forth;
Such was the bloodthirsty warrior,
Appearing like a towering mountain.
His spirit was lofty, his venom strong,
And he leveled all who attacked him.
“We are dead if we behold that butcher—
Abandon hope for your precious lives!”
Terrifying all who saw him,
He made his splendid thoroughbred
Amble proudly,
And, heading straight for Sarybai,
Prepared to enter the massed host
And attack him.
Countless broad-bladed spears
Loamed huge as they rose from men’s hands,
And steel-hafted battle-axes
Appeared in the people’s grasp.
All the warriors surged with fervor,
While the fainthearted, rash in courage,
Panted without leading the people;
The champions spread out to the flanks,
And cowards who dared not leave their doors
Were trapped in the very midst,
Wondering what would happen now.
All was torn into confusion,
And thus appeared before the countless host;
As the people mounted and stood ready,
Then Semetey
Prepared himself like a hawk,
And inspiration from the unseen
Came to the precious lion.
His departure from this world,
His confronting his enemy Sarybai,
And meeting him face-to-face had come.
He was eager to devour whoever crossed him;
His two eyes became four.
For the people, the precious lion
Was ready to sacrifice himself.
Behold, such was the conflict,
Such was the chaos that descended;
Though the broad world had not narrowed,
The cunning Sarybai
Had embroiled the whole universe in strife.
He himself seemed corruption incarnate;
Against the black eye of disaster,
Against the very taker of souls,
The great lion, son of Manas,
The steadfast, ill-fated hero,
Set all his weapons in order,
Strapped on his gleaming sword,
And took up his steel-hafted spear.
