Alymsaryk and hero Kulan,
Defenders of all the people,
Fought a vicious fight,
Standing face-to-face,
Fighting turn by turn,
Locked in a bloody struggle.
Their father, gray-maned Jeljetpes,
Was protecting the twin heroes,
So the champion's line might endure.
He turned summer's day into winter,
And plunged the Chinese and Kakan troops
Who came against them into calamity.
He sent a flood and made it water.
The three heroes raised an uproar,
Cutting through the standing Chinese host.
Supporting one another,
They were drenched in bloody dust,
And fought without turning back.
Jeljetpes moved through their midst;
Wounded in seven places,
He had a net spread before him.
After countless heroes
From the Beijing host thrust at him,
His life was truly scorched.
He was snatched from certain death,
Caught in the fatal net,
Yet Jeljetpes broke free and escaped.
Alymsaryk and Kulansaryk,
Two fair heroes with rhyming names,
Passed through the solitary foe.
Their muscular flesh was torn,
And the flesh of their ribs ripped away;
The two reached a quiet place.
Climbing to a mountain pass, they leaned down,
And on a truly sacred hill
They dozed and fell asleep.
Their closed eyes would not open,
Their outstretched legs would not draw in,
And their deep snoring did not cease.
They sank into a giant's sleep.
Jeljetpes and the twin heroes
Let the horses they had ridden to war
Cool fully and drink their fill.
They grazed them on feather grass,
Led them a little while,
And filled their throats with grass;
Led them a long while,
And filled their chests with grass.
