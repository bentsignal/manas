The swords broke and shattered;
Now their sheaths stood empty.
A bitter ordeal, a great battle,
Now it brought slaughter to mind.
Against giants as great as mountains,
What could dear Manas do?
He now stood facing them.
Kaman the giant and Zhoon the giant,
Their strength was extraordinary.
Those dogs, may their houses burn,
Their valor was considerable.
When they stood before the khan,
They spared no one they met.
No one who faced them survived;
No mortal dared approach them.
‘They might do some mischief,’ they said,
And bound them with chains.
They were champions, they were heroes;
With the valiant hero Manas,
They had come to grips.
Like the wind in a gorge,
They thundered in wrath.
Like the flood of a raging river,
The earth shook and rumbled.
Standing face to face,
Manas and these giants
Fought unlike other mortals.
Created a lion by God,
What a lion was the hero Manas!
Facing Manas,
Zhoon the giant and Kaman the giant,
What heroes were those two?
Gripping collars and pulling,
The swift steeds beneath them
Sank knee-deep into the earth.
They tore all their coats,
Standing opposite each other,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
Crashing against each other in battle,
Standing face to face.
With no other means remaining,
They fought with maces.
Scissor-eared Kaman the giant,
As he reached your bloodthirsty one,
Wolf-maned Manas now came up.
Before one could say this or that,
Before an eye could blink,
Your valiant Manas, the bloodthirsty lion,
Whipped Toruchaar
And reached them without stopping.
His steel mace in his hand,
He drew it and rode past.
Roaring in wrath,
Crying the battle cry ‘Kyrgyz!’
Your bloodthirsty lion Manas's
White teeth, like a gate,
Teeth unlike other people's,
Jutted out white and grim.
His wrath extraordinary, his menace fierce,
He would lay flat whoever fought him.
His eyes blazed like a lion's.
Wolf-maned Manas the lion's
Thirst for blood seized him firmly.
The steel mace in his hand,
He grasped it firmly and swung it round.
Vulture-tailed Zhoon the giant,
He struck him on the crown.
The mace shattered into fragments.
The helmet of blue-gray iron
Flew whirring into the sky.
Your vulture-tailed Zhoon the giant's
Life departed with a scream.
The wolf-maned one did not leave him unhurt.
Passing from this world, Zhoon the giant
Fell from his rhinoceros.
The Chinese were many, he was alone;
He began a costly spectacle.
Zhoon the giant, chief of forty zhaysangs,
Valiant Manas the leopard
Slaughtered him utterly.
They fought a mighty battle,
Fought without looking back.
He took no measure of the day's time;
He never rested during the day.
Without sleeping propped on his elbow,
Without a moment's quiet rest,
Manas fought in earnest;
His bloodthirstiness became known.
‘He is a blood-drinking wolf-maned one,’ they said.
Manas's great name,
In Beijing's Esenkan's
Register it was written.
He began a terrible slaughter.
Esenkan's envoys,
His stern land-guarding wardens,
He slaughtered, leaving none.
Who has known such a struggle?
While your valiant Manas was there,
He brought great turmoil upon the Chinese.
Seeing it, there can be no regret.
The tale of Bloodthirsty Manas,
The great strife he brought upon the Kalmyks.
He himself was born in Altai.
When he reached twelve,
Your mighty one raised uproar.
The Black Kalmyks' and Manchus'
Little valleys he burned;
Their headmen he brought low.
Their heroes, in extraordinary fashion,
He slaughtered them all and left them stretched out.
He poured blood into clear water;
He set the grassy lands alight.
He greased the little valleys;
He laid low those who opposed him.
Manas wheeled upon the ridge.
When they said, ‘Manas is coming,’
Their children old enough to understand words
Fell silent, not crying at all.
No challenger remained alive.
He brought a great struggle upon the kakan.
Even in death there was no regret.
Before, when Manas was not there,
The Black Kalmyks and those Manchus,
Upon the poor forty Kyrgyz households,
Would wreak thorough havoc.
From their horses and beautiful daughters
They took tribute every year.
If they did not give that tribute,
They did whatever they pleased.
The Black Kalmyk and Manchu people
Beat and struck them without mercy.
Before, when Manas reached twelve,
He unleashed fierce wrath.
Those who came from Esenkan,
Those who kept doing violence,
He slaughtered and left stretched out.
Many he slaughtered and left stiff.
He began a terrible slaughter.
Kochku and Döngö, their great giants,
He slaughtered them, leaving none.
He raised fierce, bitter strife.
On the Topurak ridge,
