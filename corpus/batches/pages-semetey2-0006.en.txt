Bright dawn broke,
And light fell upon the earth.
Alööke's Kongurbai
Rode the length of his horse,
Traveling four times around
The four corners of the world.
He exhausted his horse, surveying the land,
Watching the patrol and tracking signs.
He jerked the golden reins
And made his horse Algara lunge.
His great boots thrust forward,
His neck craned like a rooster's;
He wrapped himself in blue armor.
If you had seen Kongurbai,
He raged like a blue tiger.
With his gold-tipped patterned pipe,
The Kalcha huge as a hut,
With sixty champions and forty kaldays,
Their elder Kongurbai
Hurried the herdsmen.
He made his Black Horse rear
And pace over rise and hollow;
Such was the sight.
The blue-gray-bearded swine's
Chest button was the size of a fist.
At his waist was a düngürchök;
He put his hand into the düngürchök
And took tobacco from it.
He stuffed in five handfuls,
Until the hero's pipe
Lacked only five finger-widths of being full.
Striking Chinese flint and liver-stone,
He made a spark and set an ember,
And lit his pipe.
He sucked until it rumbled,
Until his craving was satisfied.
When he let it go, it rumbled;
The smoke from his pipe
Billowed like the smoke of an army.
Algara beneath him—
Did Algara not have
Wings upon his flanks?
This was the infidel's Black Horse.
Alööke's Kongurbai,
Watching the guard and grieving over the road,
As was always his custom—
His tail was sparse and his mane scant;
If he chased a kulan, it could not escape,
He was skilled at pursuing an enemy.
Algara turned his head.
Shouting in anger,
Kongurbai called Karagul.
Karagul came running,
His knee bending to the ground;
Before hero Kongurbai
He bowed like a bride.
There was the dog's bloody name:
When their chief came before them,
It was the custom of the Chinese
To bow like women.
Then Kongurbai
Said this to Karagul,
And at will spoke many horrors:
“Your father's grave, Karagul!
I reconnoitered the broad steppe and narrow road
To their very ends;
I went as far as
The great and renowned Altai.
I made Algara's back sore
And greatly pained my backside.
There was no cawing crow,
No croaking raven,
