Inscribed in its register.
When the following year comes,
Take male camels from the herds,
Take plenty of every kind of equipment,
Take a full count of the army,
And enter the army on the rolls.
Leaving out no able-bodied man,
Let us gather them all!
Making the face of the earth thunder,
Then let us go to Beijing!
Those who go on holy war may die;
The brave scout will reconnoiter the road.
So the people who go need burden no one,
And their lice do not multiply,
Let us prepare underclothing;
To enter battle fully equipped,
To strike the foe without tiring,
Let us ready armor against bullets!
The Chinese inhabit Beijing,
Whose heat gives no respite.
Beijing is no easy place;
For every man let us prepare
A coat with golden collar and copper sleeves,
For him to cast himself against the foe,
To stand fighting the enemy,
To extend his spear without fleeing—
Let it be a coat bullets cannot pierce.
Who then will have great regret,
If we stand and fight without fleeing,
And bring great conflict upon the Kakan?
If we stand face to face,
Clashing and hewing with swords,
Bathed in red blood,
Standing amid slaughter,
Swallowing the Kara-Khan,
If we stand and fight without fleeing.
So we do not die by the sword,
So untimely death does not reach us,
Your dear father's contemporary,
That confidant Bölökbai—
When he worked the bellows, it stirred;
Döötü and Bölökbai
Were friends for the world beyond.
Skilled in every kind of object,
What Bölökbai made,
He and Döötü together
Made after taking counsel.
With Bölökbai at their head,
Let us prepare eighty smiths;
Helmets of blue steel,
To be set upside down upon the head,
So all the ready warriors
May strike the foe without tiring;
Let us make mail-cap helmets,
Let us ready the weapons of war.
Let us take an exact count
Of the troops going to the Kakan!
Against the seething unbelievers,
Against hero Khan Kongurbai,
Thus let us begin the conflict!
Wearing coats without a hole,
Gathering an army fit to match them,
Let us take the host together,
My splendid young hero,
And ride thus into battle!
Preparing troops of many kinds,
For the force going to exchange fire,
Preparing abundant provisions.
My horses on the hills are piebald,
Swift horses and chargers mixed together.
There are well-fattened mares;
Choose and slaughter the fat ones,
Have the servants butcher them,
And select provisions.
If you bring great conflict upon China,
So the army does not go hungry,
Let us firmly prepare journey-rations!
If we wage battle against Beijing!
