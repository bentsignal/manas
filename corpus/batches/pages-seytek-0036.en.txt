So they plunge into intrigue.
If you ask what our people are like:
They share one heart with warriors,
Match strength with wrestlers,
Stand face-to-face with champions,
And rival orators in eloquence!
Leopards favored by the wolf’s fortune,
A singular and blessed nation,
Whom no man dares confront
And no enemy has defeated.
The bravest of heroes dwell among us—
What a splendid people are ours!
Our dark fields abound in rice;
Such is the richness of our land.
We have more silver than tin,
More camels than other livestock,
And more giants than weaklings—
What a splendid people are ours!
Herds of wild asses gather on the plains,
Argali graze upon the hills,
Ibex roam the crags,
Wolves prowl the lower slopes,
And foxes frolic on the rolling steppe.
Nightingales sing in the willows.
In our broad Talas valley,
Not a single tree lacks delight.
Red deer bellow in the thickets,
Cuckoos call from the plane trees,
Eagles beat their wings on the rocks,
And falcons strain upon their perches.
Every creature that possesses life
Lives in Talas, our land—
All living things are there.
When will we reach that broad green-mottled valley?
When will that day come?
Dearly remembered Karachach,
When will we reach our people?”
To share one inheritance,
And, if happy days returned,
To go back to Talas,
There was a woman named Karachach.
She lived as a lowly serving woman,
A soul enduring humiliation.
After Aichürök arrived,
Her weary spirit revived,
All her sorrows vanished,
Her hollow parts filled with milk,
Her gaunt limbs filled with flesh,
What had been broken was restored,
What had been scattered was gathered,
And her misery and anguish disappeared.
The wretched woman and Aichürök
Spoke of God together,
And swore an oath unto death.
When they sat, they sat side by side;
When they prepared food, they did so together.
Whenever they sat or rose, they spoke
Of returning to broad Talas
And coming into a shared inheritance.
They sealed their pledge,
And among the women and children of the house
Swore an oath binding unto death.
Aichürök would continually
Tell Karachach about her people
And praise them to her;
She praised Jylgyndy, Keng-Kol, and broad Talas,
And ever extolled her homeland.
“Our Talas is like this,” she said.
“Our Kyrgyz are a splendid people:
With jingling hair ornaments and generous hips,
Beautiful are the young wives of our land.
In red dresses, with waists curved like the moon,
Beautiful are the maidens of our people.
In black coats and white kalpaks,
Handsome are the young men of our land.
If you ask what our people are like,
When bored, they hunt roe deer;
When not bored, there in Talas
They play at leisure.
