And for the many appointed lands,
All who saw him marveled.
His beauty surpassed that of every beast,
And he did not brush the heads of reeds.
When Kulansur gave chase,
He never failed to overtake a spirit-being.
He did not graze the tops of rushes;
When that spirited beast grew excited,
He never failed to catch a gazelle.
He did not touch the tips of dry stalks;
When that magnificent beast pursued,
He never failed to catch a wild ass.
His tail streamed like a parrot’s plume;
If a ninety-fathom chasm appeared,
He leapt it without a pause.
Steel horseshoes fitted him perfectly;
His barrel was broad, his neck well-set.
“This is a steed of splendid build,”
My father Jelkayyp judged.
Look upon his foreleg bone:
It was like a stake hammered into the ground.
The breadth of his rounded croup
Was like a plateau atop a summer pasture.
His split hooves were like deep ravines,
His jaw like cast steel.
Prancing, kicking, and biting,
He allowed no stranger
To come near him.
When he trotted, he moved like a gazelle;
Stepping briskly and frisking,
When he set off, he moved like a fox.
His tail spread across his thighs;
He had been born for the fortune
Of the blessed gray-maned hero.
The peerless thoroughbred Kulansur—
Even the six grooms who tended him
Treated him as though he might bring them harm.
They held back in fear,
And the more they did, the more Kulansur
Flattened his ears like a tiger;
The restless creature
Struck and churned the earth.
Three heroes among the strongmen
Gripped his bridle firmly
And turned his neck toward the hitching post.
Another hero made ready
And prepared the saddlecloth.
The heroes spread out
The leather saddle blanket
And saddled Kulansur.
The front bow was solid gold,
The rear bow solid silver;
The golden saddle shone like the moon on his brow,
With jewels set into its bow,
As though lighting a lantern for all who saw.
The white saddle was perfectly made,
And fitted Kulansur exactly.
They tightened the breast strap firmly,
And left the rear girth loose,
For he would travel down slopes
And enter descending ground.
The eager warriors
Shortened the crupper.
It was clear he was the lion among lions;
As he pulled straight ahead,
The hero thrust his feet into
The gold-soled stirrups and sprang astride.
The hearts of all who saw were stirred;
That imposing, beloved hero
Looked like a lofty blue mountain.
“Saddle up quickly!” he cried,
Hurrying his lions.
He made Kulansur, built like a wild ram,
Stride out at a proud, swinging pace.
Rumbling and gathering force,
He spoke softly to himself;
At twilight his eyes were like mist,
His chest like that of a fearsome tiger.
Speaking firmly after taking stock,
He addressed the noble heroes.
