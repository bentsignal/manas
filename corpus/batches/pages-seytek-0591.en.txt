Let us bind him by a hair and cast him down.
Do not make Sarybai a giant,
Do not let him rule this Talas;
Let our blood flow like water,
Let us all perish!
Rather than be ambushed from the heights,
And have every valiant lion
Delivered into Sarybai’s hands,
Rather than lose mane and tail
And wander stripped and forlorn,
Rather than live with broken horns,
Polled and powerless,
Calling Sarybai our lord,
And burning in anguish,
Let us flee Talas in terror,
And, fighting seven years without cease,
Ransack every last saddlebag
Of the infidel monster’s son.
Let the multitude begin the battle;
Old men bent low and growing youths alike,
Let us abandon everything,
And rise in open strife
Against the oppressor.
Without delay, level your spears;
Let us wage a furious battle.
Brave Semetey, Külchoro,
And brave Seytek, whom we make our hope—
Let the whole people take these three
As the girdle around their waists.
Against the tyrant’s forces,
Let us stand in bitter battle.
If Sarybai is truly a hero,
Let us perish in slaughter.
The people surge like a river;
When the river floods and torrents run,
We shall see what Sarybai is worth—
Let us grapple with that dog!”
The people cried out in tumult,
In a roar that split the heavens;
Horns and trumpets sounded,
Warriors spoke and the people grew impassioned,
Voicing countless words.
Brave young men and thoroughbred steeds,
All together grew impassioned.
Wisdom took the lead;
Not only their livestock but their lives
The multitude renounced, saying,
“The hero’s words are true.”
“How can anyone say we shall not face
Cannon fire and turmoil?
If hostile death has reached us,
If it is written upon our necks, let us see it;
Let us stand as support
For the hero, pillar of our inheritance!
Sarybai is your true enemy;
Let us truly beat out his madness.”
The people cried these words aloud:
“Your words are true, hero;
What you propose is wise.
Let us all depart from Sarybai,
That godless infidel.
If we hold back, may the cliffs strike us,
May the towering Ala-Too strike us!
Your words are not in vain,
My noble-born sultan;
You have found us sound counsel.
If we spare ourselves from Sarybai,
May the matchlock’s flame strike us,
May the musket’s bullet strike us,
May the broad-breasted earth strike us,
May the whole vault of heaven strike us!
Rather than live as captive slaves,
Fallen into Sarybai’s hands,
Clad in black mourning,
Burning until we become embers,
Crushed and brought to nothing,
Rather than see our children torn away
And lie enduring humiliation,
Let us begin the true slaughter,
And make our poor souls shriek,
