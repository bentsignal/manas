Their rallying cry, like a white stallion,
Sharing whatever they acquired,
Brothers in faith, sharing even death,
Of one arm and one ancestry,
Of one wish and one blessing,
The issue of a single stallion,
The foals of a single man.
Take careful heed of this:
Sharing one nest with Manas
Was Shygay Khan in former times.
Let us tell the tale from its beginning:
Chynkojo, who stirred up strife,
Was that same Shygay’s son.
Two words can destroy a people;
He was a cunning rogue who shook whole nations.
The one who sowed strife among the multitude
Was truly that accursed oath-breaker.
Yesterday sorcery descended upon his waist,
And he stirred foul strife among his people.
Yesterday Manas’s son Semetey
Was made an enemy of his own people;
He came harboring evil designs
Against that lion Khan Semetey.
May that Kojo never prosper—
Chynkojo, destroyer of nations,
May there never be another like him.
Yesterday they bound their pinewood spears,
And made war against the bloodthirsty hero;
He scattered their days of happiness,
That glib-tongued, fawning slave,
And fled toward my elder brother Toltoy.
The clans camped along the valley,
And he greatly inflamed the quarrel;
It became a feud unto death.
He became khan of the Jediger,
Convulsing and destroying the people;
Spreading slander and ruining the nation,
The disheveled Kojo came to no good.
Shuffling about day and night,
He whispered and incited Toltoy:
“A slave has caught and mounted
The black horse no lasso could touch;
Old Bakay has entered the palace,
White as a swan;
He has spoken twisting words,
Words inspired by a white devil;
He has trampled the waters of Jeti-Özön;
More than that—even your
Wife has been seized,” he said,
Slandering them to brave Toltoy.
Thus Kojo corrupted and incited him,
Speaking words that split all apart;
My simplehearted uncle Toltoy
He corrupted by distorting his words.
Chynkojo was truly depraved:
Among the Jediger of Jeti-Özön
He stirred them into a frenzy,
Turning their elder into an enemy
And corrupting all the people.
Once the Jediger were corrupted,
They mounted stallions from the tethering line,
And took saddle blankets from the ground;
They became an army beneath whose weight the earth sagged,
Not a small host, but a multitude.
Raising a gray-and-white banner,
They filled every quarter with uproar;
Without hesitation they crossed the ridge,
For the woman he was to marry—
To seize her back,
They quarreled with Semetey.
They felled pines and made troughs,
Heaping gathered grass like mountains;
“I shall take your wife,” he said,
And laid a claim against Semetey.
Because of what had happened,
Because of Chynkojo,
They fell upon one another and fought,
And a terrible conflict arose.
