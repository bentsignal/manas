Among all the mares he gives,
Let there be swift thoroughbreds—
Karabayyr horses with bronze-like legs,
Most of them chosen racers,
And let his finest runners be among them.
Let him make my father’s khanship
Known among the multitude.
Let the captured Surkoen
Not grow restless for nothing—
Let him be mounted by Changtiybes.
This is the point, this is my word:
Let him show that he will pay the blood-price!
Let them heed what has been said;
Without quarreling or raising strife,
Let them give whatever I desire.
If they will not agree to this,
If they will not give what I desire,
Let them stand face-to-face with us.
Firing muskets and drawing bows,
Let them fight us head-on.
I have come to witness
Their courage and lionheartedness;
My own cousin Semetey,
With slaves who will not retreat,
I have come to fight you to the death.
He has armor reaching from chest to shovel-length,
A coat that bullets cannot pierce.
If quarrel and conflict arise,
What a splendid chance for a warrior of slaughter!
Streaming out from Sary-Arka,
I have an army I drove here.”
So Ümötöy,
Pouring out his wrath and readying himself,
Prepared all his arms and armor
And stood there in fury.
Like one who would spare no life,
He committed this great outrage.
The smirking madman—
God had struck him senseless.
Then Külchoro,
Standing there, said this:
“Since I became Külchoro,
Since Külchoro first mounted a horse,
Since I grew to manhood as an orphan,
And somehow rejoined my people,
Since I came of age and became a hero,
The equal of heroic sons,
Since I donned the white invulnerable coat
And charged the foe with a battle cry,
Fate has not yet brought my death.
Among all round-headed mortals,
Ümötöy, son of Khan Köksö,
I have never seen a fool like you!
We drove white deer like cattle,
Made conquered lands our spoil,
And stirred conflict in barren lands;
Through the Six Cities and Margilan,
Down to Kokand and Samarkand,
Pursuing the blood-price of Chubak,
We sacked those very lands and won a hundredfold.
In Chinmachin and Kakan,
Did your father not see true warfare?
Your father Köksö
Fought a mighty battle,
And among the tribe called Soloon
Was raised up as khan.
He exercised sovereign power
And enjoyed his own khanship.
Far from being owed a blood-price, your father,
While still alive, exacted one himself,
Fulfilling all the desires
Of every fair-faced Kazakh,
And filling all his chests
With possessions of yellow gold.
Fulfilling every wish,
He filled every treasury
With an abundance of gold.
Exalting his khanship above the people,
