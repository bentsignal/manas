The Chinese gathered in great strength;
Troops swept in from five directions,
Uncountable multitudes swept in.
Brass flutes clattered,
Drums thundered,
Unsheathed swords flashed into sight,
Shattering the courage of those who saw.
Trumpets and shawms sounded;
The massed Chinese advanced grimly,
Now reaching them in assault.
Arrows poured like rain;
The earth grew black as night.
For those who saw, it was a day of calamity.
Dust whirled into the sky;
In number, they were a swarm of black insects.
The Torguts gathered, every one;
Spearheads clashed,
Human heads were struck.
The foremost trampled the people;
Those behind swept over them like a flood.
Without a show of strength, without a word,
Tekeche Khan and Shygay Khan
Fled along the Ala-Too.
Blue-gray clouds gathered in the sky.
Tekeche Khan and Shygay Khan,
The poor men, escaped alive.
Akbaltay of the Noygut,
Your lion, was left a captive.
May God never show us such a sight!
Six san of horses on the hills—
They raided and drove them off now.
Nine san of horses in the mountains—
They drove them away without stopping.
Scattering everything in turmoil,
Thus they brought them to utter ruin.
They slaughtered many souls now,
They took the girls as plunder now,
They carried off the spoils now,
They brought devastation upon them now.
With great ornaments in their braids and broad hips,
The beautiful brides of the Kyrgyz
The Chinese took as plunder without a word.
In red dresses, with slender waists,
The graceful, beautiful Kyrgyz girls
The Black Kalmyks and the Manchu people
Took as plunder now.
First the khan named Karakan had died;
When their khan died, the whole common people perished.
Alöökö and Molto—
The torment of their rule:
They took gold and precious metal by the pood.
If that gold could not be found,
They took the livestock out at pasture.
If even one beast fell short,
They struck off a head and took a life.
The Kyrgyz who rose to attack,
The bloodthirsty one left bereaved.
Those who offered defiance,
He seized and made slaves.
He carried off the spoils,
He wrought great devastation.
Alty-Shahar and Margalan,
And farther down, the khan of Kokand,
The famed sacred capital of sheikhs,
The heartlands of Bukhara and Samarkand—
Their multitudes scattered away.
Alöökö kept hostages;
If you looked at all the people,
Every one was subject to him.
Coming from Beijing in might,
A khan named Alöökö arose.
At the voices of Alöökö
And Molto, lives were lost.
Dominion over the world
Alöökö kept imposing.
Taxes and tribute in gold
Alöökö kept exacting.
If that tax could not be found,
He sent men to be sacrificed;
So Alöökö kept doing.
The torment of Alöökö
Coiled like a dragon.
At the words “Alöökö is coming,”
Weeping children fell silent.
When Alöökö’s wrath rises,
He does grievous things.
Those who provoke his wrath
He lays flat as the black earth.
Those who come before him
He torments and wears down.
When his fury truly takes hold,
Those who come before him
He has beaten by the kaldays.
Fierce in wrath, a khan by title,
Beating his subjects—
Such has been this Chinese ruler’s custom from the beginning.
Upon Margalan, strange wonders
Alöökö kept imposing.
Stiffening, pouring out his wrath,
From how many lands his tribute
Alöökö kept collecting!
“Ah, Karakan, do not die!
Such humiliation as this—
God, if you created us as living souls,
Do not give it to your mortals!”
The populace wept, the people wept,
Women and children all wept.
When the autumn days came,
He levied a full-grown beast from every hearth.
“I will finish you now!” he said,
Bringing calamity upon them all,
Collecting everything, leaving nothing.
When springtime came,
He left them no maned mare to ride;
He gathered them all at once.
When the autumn days came,
He left them no fat-tailed lamb to eat,
He brought his force to bear upon them.
One cauldron for three households,
He made them depend upon a single home.
Those who offered defiance,
He seized and had them beaten.
The whole multitude
He exhausted with havoc.
Those who offered defiance,
He had their hands pinned with nails.
He brought utter oppression upon them.
That whole wretched people
He leveled to the very stump.
When winter days came,
Again saying, “Give them as tribute,”
He began to levy tribute in girls.
Unable to bear his wrath,
All the people
Broke down utterly.
Up and down they wailed;
Countless people writhed in grief.
“Though she was a girl,
She was a child born of our own flesh.
Alas, how could we give her up to death?”
Countless people wept.
“Alöökö’s wrath!
The one God did not deliver us
From this unbeliever’s fury!”
