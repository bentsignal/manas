Three times I unleashed a great slaughter.
On the plain of Ükürchü,
Along Üch-Kapkak,
I raided and took three of your settlements.
It was not merely your horses, my Kalmyk;
I seized your herds as well.
When I sacked your three cities,
When I raided and took your monks,
I cut off
Your monks' topknots entirely.
I took a letter written in Chinese;
I gelded your monk and made him a mount.
I brought a ruinous time upon you!
I dug your grave deep;
I hewed your road backward;
I did wonders indeed!
I traced many strange paths for you;
I burned and destroyed five of your cities.
I brought great humiliation upon you;
Five of your daughters with forelocks
I took as booty.
Have you now become a man?
Have you mounted a horse, looming there?
Yesterday, after traveling seven years on foot,
Above Jet-Kayt,
I destroyed seven of your cities at once.
I plundered much of your treasure;
I stirred up much destructive turmoil.
Eating seven batmans of wheat,
You reek of grain, Joloy;
Having killed seventy giants,
You reek of blood, Joloy.
Eating sixty batmans of wheat,
You reek of grain, Joloy;
Having killed sixty giants,
You reek of blood, Joloy.
Akkula's prize—
Do not take it away; leave it here!
I am no Manas who will let it be taken;
You are no Joloy who can take it!
Because of this prize,
I shall be steeped in bloody blood.
My sprawling Black Kalmyk,
I shall fight for six months without fleeing.
I shall divide your seven cities;
I shall pursue you to the end and die fighting.
Tell of your red spear and its sheath;
Fucking Chinese, tell the truth!”
With a battle cry, Manas
Reached Khan Joloy.
He seized his black-bladed whip
And swung it around;
Charging at Joloy,
He struck him across the jaw.
His black malakay hat
Flew shrieking into the sky.
When Manas attacked,
Everyone now attacked.
The sovereign old Bakai
Chased and scattered the infidels,
Who swarmed like black worms.
Poor Joloy ran,
Taking refuge among his Kalmyks,
Blood streaming from his jaw.
A bruise as large as a horse's head rose;
He barely bound his jaw with a scarf.
A bruise as large as a yurt rose;
Groaning, he bound his jaw with a scarf.
The strongest of the jacketed Chinese
Asked him what had happened:
“Warrior leopard of the Chinese,
What happened to your jaw?” they asked.
