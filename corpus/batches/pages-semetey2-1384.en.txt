Unable to preserve them, he scatters and ruins the people.
He is no just soul fit to be khan;
Kanchoro is a witless fool,
No khan capable of ruling this nation.
Even his ordinary speech is venom;
He is not merely Kanchoro,
But truly the wrath of God.
He is a dark and dismal night,
One of those calamities
That destroy both nation and people.
His eyes are mottled and askew;
He is the very scourge of mankind.
His mischief and his vileness
Are enough for a host of men.
The lionlike sultan, the bloodthirsty hero,
Who did not take this swine’s head,
Crush him utterly, and finish him,
Was nevertheless a true-hearted man,
Of honest purpose and pure intent,
With sincerity in his breast.
This warrior, however, had always
Plotted treachery from the start;
Yet even when he wrecked everything entirely,
The thoroughbred prince, the heroic khan,
Never said, ‘He has ruined us.’”
So the people clamored,
But did they dare speak openly?
They talked in whispers
And stood clicking their tongues;
Wrathful words reached their ears,
But not one soul spoke in protest,
And every head bowed to the ground.
They tormented her frail life,
And the people wept at the sight of Kanykey.
The venerable lady blessed by fortune,
An exemplar to the mighty nation,
The precious legacy left to them
By our gray-maned father Manas,
Was a Night of Destiny for the Kyrgyz,
A sacred soul.
Her golden womb was a treasure;
She was the refuge
Of the dear Argyn and Kyrgyz people.
For the Kyrgyz here in Talas,
She was the black fruit upon the laden bough.
Ah, blessed noblewoman,
She was a whole nation to the multitude,
Rich as pure fat and choice suet,
A mother to all the people.
Look how a valley no multitude had trodden
Has become a common road;
Look how the mother of all the people
Has been brought to disgrace!”
The people wailed and grieved,
Their cries reaching God.
The dear people might have fought,
But had no strength to rescue her;
Each bowed his head to the ground.
The brave men among them
Could not bear to look upon your mother
And collapsed upon their sides.
The courageous lions among them
Looked upon your mother in anguish,
While all the remaining people
Fled in terror and scattered.
Khan Kanchoro began
His oppression and tyranny;
The people wailed and the nation panicked,
And a great many of those who remained
Abandoned all their livestock,
Renouncing everything they owned.
Kanchoro displayed his might
And unleashed his fiercest wrath;
The people trembled and the nation panicked,
And the Kyrgyz fled Talas,
Driven away by his fury.
