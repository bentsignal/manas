My mind is baffled and divided.
My moon-beautiful kinswoman,
I came expressly to see you;
When the leopard-like hero, mighty as a cliff,
When that grey-maned warrior died,
I came expressly to see
The orphaned boy and widowed wife,
And to find a means of helping
Külchoro, whom the spirits uphold.
Seeing the orphaned, the widowed, and the wretched,
My heart was burdened,
Yet my honour was greatly restored
And my exposed shame was covered.
What was severed in me was joined,
And my tiny soul rejoiced;
My eyes have seen the spring flowing down the slope,
And I have no regret.
Today I have seen Seytek, left by the great warrior,
And my heart is content.
The orphaned child and the widowed woman—
I have seen them all and have no regret.”
So saying, Kyzylsart
Threw back his mantle and folded his arms,
Rested his golden-bladed battle-axe
Against his right side,
And, crying “My soul’s friend!” aloud,
Stood sobbing and wailing.
Your noble kinsman Kyzylsart
Spoke these words,
While all wailed aloud in true grief,
And he continued speaking thus.
Kyzylsart had been his friend,
Known as a hero from the beginning;
His dear falcon-like eyes
Did not blink as they filled with tears.
His grey beard thinned with age,
And tears flowing from both eyes
Poured down his breastbone.
As Kyzylsart stood thus,
He addressed moon-faced Aichürök:
“Your noble beauty was like paradise,
But your days have passed in servitude,
And your life has dwindled to a mere handful.
What is seen today is gone tomorrow;
Your heart holds sorrow, your breast burning coals.
This foul world strides about,
Deceiving its mortal servants.
You have no strength to fight,
No power to wrestle;
You have been treated brutally.
Your days have passed in servitude,
And you have been made a lesser wife.
Now that I have come, my kinswoman,
Your broken arm has been bound
And your true honour covered.
The Külchoro for whom you prayed
Has grown to manhood and become a hero;
Every affliction has left him,
And he is restored to what he was before.
From Black Giant, the mighty champion,
Six fleet horses came,
And a letter no larger than a palm.
Grieving at the outrage, I heard bitter news:
I heard that Semetey,
My sworn friend unto the hereafter, was dead,
And all my ribs shattered together.
My upright back was broken;
It was as though God had struck me utterly.
I was parted from the one who was my support,
From my grey-maned lion,
My towering mountain collapsed,
And my dear friend lay fallen.
The smiting God had struck;
The twelve rearguards and forty scouts
Were broken at every ridge.
I was parted from the one who sustained me,
From my true friend and lion.
From broad Egypt came the word:
I heard that Semetey
Had departed from this world.
