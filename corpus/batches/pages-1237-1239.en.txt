Behind those countless myriads of horses
Your dear fellow rode.
He drove the horses onward,
Crashing upon the war drum,
Blasting with his lacquered musket,
Making them clatter and thunder.
Almambet herded them together:
The dense myriads of horses
He set surging ahead of him,
And drove them hard, your Almambet.
Let Almambet, driving the horses,
Continue on his way.
Let us leave him there
And turn to the single combat behind him,
Beginning with the warrior Syrgak.
“Kongurbay has come out to single combat;
Let me die in a skirmish with him.
At least, if we meet,
Let me die fighting face to face.
Taking careful aim at the fierce one,
Let me strike him with my spear.
If the Chinese warrior comes close,
Let me seize him by his forelock.
This time, upon the fierce one,
Let me lay a special grief,” he thought.
He turned Kokchebich’s head,
Your wolf-man, valiant Syrgak.
“When will Kongurbay come?” he said.
He appeared upon a grassy rise,
And stood looking back.
Among the Chinese of Beijing
A khan named Alooko had arisen.
When Alooko’s Kongurbay
Roared, those who heard him
Had their souls driven out by his voice.
“Where is the Kyrgyz, where?” he cried.
“Seize him and bind his hands!”
He had worked himself into a rage.
Looking at the fierce one,
He was truly a giant among mighty men;
Of the Chinese people’s forty khans,
He was indeed the master.
When one looked at his bearing,
He loomed like a towering mountain.
The myriad troops behind him
Were divided into many bands.
But for his unbelief,
His countenance was open and favorable.
The unbelievers’ Kongurbay
Was so much larger than other men.
A single hair of his moustache
Was like a battle-axe’s haft.
His nose was like a mountain spur;
He seemed ready to eat any who struggled.
His eyes were like secluded lake inlets;
He seemed ready to swallow anyone he saw.
The corners of his two eyes
Were like graves left open.
The breadth of his moonlike forehead
Was like the upper summer pasture.
Taking no account of his surroundings,
Sparing his life in no way,
He did not turn and look back.
Look at his valor!
He did not spare his threadlike life.
His finely wrought spear of sharp steel
He thrust out with a shout.
His ravenous steel sword, Beijing’s scourge,
He laid upon his bare wrist.
His flintlock for shooting,
That too he readied,
Kongurbay had taken it up.
Black was his felt cap’s brim;
With no concern for brow or eyebrows,
He watched, licking his lips:
Khan Manas’s Syrgak.
Plain-spoken in this world,
Clear-sighted among humankind,
Alooko’s Kongurbay,
From the moment he saw Syrgak,
Came judging him, the fierce one:
“Kokchebich beneath him—
No beast can match him when urged on.
As for Syrgak upon his back,
No strength can overcome him in a grapple.
He will jolt me wide awake;
He will devastate my people,
Chasing them in helpless flight,
Making rivers of blood flow.
He will slaughter my people,
He will break my back;
Smashing everything to pieces,
He will plunder my land.
Syrgak is indeed a warrior;
He is favored in every way.
His right shoulder is broad.
But for his being Kyrgyz,
He is a lion in this fleeting world.
Cutting a path through rock,
If only he shared my mountain slope!
If only he were Alooko’s youngest,
Born of the same parents as me,
My brother from the same nest!
Syrgak is truly a renowned champion;
Syrgak stands apart from other mortals.
His whole body is well proportioned.
Reaching twenty,
He has come to that age this year.
He is at his swelling prime,
A truly valiant falcon,
In such condition he would strike a rock.
O God, O idol, what shall I do?
If only I could contrive it skillfully,
Have my swift foot soldiers surround him,
And capture Syrgak alive!
With Beijing’s beautiful maidens,
If only I could join him to them!
If a son were born to Syrgak,
I would place my golden crown upon him,
Seat him upon my golden throne,
And over Outer Beijing, forever,
Make him the khan of its watch!
Without turning aside, fighting to the death,
Let me try a face-to-face encounter with Syrgak!”
He did not shrink from his resolve;
