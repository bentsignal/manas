They began a campaign and raised a tumult,
Coming there to wage battle.
The accomplished champion Kuyaly
Turned Zhelzhetpes by the reins
And rode out to single combat.
Such was the nature of the conflict.
Sarybai, son of Zhelmoguz,
Was a fiend who had slaughtered many;
At the sight of him, abandon hope of life.
Sarybai, son of Zhelmoguz,
Had grown fierce from drinking blood
And was known among the people as a monster.
When he attacked, he never had his fill of blood;
Once he set upon a man, he never spared his life.
Of all that ears had heard and eyes had seen,
Nothing like him had existed on earth.
Of all the news the people had heard,
Nothing like him had existed among mankind.
His valor was of another kind,
And boundless was his devouring greed.
His charger was swift, and he himself a towering peak;
No such horror existed in the world.
His strength was singular, his venom fierce;
If you saw that infidel before you,
Count yourself dead and abandon hope.
He inflicted tyranny upon the age
And brought humiliation upon mankind.
He began savage conflicts;
Whoever challenged that mad brute,
He slaughtered outright.
He could drink blood and never be sated;
Among all the sons of humankind,
No such creature had ever been born.
Dust rose from those he attacked;
Life fled from those he grappled.
That swine’s ways were unlike all others;
He was born in Zhelpinish,
And the living heard of his wrath.
Does he subdue and seize men—
Was there witchcraft in his mother?
Against that black-hearted swine,
Could anyone who faced him remain alive?
Though a man girded himself, saying, “I am a warrior,”
Could any mortal go against him?
This infidel Sarybai
Was thick-muscled and stone-hearted,
And spread great turmoil through the world.
His cunning was strange, his venom fierce;
When angered and enraged,
He would level the whole wide world.
Upon his two shoulders,
If they rashly ventured there,
Two men apiece could have sat.
At the blaze of his two cheeks,
Six Dhangyt men apiece might feast.
If anyone provoked his anger,
The mortal who grappled with him
He seemed able to swallow, horse and all.
He had a head like half a mountain,
A head like a dog lying down.
He wore a coat broad as ten yurt-roof felts.
His arms were longer than plane trees.
His complexion was strange, his appearance blue-black;
He wore a black iron cap.
His nose was hooked, his face mottled;
His menace was singular, and he was wrath itself.
His nature was corrupt, though he was a warrior;
No mortal dared advance against him.
In Zhelpinish, seventy
Giants had fought him without victory.
A bane to the people, copper-snouted,
No one had seen such a savage face.
No ground remained untrodden by his gray charger;
Nothing on earth remained whole.
Nothing escaped slaughter by the accursed swine;
Nothing among the people remained whole.
When he advanced in a fit of fury,
Even titans scattered without fighting.
From cradles where they lay crying,
Speechless infants were crushed.
