Your uncle Külchoro is such a man:
Your support when you climb,
Your prop when you descend;
Your clear road across the hills,
Your countless army when he leads.
My gray fledgling falcon
Would not let a bustard escape the steppe.
I am black steel with a lamb-liver sheen,
Such as no smith could find.
I am your warrior, proven among heroes;
When I lead the first attack,
I am your keen-eyed Choro of old.
Not merely keen-eyed Külchoro—
I am one tested by many nations.
If you thrust me against a stubborn foe,
I am a sharp, steel-headed spear,
Aimed exactly where you intend.
If you unleash me upon the chosen foe,
I am a dark, double-edged blade.
I am Külchoro indeed,
The renowned lion and tiger
Who crushes the foe he faces.
Set the dawn as our appointed time
And take your uncle Kül as companion.
I shall teach you what you do not know,
And knowingly lead you against the foe.
I shall be a banner to your kin;
If you unleash me upon your enemy,
I shall be a singular black poison
That bursts lungs and spills gall.
While your tiger father lived,
He would wreak utter slaughter
Upon the Chinese encamped on the heights.
If I was left afoot in dangerous ground,
Your precious gray-maned lion of a father
Would take me up behind him.
He never said, ‘There is no room behind me,’
Nor, ‘Let this poor wretch
Stay safely behind to tend the horses.’
I possessed the strength for battle.
Your forefather was a gray-maned lion.
The baggage train and scouting,
The fighting and lionheartedness,
Every manner of heroic deed—
While my mighty uncle lived,
He would say, ‘Let my Külchoro learn.’
Parted from that mighty hero,
I became like a magpie without a tail,
Like a kulan caught in a snare.
I became like a man without feet,
Like a writhing snake.
Sobbing, lamenting, and grieving,
Parted from the bloodthirsty lion,
I was left like a lesser wife.
Parted from my soul’s companion,
I was left helpless.
Let us head upstream through broad Talas,
Land of rivers and springs.
Do not be headstrong, my Seytek;
Let the two of us go together.
Let us go and possess the land of Talas,
And lead the white-capped Kyrgyz people,
Raising an uproar before all who see,
Raising a clamor to the blue sky.
Let the two of us go together.
If you take the road alone,
You are not yet past thirteen;
The fierceness of your mother’s breast
Has not yet left your palate.
Your strength and vigor are not yet full;
The time has not yet come
For you to ride out alone.
Choose the hour of dawn for our meeting
And take your uncle Kül as companion.
If you take your uncle with you,
He will show you your grandsire Bakai.
If, my colt, you display your skill
And accomplish many heroic deeds,
When you reach the place where warriors clash,
Your servant-uncle will hold your horse.
