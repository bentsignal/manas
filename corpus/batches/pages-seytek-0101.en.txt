The grief burst from his heart;
He burned in anguish and rage,
And despaired of his precious life.
He sobbed in bitter pain,
Abandoning hope of life as though already dead.
When he saw Külchoro,
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Burst into violent rage,
And Külchoro truly
Renounced his fragile life.
He readied himself like a hawk;
What could poor Choro do?
An inspiration came to him as if from the unseen,
And when he saw brave Kyyaz,
Külchoro grew flustered.
The strength drained from his arms,
The courage fled his heart,
His legs hung loose,
His eyes darted wildly,
And he stood stricken with folded hands.
To Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
He humbly offered greeting:
“Peace upon peace be with you,
Noble, illustrious hero Kyyaz!
Your slave has come prepared,
My lord, to plead with you!”
When he saw Külchoro,
He knew his enemy at once,
And Kyyaz, intending to kill him,
Smiled beneath his mustache.
He roared in fury
And spoke to Karagul:
“Ah, Karagul, you wretch!
Why did you not start a bloody fray
With this rabid, cunning slave,
Slaughter him before now,
And finish him for good?
Did I not tell you earlier?
Did he not hear it then?
Without letting them gouge out his glaring eyes,
The pale mare shielded him.
She laid her head upon his shoulder
And stood pressed close to him.
She had no tongue with which to speak,
But showing every sign of love,
The pale mare stood sniffing him.
She has a tail and she has a mane—
Such a creature she is.
Everyone has an animal they cherish.
Oh, dear pale mare,
Rekindle my extinguished fire
And restore my dead soul to life!
She truly was a peerless steed.
That day brave Kyyaz
Appeared upon the hillock.
When enraged, from his mouth
Black flames seemed to pour;
He looked ready to strike down all before him.
His brows were like storm clouds,
And his gaze seemed ready to swallow a soul;
His eyes were like the depths of a lake,
And seeing him, one thought he might devour a man.
His nose was like a jutting ridge,
His force as hard as iron;
He seemed a soul never sated with blood,
A mighty wrestler born to rule a people.
Thick-armed and stone-hearted,
The fierce Kyyaz, if you saw him,
Was a hulking brute of massive build.
His upturned mustache
Was like a Bukhara saber;
His lower lip sagged wide,
Like a grain merchant’s sack.
His two eyes burned like fire,
Like flames leaping from a hearth;
Such was the appearance
Of grave-devouring Kyyaz.
When he saw Khan Kyyaz,
The lion-born Choro
Lost all strength in his arms.
