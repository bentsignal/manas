Made Ajybay drink,
Dressed him in Jekeyboz,
And mounted Ajybay
On the dark swift camel that flew like a cloud,
Outstripping the wind,
For I had conceived a wondrous plan,
And was carrying out that wonder.
To poor Ajybay
I commanded, “Find the warrior maiden,”
Thus giving him my order.
“Range across the breadth of the earth,
Traverse the whole world,
Reach and explore it all.
Through the world’s four corners
Travel everywhere,
And of all the created earth
Leave not one place unsearched.
Visit the five corners of the known world,
Circle them five times,
And discover the known abode
Of that valiant, ill-fated maiden.
If by chance you return without seeing her,
Then may you behold the land of no return,
And cease to walk among the living.
If you are truly a formidable man,
If you, Ajybay, are a lion,
Discover the ancestry
And lineage of that hapless one.
If you will not go and search the earth,
If you ignore your sister-in-law’s courteous words
And fail to find the warrior maiden,
Your words will become mere boasting;
You have received such wealth,
And it will bring you shame.
Your words will sound frivolous,
Disgraceful talk will fall
Upon my unhappy self.
I was the mother of the teeming people,
The fruit of the lofty plane tree,
Their queen and khan’s lady.
You, dear pilgrim, were the people’s mediator,
A unifier among the multitude.
You were a man of surpassing wisdom,
The finest of the Argyn khans,
The khan of the forty companions.
Sharp-sighted while alive,
Clear in answering an enemy’s words,
May your road be open when you journey;
While you live, ride forth against the foe.
The sun shone beside your moon,
You knew seventy kinds of speech,
You sharpened the honored tongue
And spoke eloquently among the people.
When hardship came, you found a way,
And struck a bloodthirsty enemy
With all the authority of a khan.
Great orator, cultivator of wisdom,
You were the eye of swift-footed heroes.
Sage who cultivated wisdom,
You were the very master of great knowledge.
Heed these precious words:
Learn some news
Of that formidable warrior maiden.
If you fail to learn the truth,
Do not appear before my eyes,
And do not approach me.
May poison enter your blood,
Should Ajybay fail to return
From the city of Jangdüng-bash.
I, unfortunate Kanykey,
Was once a discerning soul.
What would come and what would pass,
What would be and what would not,
Joy and suffering,
Calamity and death—
There was once a naked child
Upon my right shoulder,
Beneath my ten locks of hair,
Who foretold them at once.
