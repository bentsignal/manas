Among the fourteen horses were
The mighty swift horse Buurul;
Kanykei's courser Karakaska;
Sarykan's Tuuchunak;
Kobosh Khan's Kokborchuk;
Abyke's Aktelki;
The sturdy Archa-Toru;
Chyiyrdy's Bozjorgo;
The spotted red and the blue with a mane;
Khan Bakai's Kokcholok;
Taitoru, who came in mourning
When noble Manas died;
Bagysh Khan's Surkiyik;
Muzburchak's Telkyzyl;
Shaiymbet's Akkokul;
The maiden's red pacer;
And Chachykei's Tik-Toru.
He hemmed in the fourteen horses;
The lion roared
And beat them without restraint.
Shouting in fury,
He called, "Khan Bakai's Kokcholok
Must lead!"
Then Khan Bakai's Kokcholok
Outstripped the horses and led the way.
At the sound of the uproar he sprang apart;
He had no tongue with which to tell it,
But the marks of a true courser showed.
He reached the swiftly flowing Urgench
And entered it at the head of the line.
When Kokcholok entered the water,
All fourteen horses pitched forward
And plunged fully into the river.
At the place where they entered, the Great Urgench
Drove all fourteen horses
As though pursuing an enemy.
The Urgench flowed with a thunderous roar;
Its fierce current showed plainly.
All fourteen horses
Were swept along, floundering like kids,
And separated from one another.
Your unfortunate lion of a lord,
When the horses entered the water,
Entered the river with them.
His hands were soaked in the water;
Then the lion-like lord
Pleaded with the one God.
Behind him came Kanchoro:
"If I am left and parted from my elder brother,
To what grave can I go?" he thought.
He placed his belt around his neck,
Caught hold of the hem, crying, "Elder brother!"
And entered with him, pleading.
All fourteen horses
Strained to swim upstream;
Semetey went on then,
Struggling with the fourteen horses.
Zulpukor, the gift left to him
By his gray-maned father Manas—
He drew the sword from its sheath and swung it,
And the wave that came straight at him
He cut clean across.
They resisted the water,
Fighting against the flowing current.
He gathered force like a cuckoo;
The lion emerged from the water,
Scolding Kulchoro.
Putting forth all his strength,
The lion barely made it out, as you see;
The fourteen horses that came from Ogoi
Emerged alive, as you see.
Looming at the head of them,
The hero came out of the Urgench.
His chosen Kyrgyz companions
Numbered twenty-five souls;
Each was a tiger worth a thousand men,
And they emerged alive from the Urgench.
