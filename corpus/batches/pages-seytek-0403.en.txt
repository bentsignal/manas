You have kindled a smokeless fire.
You began with fierce wrath,
You cast your head into calamity.
You cooked him before he was ripe,
Kanchoro, destined to die when ripe,
You burned him before he could burn,
You warrior who will burn to death!
You bloodied the mouth of my gate,
You trampled Talas into dust,
You seized Ala-Too for your land,
You oppressed the Kyrgyz and made them your subjects.
Poor women and young children,
Bent old folk and growing youth—
Why did you abase them so?
Having deprived me of my hero,
Is courage and valor yours?
Was he one to be given as spoil to the enemy?
Was your protector’s vengeance ours to bear?
Yesterday, smashing and toppling the house,
Felling and bringing down the plane tree,
The inheritance left by our fathers,
The far-sighted one who saw from afar,
Before whom the earth split and shook,
My lion father who passed into the other world,
The one befriended by my lion father,
Who sharpened steel and shared his heart,
Who spoke with the smith David,
He was the keeper of the treasury,
The guardian giant of the khan’s wealth.
No man could ever encounter his like;
Among all sons of humankind,
Even Baabedin could not find one like him.
The master smith Bölökbay,
An eagle on the narrow cliff,
The inheritance left by our fathers—
You made him demand the treasury,
Driving him clamorous like a lean sheep,
With forty Kalmyks, Bölökbay
You contrived to settle his account.
Shouting as you handed him a sword,
The countless riches left by the hero,
As booty among the Jediger—
I did not come to take them, dear brother-in-law.
Where is your magnanimity, where is the lion?
Where is the strength on which we relied?
Born superior to mortal men,
Who struck terror into the enemy,
When sent in front, a bringer of fortune,
From behind, a vast army with ridged ranks.
Where are our blessings, where is our strength,
Where are the people who gave us hope?
Having destroyed your bloodthirsty lord,
You have taken the palace and become khan,
Both reins and the single bridle
You have now taken into your hands.
You no longer look as you once did;
You have become a sultan!
Yesterday, when the gray-maned wolf died,
When all the world was made into plunder
And apportioned by the measure of a whip,
You loaded mourning goods on the pacer,
You abandoned the palace to pillage.
You had trench upon trench dug,
The great palace left by the khan
You led away amid cheers and much urging,
And drove it into Jediger hands.
Making me render my account,
Making wounds split across my breast,
You utterly exhausted my tears,
You made my head a prize.
You let the loathsome enemy drink
The funeral feast left by the gray-maned wolf.
You disgraced all my hopes and desires;
Alas, there is a God to judge your cruelty.
You made them call him another man’s son,
My child left of my khan.
Every day you seared my soul;
By giving me as booty to the enemy,
You burned my soul again and again.
You became my enemy and cast me aside,
