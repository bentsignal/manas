He was a man without a child;
Calling Karadöö his son,
He had adopted him as his child.
He dressed him in the finest coat,
Mounted him on a champion steed,
Raised him to maturity and made him a man,
Made him equal to every hero's son;
No one knew his renown.
The great spectacle and mighty commotion
The gathered people watched,
Saw the challenge he would bring against Kyyaz.
There is much that could be told;
He had been abused and plundered,
And bore a grudge against Khan Kyyaz.
Karadöö, a mass of fury,
Burned, unable to endure it;
To start a true conflict,
His four eyes strained,
Burning and blazing like an ember,
Restrained only by his being an only son.
If Karadöö had a companion,
He would spare his fly-sized life for nothing;
He himself stood ready to fight on foot.
He went on drawing the line,
Ready to begin the disorder,
Disturbing every mind;
The hero Kyyaz was the one he meant to attack—
That was the calamity he intended.
This gray-maned one was different, his gaze direct;
Many people spoke of him plainly.
He shot forward like a striking snake,
And would not stop
Until he reached the renowned land of Talas.
Then Karadöö appeared:
His courage was extraordinary,
His strength was immense.
He was a ridge and a mountain,
A hero one could lean upon;
He strode forth to meet the wrestler
And faced him in the ring.
The Zhediger wrestler—
He seized him by the nape;
Before anyone could say a word,
In the blink of an eye,
He lifted him and hurled him down.
The abundant wealth offered as prizes
His father, old Kutman,
Gathered and kept together.
In strength and courage
There was no one like Karadöö;
No one cared
That he belonged to Semetey's people.
When the poles bent,
For staking victory in the arena,
The gray five-year-old among the booty
Was an extraordinary champion steed.
He mounted the gray five-year-old;
The mail that no bullet could pierce,
He wore against his body.
Over it he wore
A broad black felt cloak,
The poor fellow striding grandly.
Would he retreat even from flame?
Would anyone call the guarded Zhediger
A true man?
Would this giant turn back from the contest?
With their spears made ready,
For the arena on the stony hilltop,
He prepared to enter the mounted contest.
The gray five-year-old beneath him
Had mattock-shaped hooves and broad hindquarters,
A champion steed of broad Talas,
Well proportioned in every way;
Its fatness was like a gun barrel,
It leapt like a flea,
Flattened its ears like a kulan;
The noble creature came of champion stock
And churned the earth like a whirlwind.
