Wearing armor no bullet pierced,
Clad in deep-blue iron,
When told he was going to Beijing,
As though going to his in-laws,
Ill-fated Külchoro
Laughed openly in joy.
The sight stirred the heart;
In the companion going to Beijing
The aspect of a gray wolf appeared.
A gold-cheeked battle-axe
Was fitted snugly at his wrist.
Beyond him came
Kanchoro, son of Chubak—
Huge as half a mountain,
Fit to overcome any opponent,
His courage precious, his wrath strong,
He leveled whoever attacked.
This was the strength bound at the waist,
This was the hero left as a legacy!
The figure there in front
Was the young son of Manas,
His support on the road to Beijing!
His form was like a craggy mountain,
From lion hero Chubak,
The young man left as a support.
Semetey's dependable men,
Kanchoro and Külchoro,
The two dependable twins,
His sole companions at the cairn,
The comrades who would support
Your mighty Semetey.
When their figures came forward,
Yesterday, preparing to besiege Kakan,
As your dear prince stood there,
Like an army of six divisions appeared
The figures of those two.
Külchoro rode
Hero Toltoi's Surkoön.
Kanchoro rode
Khan Köbösh's Köktelki.
It never tired when ridden on reconnaissance,
Never ailed when ridden with the baggage train,
Never wearied when ridden into gunfire;
Until it reached sixty years,
Its tusklike teeth did not age.
Unless not a handspan of rein remained,
It would not yield to a determined foe;
Unless a thousand true disasters struck,
It would not let its rider be felled.
Driving fourteen horses from Ogoy,
Saying, “I shall gird my waist firmly,
From Kongurbai in Beijing
I shall exact my heroic father's blood-price.
Kongurbai is the one I shall face;
I shall bring chaos.
If gracious God supports me,
I shall avenge the bloodthirsty hero.”
Settling himself like a lion,
For seething Beijing
The four warriors made ready.
Saying, “If death comes, I shall die;
The countless dark livestock gathered as tribute,
Kongurbai's myriad herds,
If I live, I shall raid and return.
Across the tops of the teeming herds
I shall ride,
The Kalmak who descended upon you—
I shall scatter all four kinds of his livestock.”
Angrily making ready,
With Khan Bakai at their head,
The bloodthirsty youth mounted.
His grandmother Chyiyrdy
And his own mother Kanykei
Had a white-gray mare caught,
Had it sacrificed to the moon-shaped hoof,
And spread their hands, saying, “Amen!”
The sorrowing elders
Spread not a few but many hands in prayer,
