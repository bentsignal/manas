Has he fallen into their hands?
The decree laid down by God—
Has he met it?
He was a stouthearted boy;
Did he strike into a massed army
And fall dead?" he wondered.
His fire-like eyes blazed fiercely;
His true bloodlust seized him.
He threw the blue armor-coat around himself
And pleaded with the Almighty.
To train the spyglass upon the boy,
To learn news from the maidens,
He strained like a blue tiger
And made ready with the spyglass.
From an unexpected place no horse could cross,
From behind a low, forked rise,
He concealed his dark body
And cautiously raised his head;
He hid his whole frame
And brought his head into line.
He took the great spyglass inherited from Manas,
Turned its wheel,
And set it squarely to his eye;
He turned its screw
And focused it upon his eye.
When he turned the wheel,
That wondrous spyglass
Divided its lens into six,
And a road six days distant
Appeared clearly, without distortion.
Its lens was made of precious stone;
When he turned it to its full extent,
It divided into seven parts,
And a road seven days distant
Appeared as close as seven rope-lengths.
The great and precious spyglass
Was wound with gold inside;
Master caster Bolokbai
Had repaired every damaged place.
The lion grew furious,
Turned its wheel,
And set it squarely to his eye.
Beside the flowing river,
Across the plain of Great Opol,
The lion, the son of Manas,
Looked through the spyglass.
Without being consumed by grief or worry,
Without saying, "My uncle stands
Stranded behind me,"
Thirty brides and thirty maidens,
The immoderate Churok, and the boy—
All of them, struck with amazement,
Had gathered around him;
Look at their fawning attention:
They had encircled him.
Seeing this, Semetey thought:
"When he reaches his full maturity,
He will surely conquer a mighty realm;
When he grows into a true man,
When he equals the sons of heroes,
He will indeed show me
Marvels beyond imagining!
