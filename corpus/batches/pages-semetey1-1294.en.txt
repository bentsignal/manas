When Semetey granted him his turn,
Those dear eyes like black currants
He nearly gouged out.
That black-hearted dog Khojo
The orphaned son of Manas
He nearly slaughtered.
His kinsman became his enemy,
And within the leopard prince and sultan
An enemy arose.
Unable to stand his burning rage,
The falcon prince and sultan
Revealed his gray-wolf nature.
His dear teeth, broad as a gate,
Teeth unlike those of ordinary people,
Showed apart like grains of millet;
From his mouth blue flame
Poured as though from a loaded gun.
His eyes burned like embers,
Stirring the heart of whoever saw him;
He looked like a blue tiger.
His wrath darkened his face;
The great and towering Buurul—
He pulled the reins and turned its head.
The sultan’s Syrnaiza, which drank blood
Whenever he leveled it,
He gripped at full length and thrust.
Seizing and swinging his spear,
He drew the great Buurul
Hard beneath his thighs;
Without looking around,
Your ocean-like hero charged Khojo,
With no care at all for his life.
On the spur of the broken mountain,
At the looming promontory,
He arrived ahead of an arrow;
On reaching him, he drove the spear.
That expert Khojo
Took his battle-axe in hand
And knocked the spear aside before it struck.
When the spear passed beyond him,
And flew out without touching him,
At his side was a small golden pouch,
In his right hand the gold-cheeked battle-axe.
The lion-hero, seized by rage,
Gripped it and swung,
At the lordly Khojo
He brought it down upon the crown.
Lord Khojo then
Semetey’s violent humiliation
Was made to behold.
From the axe that struck his crown,
He nearly fell dead.
From the axe that struck his crown,
His shield burst apart;
His fly-like life shrieked,
His foot slipped from the stirrup,
And he slid backward from the saddle.
His two eyes rolled,
His two legs hung loose,
His wineskin-like nose flapped,
And both coat skirts fluttered.
He lost his wits,
And toward a saddle-shaped ridge
Khojo fled upward.
Your command-bearing gray wolf
Now turned Buurul’s head.
Akkelte, inherited
From his father, lion Manas,
He took from his shoulder.
Its match burned like an ember;
It had long stood loaded.
When he took it in hand
And leveled it at Khojo,
The purest aid to marksmanship,
Its forked rest stood ready.
“At the joint of the elbow—
This, as you see, is truly
The hour of Khojo’s death,” he said,
