Give my greetings and blessing
To your openhearted prince;
Do not let it be said that, coming for nothing,
He was killed by Toltoi."
When Külchoro had said this—
Surpassingly eloquent and also a lion,
The quick-witted boy Kulchoro—replied:
"The man upon whom you have set your hopes,
Whom you have found as your firm support,
Is he your famous husband Kojo?
I shall break down your fortress walls;
I shall smash to pieces
Your hulking fool of a husband, Toltoi!
You have found the Kara-Kyr ridge,
Over which even a goose will not circle, as your support;
You have found Tolok and Tolobai,
Kalcha and Kadyrbai,
The finest of the Jediger,
To be your husbands!
The men upon whom you have set your hopes—
If my precious head does not die,
I shall crush to flour
The husband destined to take you!
My uncle's Akshumkar
Does not gather its wings and swoop,
Nor send the geese flying from the lake;
It does not gather its tail and swoop,
Nor send the swans flying from the lake.
Upon Akshumkar's feet
Are finely fitted by Kanykei
Golden jesses with knobs.
May your road be fortunate, sister-in-law;
If you have lost the falcon,
Seventy maidens like you
Have a claim against you!
Its hood is exceedingly fine;
It has golden, knobbed jesses.
If you have lost the falcon,
Among maidens like you,
Ninety-one have a claim against you!
Let us announce the reward
For the falcon that disappeared;
If you make us wander, we shall turn back.
If you make us wander, sister-in-law,
The blame will fall upon your father, Khan Akun!"
When Aichurok heard this,
She sat looking sidelong
At Choro, the son of Almambet,
Taking in his full bearing.
She stood watching, deeply moved;
What could your dear sister-in-law do
But plead with Kulchoro:
"This Kulchoro standing here
Has every fortune in balance.
Except for his being a child,
His uncle Semetey
Must be an even greater lion than he.
There are boys of twelve
Who cannot manage to go out through the door,
Cannot cover their hems when they urinate,
And feel no shame before their mothers;
Far from speaking at length, those boys
Cannot utter two or three words.
For a boy such as this,
Let my dark life be a sacrifice!"
Having said this, Aichurok
Abandoned her taunting words
And began to speak plainly:
"My dear son,
What you said is also true.
Yesterday, stripping the weariness from my horse,
I flew and wandered
Throughout the whole wide world.
To distant Beijing and Kakan-Chyn,
I flew all the way;
Finding no upright hero to my liking,
I turned and came back.
