He terrified my people.
Grieving that I could not reach him,
I despaired of my borrowed life.
I let him consume all I had amassed;
Before all whose ears had heard
And whose eyes had seen,
I was called the powerless Choro.
Breaking through the herd and opening a road,
He plundered all my property.
He wrought many a violent outrage
Upon my happy, fortunate days.
He raided my horses on their pasture
And utterly laid waste my wealth.
My power exhausted, my strength gone,
I fought but could not prevail.
Writhing in shame, my wretched soul
Burned beyond endurance,
And I grew bitter with myself.
I wished to die, yet could not die,
Unable to cast away this needed life.
Those who grappled with the infidel,
The son of Sarybai, swiftly died;
A hero died when he faced him.
From the venom of the infidel
Who came out to stand guard,
All who grappled with that slave died.
I fought him alone in single combat
And was deeply aggrieved by that swine;
To be left alive where others died—
Of all that ears have heard and eyes have seen,
I have seen no such thing among mankind.
Of all that people know and nations have witnessed,
I battled with that infidel slave;
I filled myself with a lamb’s fat,
Yet found this hell unbearably vast.
I strained, but my strength was not enough;
I could not defeat that mighty one.
My death had come, yet I did not die.
Never before had I seen such hell
Upon the face of the earth.
Since my father watched and my mother bore me,
Since I became Külchoro,
Since I grew up an orphan,
And joined the ranks of men on earth,
Since I sprang up and became a hero,
The equal of heroic sons,
Since I mounted a horse with legs astride,
Became a warrior and sought the foe,
And went through life as Külchoro;
Since I girded on my keen-edged sword,
Our uncle, who stood in our father’s place,
Led me with him and made me a hero,
And made me equal to heroic sons.
By raiding, he brought me fortune;
By building, he gave me a home,
And made me a khan of noble lineage.
He delivered me from orphanhood,
And when the time came to live in joy,
Saying, “May his heart be rich with fat,”
He fed me slices of the choicest meat.
Saying, “Let no difference remain between them,”
He had me share the breast
Of my mother Kanykei equally.
She tucked a letter into her hem
And, saying, “May he grow immense,”
Gave me the name Külchoro.
She hung an amulet about my neck,
And my sorrowing mother Kanykei
Had Kanykei herself raise me.
She erased the name of slave,
Saying, “He is your brother beneath the sun,”
And restored this ruined soul.
Raising and nurturing me, she made me great,
And made me renowned throughout the world.
My weapons numbered eighty-four,
And when I struck, I struck like fire.
At the hands of me, Külchoro,
Giants died who came to wrestle,
And peoples of many nations died.
Khans died who came to grapple,
