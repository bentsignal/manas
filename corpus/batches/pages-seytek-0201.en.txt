A descendant of Er Kutubiy,
The very apple of the hero’s eye,
The son of the warrior Muraapy,
Black Giant, who had come as a captive,
Had remained behind seeking a remedy
And had found Kyzylsart.
This poor heir of Muraapy
Had joined what was severed
And gathered what was scattered.
Across the four corners of the world
He had galloped in every direction,
And Black Giant had found and brought
The great healer Kyzylsart.
God had granted their wish!
When he came and looked inside the tent,
The heart of Chürök, the hero’s daughter,
Was overwhelmed with emotion,
For Kyzylsart, friend of the grey-maned hero,
Appeared there in the tent.
When he saw Aichürök,
His heart was overcome,
And seeing his friend’s wife, Kyzylsart
Shed tears from his eyes:
“My dear kinswoman Aichürök,
Parted from your noble lord,
You have wandered homeless and forlorn,
Wrapping yourself as though to bind your shroud,
Yet swallowing hard as though you would never die;
You became a captured soul,
A miserable prisoner of Kyyaz,
Yearning for just one glimpse
Of the countless Kyrgyz clans.
While the sea-like grey-maned hero lived,
He was an encircling net of iron;
But when that sea-like hero passed away,
You became Kyyaz’s despised spoil.
You have faded pale as white feather grass,
Parted from your noble lord,
And become Kyyaz’s captive prize.”
The bloom had fallen from her face;
Tormented by thoughts of her brother-in-law Choro and Seytek,
The woman trembled with grief.
She crossed a rise curved like a saddle’s back,
Bending forward as she passed over it,
Then entered the broad valley
Of the dense, tangled forest.
On the gently rolling forked ridge,
Upon a rise no wider than a palm,
A reddish mist appeared,
And smoke rose beyond the crest.
Brightly coloured bundles
Marked where Kyzylsart lay.
Smoke streamed into the sky,
A white tent came into view,
With a bearskin and a white gyrfalcon,
And the horse of Black Giant could be seen.
When Chürök saw all this,
The poor woman rejoiced
And tears poured from her eyes.
The great healer Kyzylsart—
A star had stirred in the heavens at his birth;
His fathers and forefathers
Had been friends with the noble lord.
They had shared grains of white wheat,
And, vowing to stand by one another,
Had clasped the holy creed;
They had licked the edge of a sword
And sworn a solemn oath.
This was the great healer himself,
The cherished dark eye of those who awaited him;
Honouring the ancestral spirits, the poor man
Had arrived at just this time.
God had granted her wish:
For Külchoro, hero of heroes,
His helper had arrived.
Formerly my heir, Kutubiy,
Was precious in the eyes of my princely father,
And to my forefather who passed before
He was his own appointed heir,
A descendant of the modest line—
