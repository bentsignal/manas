Disaster has overwhelmed him.
The blue coat upon me,
Its hem worn out and sleeves turned gray—
How can I wear it now?
Algara beneath me,
His bushy tail and beauty gone,
Looks about with his rump twitching—
With what face can I ride him?!
Today, my people, mark the date;
Eighty days from today,
Enter it in the register.
Take off your boots, stretch out your legs,
Grow fat and regain your strength!
When those eighty days have passed,
Then gather together,
Assemble without breaking ranks,
And obey this command.
Let not one of you remain behind;
Come to me, every one of you…”
Thus Kongurbay declared,
The great blue-gray-bearded Manchu,
The hulking swine.
He began his fury in earnest,
Saying, “This is the ruin awaiting whoever stays behind.”
Raging and snarling,
Roaring like a mountain boar,
He gave orders without cease.
He began a costly spectacle;
As for the people who had come,
And saying, “One who fails to come will see,”
See the tyranny of the Kalmyk—
He had them slaughtered.
He exalted his bloody rule above the people.
The tail of Algara
That hero-swine had fashioned
And ornamented with gold.
He had a tail stretched out in gold,
And to the craftsman who made it
He gave sixty fine young maidens,
Forty girls, to the craftsmen.
When the false tail was finished,
He had it fitted onto Algara.
Alooke’s Kongurbay,
Mounted Algara, whom no bullet could reach,
That playful thoroughbred steed;
He donned the blue coat no bullet pierced,
Such was the coat he chose to wear.
When Kongurbay grew enraged,
His hair bristled toward his face;
Such was the nature of the doomed one.
He fastened on his arms,
And pulled a deep-blue iron helmet
Down over his head.
His black hair, thick as a cudgel,
The infidel tied behind him
In two knotted sections.
Saying, “If I fight, I will be ready,”
He left none of his weapons behind;
The fortunate one put them on.
Double mail with lark-like rings,
Its collar distinct, its sleeves brass,
Made those it disliked curse;
Steel had been filed and worked into it,
And poured over every doubtful place.
No gun muzzle could bite it,
No spear point could penetrate it;
The mesh armor for his ribs—
The formidable Kalmyk donned
The iron that guarded his life.
Over the breadth of his shoulders
He again drew on iron.
The reason he equipped himself so
Was that when mighty Manas lived,
When Akkula and Algara
Pulled evenly, reins turned,
And they shouted from horseback,
Trading spear thrusts,
He had taken many a spear blow,
