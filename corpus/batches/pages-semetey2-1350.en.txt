When I descend, you are the one I lean on.
Kanchoro and Kulchoro—
Both are my own younger kinsmen.
Almambet and Chubak are my two fathers;
These two are the legacy they left.
These heroes are like twins,
Lions who could shake the world.
With what face could I divide them?
These two youths whose names rhyme—
With what face could I part them?
If anger seizes this poor soul,
I shall gather my white-capped people.
I shall bind the two namesake boys
As a talisman at my waist
And face whatever enemy approaches.
Ah, unfortunate Mother, what are you saying?
Mother, who should hear these words of yours?
Let the sightless night hear them;
Let the towering mountains hear them;
Let the enemy shouting fierce cries
And hollow battle shouts hear them;
Let the dug black grave hear them;
Let the deep earth beneath hear them.
Pretending wisdom, suffering Mother,
You sent my father, now gone to Judgment,
Creeping by day
And whispering by night,
Out on poison raids.
Estranging him from those close to him,
Making him endure countless afflictions,
Mounting him for holy war,
Making him grieve and burn,
You sent him on his way.
My radiant father Manas—
You, Mother, sent him on that road!
My magnanimous father Manas,
Mountainlike as Ala-Too,
You broke and drove from his path,
Turned his mother's kin into enemies,
And brought him to sudden ruin!
Ah, my valiant, unfortunate mother,
You would strip him of mane and tail
And leave him like a wild ass;
Deprive him of walking feet
And leave him like a snake;
Like a stone a hunter casts from a mountain,
You would leave him a stone;
Like something gnawed clean by a ravenous man,
Mother, you would leave him like a bare head.
Bending reason and devising thoughts,
You would turn him into an enemy,
And afterward, upon my head,
Unfortunate Mother, come to no good,
You would raise an enemy!
Upon that sheltering rounded hill
You would raise a mountain;
Upon the head of my closest one
You would raise an enemy.
Do not divide my thoughts;
Do not speak strangely like this
Of the boy who is our support.”
So Semetey spoke.
Though he was khan and lord, he would not listen;
He could not understand Kanchoro’s conduct,
My ill-fated son.
My words meant to calm him
Only brought disgrace upon me.
He called my words corrupt
And put me to shame.
Because of past events,
Because of Kanchoro,
For six months he would not look at me;
When I spoke, he would not heed my words,
Having little trust in me.
He had an amulet hung at his neck,
His grandmother was Chyiyrdy;
He had Chyiyrdy raise him,
With Ajybai carrying him on his back,
