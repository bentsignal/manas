Unless the one Creator alone knows how,
My child, no living human
Can withstand him.
When he charges in fury,
Every living creature
Flees without looking back.
Sarybai, son of a monster—
Such is his savagery.
Father, what a black-hearted swine!
He has fixed his eyes upon the khan;
When they meet in collision,
He will rend him till his life is stilled.
Many champions have fallen before him;
He tore all the honorable people
Like tufts of spring wool.
Because of that ill-fated swine,
That infidel who devastated the nation,
That calamity fallen upon the people,
The host wailed and the khan died;
All who fought him died.
Heroes died in the mounted struggle,
Many mighty wrestlers and lions died.
Our young warriors were destroyed;
Through Sarybai’s terrible violence,
Our finest heroes wept.
The looming ridge was leveled;
Sarybai’s conflict
Became a deed never forgotten.
Fighting on in grief,
Deprived of his great host,
The lone scourge shrieked,
While many stood trembling.
Ready for this conflict,
Sarybai came to single combat;
His victory promised terrible calamity—
Such was his customary way.
Blood burned from his fury,
And his voice drove out men’s souls.
When he appeared directly in single combat,
The heart of any mortal man
Was torn apart at the sight.
Pouring out his wrath, he armed himself,
Like a black camel bull entering the herd;
The ravager came out raging.
Whoever approached that baleful figure
Would see what awaited him, they said;
Any multitude that grappled with him
Would learn their fate, they said.
Against this infidel Sarybai,
They spread turmoil for six months,
Clashing without respite;
Those whose doom had come fell.
“This is utter calamity,”
The white-capped Kyrgyz wept.
The reason for their weeping was this:
When he strained and charged,
He was like a burning plane-tree ember.
The infidel’s violent heaving
Was like an unbreakable host of warriors;
The infidel’s battle cry
Was like a bolt of lightning—
Such was the sight of his form.
Oh, Sarybai, you swine,
A fierce conflict had arisen;
He let none who met him turn back,
But seized and slaughtered them thus—
Such was his manner of fighting.
Against this infidel Sarybai,
They sent scouts to learn his strength;
Unable to face him, many lords died,
And so many valiant heroes perished.
His dreadful fury became overwhelming;
Those who met him were laid flat.
You were a great one, you were a lion;
The pillar of all these people,
You alone were lion-maned Seytek.
Short is the pointed blade,
Scion of a precious lion,
My mirror left by the noble,
My champion desired by the people,
