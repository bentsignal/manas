Oh, blessed and beloved people,
They encamped in rows upon the ridges.
The people settled upon the summer pastures,
And their elders feasted on fat tails.
If an envious enemy came,
They chased him away like tumbleweed.
They climbed the ridges and summered there,
Tethering their prancing steeds.
When their many enemies confronted them,
Woe seethed for any who met them.
As long as our falcons lived,
When an enemy came against us,
In the days when joy endured,
We spilled his blood like water.
At Kara-Buura and Batkal,
When the countless Kyrgyz dwelt there,
Eating horse haunch when hungry,
When the Kyrgyz lived in high spirits,
When did the Kyrgyz ever grieve
Or consume themselves with fruitless sorrow?
If a foe came forth to fight,
If some deep and dire ordeal arose,
If there was a trench already dug,
If there was a challenger bent on enmity,
We said we would don
Impenetrable coats of mail.
Against the Chinese on our frontier,
Against Kangai on our flank,
Against the Kara-Chinese and Torgouts,
The Solon, Shibe, and Manchu,
Our champion used to say, ‘When my thoroughbred knots its tail,
My hour has come.’
When enraged at the kalda,
He said, ‘The time for my plea has come.’
While our valiant lions lived,
We crushed whomever we encountered
And defeated all who faced us.
While the dark lions bore
Their black-shafted Zulfiqar spears,
We said we would drive the foe away.
While the doomed heroes’ eyes were open,
We carved and ate the horse’s haunch.
We went about saying
We would make the whole wide world submit.
For the countless Kyrgyz people,
Where is another night like the holy Night of Power?
By day there was laughter, by night there were games;
Where now shall we find
A bright and radiant day?”—
So lamented the countless people;
The multitude wept and the nation cried out.
All the teeming people wept,
The white-bearded elders wept,
The children at play wept,
Every soul in the mighty mountains wept,
The trees on both banks wept,
And every living soul sobbed.
Their hearts were broken by grief;
The multitude, all the many young,
Could not laugh, their hearts distraught.
The vanity of this deceitful world
Was plainly revealed;
Great and small, old and young,
Not one was spared from heartbreak.
“Our dear lion will leave us,” they said,
And tears streamed from their eyes;
All the nation cried aloud,
And all the people raised a clamor.
For these people, Bakai and Kanykei
Were a mighty fortress,
A vast and surging mountain ridge.
Semetey and Külchoro
Were Mount Kap-Too, untouched by storms or wind,
Yielding no livestock to a claimant,
And no life to a demon—
A mountain that could never be broken.
They were the teeming Kyrgyz people’s defense,
A vast black fortress.
While the fierce gray wolf lived,
Though suffering descended upon the world,
