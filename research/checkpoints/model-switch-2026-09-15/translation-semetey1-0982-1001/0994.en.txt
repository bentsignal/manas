No enemy could venture near.
At the hero's right hand
Was a battle-axe with silver cheeks,
At the hero's left side
Was a gilded small pouch.
The outstanding one was worth the world;
His splendor was unlike other people's.
Boiling his small kettle,
Spreading out all his belongings,
Your merry dauntless hero
Had the tablecloth spread,
Had everything brought forth,
A fat lamb and a barren yearling,
Had sliced across,
Had sugar heaped like snow,
Had yellow butter piled high,
Let them nibble sugar like snow,
Let them sip honey like water,
Slicing horse-rib fat and eating neck fat,
Dipping bread in honey and eating.
My poor mother Kanykei,
A woman who understood worth,
'He will need it on the journey,' she thought,
And put all her wisdom to use.
Along broad Talas,
She had young mares tethered.

'Let my boy drink on the road,' she said.
The sweet kumys of young mares,
From this she had liquor distilled.
With boisterous merriment and fun,
They made great rejoicing.
The small cups carried on journeys,
They filled to the brim.
Drinking liquor, they grew merry,
Resting on an elbow, they slept,
Took a short nap and rested,
Lay until their hips grew numb,
Reclined until they had slept their fill.
Having the noble steeds grazed,
As the cold dawn wind whistled,
The tips of the grass swayed,
The bird that sings at dawn chirped,
The earth opened and revealed itself.
All the dusky fog
Scattered in every direction.
Looking at the face of the world,
It opened bright as a mirror.
Then dauntless Semetey,
Telling tales for solace,
The dear hero upon his journey,
Now raised his spyglass to look.
