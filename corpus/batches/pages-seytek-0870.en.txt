His shouting split heads,
His eyes flared like fire;
The hair of his body stood on end,
And he roared loudly.
Like a rutting camel in winter,
His wild temper raging,
He crackled and thundered;
Every mortal human being’s
Whole body shuddered.
Growling and working himself into fury,
He poured out his wrath, thundering
At the messenger who had brought the news:
“Who would listen to these words of yours?
Let the dog that came to the ship hear them;
Let the fluttering bird hear them;
Let the hard earth below hear them;
Let the unbroken sky above hear them;
Let the surging river water hear them;
Let the pitch-black night hear them;
Let the demon from Mount Qaf hear them—
Who else but these would listen?
Is Zhelpinish some cheap and worthless land?
Is Sarybai some feeble hero?
The wretched descendant of Manas
Is far beneath me in valor.
This is the path by which I surveyed
The rugged hills and hollows of Talas;
This is the arm that speared
That vagabond Choro six times!”
He thundered, working himself into fury;
The crackling madman
Spoke like lightning:
“If what you have said is true,
Then, bereft of my vast army,
I have indeed been a brutish fool;
My horn has broken from my head,
And I have been left hornless.
Taking my pit for level ground,
Taking my wretched self for a hero,
I have come to utter ruin.
When I consider the calamity I have met,
I see that I am finished.”
Saying this, Sarybai
Recoiled from his true army;
He saddled his horse Tookyzyl,
Put on his bulletproof armor,
And his savage fury showed.
When Sarybai strode forth in rage,
Those crowned and seated on thrones
Despaired of their royal lives;
Those who grazed the black earth and saw the sun
Despaired of livestock and life;
Worms, insects, and every creature
Despaired of their lives.
He fully unleashed his venom,
And it engulfed the whole world.
Hearing it clearly, Sarybai
Poured out his wrath like this;
He doubled so many over
That the black earth could not stir,
And the great multitude halted.
When the warrior stood roaring,
When he shouted his command,
Heads spun in bewilderment,
And the world filled with dust.
When he raged and lunged,
He began a mighty upheaval;
Under the force of his fury, many men
Were left without a single hair,
And began to perish.
Startled by the words he had heard,
He saddled the peerless steed Tookyzyl,
Saddling Tookyzyl for battle;
He seized his cannon and roared,
Summoning those skilled at mounting horses,
The strong men resourceful in danger,
The tents that sheltered them from the cold,
Every hero’s stratagem,
And warriors who came without being summoned—
United in soul, sharing death together.
