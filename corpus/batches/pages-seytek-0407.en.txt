It was his own doing.
What manner of man was Kanchoro,
Who fired arrows at his own kin!
The wrathful Chürök began,
And she and Kanykey flared with rage;
With the fluttering life within her breast,
The furious woman launched
A great and dreadful conflict.
That leopardess, your mother Kanykey,
Who knew custom and governed the people,
When in the palace built at Talas
She became mother to the whole world,
There was no person as mighty as she.
Your precious, fierce mother
Would leave no necessary deed undone.
Her dear kinsman Bölökbay—
For the master smith Bölökbay himself,
For the black eyes of the smith David,
She poured spirits and offered butter,
Serving him and submitting,
Pleading with the smith David.
She had racehorses tethered to the line,
Six thousand gelded camels and eighty breeding camels—
All these she had given
As payment to master Bölökbay.
“Make it like Jangkol’s,” she said,
Pouring out her wealth in supplication;
“My dear kinsman,” she cried, wringing her hands,
And pleaded with Bölökbay.
He understood her service;
The noble master smith Bölökbay
Obeyed Kanykey.
“My child, dear kinswoman, I shall agree;
From generation to generation,
Your descendants shall carry it as a gift,
And smile whenever they behold it.
They shall value and understand
The worth of the object made by David;
When it strikes a grief-stricken, aching heart,
It shall split it into two.
Can this wrathful pig of old
Become your brother if you shelter him?
Is it right to call the roaring wretch
Your brother?
He made us slaves with blood-red backsides,
Burned multitudes and made them booty,
Showing his enmity openly;
You burned us outright and set us aflame.
He made us claw our faces bloody,
And let down our black hair in mourning;
He let the Jediger ride
Kambar-Ata’s gray stallion,
And placed the khan’s treasury and palace
Under Kanchoro’s command.
Rather than stand watching without revenge,
Rather than commit this deed,
Let us sink down, never having been born;
Death is better than life—
Better that we die!
Having blunted our blades,
And destroyed our worthy heroes,
What shall we gain by making
That brazen pig our brother?
Let us cut off this mane by the handful
And perish in the slaughter!”
So saying, Aichürök
Stiffened in fury;
Her eyes blazed with fire,
And her white teeth, gleaming like crystal,
She ground one against another.
When she hissed like a dragon,
What living soul could restrain her?
Kanchoro upon the throne
Leapt like a hunting eagle.
You should have seen your wife Chürök,
That flashing, fierce woman—
She lunged like a blue tiger.
From the hell and calamity he had inflicted,
From the cup he himself had filled,
How could Kanchoro escape?
