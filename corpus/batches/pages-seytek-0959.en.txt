So saying, Sarybai
Gathered as much courage as he could,
Venom touching his blackened mouth;
He took heart and thundered,
And the mortal frame
Of humankind shuddered from head to foot.
When he raged, calamity arose,
And wherever he struck was leveled.
Wrath burst from his courage,
Venom poured toward all he faced—
Thus Sarybai advanced.
From the depths of hell itself,
Born as the Antichrist,
Sarybai had come.
Mustering all his courage,
His glaring eyes became fire,
His insides blazed like embers;
His bristling hair stood wild,
His inner nature was transformed,
Revealing blood-drinking ferocity.
When he cried out in rage,
He seemed about to swallow the whole world;
Anyone who witnessed his full fury
Fled in panic, crying, “I am dead!”
Had he fought for six years,
Had he slain the armies of the world,
Had he endured grievous hardship,
His strength spent and his head aching—
Such was the fearsome boar’s
Demeanor when you saw him.
Onto the broad field of death,
He came forth and faced the fray,
Bearing twelve different weapons—
May they be accursed.
Let me learn whether he is a man:
Rejoicing at the thought of death,
He prepared to unleash his wrath,
Wrapped himself in battle gear,
His ferocity plain to see,
And donned his armored coat.
His manly courage showed
As he put on his precious mail,
Starting as though seized by frenzy,
And saddled Tookyzyl.
He girded on the black-steel, blood-shedding
Zulfiqar sword.
Fleeing before the sight of his wrath,
The smoothly flowing river,
The plane tree and fruit-bearing willow—
Before his courage and fury,
Even speechless beasts submitted.
Sarybai, son of a monster,
Armed himself and rode to war.
The ill-fated boar
Found the strife he had sought.
When the seventh day came,
The appointed, promised day,
And its time was fully reached,
When dawn shone clear,
And light fell upon the earth,
Brave Seytek’s seer,
Who knew the face of the world,
Went out to the watch and saw him;
Such a thing happened in the world.
At that very watchpost,
At the dark eye of the spring,
Beside the great plane tree,
When your khan-father Manas lived,
Scouts stood nearby.
At that same watchpost,
After ranging through many lands,
Scouts who knew every country
And guards who bent beneath their vigilance stood.
Brave Seytek, the prince’s son,
Seytek, whose four kinds of livestock stood ready,
Kept eighty sentries and five scouts
Patrolling the land.
To spy an enemy from afar,
They struck the great drum with a crash,
As though ready to devour any raven they saw;
