Become the leader of all the people,
My son Seytek.
Learn to join what has been severed;
Go forth and gather the scattered nation.
The ignorant who refuses to learn,
The one who will not heed spoken counsel,
The one who will not go where he is led—
Though he may be a close kinsman,
The scoundrel who knows no propriety,
The brute who understands no words—
In the end, do not hold him dear!
The one who knows nothing himself,
Who will not heed those who know,
Who pays regard to no one,
The fool duller than a camel—
Call him neither kinsman nor soul.
Do not say of the brute who knows no propriety,
‘He is one of our supporters,’
My son, or the people will speak of it!
Without a lion there is no hero;
Without heroes there is no war;
Without a mound there is no land;
Without eccentrics there is no people.
My son, without justice there is no khan;
Among the vast people no soul
Grows white-haired without a visitation from Khidr.
The common folk in your hands—
Value them like a moonlit lake.
In their depths, preserved from Kyrgyz Manas,
Lies the legacy of the treasury and wealth:
Slaughter and consume them, and they last one day;
Beat and drive them off, and they last one day;
Weigh their worth, and they will last a thousand years.
Your fortune is an iron enclosure;
Hero, your people are your countenance!
My son, become khan of a royal court,
A man of dignity,
Splitting a black hair with perfect judgment,
A soul endowed with sacred grace;
Become the giant called Khan Seytek!
My son, rule a blessed people!
If you seek land, here is the land of Talas;
Here is the mighty Kyrgyz nation.
My son, one who drinks when offered yet is never filled,
Whose greed never ceases,
Who, when driven through darkness,
Stalks like a tiger;
No mortal has dared confront him,
And no mortal facing him has prevailed.
This is the blessing I bestow
Upon my heroic Kyrgyz people.
If you receive them as your own father would,
Your road ahead will be white;
If you lead your countless host,
Be discerning when they stray;
Observe and value your inheritance.
Do not blurt out your innermost secrets,
For the greatest treasure and richest wealth is this:
My son, do not wound the people’s hearts!”
When he had given this blessing,
He was both their shelter and their mountain—
The heroic elder Bakai,
A saint offering his prayer.
Receiving the blessing of Khan Bakai,
The hero Seytek became a warrior,
A prince upheld by the spirits;
All twelve parts of his body reached perfection,
And he became a lion who maintained a court,
A man with multitudes behind him,
Khan over the whole universe.
He joined what had been severed,
And the nation thundered with joy.
Then Seytek’s
Forefather, old Bakai,
His dear grandmother Kanykey,
The sacred saint,
Semetey, son of Manas,
That dear lion upheld by the spirits,
The hero raised by fairies,
Who was found after twelve years—
