Beyond Echki-Ölbös,
At the projecting nose of the towering mountain,
Upon the spur of the ninth ridge.
When they reached Ak-Urchuk,
A dreadful ordeal unfolded.
Chongbilgich took the lead
And sent roaring fog before them.
His fury seized him fiercely,
And in a raging fit the wretch
Worked his spell upon the sun and weather.
Boz-Talaa, the broad back of the earth,
Lay across the gray plain.
It was a curse upon the open flank,
A road from which none returned,
The vast earth-trap of Kordoi,
Where no human ventured
And not a single four-legged beast
Had ever passed or trodden;
It was called Meenet-Talaa, the Plain of Tribulation.
Every living creature
Said, “If I meet it, I am dead.”
Earth was heaped as high as mountains,
And a man who traveled there by day
Had his fortune cut short that very day.
A horse sank to its knees;
Among all the sons ever born
No one had known such hardship.
Sand was piled as high as mountains,
And a person who traveled there by night
Had his fortune cut short that very night.
It was called Boz-Talaa,
And people who happened upon it
Prepared themselves for death.
There were bull-like beasts and camel-like beasts,
Implacable, unyielding predators,
Wild asses frolicking,
And deadly venomous snakes.
There were creatures called börsö,
Things no ear had heard and no eye had seen,
Every sort of unheard-of creature was there.
There were beasts that devoured rhinoceroses and elephants,
And one called Sword-Tail,
Whose name no living soul knew.
They pressed on through the narrow passage,
Encountering a grievously difficult road,
A devilishly cruel road.
When they beheld this plain,
Chongbilgich saw it from afar,
The nobly born Chongbilgich,
And cast his customary weather spell.
The wide world became constricted,
And over all the level lands
Snow rose to the horses’ flanks.
Six were overtaken by death,
While ninety-four remained alive
And escaped that perilous road.
Watching the stars, they corrected their course,
And their lionheartedness became known.
The jagged Ala-Too loomed before them,
And they reached the Ala-Too.
They traveled along the mountain crest,
Pressing on far without halting.
They set lookouts and surveyed the land,
Searching gorges and choosing passes.
They followed the Ala-Too,
Shoeing their ravine-bred steeds,
Until they reached a place from which to scout.
Let us leave this matter here,
And begin the tidings of brave Seytek,
The panther’s son.
All his ancestors were khans,
And from the beginning Khydyr had blessed his line.
His forefather was a mighty champion,
His first ancestor was a hero.
Against the many enemies who came in hostility,
His courage was manifest to all.
Of the foes who faced him,
Blood poured like water.
Wherever he had spent the night,
A shrine rose with a rumble.
