Reducing me to a smokeless ember,
You have set my soul ablaze,
You have drained away my strength.
You carried off as plunder
So many of my swift steeds;
You ignored my pleading words
And struck down, before my eyes,
My people as well.
You brazen bitch, you have chilled
The proud strength in my back.
Over narrow cliffs and rugged, arduous roads,
I have come to exact vengeance.
Bringing hardship, affliction, and strife,
Fighting tirelessly for six months,
You renowned woman, warrior maiden,
I have come to take revenge on you!
If you accept a fair settlement, bitch,
I have come to divide
The treasure of Kayypchal.
But if you bristle in fury,
I have come to fight day and night without fleeing,
Until one of us dies.
If you do not accept these words,
If you do not pay the blood-price,
I shall not let you sow your crops,
Break open the earth,
Or carry on your livelihood.
I bristle with fury;
You have sorely provoked my rage.
My very being thirsts for blood.
I shall die before suffering disgrace—
Mark well the words I speak,
And do not rouse my savage wrath;
Come against me, raging bitch!
Let it be said that mighty Kuyaly
Was taken by warrior Sarybay;
Let the dread banner of dominion be raised,
Let there be an uproar that rends the sky.
Let it win fame among every nation
And be a lesson to those who remain.
Red-robed maidens with moon-slender waists,
Let your beautiful leader of girls come forth;
Let your nimble young men come forth.
I shall take you all as plunder,
I shall slaughter every one of you!
My name is Sarybay,
And now my fierce rage is roused.
When wrath takes me, I stand unyielding;
I roar with menace,
And today I have summoned
The turmoil of the whole world.
My true fury has seized me;
If I do not utterly destroy your people,
If I do not make girls like you
My women upon the open steppe,
May I die unworthy of the name Sarybay,
May I sink into darkness as though never born!”
Thus Sarybay spoke,
And when he made that threat,
Her thoughts pierced and scattered,
A thousand grievous troubles
Pressed close upon her soul.
“I stand upright and free;
Driven by honor, wretched soul that I am,
I shall strike this wrathful infidel,
Who has inflicted such abundant sorrow;
I shall crush beneath my feet
Those other wretched wretches.
I shall utterly annihilate them
And slaughter their champions.
Sarybay is indeed an infidel;
I shall beat his far-famed fury from him.
After fighting fiercely for six months,
I shall find a way to settle his account.
I shall slaughter his renowned men;
And if my strength proves insufficient,
Let me die by this shameless dog!
Whatever venomous calamity and hardship
Descends upon my head, I shall face it.
