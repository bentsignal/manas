Why did I not die while plundering them!
Making the Manchu crash,
Why did I not die while striking them!
When we seized the Kakan's khanate,
Why did I not die!
When we brought chaos upon Beijing,
Why did I not die!
Gripping a spear and breaking formations,
Why did I not die in battle!
In the mace-blows of the general melee,
Why did I not die in combat!
You have halted me at the frontier,
My young hero, son of Manas,
And made me mourn the old days!
Counting all that came before,
I sit here overturned with grief!
Against the seething Chinese Kakan,
When we charged and began war,
When we seized his khanate,
Alas, what a desolate day,
Why did I not die there!
When I poured my courage
Out against the Chinese,
When my humanity
Overflowed from my head,
Why did I not die there!
When, with courage, I hemmed in
The Kalmak,
When I struck
The Kaldai and Zuntung,
Why did I not die there!
When I smashed those who opposed me,
And cut them down,
When I inflicted humiliation
Upon the Kalmak like sheep,
Why did I not die then!
Filled out like a lion,
In your holy uncle Bakai
The old anguish stirred!
When my journey grew feeble,
My young son Semetey,
Do not make my days turn yellow.
While my gray wolf Manas lived,
He waged holy war in many lands,
And always heeded
The words of your holy uncle Bakai.
While my gray wolf Manas lived,
I raised the banner in many lands;
Defending the Kyrgyz people,
Taking the blue-mottled spear in hand,
I speared and shattered many lands.
While my accomplished gray wolf lived,
I girded the jekene at my waist,
And amid enemies covering the earth,
I rode alone through them and attacked.
Since my accomplished Manas died,
I am dead, not living;
Weeping long for my bloodthirsty hero,
My poor soul has seen hell while alive.
Reason has left me,
From weeping long for my magnanimous lord,
The light has left my eyes.
When my lion died,
My black beard turned white;
For your mountain-like uncle Bakai,
The end of days has nearly come.
I have spoken all these words—understand;
My needed only one, heed me!
Unbeliever and Muslim—
Noble orphan, think!
Such is the nature of battle.
Your uncle, dear hero Bakai—
An iron coat no bullet pierced,
Do you think he did not wear it?!
In the holy war where peoples clashed,
Do you think Bakai did not march
At the head of the people?!
The many people of forty Chinese tribes,
