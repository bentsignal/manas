Who fashioned this life with his hands
And lodged every soul upon its perch?
Is there any life wrought by human hands?
Until the whole world passes away,
Until the earth collapses and is gone,
Is there any soul that will not die?
Sarybay, son of the monster,
That insolent rabid wretch—
I shall surround him and wage war.
If my strength is enough, I shall seize him,
And carry him dangling from his horse.
If fortune favors me, I shall cast the enemy
Flat upon his back.
He was born to be my enemy;
If only the monster’s son would come,
If only my eyes might see his arrival!
To measure my spear-thrust against him
Has always been my desire—
To grapple once with that dog.
My early words have come late;
My two eyes have become four with waiting.
If I break through his flank and open a road,
If I shatter his frontier and attack,
If I crush his multitude and take spoils,
If I meet him face-to-face,
And pour out all my fury,
Clashing and fighting savagely,
May I die more bravely than all others.
May the tumult and strife I unleash increase,
May deeds like these multiply,
May my name remain as a memorial,
And may it make my renown endure.
Will my true enemy come?
This alone has filled my heart;
May the Almighty truly grant it!”
So spoke Külchoro,
Donning his bulletproof coat,
Raging and seething,
Scowling without a tremor,
Standing defiant without a murmur.
“For the united Kyrgyz people,
We shall bind our fleeting lives to a thread
And offer ourselves in sacrifice!
After calming the people and ending the turmoil,
We shall make young Seytek
A banner for these Kyrgyz,
And return rejoicing to our homes.
If the Creator grants us favor,
We shall overtake the infidel charging in arrogance
And give him his answer.
We shall behold his vaunted courage.
If calamity befalls us,
We shall see what fate has written.
If death comes from the infidel,
We shall surrender our precious lives.
If hostile death arrives, we shall die.
For our villages and our land,
For the Kyrgyz people of this country,
We shall fight seven days without rest,
Contending for our homeland.
Let us wage this struggle for the land,
So that people may say, ‘Our champions, our lions,
Sacrificed themselves for the nation,’
And their fame may remain among the people!
When the final reckoning draws near,
When hostile death makes its claim,
The tongue falls silent and cannot speak.
From this deceitful world,
What soul can flee and escape?
Will not the dug grave take everyone in—
All beneath the moon and sky,
Young rulers and old alike?
This earth crushes and devours them all.
Can one prosper by fearing death?
Has anyone lived forever in this world
With a backbone made of iron?
Champions have died, and khans have died;
This world before us is false,
And every soul whose hour comes dies.
When the water is spent and one’s days are done,
