His heroism was known.
When he reached eight years of age,
"He will lay siege to Beijing," they said,
And in the records of Beijing's old khan
It was written down.
When he reached nine years of age,
He rode out to wage war on the foe.
"He will bring calamity," they said,
And one account of Manas
In the records of Beijing's old khan
Was entered and preserved.
When he came to the age of ten,
He boldly went out and fired his bow,
Destroying whoever faced him.
When he reached eleven,
He began the tumultuous battle;
Unable to withstand his wrath,
The black Chinese and the many Manchus
Broke ranks and fled away.
When he reached twelve,
He stormed a palace and took booty.
When he reached thirteen,
He shot the white maral and made it tame;
The black Kalmyks and Manchus
He drove about and made obedient.
He shot the blue maral and made it tame,
And subjected every land he sought.
When he reached fourteen,
They barely escaped with their lives.
If there was a giant on the earth,
He traveled in search of him to fight.
Whenever a fierce quarrel arose,
Among the black Kalmyks and Manchus
He laid their champions low.
When they provoked his stubborn wrath,
The insolent monks came of age;
When he unleashed his fury,
He made them call him "blood-drinking Burut,"
And began the tumultuous battle.
Bakburchu of Chinmachin,
When he learned the truth of Manas,
Was utterly shattered.
The mighty men of the coat-clad Chinese
Roared aloud,
Yet none dared advance and face him;
Against the hero Manas
Across the whole face of the earth
No one remained to twirl a lasso.
His fortune was perfectly balanced;
If you looked within him,
He was broader than the whole earth.
When he reached fifteen,
He found a land like Talas,
And found the Argyn and Kyrgyz people.
He devastated the Kyzylbash realm
And made its girls and women weep.
No land remained anywhere
That the racehorse Akkula had not trodden;
No people remained whole
Who had not heard his battle cry.
He broke through Ombu and opened a road,
And took booty from many lands.
He entered the Caliph's court and received a writ,
And received a blessing from the prince.
He raised strife in many lands,
And throughout Kakan and Beijing
He came to be called blood-drinking Manas.
Kongurbay, son of Alooke,
Without even racing Algara,
Had taken the prize away;
The merciless turmoil
Had then been set in motion.
When Akkula was entered in the race,
Your gray-wolf lion Manas
Was left with nothing.
