The princes fled in panic,
But is there any reason to flee?
Ah, ill-fated children, where are the people?
Will the day come when we reach them?
I sing and tell my song,
I tell my secret as truly as my faith.
My father’s confidant,
The lion Bakai, his comrade—
Can my grandsire still be safe and living?
Seize Kanchoro,
O wise man whose eyes have seen much,
Who has surpassed so many rivals.
A herald grown old among the people,
A saint from ancient times,
Whose valor is unlike another’s,
My heroic uncle Bakai,
My grandsire, elder of the people,
Has medicine in his pouch,
And all things beyond what others possess;
He has yet another remedy.
One medicine is called “Eбep,”
In dear Bakai’s hands
It used to soothe the aching place;
Another medicine is called “Sebep,”
It used to open eyes sealed shut.
Have him scour the face of the earth,
Mount him upon Jelmaian,
Clothe him in the Jekei-gray coat,
And have it fetched from Kakanchy.
Bring all his remedies;
From this infidel enchantress,
May he have the power to free us.
Before the five argali stars had set,
She had suckled him five times over.
His valor was extraordinary;
When his mother’s eyes failed,
They said he would vanish into the unseen.
Yesterday, when I was orphaned of my father,
She seated me upon the moon-gray steed,
And made Karakan flee before me,
Then spirited me away by enchantment
And brought my horse Buurul to me.
She placed my spear within reach;
Escaping from the sorceress,
Until I reached the far-flung people,
I could find no course of action.
She tethered the racehorse Buurul,
Kept me deluded and away from the people,
And placed a falcon in my hand.
She showed me Aigan,
And placed within my hands
The white sighthound Kumaıyq.
I turn my heart toward sorrow,
Unable to find what I should do,
I remain a captive.
Though without fetters or a net,
I remain bound.
I no longer count among humankind;
I remain under enchantment.
On every day of the week,
I go out for pleasure.
I have witnessed a monstrous thing:
For the sons of mortal men
I have yearned to rejoin them.
Led by Kökmonchok, eighty maidens—
So many wives from the unseen are mine.
If one says I died, I am not in the grave;
Though alive, I am not among the people.
Such filth has the world become;
My breast is aflame, my liver an ember,
And there is no soul with whom to share my secret.
“If Külchoro is alive,” I say,
And place my trust in the Creator,
While my heart blazes and my liver burns.
My two hands are bound,
My two eyes stare wide;
Such filth has the world become for me.
Princes wander distraught—
Is there any soul who should flee?
O children of this dark day, where are the people?
