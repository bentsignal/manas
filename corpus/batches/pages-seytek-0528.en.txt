“I am famished—grant me blood,” he cried.
“My true father, the lion Almambet,
I am afflicted—grant me strength,” he cried.
Against the enraged infidel,
The warrior Khan Sarybay,
To keep him from reaching the hero Seytek,
He readied the steel-shafted spear,
Leveling it straight,
Resolved to die as a sacrifice
Before the hero Seytek.
“My true enemy is Sarybay;
I shall meet him in combat.
To inflict grievous torment,
And rescue the renowned young Seytek
From the mighty demonic infidel,
The warrior son of the Monster,”
He came to deliver him.
When he lashed Kulansur,
The horse revealed his pedigree as a tulpar.
Across sixty defiles and eighty ridges,
He gathered himself into a sweeping stride,
His true tulpar form revealed.
When his hoof struck the black earth,
It split apart in every direction.
From before Sarybay
He flew and bounded like a cloud,
Making the broad common land
Collapse on every side.
The ground he trod burst asunder,
And stones a spear’s length high
Whirled like tents
Above the choro’s head.
Steam poured from his nostrils,
Billowing like smoke from an army.
The earth seemed to end in shimmering haze
As he drew near Sarybay.
The lion choro favored by fortune
Reached the wrathful one,
And thrust the infidel Sarybay
Straight through the collar.
The hero Seytek had staked
His fly-like life upon a hair.
He arrived swifter than an arrow
And struck hard as he passed.
As though he had punched a stone,
The steel spear bent,
And the force bore heavily upon the mighty man.
A white cloud as broad as a house
From Sarybay’s flank
Burst forth, wheeling like a vulture.
The spear that struck so violently
Shattered into eighty pieces.
Among warriors and lions,
Among heroes of uncommon power,
Mane and tail streamed free.
Telkyzyl, driven with all his strength,
Was like a foul blow struck
Against the shinbone;
He plunged into the black earth,
And the lion’s true prowess was revealed.
Before Seytek could recover,
Sarybay, son of the Monster,
Rushed upon the hero Seytek.
Seeking some means to defeat him,
The accursed one found his opening
And prepared to strike with his mace.
He braced himself and gathered strength,
Swaying as though about to strike,
His gorge-like mouth gaping,
The gray foam pouring from his mouth
And scattering across his chest.
Just as he reached him to swing the mace,
Just as he was about to take Seytek’s head,
Külchoro, favored by fortune,
Shouted, “Calamity!”
The gray-wolf servant, deftest of all,
Raised the battle cry, “Manas!”
Drew his scabbardless sword,
And appealed to the spirit
Of the magnanimous hero who had passed into eternity.
