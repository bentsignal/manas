As though they beheld my khan and father Manas.
Let the Khagan despair of life.
If you give me Akolpok, I shall put it on,
Make Buurul prance,
And ride among you.
If the Chinese do not deceive me,
I shall drive them all the way to Beijing itself.
I shall buckle the gray purse at my waist
And, looking like Khan Almambet himself—
If not himself, then his dark-eyed likeness—
I shall proudly ride Buurul
And enter their midst.
Upon the forty tribes of China and Beijing’s realm
I shall lay a terrible oppression.
Thus shall I avenge
The lions who passed into Judgment
And the heroes who died untimely deaths.
If you bind six pennons to its shaft—
Its haft stout and its point sharp—
Give me the patterned spear as a gift.
I shall accept the spear as a gift
And tread the road of hardship.
When he scowls and bristles—
The infidel son of Kaldai,
The warrior Khan Kongurbay—
I shall bring slaughter upon mighty Dyngshaa,
Massacre the Chinese,
And destroy this multitude.
I own no livestock to halt in the valley;
Against my own kinsman, the Kalcha,
I have no life to spare from death.
I own no herds to spread upon the river terrace;
Let the countless Chinese wage war—
I have no life to spare from battle.”
Then your prince spoke again
To the radiant Choro:
“For a son like you, who turns the foe,
Father—my dear Külchoro—
Let my Buurul be sacrificed!
For a son like you,
Let your uncle’s racehorse be sacrificed!”
Unable to say, “I will not give it,”
Or rebuff the valiant man’s desire,
Unable to say, “Leave it, boy,”
Or break the desperate hero’s heart,
He dismounted and gave him Buurul,
And unfastened and gave him his sash.
He readied all the war gear,
Strapped on the naked sword,
And placed it in mighty Külchoro’s hands.
He clothed him in armor no bullet could pierce,
Revealing him as a true lion.
Into the hands of the flashing Choro
He gave the equipment,
And sent the warrior on his way.
Do not ask what was done for the young man:
They had ninety black bull-calves
Slaughtered and sent forth.
Lest someone’s evil eye strike him,
They offered them as sacrifice for the Choro.
Külchoro asked his uncle
For the racehorse,
And sprang up without touching
The stirrups with golden soles.
Was he not gray-wolf born?
To the marsh of Üyrülmö,
To the summit of Teek-Too,
Went the true falcon, gray-wolf born—
Külchoro, the intercessor,
The swift-moving lion Choro,
The nimble, kinsman-warrior Choro,
Born after Almambet of old,
After your lord and champion,
That noble elder who subdued the world—
A lion Choro born in his wake.
The precious prince Semetey
Stood upon the ridge of Kök-Sengir,
Upon its overhanging spur.
