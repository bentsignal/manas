Your warrior Sarybai,
The boar who devastated the nation,
The villain long accustomed
To drinking blood,
Who had waged war for many years,
Dominated the whole age,
Gnawed down all he encountered,
And, hardened by bloodshed,
Had become renowned as a bloodthirsty killer.
When Seytek leveled his spear,
And drew near
To the field of combat,
“Look, the boy they rely upon
Has come out to fight!
Now I shall settle the account
Of this ill-fated boy
And beat out his ancient madness.
Before he reaches maturity,
Before his coming day can dawn,
I shall finish him altogether.
I shall see the full courage
Of this boy who boasts of strength,
And carefully spill his blood.
I shall curb his imperious rage
And quench him like a dying fire.
Ravening as I wreak havoc,
I shall crush whoever assails me;
I shall leave none around me alive,
And seize him by both skirts.
You wretch who boasts of courage and lionhood,
Who boasts of strength,
I shall put an end to you indeed.
I shall unleash upon this boy
My fierce courage and surpassing fury.
I shall overcome every ailing foe,
Crush whoever encounters me,
Bend down whoever confronts me,
And defeat whoever crosses me.
If I do not conquer their champions,
If I do not swallow all who ride against me,
Then before Sarybai can be called a mighty warrior,
May I sink away unborn;
May I die rather than live!”
They stood opposed to one another,
And Sarybai, enraged,
Roared and leveled his spear.
At the boy, that wolf of war,
He thrust his steel-shafted enchanted spear,
Aiming it at his liver.
A renowned master among spearmen,
A consummate master of cunning,
He had faced heroes in many lands
And met countless disasters as an equal.
The cunning dog knew well how to thrust a spear;
Though the whole world surrounded him, none had defeated him,
And he was a master of stratagems.
When Sarybai thrust his spear,
He gathered all his strength.
When he thunderously drove the spear
At the mighty gray-maned Seytek,
As the spear came for his blood,
And was about to strike its mark,
The spirit-steed Kulansur
Flickered aside like a hare
And darted away like a wild ass.
The arrow-swift, accursed racer
Would not let the spear reach him;
No wind could match his gallop.
The blessed horse was like a spirit of the wind,
And no beast could match his stride.
He flew whirring like an arrow,
And surged like a flying cloud;
A mighty clatter of stones and earth
Whirled above his head.
He bounded as though shot from a bow,
Soft-mouthed and perfectly responsive;
When he turned, he darted aside,
Before one could cry, “This way or that!”

Before one could say a word,
