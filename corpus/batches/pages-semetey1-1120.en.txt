How many souls bore
Broad-strung bows and secret arrows?
If he found fault with the Kyrgyz,
He would start a quarrel.
Seeing the appearance of these people,
One would say they had come together to finish them utterly.
Look at Kongurbay, look at the mountain;
We shuddered at the sight of his vanguard.
Look at the enemy who would fight!
With Koshoy and Töshtük at their head,
When they saw Kongurbay,
They stood as though split in two.
When Kongurbay went to the feast,
His drums were unlike any others.
Kongurbay, son of Alööke,
Had horns and trumpets sounded.
If you saw how great his figure was,
He loaded his baggage upon rhinoceroses;
The souls who came were of another kind.
His champions were of every sort;
If Kongurbay gave the order
And his champion unexpectedly attacked,
A man's shoulder blade would come out.
The Chinese called this dyngshoo;
Kongurbay was such a hero
That he made his warriors act as one.
Kongurbay, son of Alööke,
Had reached the time
When his rib bones bulged,
His right shoulder rose high,
And the earth bent when he walked.
He had reached such a time.
His courage was full, his heart stone;
Do not ask of Kongurbay's station.
If his wrath fell upon the Kyrgyz,
He would bring great turmoil on this road.
Vowing to gird his waist firmly,
He had come prepared from Beijing;
If he met renowned Manas,
He would pursue Alööke's blood-price.
He donned an arrow-proof coat,
Thinking his plans and words,
Thinking the bloodthirsty Manas, that Burut,
Would come to the feast.
Thus Kongurbay had come,
Planning to overrun the feast,
To fight amid the feast
And spill human blood.
Kongurbay was bloodthirsty;
He would be filled
By eating a whole pig at once,
And, receiving an order from his khan,
Would slaughter whoever attacked.
In prowess he had brought
Horse acrobats and runners on foot,
And marksmen who never missed
In the darkness of the moon.
From marksmen such as these,
He chose one from every thousand;
Of champions such as these,
He brought exactly twelve thousand.
Beyond them
Were the Kanjarkol champions;
The shoulder blades of attacking enemies
Would unfailingly come out.
Your gray wolf, Khan Kongurbay,
Said, "What will the Burut do?"
Of the Kanjarkol champions,
He brought exactly ten thousand,
Who descended fully prepared.
Beyond them came
The most skillful bowmen,
The most skillful lasso throwers,
Who caught men and spared no soul;
Mingled behind them came
The finest wielders of battle-axes.
To Kökötöy's feast
Came the Chinese people with their army.
