Your dear sister-in-law Aychürök
Staged revelry of this kind:
"Let my precious one enjoy himself;
Without wondering, 'Where is my elder sister?'
Or bringing us to mind,
Let men and women
Play delightful games," she said.
Wise Chürök devised this.
Earlier Chürök had provisions prepared:
"If there are games, let them drink."
There was black arak at the side,
And arak in a Kalmyk-style flask.
Receiving Chürök's command,
They all ran for the arak;
A young man pulled the stopper and a maiden poured,
And among the young women, poor Kermekash
Held it out.
They poured it into wooden bowls,
And all gathered round.
They found delight in the games,
Leaving not one person empty-handed,
And exchanged sarymerden verses.
Drinking arak and raising a din,
Women and men mingled
And entered into the clamor;
An irrepressible merriment
Had entered the festive throng.
They played in pairs,
One woman and one man—
If you would see games, see them here.
They led one another away,
Coupling two by two;
Many there were behaving badly.
"Come now, what kind of person are you?
You will tell someone,
And mock me with the tale," they said;
Many quarreled with young men,
And would not consent.
"Oh heavens, here is gold," they said;
Many more pleaded.
"You have ruined what was mine," they said,
Scowling at one another;
Many took offense.
Their anger flared beyond words,
And many grappled together;
Out on the steppe, scowling,
Many came to blows.
Gripping one another's waistbands,
"My love, please consent," they said;
Many hurried about.
"Someone will see us
While we are doing it," they said;
And many ran away.
Akylay, daughter of Shooruk,
That lively poor girl,
Was seized by Külchoro;
They went to a dark place,
And remained there embracing,
Taking a shortcut by a narrow path.
Baytaylak, son of Bakay,
Led Kermekash,
The foremost of the young women;
Kalyk of Sarykan
Had taken hold of Umsunay;
And Shayymbet, son of Zhamgyrchy,
Mounted the beauty
Named Ayymkan.
Women and men mingled,
Played until they were spent;
A great revel appeared.
They divided two by two,
Finding the games delightful,
And raced three-year-old horses against each other;
Maidens and young men side by side,
