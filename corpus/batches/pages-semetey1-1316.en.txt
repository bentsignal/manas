It was the time when he counted him.
Straight ahead stood the house at Keng-Kol;
Do not ask the falcon's condition—
No wretched mortal came within a handspan.
At the treasury gate
He had the doolbas beaten;
When the doolbas was struck,
Its sound deafened the ears.
From the fortress with gates,
From Chachykei's palace,
From below Tört-Kül,
From the garden planted by his princely father Manas,
From opposite Bar-Köl,
From the garden planted by hero Manas,
Kanchoro and Külchoro—
For the two out among the horses,
The doolbas was beaten hard,
And the horses stampeded together.
Bounding and bunching like roe deer,
Külchoro and Kanchoro,
The two of them in great haste,
Readied and saddled their coursers,
Alarmed by Semetey:
"The doolbas has been beaten hard;
Its sound makes the ears ring.
Is there a trench already dug?!
Has an army engulfed us
From forty Kalmak tribes?!"
Unable to stand in their alarm,
The two companions raced,
Their stirrups striking together,
And rode into the palace.
There, wearing a blue lynx-fur cap
And wielding a blue-mottled shield,
Was old Bakai the gray wolf.
All their khans were there;
Gathered in the palace,
All the heroes were there,
And wise old Bakai.
His moonlike face gleaming,
Semetey spoke in a resounding voice.
He began his words to Khan Bakai,
Speaking softly but laying down firm resolve;
Your lion-prince began:
"We raided Andizhan,
Where blue caps perch high
And argamaks hold their heads proud;
We made horse-tails reach the knees,
And relentlessly sought the blood-price
Of my ritual father Chubak,
But nothing came of it.
We attacked Samarkand
And took much booty there.
We raided Tashkent and opened the road,
And brought humiliation upon many souls.
We followed the transmitted accounts
And asked those who knew;
Unable to learn Chubak's fate,
We withered long in humiliation.
The horses have grown fat and restless,
The heroes fat and pent-up;
For vigorous youths and swift horses,
The time has come to prove their worth.
They say there is no remedy against fate;
We stand seeking counsel
From you, uncle Bakai.
They say Kongurbai, who dwells
In Great Beijing,
Is a hero who broke the combined strength
Of a handful of Muslims;
A wise man among spearmen,
A lion born among the Chinese,
Who knows the enemy's stratagems.
A resourceful sage,
A hero guarding
Kakanchin's Beijing.
Those who fought him did not win.
No man has bent him down;
No mortal has dared face him.
None who confronted him remained alive.
