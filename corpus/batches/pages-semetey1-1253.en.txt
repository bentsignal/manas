All the khans made ready;
All the warriors
Readied sword and gun
And prepared every weapon.
Their hearts were overcome,
And they looked ravenous.
Their champions put on iron
And marched in a line.
The Afghans' great army,
Wrapping turbans about their waists,
Made ready in frenzy.
With blue caps perched high
And argamaks holding their heads proud,
They wrapped broad sashes around themselves,
Prayed to the Almighty,
And made ready at the word "enemy,"
Preparing in utter commotion.
Tümönbai, son of Tülkü,
Prepared his various troops.
Azhibai, son of Azhy,
Did not fail to make ready either.
Army struck against army;
Refusing to leave their people ungathered,
Those advanced in years,
Between fifty and sixty,
Went into the middle ground.
There were khojas in white turbans
As large as cauldrons,
Whose shouts rang like the azan;
There were mullahs in mottled robes.
Khoja and mullah took command,
Gathering all their people together.
Khan Akun formed one division,
And he too came out prepared,
Taking troops of every age.
Tümönbai's force formed one division,
Driving a vast, great army,
Bringing forward in numbers
The lions of the many Afghans.
Azhibai's force formed one division.
Their dust struck the sky;
When the Zhediger engulfed them,
The Afghan people gathered.
The black road seethed with dust,
And people's heads reeled.
Many among the Afghan common folk,
Like unbroken horses,
Bucked about up and down.
Many went weeping, saying,
"Poor wives and young children
Will all be killed."
Many trampled about, saying,
"These are people who have seen hell."
Many reviled Chürök,
Saying, "May hellish Chürök come to no good."
Many pondered Asan Kaigy-like grief,
Thinking, "Now we have been slaughtered,"
And rushed about.
Despite their panic,
They gathered them from the city,
Good and bad mixed together,
And assembled them all.
Khan Akun's gatekeeper,
And the keeper under the gatekeeper,
All mounted without exception.
The patrolmen and guards
Also mounted without exception.
Thundering warriors
And roaring heroes
Mounted, not one remaining.
They mounted to gird themselves,
To set their faces toward battle,
And to die if death reached them;
If their strength sufficed,
To keep them from facing the Afghan city
And defeat the Zhediger.
