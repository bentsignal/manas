Now God struck the Chinese;
The Manchus, rumbling,
Spoke harsh words.
The war drum that summons men to battle—
They beat that drum hard.
The war drum sounded loudly;
Black Kalmyks, many Manchus,
And the seething multitude of lords
Closed ranks as if to engulf them.
They armed themselves in confusion,
Strapping on battle-axes, armor, and swords
Every which way.
The Manchu people roared;
Squad leaders strutted,
Leaders of fifty sprang about,
Leaders of hundreds ran,
And commanders of thousands despaired of life.
When the Chinese showed their fury,
Saying, "He steps over the khan's head,"
They took it as an insult.
The Muslims who came to the feast
Were pressed into dreadful straits.
Their khojas in white turbans,
Their mullahs who recited lessons,
Wrapped their belts as turbans,
Implored the Almighty,
Lost their wits,
And fled then,
Crowding toward Manas.
Then leopard Manas
Stepped apart with courage,
Showing the form of a lion.
Almambet and Chubak together
Led his prancing Kula horse,
While twelve companions held the reins.
Manas stepped away from the companions,
Showing the form of a dragon,
Straining and heaving in fury.
Your uncle, leopard Bakay,
Seized Manas as he struggled:
"We invited you to the feast as people,
But you Black Kalmyks and Chinese
Have become depraved;
My roaring Chinese,
You have become impossible.
If you mean to fight to the death, say so plainly.
If I do not mount Akkula
And drive your lord shouting
Toward Beijing—
If you are guests, state your purpose;
If you would domineer, say so plainly.
I invited you as people to the feast;
Put aside your pride and eat your meat.
If you want slaughter, give your answer;
Let us make the carnage a spectacle.
Accursed Chinese, give your answer!
I am not Manas who will bow,
And you are not Chinese who can overpower me."
Glaring fiercely,
Venom spilling from his mouth,
Looming like Ala-Too,
He strained in fury.
"Let me go!" he cried to Bakay,
And Manas struggled with a deep roar.
The forty companions split in two,
As though about to begin slaughter,
Looming like Ala-Too.
Wearing a coat of yellow gold,
Mounted on a piebald horse,
Wrath showing on his face,
A sheathless sword at his side,
He looked ready to slaughter them.
Wearing a blue rapys coat,
Mounted on a mottled blue horse—
Such was Chubak's way.
