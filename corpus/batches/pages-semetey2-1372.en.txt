If you do not go to Kyyaz,
The hair on your crown will be ripped out,
Your precious head will be cut off,
Your words will go unheard,
Your two eyes will be gouged out,
Your body will be torn to pieces,
And all you say will be crushed!
Plundered and put to shame,
You yourself will be brought low.”
When Kanchoro said this,
Aichürök, daughter of Akun,
Had no choice but to hear him.
No child remained for her to cradle,
No descendant of the magnanimous hero;
Such filth, it seems, is this world.
As Kanchoro spoke,
She wished to die but could not,
Unable to take her own life.
The earth was too hard to enter,
Yet life was sweet when she wished to die;
She wailed, unable to stand,
Unable to surrender her fly-like soul.
The hapless slave with a hero’s form
Had no choice but to hear it.
Aichürök wept and mourned:
No male child remained behind her.
At that moment Aichürök,
Your poor heroine of manly stature,
Grew wrathful and grim;
The blood drained from her downcast face.
She pondered but could find no counsel;
The faithless world had overturned,
And she could find no bearings.
She clenched her teeth and summoned her courage,
Thinking, “If only I could find a way
To strike off the head of one of them—
Kanchoro Khan or Kyyaz Khan!
If only these men came near,
These villains who ravaged the people,
I would seize one and rip open his belly,
And swallow the black blood within
To quench my thirst.
If only I could tear one apart and slaughter him,
Drink his blood like kumiss,
And glut myself upon it;
Then my burning heart might be soothed,
My dimmed eyes might open,
All my anguish might scatter,
And my brow brighten like a mirror—
That would fulfill my desire.
If only I could bring greater havoc
Upon Kanchoro Khan and Kyyaz!”
So thought the warrior woman Aichürök,
That extraordinary, wrathful woman.
But the thought came to her too late:
If she met them, she would take their heads,
Bring grievous misery upon them,
Strike when they came near,
Count their vile lives as nothing,
And sever their heads with her sword.
Even if it meant her death,
Dear sister-in-law had reached
A resolve from which she would not retreat.
She was ready to fight them to the death;
The poor woman braced herself
To sacrifice her fly-like soul.
When she saw the tyrant khans,
The indomitable woman Aichürök
Coiled like a striking snake,
But there were no Argyn or Kyrgyz warriors
To break through and rescue her.
Your sister-in-law contemplated true battle,
But many wrestlers surrounded her.
Alone and suffering,
This woman bore endless sorrow;
Many champions encircled her.
Your heroic sister-in-law Aichürök
Was about to unleash a mighty conflict.
