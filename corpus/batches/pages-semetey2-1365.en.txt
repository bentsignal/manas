This woman of yours is terribly dangerous.
When you take her to the Seven Rivers,
My friend, this raging wretch of yours
Will be the one to heap earth over you.
She is a consummate tyrant, a great predator,
A lone and cunning creature.
When you take this roaring infidel as wife,
May she not put poison in your food
And finish you off.
May she not pour poison into the bozo keg,
Poison that burns,
And blind you by gouging out your eyes.
May the age-old people not mock you,
Saying, ‘Kyyaz died by a woman
And saw in his breast
The true abode from which none return’;
May no fiddler in a house of daughters,
No servant attending a woman,
Make you the laughingstock of the age.
My friend, may the striking God not strike you!
May no champion who hears with ears and sees with eyes
Make mockery of you.
Let us not take this savage woman,
This roaring shrew.
When we take Aichürök
By fighting and dragging her away,
May she not bring us shameful disaster.
May she not neigh like a stallion
And keep us in torment;
May she not bring savage calamity.
Friend, heed me: Kyyaz must not take her.
As a wife she divided nations;
As a maiden, the notorious woman
Shook the age and divided peoples.
This is the celebrated Chürök;
No Chürök—this woman
Is a calamity that has ruined the world.
Those are the black eyes of the shrew;
No Chürök—your infidel
Is hell itself!
Let us not go down the path of ruin.
Let me turn back to my people;
My friend, I will not take this woman!”
Then Kanchoro
Readied himself like a hawk.
From Kanchoro’s mouth
Came wisdom as though from the unseen;
He shook himself from his agitation,
Stamped the earth in fury,
And spoke to brave Kyyaz:
“Your father’s grave, brave Kyyaz!
No brave Kyyaz, but earthbound Kyyaz!
Kyyaz, a span beneath a woman,
You are an infidel Kyyaz.
You regard even the lawful, white-paired wife
As something forbidden;
Your head bound up in wickedness,
You will not die leaving a single ewe.
If you continue like this, you will come to nothing.
The Seven Rivers, your land,
I had thought a true land.
Ah, brave Kyyaz fit to sink into the earth,
I had thought you a man.
You are no man, but mere earth;
You must be witless Kyyaz,
Strong only in your muddled fear,
Who imagines evil in a woman.
I had called you warrior Kyyaz—
Oh, my boy, you are womanly Kyyaz.
My friend, never until now
Have you twisted my words
And grieved me so, Kyyaz.
If you do not seize as spoil
The impetuous woman Chürök,
And return boasting
To your Seven Rivers, your land of Tarkan,
Saying, ‘I raised a tumult
Over Chürök, for whom Toltoy died;
