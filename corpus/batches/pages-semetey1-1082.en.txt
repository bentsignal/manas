For as long as meat took to cook,
All the Zhediger
Remained without moving.
Brave Toltoy, son of Bagysh,
Jerked his head up from the ground.
Honking like a young goose,
He spoke words of substance;
Brave Toltoy voiced
His lament to Kozho:
"Stop, Kozho, my dear friend;
Let us not take Chürök,
And let us not incur her calamity.
Let us not be entangled together in torment,
Go and join ourselves to death,
Or meet humiliation like sheep.
Standing there with proud posturing,
Let us not unexpectedly become a slaughter.
Let us not turn back from death in vain;
Let us not be defeated and retreat.
If we are unexpectedly defeated,
If we are suddenly pierced through,
And made into a great spectacle,
Let us not have the vast black Zhediger
Slaughtered for Aychürök
And bear the guilt for them.
Let us scatter our ashen provisions
And flee from this Opol.
What shall we do with Aychürök?
Come, my friend, let us leave.
Let us turn back and remain alive;
Let us go to that darkened mountain.
Let us study its pass and cross;
On the far side, let us overrun the Karabash people.
Let us train Zulpukor
And overrun the Karabash land.
Let us take a dark bay maiden,
Name her Aychürök, and praise her.
Having seized her as plunder,
Let us slaughter ninety beasts for her feast
And abandon this conflict.
Let us travel along the mountain ridges
And choose a sloping way across.
On the far side, let us overrun
The seething Kyzylbash people.
Let us take a reddish-bay maiden,
Name her Kyzchürök, and praise her.
Let us not take Aychürök
And remain needlessly in torment.
We will not prosper by taking Aychürök;
Let us not become the black donkey
Of these peaceful people
At dawn on the day of Mahshar.
My friend, my Kozho, hear my words!
Akun's daughter Chürök,
That accursed source of turmoil,
Has a waist already girded,
And a lion lying hidden.
She has a hellish tale of hardship
That would not end in five or six days.
When Chürök's desire comes,
She has a husband she keeps concealed.
I know Chürök's origin,
The turmoil that has come to an end.
Alas, my Kozho, hear my words!
There is a flaw in what you say:
That man is Aychürök's husband.
There is a son left by Manas.
Will he submit to your violence?
Will he give us his wife?
One of my ears has heard this:
When Semetey was born,
He consumed the Russians' feast.
If he lived and grew,
They said he would shatter the Oirats.
He consumed the Chinese feast;
If this boy remained alive,
They said he would shatter the age.
He has a tale that does not end—
