He made an iron-fenced fortress,
Renowned throughout the world,
And made you, Kanchoro, a lord.
From the fat-tailed sheep and the mare
He carved the finest meat and fed you;
Saying, “You shall be like my own brother,”
At his mother’s breast
He let you suckle, treacherous son.
Before all the countless people
Whose ears had heard and eyes had seen,
When did he ever let anyone call you a child of misfortune?
He had your belt bound firmly;
The Six Cities and Margalan,
All the way to Kokand and Samarkand—
He made them all submit,
And had you exact the blood-price for your father Chubak.
Calling it “Chubak’s unpayable blood-price,”
The multitude clamored and wept in anguish;
Most of the Uyghurs and Margalan,
All the people who witnessed it, wept.
Kanchoro raised you well;
Your father’s noble Semetey—
It was my Kanchoro who fostered you.
What is seen today is gone tomorrow.
Ah, my son Kanchoro,
Your mind is shallow and your heart is foul;
You fired upon your own elder brother—
There is no fool like you in all the world.
Your mighty, thundering mother’s
Silver has turned to charcoal;
You have grown arrogant, Kanchoro,
Fattening without knowing the pain of burning grief.
The fierce wolf, the son of Manas—
His gold has turned to charcoal;
You brought calamity and strife upon his head.
Damn you, Kanchoro,
For fattening without knowing what you received!
These were the words Kanykey once spoke:
“Ah, that wretched woman was a seer;
In the end, may he perish,
She said he would prove no true kinsman.”
Affairs collapsed and conflict broke out;
The fierce, heroic Kanykey—
The poor woman’s words came true!
Had I heeded Kanykey’s words
Yesterday, at Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
When the Kyrgyz lay spread across the land,
When you were still in your cradle,
Why did I not gouge out
Your blackcurrant eyes?
Why did I not seize you, put you to the sword,
And slaughter you?
Before you began your wicked schemes,
You rabid cur,
Why did I not finish you for good?
Our blood and judgment failed us—
Old Bakay and all of us.
My son, posing as a younger brother,
You were only narrowing your eyes in deceit;
It turns out that all along
You were our enemy.
You thought, “I have enjoyed Semetey’s glory;
Now I shall destroy him.”
Through all your treachery, you thought,
“I shall bring him to his end.”
You forsook your own borders, you madman,
And thought you would go
To the people of Jediger.
You will not dwell in Jeti-Özön;
You fired upon your own elder,
And since your very origin is accursed,
You will find no refuge among the Jediger!
You will not camp along the hillsides;
Since your guide is evil,
Your end will never prosper!”
So spoke Sary Khan,
His eyes darkening and seething,
His broad teeth, like rows of pegs,
