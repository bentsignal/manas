If your forefather gave you Zulpuqor,
Then the sovereign God Himself
Has surely granted you a child.
A hero full-grown—a son—
Oh, my dear, He has clearly granted you one.
That Koshoy gave his blessing
Means he blessed the child as well.
Dark gray, a hero in his purest form,
Dark-flanked and blue-gray without,
He will have a noble bearing,
And a mane flowing down his back.
His hand will be fierce and open against the foe,
And when he sets out, his road will lie open.
He will subdue whoever he confronts;
His valor will be beyond compare,
A peerless champion, a foremost eagle.
You will have a gray-wolf son,
And you will name him Kenen.
That son whom you saw
Will become an unwavering banner;
Wherever his desire turns, an uproar will rise.
Those who heed his words
He will master through speech,
While fools who will not understand
He will scatter in direct slaughter.
He will make the witless plead for mercy
And subdue the hot-tempered.
His surge will be like a lake’s swift current,
His courage like a blazing flame.
You are destined to have a son.
May this dream of ours come true,
May true sacred power prevail,
May fortune and abundance come in full.
May blessings come to the teeming people,
Inexhaustible however freely they partake.
May God grant him land on which to live,
And, so that he may sustain the people,
May He grant a tiger-limbed hero
Whose keenness is like Zulpuqor’s.
May every word he speaks be fitting;
Trusting in the Creator’s protection,
May Kenen’s cradle-cord be firm!”
Thus Akjoltoy spoke,
Interpreting the dream with precision,
Explaining it fully without tiring,
Unfolding meanings of many kinds,
Interpreting it with surpassing accuracy,
And so he finished reading the dream.
“Here are sixty head, beginning with horses,
For my uncle Joltay,” he said,
And gave them as his interpreter’s reward.
He gave double-humped camels,
And livestock of all four kinds,
Red gold and ornaments,
Yellow-mottled cloth with a silken weave,
Heavy black-mottled fabric,
Bales of goods bound for transport,
And red-gold coins.
He took a red camel won as booty,
And an elephant fit for bearing loads.
Joltay’s heart was content;
He became immeasurably rich,
His hollows filled with milk,
His wants filled with meat.
Joyful and at ease,
Through all of this Akjoltoy
Became a wealthy man tending countless herds.
