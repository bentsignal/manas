When the people gathered to bury him,
The confidant of the noble one,
Your forefather Manas’s companion,
The sorrowing hero Bakai remained.
While your princely father, the sultan, lived,
The elder Bakai did not die.
The people cried out and the nation lamented,
Yet we did not bury my khanly uncle.
Dust soared into the sky;
“The sovereign Bakai has died,” they said,
And the army surged in clouds of dust.
Fifty thousand animals led by mares
We did not slaughter for the lord’s funeral feast.
For valiant Bakai’s thunderous funeral
We did not hold rites like Kökötöy’s.
We arrayed the white-bearded elders,
And made his noble bones gleam like copper.
We had him washed with Zamzam water
And wrapped in cloth of gold.
We had him washed with kumis
And wrapped in brocade.
We dressed the daughters-in-law in mourning robes,
Letting the hems of their silken coats
Spread wide and touch the ground.
We dressed the maidens in red,
Letting the hems of their fine coats
Trail diagonally along the road.
The lion Bakai did not die;
The people did not witness his death.
Sixty thousand animals led by camels
Were offered as prizes,
And bright crimson fabrics
Were spread as funeral adornments.
Countless dark and fallow beasts
Were arrayed in ceremonial ranks.
Our forefather Bakai did not die;
Your heroic father Semetey
Did not bury Khan Bakai.
We did not remain after burying the prince.
For my heroic uncle Bakai,
We ask whether, if he returns to us, he will fight
And rescue the widows, orphans,
And the poor from bondage—
That is what we keep asking.
We wandered here as plunder,
Parted from the mighty lion.
We were captured and imprisoned,
And by a wrathful enemy
Bound without rope or cord.
We wandered, finding no escape.
Thinking Kyyaz would issue a decree,
We stand here helpless.
“A protector remains from the hero,” we say,
And make you our protector.
We came as slaves, sobbing and wasting away;
We came as bondwomen, taken as spoil.
I have longed to die from shame,
Yet I remain alive, my foal,
Because worthy Seytek lives.
I have longed to depart from shame,
Yet I remain here alive
Because Seytek, whom I need, lives.
From your princely father, gray-wolf Semetey,
From your lion-hearted father himself,
From the black eyes of the Karakhan line,
The broad land of Keң-Kol remained,
And the happy Kyrgyz people remained.
Riding colts and flying hunting birds,
They once lived in wild delight—
The poor Kyrgyz people remained,
And the settled land of Talas remained.
The ruined Argyn people remained.
Red gold and abundant goods,
Countless equal treasures remained.
Many lamenting heroes remained.
When his beard was white as a swan,
When his journey drew near,
As he mounted to ride toward death,
When Almighty Allah was to grant
The true abode from which none return—
