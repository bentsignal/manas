And anyone who witnessed his fury
Was left feeling swallowed whole.
He roared in menace,
Like the lion of the divided mountains,
Bellowing like a lion.
He struck the royal war drum,
Sounding the signal,
Then whipped his great rhinoceros mount
And rode out onto the battlefield.
“Akjoltoy of the Kyrgyz,
Your tongue speaks a babbling speech;
In your heart, you are my enemy.
If my strength fails and leaves me,
I shall meet my death at your hands;
But if my strength is enough,
I shall hew your borders into countless pieces.
If I fight in wrath,
I shall scatter your honor like ashes;
And if rage seizes me again,
I shall rip apart your ribs.
If my strength fails and leaves me,
I shall depart this world;
But I shall tell Jadygoy,
‘I came and destroyed the Buruts,
And delivered the punishment
Of all the black Kyrgyz into his hands.’
I shall ride to Kakanchin, saying,
‘I slaughtered the Buruts.’
When my wild rage seizes me—
And now my fury has risen—
You mother-forsaken wretched Burut,
I shall awaken your ancient madness.
Let one of you come, or let a thousand come;
You snarling madmen,
Let all your strongest heroes come.
If I flinch, can I ever recover?
My forefather was a mighty lord;
I have come stalking from afar.
If I quail, am I even a man?
You shall behold my great calamity.
You are not alone, Joltoy;
All you Buruts together shall die!
If I do not turn my thoroughbred
Onto the passes where you graze your herds,
Onto the lands where your lineage multiplies,
May I die unworthy of the name Bilgich;
If I do not slaughter a thousand of you at once,
If I do not butcher your people before returning,
Crushing and doubling over your backs,
Plundering your Kyrgyz nation,
Seating your tall sons at my hearthstones,
And seizing your daughters as lawful booty,
Binding your mighty men in the marsh,
You damned Buruts,
And boiling you in your misery;
If I do not tear by force
The robes from your backs,
Strip away your worth,
And bring your power to an end;
If I do not make your people wander
In uproar like victims of yellow fever;
If I do not bring ruin upon your blood,
Destroying all who cross my path,
And reducing them to utter silence;
If I do not thwart your desire,
And heap boulders like Opol
Across the road where you roam at will,
You accursed, wretched Kyrgyz;
If I do not crush your petty rage,
Find a way to overcome you,
Then turn back and raid your village,
May I die unworthy of the name Bilgich,
May I sink stillborn, never truly born.
Through hardship and sorcerous learning,
I became a hero who knew distant things;
I was lord over the lands of forty khans,
The very embodiment of a noble hero.”
So saying, Chonbilgich
Gathered all his wrath
And would not rest without combat.
