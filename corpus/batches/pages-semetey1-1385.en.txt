Have no share of good fortune!
Had we lost the horses by day,
Had we faced the enemy openly,
Had we stood face-to-face,
What regret would we have
If we had clashed in battle?!
Had death reached us and we died,
We would have had no regret.
Enough, my hero-sultan;
If you wish, let us live.
If you are angry, my sultan,
Seize us and slaughter us!
When we lie dead,
We shall be slaves worth a dog's hide;
While we walk alive,
We are slaves worth a sheep's hide!
Our slavish worth has been revealed:
We lost the fourteen horses
When sleep closed our eyes.
If you wish, let us live!
If you do not wish it, uncle,
Seize us and slaughter us;
We were but two wretched slaves.
He who girded his waist firmly,
My hero born together with me,
Came kicking through the womb
Of my dear mother Kanykei,
Sprang straight from the loins
Of generous lion Manas.
When were we ever sons of Manas?
Could youths like us
Ever be called brave men?
We let the tulpars be taken—
Could any man be like us!”
While the boys stood grieving,
The hero rose and said:
“Do not grieve, boys, do not grieve!
For kinsmen such as you two,
An uncle like me would give his life!
My morning star in the sky,
My sable fur upon my collar!
My support when I climb upward,
My prop when I descend!
The lost horses will be found;
My two matched companions,
Tomorrow our honor will be restored.
There is no remedy in grieving over what is gone,
And no profit in regret.
Upon the swarming Chinese
We shall bring terrible slaughter,
And raid the horses
Of the red-naveled Kalmaks!
Breaking through the stones, taking their migration,
We shall crush them and take revenge;
Breaking through the desert road, taking their migration,
We shall pursue them and take revenge.
My kinsmen, boys, do not grieve!
We shall scatter the four kinds of livestock
Of the lordly Manchu,
Of the Kalmak who descended upon you!
We too shall divide
The city of Kün Jang-jun;
We shall contend
With the slave who boasts of strength!
Whose robe hem never touched the earth,
Who does not know which one
Is Almighty Allah alone—
In the camp of those bloodthirsty men,
We too shall bring splendid slaughter.
To Khan Kongurbai's city,
To precious Beijing,
We shall go and bring slaughter!
Against the people of Madangshan,
Against their chief, Khan Zuntung,
You shall see your battle
In the city of the Kakanchin!
The land you will face is Beijing;
Look at its multitudes, like black worms!
