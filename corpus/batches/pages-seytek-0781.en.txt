A great upheaval cast upon the world.
Lion born of Akbaltai,
My lion, watch over us.
Offspring of the lion-hearted hero,
From this present grievous turmoil
Come and deliver us.
Agybai, son of the Argyn khan,
White-browed champion who entered the fray,
Noble-browed gray wolf among all.
You knew the tongues of sixty tribes,
Broad of jaw and fluent of tongue,
You were a hero who never faltered.
Sent ahead, you brought good fortune;
Leading the way, you were like a myriad troops,
You were one of the gray-wolf heroes.
You were the dark eye of the gray wolves,
You were nobility itself.
Watch over us,
Protect my child.
Each of you was a living hero:
Yrchy-uul of the Katagan,
And valiant Shuutu in his felt-cap;
Tölöchü, son of Tölöman,
The darling of your four households;
Baichoro, son of Baabedin,
Watch over us.
All of the forty companions,
Their elder, led by Kyrgyl,
Remain his comrades!”
So mighty Bakai spoke,
Shouting out his blessing,
Stirring the whole world,
And calling upon the spirits
Of the companions gone to the next world.
His face grew pale and drawn,
And tears streamed from both eyes,
Pouring down upon his breast.
“Wretched, meaningless world!” he said,
And sought refuge in the warrior maiden,
Slung his prayer-pouch around his neck,
And pleaded with the Creator:
“May the Maker grant us favor!”
In his old age, Bakai’s
Heart burned and grieved.
A man’s deafness is a bitter thing,
Leaving him no better than the black earth.
It was wise of Almighty God, who created us,
Not to let life linger overlong,
But to take it away.
The burden of years is a snare,
A living head is brought to shame.
It was wise to take a man
Before leaving him utterly feeble.
Allah Almighty, the one God,
What did He intend for His living servant?
Yesterday, in my blue-lynx and sable cap,
When I strode with a swagger,
When I ruled as khan over the wide world
And lived in joy,
Why did I not die then, before seeing
The misery of this strange world?
Why did I not die here
While my glorious fame endured,
Before they called me sorrowful Bakai?
When life burst from my voice,
Dust rose beneath my tread,
Blood streamed from my sword,
And lament rose over those dear to me;
When my words laid the world
As desolate as a desert;
When I held dominion
Over the whole wide world;
When anyone who defied me,
Or ventured too near,
I struck down;
When I made this world my slave
And held it bound;
When I hemmed in hostile Kakan
From every side;
When I made the world resound with blows,
