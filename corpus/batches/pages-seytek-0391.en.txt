Her heart aching, her insides boiling,
Distraught and burning with grief,
Tensed like a steed beneath a hero,
Her eyes dimmed and reddened,
Her whip-slim waist twisting,
To confront Kanchoro,
To stand as protector
For her brother-in-law, the gray-maned warrior,
And, if she found the chance,
To subdue the mad Kanchoro,
To destroy him and spill his blood,
To do all that she knew how,
To drink his blood by the spoonful,
The heroically formed Aichürök
Struck out in front.
Following behind them
Came the finest of the long-haired,
The worthiest of women,
The purest in a flowing gown,
The foremost wife ever born,
Your aged mother Kanykey,
Coming into this horde
To take her ancient vengeance
On blood-spilling Kanchoro,
To prove her breast milk pure
By offering him as a sacrifice,
To make his noble head bow,
To drink Kanchoro’s blood,
And swiftly cut off his head.
That old conflict held many torments,
And she came to take vengeance in full.
The reason for her vengeance was
That he had heaped endless humiliation on her,
Beating and stretching her out like a hide.
He had tied her by the waist
To a barren cow’s calf and had her dragged,
Thus inflicting his punishment.
Her aged flesh was torn apart,
Not a spoonful of blood remained,
And your mother had no strength left.
She came to hew his executioners into seven pieces
And spill their furious blood.
Dragon-like Kanchoro—
Of all the property and boundless wealth remaining,
She came to demand what was hers.
Moon-faced Aichürök,
A wonder unlike any mortal,
Of serene and stately bearing,
Precious as a fairy,
Whose curse never missed its mark,
Whose wrath was never spent in vain—
The formidable woman Aichürök
Gathered her dear hair, long as river grass,
And bound it tightly atop her head.
The impetuous, ill-fated woman
Put on a man’s attire.
“I shall stand guard for the warrior,” she said,
Her supreme courage showing,
Springing forward like a doe,
Stretching tall as Surayyl,
Striding jauntily and eagerly,
Arching like a white doe,
Rushing on without sparing her life,
Driven by a furious spirit.
Her life, merely held in trust,
She staked upon a single hair.
She broke away in fury,
Her heroic courage plain,
Her eyes dimmed, her heart aflame;
Though many cried out, she strode on.
When courage rose, the head was forfeit,
And from her currant-dark eyes
Tears mingled with blood streamed down.
She began the true battle;
Straining every sinew, the poor woman
Cast away her fly-like life.
She summoned fierce resolve;
This raging woman cast her precious life
Without hesitation upon the black earth.
His blood draining, he ground his teeth.
