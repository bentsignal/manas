His gait was like the high wind.
Toward the great herd
Buurul advanced,
Tossing his forelock to the sky
And pounding his hooves into the earth.
He broke away at the head of the horses;
As the beast drew straight ahead,
His tulpar form appeared.
He had no tongue with which to speak,
Yet was wiser than an animal.
Neighing and pawing the ground,
He displayed his thoroughbred form,
Flicking his forelock toward the sky
And striking his willow-silk tail
Against his hind legs.
Neighing with a rolling call,
By God, the proud creature,
Towering Arbuurul,
Pounded the black earth.
Buurul horse left behind
The prince, son of Manas.
The countless dark livestock gathered as tribute,
The myriad treasury herds,
Before a white cliff a rope-length high,
The creature crossed in a single bound.
After that Buurul,
The black stallion, father of horses,
Ran after him without remaining behind;
A bay stallion like a falcon hood,
A yellow stallion with jingling ornaments—
The great thundering herd
All crossed after him.
The pattering foals and yearlings
Died in the water;
The whinnying mares and fillies
Were left completely exhausted.
He raised the whole dark mass
And thus Semetey took
The thundering herd.
Do you see Karagul,
Preparing treachery?
Semetey stood here
With a sheathe-less sword at his waist.
Karagul stood in anguish,
Preparing the way to flee.
Do you see Semetey
Begging Karagul to tell him
Which was Kongurbai's horse?
“Hey, my dear uncle,
Did you see him leave from under me,
My thoroughbred Arbuurul?
Old Joodur, left from my father,
My kinsman found upon the steppe.
Which is your infidel's Black Horse,
So that I may ride it?
Uncle, kindle my extinguished fire;
Restore the dead soul.
Let me know your kinship;
I shall thunder after the herd,
Drive it from behind,
And leave none at its roots,
But drive them all into Talas!”
Behind that herd,
The black Kyyduukara horse
Had remained apart.
Its figure and bearing,
Its horse-like form,
Stirred the heart at sight;
The marks of a tulpar appeared.
God had struck the slave:
Telling Semetey to ride it,
He greatly praised
The black Kyyduukara horse:
“My dear Semetey,
The great Black Horse, huge as a mountain,
Was Algara himself.
This year that desolate swine
Was seventy years of age.
