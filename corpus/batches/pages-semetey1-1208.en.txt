They have deceptive words;
If we shriek and quarrel,
They themselves can strike us down.
They have a people called Machin,
Their crown hair bristling,
Ready together to attack.
This gathering that has come
Is Khan Kokotoy's feast;
If we take heed, people,
It is the start of great strife.
Your Bakay who speaks
Has words without end.
The smoldering Chinese
Are filled with vengeance.
When your stallion comes in,
May they not seize its prize by force.
If I quarrel over the prize,
May they not make sport of us.
The snarling infidels,
The warriors of Khan Kongurbay,
If the Muslims' stallion
Should suddenly come in,
May they not cause slaughter
In a sudden frenzy.
Let us choose an envoy
Who knows seventy foreign tongues:
Agybay of Argyn Khan;
Hero Urbu, son of Taz,
Who knows seventy foreign tongues,
Sharpens his eloquent tongue,
And, if sent without warning,
Speaks the Chinese language.
Almambet of Aziz Khan
Could not be subdued by force.
His ancestor was Chylaba;
He disputed Chylaba's khanship
In true Beijing.
Calling him a true infidel,
The long-haired Chinese
Lost track of his origin.
Mighty city and powerful people—
The noble leopard Almam,
Because of their jealousy,
Fought Kun Jang-jung.
He found land at the turning place;
Unable to fit among these Chinese,
He found a people among the Muslims.
If you explain the words
Spoken by lion Lord Bakay,
Put Chylaba's coat
Upon your elder Ala;
Send him unnoticed,
As a Chinese of Chin-Machin,
As a genuine Chinese.
If he prays to the true God,
When the tulpar comes in,
Your foreign-born leopard Almam
Will raise the Chinese battle cry,
Cheering, 'Chin-Machin!'
Then truly God will grant it,
And the Muslims will see
The arriving tulpar safe.
If we do not use this device,
Your tulpar that comes in
Will die at Kongurbay's hand.
There where the horses were driven
Is a corner of Urchuktu.
Very like Akkula,
Hero Joloy's Surkula
Bears this dark misfortune.
Orongu's rough-gaited bay mare,
And young Keney's bay yearling,
Was a yearling gone in sorrow.
It had great regret.
Do not demand it outright,
Saying the yearling came first.
Do not tear it to pieces
Before it reaches maturity.
