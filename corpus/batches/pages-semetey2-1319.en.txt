If a living soul appeared, he wrapped himself,
He wore a person's nose and ears
As an amulet upon him;
At the black figure's grim aspect,
Whoever saw him from afar submitted.
On either side of him
Stood formidable wrestlers;
If even one wrestler broke loose,
A man's very entrails would spill.
Your noble mother Kanykei
Had the white palace built to her desire;
Its walls were this thick,
A palace suited to every need.
When noble, luckless Aichurok came,
She said, “Take it as your dwelling,”
And your heroic mother Kanykei
Gave it to the unfortunate woman.
The noble lady Aichurok
Made gardens all around it,
Set a golden throne at its center,
And made it worthy of your mighty stature—
An immense white palace,
A palace fit for the gray-maned lion.
The splendid white palace—
Such was its magnificence;
Ah, how wondrous that palace was,
Thus had she adorned it.
With ruby and jewel stone
She made it gleam in splendor;
All around the palace
She had ornaments set,
Painted it with gold,
Bound it about with silver.
If two people spoke within,
It sang back of its own accord.
She fashioned beams from fir,
Heaped stone into a mountain;
A spear thrust could not pierce it,
And enemies despaired of taking it.
No giant warrior could reach it,
And of four-footed creatures,
Not even a mouse could crawl through.
No enemy crossed its ridge,
No man dared approach it head-on.
Those who saw its outline lamented,
Unable to advance, they stood amazed.
Lions guarded it all around—
Such, if you understand, was that place.
In that palace amid the marsh,
The unfortunate lady Aichurok
Lay overwhelmed by grief,
When the master gardener Akjoltoi
Came running to her.
He told of Kanchoro, her enemy,
And recounted the whole quarrel;
He told what his own eyes had seen,
And all the words he knew.
He related the great,
Unhealed calamity.
He told of an uproar
Too dreadful for words;
He told his sorrow that beneath him
He had no swift stallion.
He told how the many Black Jediger,
Forty-two men, surrounded the hero
And captured him.
He told how the Greathearted One,
Left without a horse,
Had remained behind.
Sharpening his bitter tongue,
Khan Akjoltoi spoke on.
When the Jediger closed around them,
Kanchoro gave the Jediger
Every encouragement;
An enemy arose from among their own,
And pain welled from the very core.
