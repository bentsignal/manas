Bokmurun, who gave the feast,
Was found out on the steppe.
For the child he had found,
He slaughtered nine thousand mares.
The four corners of the world,
The forty corners of the qibla,
Kökötöy gathered together;
At Kökötöy's feast,
Everyone who came ate his fill.
The great multitude that came to the feast
Came to the feast and ate its fill.
After he had eaten his fill,
They named him Bokmurun.
Thus Bokmurun
Became Kökötöy's son.
Until he reached six years of age,
What did wealthy Kökötöy do?
Upon the child's head
He fastened a waving owl-feather plume.
Kökötöy's seven wives
Led the child about and raised him.
For Bokmurun, Kökötöy's
Heart melted with affection;
But in truth this Bokmurun
Was the son of Er Töshtük.
His spoiling was extreme.
While Bokmurun slept,
Each morning Kökötöy
Would open the treasury,
And as a sacrifice for him,
Would bring out red gold and white silver
And scatter it like seed
Across whole provinces.
On every side, Kökötöy,
Saying, "Someone may do him harm,"
Would set a strong guard.
For every day he passed the night unharmed,
Six wethers and four kids
Would be slaughtered as a sacrifice.
So great was the care with which he raised him;
At twelve, Bokmurun
Was Kökötöy's son.
Growing up this way and that,
His daily affairs prospered.
When Kökötöy's Bokmurun
Reached twelve years of age,
He became a blazing ember,
And wealthy Kökötöy's son
Became a mischief-maker unmatched in the world.
Kökötöy's Bokmurun
Found no mountain to strike,
Found no enemy to fight;
He had merchants beaten,
And caravan men imprisoned.
Wherever his hand could reach,
He had plunder seized,
And caused much disorder.
He ate barren yearling mares,
And ate frozen goat fat;
Bokmurun had grown wanton.
"They say my father Kökötöy's
Wealth has been seen by the whole age;
They say Kökötöy's vast riches
Will die like grass in spring.
They say this world is worthless;
Why should I keep tending
Great herds that bring no benefit?
Even if I lose a great deal of livestock,
My dear father's vast riches
Will last me until I die," he said.
Such were Bokmurun's deeds:
Making their fangs protrude,
Making their lower lips hang,
Making their humps stand high,
He had a thousand male camels saddled,
Had all their coverings put in order,
Had them drawn along with ropes,
And loaded with riches of red gold.
