Guarding what she carried within,
To entrust her cherished charge.
To discern the hero’s valor and courage,
For Bermet to behold and know,
To satisfy her heart,
And only then to marry him.
The doubly ill-fated one,
A world as fleeting as the setting moon.
The wondrous, incorrigible one,
Wearying horses and ranging the land,
Running hounds and flying falcons,
“If he bears the mark of valor,
In the end he will come seeking me,” she said,
“If he comes seeking, he shall see,” she said,
“But if his heart is faint,
He will die before his destined hour,” she said,
“If he should die, Akbermet
Will then enjoy at least one day,” she said.
She gathered an extraordinary band of wrestlers,
And several mighty warriors;
Looking farther beyond them,
She had them stationed there by magic.
Her tigers had borne twins;
None who encountered them returned alive.
Her leopards had borne twins;
None who met them remained alive.
Her lions had borne twins;
None who faced them returned alive.
It was a place no human had ever entered,
No mortal creature walking on two feet.
Still farther on was another menace,
Another strange wonder stood there.
It had a sword-pointed tongue,
Which could cleave a mountain when it struck,
And lay a forest flat when it entered.
No traveler could pass that way;
Whoever met that ill-omened, monstrous beast
Could never remain alive.
Knowing this, Akjoltoy
Had set it there by magic.
Knowing its wickedness, Akjoltoy
Raged without a care:
“Whether fairy or giant,
In the end,” said wily Joltoy,
“I count it no more than the hem of my robe.”
Toward the gray hill where no living soul walked,
He turned Kulunsur’s head.
His silhouette loomed like a mountain,
And the hearts of those who saw him were shaken,
As he stood gazing at the maidens.
The splendid man, proud as a rooster,
The steadfast, lionhearted hero,
For the unfortunate fairy maiden,
For that radiant precious one,
Had journeyed far and crossed mountains,
And forded formidable, surging waters,
Coming in search of her, they said,
Leaving behind the fairies’ bazaar.
For the sake of hero Seytek,
She had sought to satisfy her longing.
Unable to reach the gray-maned hero,
She had camped along the Broad Valley.
Because of passion’s fire,
Many champions and countless armies
Had been crushed to dust upon the road.
“For this fierce woman who came seeking him,
Will he grant days of happiness?” they wondered.
“To Bermet, who has lived in this way,
What will hero Seytek do for her?” they wondered,
As he drove his spear into the ground.
Unable to collect his thoughts,
His anguish burning fiercely,
He turned toward the qibla
And wheeled Kulunsur around.
Marveling at this fleeting world,
Wily Joltoy stood watching.
