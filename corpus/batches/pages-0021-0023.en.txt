Mounted his Manchus on them.
Into my royal treasury and camp
He brought his mighty men,
Their jackets bulging out.
He brought havoc upon my people,
He brought black night upon me.
When he stirred, ruin followed;
Alöökö of the Manchus
Rose in immeasurable might
And inflicted endless suffering.
When he roared and levied tribute,
The Noygut people could not bear it.
That merciless Alöökö,
That Chinese whom God had cursed,
Six thousand silver ingots, a thousand beaver pelts—
Such was the tribute he collected.
A grievous calamity indeed
Was what this Alöökö did.
If we did not obey his word,
If we did not pay his tribute—
Coral ornaments and precious stones,
Beside that man whom fire should take,
The khans of the lung-tung stood together.
If he stiffened in his wrath,
Our poor heads would be torn to pieces.
So fierce was Alöökö’s wrath,
No mortal dared confront him.
When Alöökö raised his battle cry,
No human life was spared.
In Alöökö’s service
Were giants of every kind.
Bringing his giants from Beijing,
Saying, “You Burut, you!”
That mighty man had ridden forth.
The twelve peoples had offered no resistance;
Since Karakan had died,
Against the raging unbeliever
No one remained to turn a catching-pole.
His champions were strange and hideous;
No one who challenged them could survive.
Unless Allah decreed it,
No human strength could overcome them.
His champions came in wave after wave;
Those who met them in battle
Had their strength drained away.
Wearing coats of blue steel,
His warriors were unlike others.
Wrapped in blue-steel armor,
If you could see Alöökö,
Lunging like a blue-gray tiger,
To slaughter the Kyrgyz
He had come prepared.
Taking soldiers, gathering an army,
Raising clouds of tumult and uproar,
He had come to take hostages.
His wrath took men’s lives;
How many people had died!
His champions were unlike others,
His warriors were many;
He had troops gathered from China.
Those who defied him—
It was his custom to drink their blood.
To amass a mighty treasury
That powerful man had made ready.
He had rhinoceroses, he had elephants.
Alöökö was no easy enemy;
Know this, children: it is grievous!
The growth of Alöökö’s power
Was beyond human reckoning.
Of the mortals who fought him,
Not one could remain alive.
His sentries on the heights,
If we counted them, were eighty-four.
That Chinese, may his house burn—
Wherever he struck was crimson fire.
His hideous champions,
That swine ablaze with fury
Had gathered every one.
Some spoke a tongue unlike men’s;
Among his champions
Were hideous men with a single eye.
Every one of his champions
Destroyed whoever grappled with him.
The meat of a whole pig
Would not fill him at one sitting.
His dread presence was unlike a mortal’s.
He had the aspect of a lion;
He bloodied whoever he seized,
He slew whoever he met.
When men saw his true might,
None who met him survived.
Could any mortal make him bow?
His father was a raging Chinese;
Unknowing of human speech,
A swine resembling a beast—
Alöökö’s champion.
When they plundered the villages,
His monstrous champions
Wore human noses and ears
As amulets about themselves:
His champions were practiced executioners.
Alöökö sought
The riches of this world.
At this very time,
Against Alöökö’s might,
Could any mortal escape death?
When I look at the havoc,
His time has come to unleash it;
Across the face of the earth
His time has come to conquer in battle.
The great ruler named Alöökö
Was more skillful than other spearmen.
He had studied wondrous lore;
He was a khan of accomplished wisdom.
“You damned Burut, you!”
When Karakan died,
He swore his bitter vengeance.
Born to Alöökö
Were sixty sons.
Of Chin-Machin and Beijing
He was a truly mighty khan.
Indeed, saying “You Burut, you!”
He fixed his enmity upon us.
Gold and silver
He could take by levying tribute.
If one day his fury rose,
He could slaughter us all.
Take heed, Jakyp my son, and understand:
This unbeliever Alöökö,
This warrior never sated with wealth,
Has a treacherous gleam in his eye.
Having exhausted all our strength,
He now stands ready to strike me down.
Something else lurks in his eyes,
Something else lies in his words.
If this unbeliever has the strength,
He stands ready to tear you apart.
Alöökö is enraged:
He will make your women bondswomen,
He will make your men slaves.
Against the raging unbeliever,
Can anyone who answers back prosper?
There is no man who can match that dog.
Must such a black day fall upon us?
