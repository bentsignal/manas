He ranged along the Sombuk River,
And slaughtered every man who fought him;
None who faced him remained alive.
Your forebear, your ancestral sire,
Crushed all who crossed him,
And laid waste all he encountered;
He drove mad all who faced him,
And none who traded shots survived.
Such a khan was your father,
And that father’s younger brother
Was the renowned Shygai.
Shygai’s son was Chynkojo,
Who grew arrogant and divided the people,
Stirring the masses and ruining the land.
They bound tassels upon pinewood spears
And made war upon your father.
He fled and joined Toltoi,
Crossing defiles and leading forces over ridges;
The mad wretch died in the blaze of war,
After gathering the people and leading an army,
And raising strife against your father.
They loosed arrows like pouring rain
And fired bullets like hail,
Seeking to unleash chaos
And seize your mother, Khan Akun’s daughter,
From your father.
For ages armies filled the land,
And the bloody turmoil grew immense;
For forty days they fought without rest,
The Jediger and the Kyrgyz facing one another.
A hero favored by the spirits died,
A vigorous warrior in his prime died,
The skillful champion Aizaker died,
And mighty Alymy Toltoi died.
They kept launching mass assaults,
Firing muskets and loosing bows,
And we fought in bloody close combat.
As for Kojo, the son of Shygai,
He began the conflict, and we slew him.
Yesterday we began a glorious slaughter;
Among the champions, valiant Bakai
Must have moved like a wolf in the goat-game.
My heroic mother Kanykei,
Under the cruel khan’s bullets,
Was captured as plunder and made a slave,
Burning to ashes in her anguish,
Like ragged, matted wool.
She became Kanchoro’s slave,
Her back strained and stooped,
Her eyes bulging as she tended calves;
She must have aged before her time.
Unable to escape humiliation,
That poor woman heard no word from us
To tell her we were safe and well.
She could neither cease her sobbing
Nor surrender her soul and die;
Her wits scattered in bewilderment,
A film veiling her darkened eyes,
Quarreling even with dumb beasts,
Her dear face wrinkled,
She must be living in torment.
Blazing in anguish, turned to embers,
Her soul embittered and tightly knotted,
Now dying, now flaring like fire,
This must be what became of her.
“My son Seytek, listen well:
When your poor uncle speaks,
He has suffered countless evils.
His sorrow is a wondrous tale to tell.
From our own kin we suffered humiliation;
From those nearest us we met disgrace,
Affliction, misery, and great hardship.
Yesterday in Kara-Buura and Chatkal,
With your warrior father at their head,
When the Kyrgyz ranged proudly there,
Your ancestral sire was of noble stock,
And every one of your fathers was a khan;
Whoever fought them met his grave.
Your ancient forefather was named Nogoi—
And Nogoi was no easy foe.
