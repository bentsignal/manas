Its strength gone, it waddled,
And that white swan stood exhausted.
Semetey’s great war drum,
Covered in waist hide—
The mighty drum was made to resound perfectly;
Was it not Bölekbay’s skill
That fashioned the mighty drum so well?
When it was struck in the central land,
Its blow could truly carry
Across a journey of six days.
When the war drum was beaten hard,
The earth split with its rumbling,
And the mountains echoed and roared.
The leopard struck the war drum;
In the marshes of Ala-Köl,
While the tiger’s Akshunkar
Fought with the white swan
And remained locked in struggle,
He shouted, “Manas! Manas!”
And raised the battle cry of Manas.
Overcome with joy,
He pleaded with the two boys beside him,
Calling, “Hurry and reach it!
Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
My two equal supports,
You two, my younger brothers,
Reach it before an arrow could;
Get there and cut its throat.
Bind it securely again
And fasten it to the saddle straps.
Slaughter it lawfully,
And take it as a gift
To my dear mother Kanykey!”
After the falcon took it,
Kulchoro and Kanchoro,
The two choros, raced each other,
Their stirrups striking together.
Before they could reach the swan,
Wearing his handsome patterned coat,
Semetey, brave son of Manas,
Thundered along the road.
He made his neck turn like a stag’s;
Buurul was a true courser,
And when game appeared along its path,
It sprang without delay.
He charged straight toward the swan;
Poor Buurul’s hoofbeats
Shattered the ground beneath him,
And he flew roaring like a cloud.
Before one could say this or that,
In the time it takes to blink,
Semetey arrived like a master.
He had gone the length of a spear,
Keeping his eyes fixed on the white swan.
Wondrous Akshunkar lay there,
Clinging to the white swan’s neck;
The white swan lay thrashing,
Locked in battle with Akshunkar.
Raging, he made a sudden move,
From tireless Buurul
He sprang and leapt down.
The swan fled, and the falcon was gone;
When Semetey opened his eyes,
Such is the worthlessness of this world.
He saw the swan crawling away;
The blue-maned tiger
Reached out his hand to seize it,
But caught the joint of its wing
With an unsteady grip.
The white swan tore itself free
And escaped into the deep water.
Where he waded into Ala-Köl,
The lake caught the swaggering madman
By the throat.
He pulled back in grief,
And Semetey, still bewildered,
Ran toward Arbuudan.
What could the desperate prince do?
