The three heroes surrounded him,
And when they leveled their keen spears
And struck him from every side,
He did not so much as blink or frown;
He never bowed his head before conflict.
They could not overpower him in combat;
They roared but could not break his strength.
When they grappled, their weapons could not pierce him;
Against that wrathful, calamitous infidel,
Even blazing saxaul wood could raise no ember.
The whole world could not overcome him;
At anyone he faced, Sarybai
Kept thrusting his spear.
A savage struggle, a great calamity—
Sarybai kept creating it.
Like a wolf, Sarybai
Kept tearing apart all he encountered.
A fierce struggle, a mighty battle—
Sarybai kept waging it.
Though the three heroes surrounded him,
He did not flinch when struck with clubs,
He did not groan when cut by swords,
He did not stir when pierced by spears.
Conflict could not subdue him;
When they contended with that swine,
They could not find the slightest means against him.
Then Sarybai
Was seized by still fiercer wrath
And did not stand watching without fighting.
He was no mere nail as before;
His fury mounted and possessed him.
Brave Seytek, born of a lion,
Turned the gray kulan steed’s head;
Kuyaly’s men and Külchoro
Had taken up this cause
And now sought the way forward.
When Sarybai, son of a monster,
Unleashed his wrath,
The wide world grew narrow.
The mighty, broad-shouldered man
Grew old beneath his trials.
Under Sarybai’s venom,
Under the calamity of his wrath,
All the people despaired.
The savage lion ranged unchecked,
And all who faced him were slain.
He hunted, encircled, and cut them down;
The people endured terrible hardship.
He greatly multiplied their wounds
And showed them endless suffering.
He made his guests spend the night
And forced them to recount long tales;
He brought many forms of destruction
And shot men down from above,
Making their days one unbroken torment.
When the people spread over the summer pastures beneath the blue road,
When summertime arrived,
When snowdrop blossoms bloomed
And nightingales perched in the willows,
When living souls spared from death
Swarmed like tiny blue ticks,
He poured out his fury more fiercely.
Seizing anyone left behind,
He tore apart their ribs.
Heroes died and khans died;
All who confronted him died.
The eyes of the skillful were pierced,
And under this infidel’s venom,
All he met were beheaded.
Army upon army surged together;
A great many people were slain.
He unleashed a raging, disastrous conflict—
Such was his lionlike ferocity.
All who survived death saw
His smoke set the heavens ablaze,
Tormenting my soul;
He mangled all he encountered,
Gnawed apart all he met,
And grew fat from drinking blood.
He slaughtered livestock down to the black camels,
