With the black Kalmyks and Manchus,
The poor Kyrgyz of forty households,
He made them equal to those peoples.
He cut the arrogant down to size.
Those who oppressed others,
With battle-axe blows and pounding,
Lion Manas set straight.
If they chanced to come,
Not one remained alive.
Dust rose from his advance;
His voice drove souls out.
Among the Kyrgyz of forty households,
Manas the leopard was born.
Ears heard and eyes saw;
Among the Manchus who heard his name,
Your generous hero caused an uproar.
No one dared approach.
Before your tiger,
No one who met him survived.
No one dared come calling.
Your bloodstained hero himself—
No one who attacked him survived.
The poor Kyrgyz of forty households said:
“Manas has mounted his horse!
For the forty households who have suffered,
He has become a lord and protector.
What God once inscribed upon our brows
Has become a giant blessing for us.”
They pledged offerings to Baabedin;
In the path of Allah the Most High,
They pledged a horse with moon-shaped hooves.
Remembering how they had faced death,
“From the black Kalmyks and Manchus
We have gained deliverance,” they said.
The wretched Kyrgyz held a feast,
Pledging their offerings to Baabedin,
Making sacrifice with fervent cries.
All the Kyrgyz cried out;
They hung tether ropes around their necks,
Raising their voices in a clamor,
Imploring the Creator.
All their grief was released;
All their inward anguish eased.
Their exposed backsides were covered;
All their honor was restored.
What had broken was joined again.
Thus the Kyrgyz rejoiced:
“Our extinguished fires have kindled,” they said.
“God has granted us Manas as our destiny.
A gray shelter, a secluded thicket—
Warmth untouched by wind—
God has given us,” they said.
“Parted from our homeland with its court,
We were a people who came wandering.
The black Kalmyk and Manchu peoples—
We used to be caught between them.
If we answered back,
If we resisted even once,
We would be slaughtered.”
Devising wonders no one could imagine,
The wretched Kyrgyz held a feast.
They played kök börü, raising dust.
“We have escaped the Kalmyk people,” they said.
They raced two-year-old horses, raising dust;
The Kyrgyz rejoiced wildly with loud acclaim.
“We, the people, have been delivered,” they said.
The countless Kyrgyz held a feast;
The people's lives were now at peace.
This was where Manas became Manas;
This was where Manas won his name.
He now put on the white kalpak;
He now sat astride his horse.
The two reins and single lead rope
He now took into his hands, they say.
He established a court and became khan;
He became a man of saintly lineage,
A man of settled standing,
With all twelve parts of his body sound.
For the grieving Kyrgyz of forty households,
He became a lion to shelter them.
To the surging black Kalmyks,
He became a dark flood as high as a mountain.
Then Manas the leopard
Scouted and ranged over the land.
When restless, they shot deer;
When not restless, in Altay
They played at ease.
He chased wild asses over the plain;
He grappled with Kakanchy;
He raised an uproar in Beejin.
He was the champion, he was the support;
He was the famous lion Manas.
They spread their wealth about;
Fat lambs and tender heads,
They set boiling vigorously.
They banished all their sorrow.
Wealthy Jakyp's forty yearlings
They slaughtered on a single occasion.
Old Jakyp, whose life was his livestock,
Said, “They killed my forty yearlings in one day!”
He knitted his brow and frowned.
Unable to speak against it,
Jakyp scolded inwardly.
When dawn spread clear and bright,
When light touched the earth,
When true dawn broke,
When the dawn had opened,
When the cold wind of daybreak blew,
The reed tops whistled;
Young larks chirped.
Light touched the earth.
The forty boys beside him,
When they looked at the rising ground,
Had their attention drawn away.
Blending with the rising ground,
To the forty boys and Manas,
A wolf appeared.
When he saw the wolf,
He surged like a lion;
Counsel came from the unseen.
His body started and shuddered.
Lion Manas, looming heavily,
Saddled the horse Toruchaar.
He fitted his sword snugly at his side;
He fastened his mace crosswise.
He armed himself and girded for action.
Your gray-maned, valiant Manas
Swayed as though struck by a peri.
He readied his excellent steel;
He prepared and fastened his gear.
The forty boys beside him
Grew fierce and made ready,
