I will smash you down upon the earth!
Across the rough and broken steppe,
Along ridges no horse can tread,
I will stretch my stride like an argali.
I will deliver punishment
To that deceitful, cunning woman!
If a ravine with cliff-bound hollows appears,
If a deadly mountain rises,
I will wheel and twist my neck.
That beautiful woman,
That cunning and clever infidel,
That woman with her tinkling laugh,
That infidel who thinks herself so bold—
If I do not veer and wrench my neck,
If I do not bring her crashing
Against the deep black earth below!
I will break her arms and leave them dangling;
That mincing wretch—
I will end her life in this fashion
And cast her sprawling on her back,
Unless I avenge the blow she struck me.
Though my flesh be torn apart like a sheep’s,
Though my path be straightened like a felt mat,
I will open my mouth like a cavern
And scatter blood-flecked white foam
Across my chest.
I will traverse the four corners
Of the whole round world forty times.
My back shall bend without arching,
My shriveled hooves
Shall spread as broad as a great cauldron.
I will stretch my mouth against the reins
Until I deliver your punishment;
I will bend my body from head to foot,
And make the woman who asked for the horse
Despair of her fly-sized life.
I will truly open your eyes,
Carry you off and subdue you,
And kill you.
I will run on and gouge you.”
Kyyaz returned to his throne.
At that moment Aichürök
Lashed the cloud-swift bay horse
Wildly from side to side,
Digging in her heels left and right,
And set out upon the road.
The ground crumbled beneath his tracks,
And he thundered faster as he went,
His soles pounding upon the earth,
The bit of black steel
Clanging in his throat.
Though only a beast, the great bay horse
Raced against the flying birds;
The pale reins, knotted at the grip,
Stretched taut between both hands
Of the terrified woman,
As she struggled with the bit
Of the plunging bay horse.
Wiser than a mere beast, Tootoru thought:
“If I do not twist my neck like a stag,
Suddenly bolt away
And dash her into a pit,
Until she is utterly smashed to pieces!
If I do not pour out your blood by the flaskful
And truly drain your strength,
You wicked daughter of Akun,
If I do not tear out your life!
If I do not cast you down and spill
Your black blood by the flaskful!
She wears a coat befitting a hero,
And mounts a mighty horse as though she were one.
The ravine is deep, the cliff sheer,
You accursed, blazing wretch,
You brazen woman,
Who deceived brave Kyyaz,
You surpassingly cunning infidel—
I shall wrench my neck like a stag.
If a cliff a spear’s height rises,
I shall leap without turning aside.
I shall dash her brains out on the stones,
