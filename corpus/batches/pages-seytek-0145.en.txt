He alone was the healer,
He had keen black eyes,
In Kyzylsart’s own keeping
There was a compound red medicine,
He had every kind of remedy.
He had searched great Baghdad,
And from a deserted place obtained
A medicine called matchless.
He had searched great Egypt,
And the potent medicine
He had obtained from there.
With one dose of his medicine,
The pain subsided.
With one dose of the remedy,
The eyes opened wide.
Of those potent medicines,
Of all his remedies,
Even one that could stave off death,
From the black heart of medicine,
Kyzylsart spared no effort
And carefully applied the cure,
But here the medicine did no good,
And Choro did not recover.
Grieving in all sincerity,
Despairing of his fly-like life,
Choro burned with humiliation,
And despaired of his entrusted life.
As Choro stood there thus,
Your foster-father Kyzylsart
Was himself the healer,
A keen and careful eye,
Weighing thoughts and shaping words,
He also had discernment,
A remedy for every illness,
And he also had resourcefulness.
When the sun sank shimmering,
When the foremost of the people lay down,
A blue mastiff with a leather-capped head,
A dog left behind by the gray wolf,
Howled and whimpered in grief.
Parted from the magnanimous lion,
We stand here powerless.
While my lion-hearted uncle lived,
We grew to manhood as lions.
After my uncle Semetey passed away,
We who were white steel became notched,
We who were heroes fell into sorrow,
We were parted from the renowned lion.
Foster-father Kyzylsart,
Our backs made sore, to Kyyaz
We came as docile pacers.
Parted from our good fortune,
In Jeti-Özön, among the Jediger,
To Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
We came as spoils of war.
Our herds were plundered,
Our blood was utterly drained,
Separated from the six divisions,
Our strength departed long ago.
I bear a deep-dug wound,
I bear a vast, seething misery.
Longing to die, I cannot die,
Yet cannot take my own life.
Our herds were given over to plunder,
Barely a spoonful of blood remains.
I am fading away.
Foster-father Kyzylsart,
Please take us as your companions.
The stars in heaven have turned pale,
My father was your old friend.
I am frozen by the cold—give me your coat;
I beg you with both hands outstretched.
My strength is gone—give me your medicine,
O noble, khanly father,
Give me all your remedies.
I beg you, father, grant me your best;
Give me your hand and do not abandon me.”
Thus did Külchoro
Speak in his wretchedness, and then
His foster-father Kyzylsart—
