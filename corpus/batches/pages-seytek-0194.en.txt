You became khan over the waters of Jeti-Özön,
Yet you rage and will not listen.
Go into the earth, Kyyaz—farewell;
You shall never again ride upon my neck!
You shall no longer charge raging at the foe,
Nor roam Jeti-Özön again!”
Thus Tootoru spoke these words,
Tootoru the horse speaking like a man,
And saying this—
Aichürök, daughter of the peri,
Thought, “Should Kyyaz hear this speech,
He might suddenly start a quarrel.”
Such was Chürök’s nature:
Whoever saw her fame was startled,
For she had learned wondrous spells.
Eighty charms and a thousand incantations—
She had memorized and recited them all.
She had no equal in sorcery,
That radiant infidel;
Her cunning surpassed all, and she was most knowing.
Behind the sorceress
Hung a tassel from her hair;
Within that very tassel
Was a dangling yellow key,
A key that opened every single thing.
Many were her graces—
If only you could see poor Chürök!
Thinking, “If the horse speaks, he will hear it,
And the peerless hero Kyyaz
May suddenly start a quarrel;
If he learns of it, he will raise a dispute,
And mighty-armed Kyyaz,
Shouting and starting a brawl,
May tear my collar apart;
Yet if I ignore it, that too will be wrong,”
With matchless quickness
She seized and swung
The beaded tassel bound to her hair,
And with a resounding crack
Struck Tootoru on the forehead.
You care about nothing,
You swaggering Kyyaz.
You let a woman take your horse—
That is what you have become, Kyyaz.
Bloodily afflicted, you grieved;
May witless Kyyaz come to ruin—
You have lost the horse Tootoru!
Your wing is broken at your side;
You are undone, brave Kyyaz!
Parted from your swift steed,
Disaster has overtaken you, Kyyaz!
Jagged cliffs and a severed road—
Alas, Kyyaz, farewell!
Suffer misery and come to grief—
Alas, Kyyaz, farewell!
This woman will take you as her spoil,
Gouge out your eyes,
Strip the brows from above them,
And though she now caresses you,
In the end she will cut off your head!
She will dig your grave deep
And show your eyes a marvel.
Though you plead, she will not heed your words;
Like water poured on fire, she will quench
Your breath, brave Kyyaz!
Though you plead, she will not heed your words;
She will fold your body double while you live,
And, fawning slave Kyyaz,
This woman will cut short your breath.
You heed no word spoken,
You follow no road shown,
You stubborn Kyyaz.
Hero Kyyaz, brave gray wolf,
Your wings have been clipped;
You have lost Tootoru,
The steed you cherished!
The renowned steed’s tail
Was once braided five strands at a time;
Would a lionhearted warrior
Ever give his horse to a woman?
