“For the grunting boar,
This is the moment of death,”
He said, taking aim with measured care.
At the raging infidel,
The warrior who had destroyed nations,
The youth rode up and thrust.
Both spears struck together,
And when the two spears hit,
They burst and splintered to pieces;
Fragments as large as tents
Flew whirling into the sky.
Those who watched and saw them,
As the two stood face-to-face
And in turn drove their spears
At one another,
Despaired of their lives.
Their bullet-proof armor split,
And Kulansur and Tookyzyl
Sank hoof-deep into the ground.
As the heroes met,
Their valor was revealed.
When they struck spears with cries of “Manas!”
The earth split five feet deep;
They trampled the ground beneath them,
Urging Kulansur on.
He flung forth his fury,
While that monstrous warrior, the wretch,
Grew angry with Tookyzyl.
It turned its deer-like neck;
Kulansur would not stand still,
While Tookyzyl was a true thoroughbred
That sprang up from the earth.
When they came beneath the horses,
Would Sarybay, son of the ogre,
Refrain from fighting?
His cavernous mouth opened,
And burning embers from the bit
Scattered in volleys.
His savage courage showed,
And he burned in bitter grief.
The cause of his grief
Was that Sarybay
Had never paid anyone the slightest heed;
No mortal had dared face him,
And he had never deigned to notice a mortal.
No one had ever leveled a spear at him;
He had bowed his neck to no one in the world,
And no one had ever challenged him.
Against that rabid boar,
None remained to ready a lasso.
Khans had died fighting him;
Every khan who clashed with him had died.
Warriors proud of their strength had died;
Lions who sought combat had died.
He had begun savage battles,
Renowned in name and fierce in venom;
He leveled all who crossed him—
At the sight of his wrath, abandon hope.
Yet on that day the madman’s
Evil purpose came undone.
Seized by fury,
Sparing no precious life,
He cried out in anger,
Shouting menacingly,
Churning the blue heavens.
His spirit seethed and madness boiled;
Raging, he ground his teeth,
And doom boiled over the warrior who opposed him.
His form bristled and transformed,
Revealing his intent to destroy.
Then Sarybay
And the warrior Seytek
Fought without sparing their lives,
Drenching themselves in crimson blood.
They stood thrusting spears,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
Striking each other in furious combat,
And hewing at one another with axes,
Until the axes splintered apart
And only their shafts remained in hand.
