Damn you, seers!
Saying you knew the hardships of the Altai,
You told me many tales.
Who would believe your words?
You have become my enemies.
You muttering seers,
You appear as foes in my eyes.
Keep far from this path, seers;
Never appear before my eyes.
When I mount for battle, none can humble me;
What man could ever catch
A winged racehorse?
You have shown hostility
Toward my kinsman who has returned.
You glared at him with hatred
When my brother came home.
Our father is Khakan Chilaba;
We wandered astray and grew up apart—
Ask no more about our kinship.”
Thus Kongurbay spoke.
His great boots curved at the toes,
And his neck arched like a rooster’s.
His spectacles gleamed,
While Zulpukor, which cut with every stroke,
Flashed upon his wrist.
When he arched back and drew his dagger,
From the dagger’s edge
Sparks rose as large as a nomad’s camp.
Six thousand Chinese san-kaldai
Stopped without uttering a word.
“Your hearts are hostile and your tongues are venomous;
You Chinese—I bear a grudge against you.
You have set out to block
My broad and open road;
Falsely claiming to be seers,
You cunning men have sought somehow
To put my heart in order.
By making my brother a stranger,
You have sought your opportune hour.
Saying you wished now to learn
Of the six-month ordeal,
Look how the ill-omened Manchus
Have spoken lies.
I chose to listen to your words;
By babbling such nonsense,
Whom have I ever corrupted?
When I stiffen in rage,
I will not leave one of you untouched.
If you boast that you are seers,
Do not stir my Chinese wrath.
When I rage and storm,
I will not leave one of you untouched;
Until now I have endured your conduct.
You have committed countless deeds;
While I continued to spare you,
You brought violence upon my golden head.
I called you clairvoyants, seers,
But you did not do this once—you did it a thousand times;
You passed a judgment no man could bear to hear.
By openly calling him a Burut,
Damn you, swine,
You stirred strife against my brother.
You have grieved me, seers;
Shall I dig your graves?
Shall I fall upon all of you
And make a glorious slaughter?”
Thus Kongurbay spoke,
Arming himself for battle,
Slung his great gun across his body,
And brandished in his hand,
Ready for use, his keen sword.
Such was the rabid Kalcha,
Hunched in fury,
The hair atop his head standing on end,
Bristling as though he meant
To slaughter the snarling Chinese,
His thoughts dark with rage.
The Manchus and their khans—
