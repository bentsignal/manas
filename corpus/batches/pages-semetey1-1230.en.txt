Your measureless sultan
Struggled with Buurul.
At twelve years old, Külchoro
Possessed perfect fortune;
From your ocean-like Almambet
The warrior remained as a gift.
Your spring flowing from a river,
From mighty Sultan Alman
Your young champion remained as a gift.
Leaning back and watching the pass,
Your valiant hero stood looking.
He rode a horse blue as water,
Its hair tail tied in a hard knot,
The kasaba banner fastened on.
Like a cloud about to rain again,
Wrath covered his face.
When one looked at his battle-axe,
It seemed as large as a house;
The heart of anyone watching
Was torn apart.
Hero Toltoi of the Jediger
Was a lion among fathers’ sons,
But lacked in repentance.
Had his repentance been complete,
He would have camped among the people
As a companion, not a swine.
What could dear Toltoi do?
He turned in anger,
Sword, gun, and battle-axe
Fastened together at his waist.
Swelling with terrible spirit,
Saying, “I shall take Aichürök,”
He came blustering onward.
The patterned spear left as a gift
By his father, noble Manas,
Valiant prince Külchoro
Took out and leveled toward him.
“Ah, Almighty, grant me faith;
If death has reached me, let me die.
Against Toltoi of the Jediger,
Let me face him once and test myself.
To this brother-in-law who comes to take a wife,
Let me settle his account now.
If Toltoi is a hero,
Let me wrestle him one against one,
And fight for seven days.
I have reached twelve years of age;
If fate has reached me, let me die.
Against Toltoi of the Jediger,
Dueling and spearing one another,
Let me grapple once and test myself.
Standing one against one,
Going out to fight in single combat,
At Toltoi, who comes to take a wife,
Let me charge and level my spear;
Against the brother-in-law who comes to take a wife,
Let me summon all my courage.
If death has reached me, let me die;
With Toltoi, huge as Ala-Too,
Let me clash and test myself.
At thundering Toltoi,
Let me level my spear straight;
Fighting in fury,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
Standing my ground without fleeing,
Let me learn now the courage
Of Toltoi, who comes to take a wife.
Seizing thundering Toltoi
By the hem of his coat,
Fighting each other to exhaustion,
Fighting with courage,
Facing Toltoi directly
And clashing together,
Let me make this spectacle.
Before anyone else, Toltoi—
Akkelte, left by my father,
Surpassingly mighty and straight,
Let me strike him with that;
