I never yielded
Or stepped aside from the straight road.
When I sought what I desired,
I served as mediator before khans,
Going freely among them.
In the narrow passages of the wide world,
While the lion-hearted noble lived,
Where even a surging flood could not enter
And no attacking man could reach,
I went to Bakburchu of Kambal Khan,
Out upon his plain.
Through the gate where the khan was fortified,
In the narrow passages of the wide world,
While generous Manas,
The youngest of six lions, still lived,
When death was shared amid the tumult,
And among the people of forty tribes
The dear hero still lived unharmed,
When one or two great fortunes
Were flourishing,
I bore a golden shield and spear
And rode proudly,
Rejoicing before
The generous khan.
To the plane-tree garden of the Khagan,
To Khan Esen’s throne,
I went as an envoy.
I then petitioned for
Khan Esen’s golden throne.
I did not flinch or lose heart,
Nor did I suffer the least distress.
Nowhere on the face of the earth
Have I seen suffering like today’s.
Though death might come and my days end,
I did not die for Kuyaly.
Never while alive have I seen
A calamity like this,
Because of deeds long past,
Because of this lady,
Because of her fortress high as a craggy mountain,
And because ancestral spirits stand between us.
How can I go to that fierce-tempered
Warrior maiden, saying,
‘I will bring this matter before her people’?
That warrior maiden is a hero,
An infidel who lays nations waste,
Yet she is a broad-hearted soul,
Slender-waisted beneath her red dress,
A graceful beauty.”
Knowing secrets from the cast of her mind,
Such was that graceful warrior.
Her own words were sweet as sugar,
And her gazelle eyes shone brightly.
She was slender as willow silk,
Her eyes set like a young camel’s.
She had black, kohl-darkened brows
And long, flowing raven hair.
Her speech was marvelous;
She alone had been created
With beauty such as this.
She was gifted and gracious with people,
Her nature lovely, her speech honey,
And every word she spoke was sweet.
Sixty young wives and eighty maidens
Shared in her pleasures,
Slept within one palace,
And told each other their inmost secrets.
So many souls formed her retinue;
Among the warrior maiden’s attendants
Was a beautiful lady.
She was the light of sparkling eyes,
The fairest of the attendant maidens,
The most eloquent of living girls,
The ablest of humankind.
To the moonlike, beautiful maiden,
To that exalted one unlike other mortals,
To that infidel, vigilant and prepared,
She could unravel the knot of any matter,
A poised and open-hearted woman.
