Hiding his pastures beneath clouds of dust,
Driving humiliation away like sheep,
Hiding his enclosures beneath clouds of dust,
Raising his khanship above all others,
Your father conquered Beijing.
Your father became a sovereign;
If you challenge the judgment he received,
You will be maimed—
God has struck you senseless.
Yesterday, when our handful of Muslims
Waged a holy campaign against Beijing,
Your father, Khan Köksö, died;
My father Almambet
Died there as a martyr.
The pride of the forty warriors—
Mighty Chubak—died there too,
And many champions died beside them.
Semetey, your own cousin,
Do not incur his wrath—heed my words!
Such is the custom; this is my word—
Let Khan Semetey understand.
Crossing the lands of Ach-Maral and Merki,
Passing over the Ayry-Kaykang ridge,
The famed Keng-Kol and great Talas
Are not lands for you to besiege.
Semetey, son of the bloodthirsty hero—
Do not test my khanly uncle’s mettle;
You will lose your honor for nothing.
These are not people you can intimidate.
Let not your pretentious khanship
Be revealed for what it is right here.
If you provoke him,
He may slaughter every one of you,
Not sparing so much as a hair.
He may slaughter all of you,
Young and old alike, leaving none.
Let your wise men not act rashly,
For if your leader is a fool,
What fault lies with all the people?
Let the surviving Kazakhs
Not be forced to flee back to Sary-Arka.
Your people, champions, and khans—
As if another massacre had fallen upon you,
You will all be slaughtered.
If the bloodthirsty hero runs wild,
Can the combined right and left wings withstand him?
When that fierce warrior grows wrathful,
Does anyone who fights him escape alive?
Do not squander your own wealth,
O Kazakh people swollen with arrogance;
Do not let your blessed men be slaughtered
And then return home, all of you pleading,
Your heroes disgraced as cowards!
If war should break out by mischance,
And we meet spear against spear,
If by mischance we slaughter one another
And innocent people die,
If every district is devastated,
And we despair of precious life,
If the flower of the great nation,
The innocent people, are slaughtered,
Let us not share in such wickedness.
If the famous rumors prove true,
May He show no savagery,
And on the doomsday of resurrection
May He not make black donkeys of us.
May the smiting God not strike us;
May this handful of faithful people
Not stand on Judgment Day’s narrow path
Demanding their blood from us.
May He show no brutality
By killing the innocent,
And may He not humiliate the two of us,
Turn us into black donkeys, and ride us.
Understand what I have said;
Without quarreling or raising strife,
My noble cousin, heed my words!
I have a vessel upon the water,
