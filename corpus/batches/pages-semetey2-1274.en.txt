Er Shuutu, whose cap was broad as a roof flap,
And Yrchy-uul, son of Yraman,
All the falcon warriors,
And Kutubiy, blessed with good fortune—
Who cut off a head when told to take a cap,
Who spilled his own lifeblood when told to shed blood,
Who drank poison when told to drink water—
The truest of the valiant companions:
If only all those falcons
Had turned and stood watching!
Then let Kanchoro
And brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Have inflicted this disgrace!
Only then let them have wrung out my wretched, screaming soul
And taken my life!
My strength is exhausted and my vigor gone;
I have no power left to fight,
And dark blood drains from my body.
All the Jediger came swarming;
My fly-like little soul was hard-pressed.
The settlement fled and scattered;
The hostile foe, like a dragon,
Appeared close before my eyes.
Jylgyn and Keng-Kol remained behind,
The Argyn and Kyrgyz peoples remained behind;
Now only the grave awaited Külchoro.
I have no swift steed to seize and mount,
No kinsman to look to,
No forest into which to flee,
And no brother to stand beside me.
Could fate grow as dark as this?
If I turned away and fled, damn it,
If only I had a thoroughbred beneath me,
If only I had my former strength,
Would I not draw a trail
Across the whole wide world
And make such sport as never before?
I have no charger to choose and mount,
No kinsman to be my companion.
Flames fill me within, embers cover me without;
My confidant and uncle is not here.
If poor old Bakay—
My uncle above all—should die,
And I, his wretched warrior, should die,
If the moon were veiled in darkness,
“What would the night become?”
What would become of Kanykey in her old age,
And of the wretched aged Bakay?
“What would their days become?
Bakay will be left without burial;
The stooping old and foolish young,
Left alive in this false world,
Will likewise remain unburied.”
So Külchoro pondered deeply,
Because of every enemy,
And because of his life held only in trust.
His wrathful kindred khans,
Those precious souls, could not be hidden.
Unable to find his bearings,
Unable to cross Keng-Kol,
Unable to reach Kara-Üngkür,
The Black Cave of the holy seer,
His fly-like soul screamed.
His whole body was wounded;
All his bones and joints
Were shattered and crumbling like sand.
His broad boots with crescent heels
Flapped about his feet;
His khan uncle’s gray cartridge pouch
Slapped against the hero.
His father’s long matchlock rifle
Gleamed in his hand.
He had no single charge left
To fire the matchlock
And make Kyyaz and Kanchoro
Turn their backs and fall.
A dreadful calamity, a black fate—
Külchoro had no strength left.
