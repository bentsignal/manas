He was the true-born son
Of the finest stone-armored Chinese.
Hardened by entering battle,
He had won renown as a lion.
He had pledged himself to the twelve holy patrons;
God had fashioned him aright,
Favored him and opened his path.
When he struck stone, he never faltered;
No enemy survived facing him.
He turned pursued deer into cattle,
And made the whole world his subject.
He bore eighty-four pieces of armor;
Wherever he swept, there was fire.
Whoever faced him met destruction;
He loaded his bow with arrows
And annihilated all who fought him.
The lion’s own son,
The young prince,
Poor Khan Almambet.
He furiously attacked,
Kindling a fierce and violent struggle.
The horse he rode was Surkoon,
With mattock-like hooves and a broad rump,
A stallion fit for open country.
Ridden into combat, it never flinched;
Ridden on a raid, it never ached.
Ridden scouting, it never tired;
Ridden into battle, it never faltered.
With a broad rump and owl-like feet,
It never wearied when ridden into combat.
Its tail streamed like a parrot’s;
Though the road were ninety days long,
It galloped without stopping.
That creature Surkoon
Had endured ninety battles
Of turmoil across the hills.
When a spear was thrust, it darted aside;
When a battle-axe fell, the brute
Suddenly swerved the other way.
Such was its habit against the foe.
“The prancing beast
Is about to fall,” he said,
And with a shout thrust his spear.
Your hero, the ogre’s son,
Did not flinch, standing firm as iron;
He counted the boy’s thrust spear
As no more than the hem of his robe.
He found its true measure,
Seized the outstretched spear,
Snapped it at the middle,
And chopped it into twelve pieces.
“Come, you celebrated champion!” he cried.
“Come, you mighty warrior!
If your youth cannot come, let your elders come;
Let the strongest of you all come!
Leave the rest—let your khan come;
Let every Kyrgyz swarm upon me!
I will not cease speaking the truth.
I shall strip away your strength,
And swallow your blood like kumis.
Not leaving even a hair of you,
I shall slaughter you all!
When I roar, most of you will die;
If by chance I pounce,
You will go where none return—
Then you will behold my brutality!”
Thus he stood boasting of his valor.
God Almighty had struck
The infidel son of an ogre.
When Külchoro heard this—
Külchoro, whose fortune was complete,
Who was a steel bullet against the foe,
A glowing bed of crimson coals,
Whose equal did not exist among all men,
Whose fortune was complete—
He was descended from his father Almambet,
A lion born greater than his sire.
His father was gray-maned Almambet,
Descendant of the strongest
Among the armor-wearing Chinese.
