“Every one of you, swarm over her!
Trample this creature called a woman,
This bitch, into the black earth!
The madwoman is a champion—
Overwhelm her in a mass,
Fall upon her with spears and axes,
And tear her limb from limb!
Slaughter her giant Ulukman,
Gouge out his eyes and blind him!
Close in from every side,
And thrust your spears all together.
Plunge into turmoil and battle
And take this bitch’s life!
My intimates and confidants,
My companions who grew up beside me,
Who shared adventures with me,
Who joined me in hunting argali,
Who rode on raids with me,
Who walked beside me
Into the seething ranks of foes,
Facing the dire bullets meant for me!
She is molded like pure wheat dough,
Slender as a white gazelle;
Unable to spear this bitch,
My tiny soul has burned away.
My spear bent at my waist,
And such force has come upon my body
That my spine aches,
My right ribs have folded,
And ruinous pain has struck my head.
My sight has dimmed and my strength is gone;
After jousting with such mighty force,
The ill-fated bitch of a champion
Has shown her stubbornness.
She stretched forth and struck once with her spear,
And Sarybay despaired of life.
His insides burned like embers;
Nothing like it had been seen in the world.
Sarybay was shattered to pieces
And seemed utterly consumed.
My dear champions,
Comrades who share death with me,
Companions gathered in life,
Faithful mourners if I depart,
Stand not idle—thrust your spears!
Friends dearer than brothers,
Ever since I became Sarybay,
Ever since Sarybay mounted a horse,
I have worn a golden crown.”
Then Sarybay,
Roaring menacingly,
Sat astride his horse with legs spread wide.
“I have seen countless holy wars and conflicts,
And never died amid their seething tumult.
Never upon the earth have I seen
A calamity like this warrior girl.
Swarm over her from every side!
The work of the many is a lake, a river—
Stand not idle; level your spears!
Stand not idle; unleash the battle!
Rescue your falcon, Sarybay,
And bear him away!
Confidants who heed my words,
Companions who restore life to me,
Men who stand beneath my banner,
Men ready to sacrifice their lives!”
When Sarybay had spoken thus,
The vast host surged forward;
Such is the way of a general mêlée.
Arrows filled the air like rain;
Those whose appointed death had truly come
Fell to the shafts and boiled in misery.
The earth was torn apart and night descended;
Think of it—a day of calamity had come.
With frenzied snarling,
Musket balls whistled,
And arrows from bows flashed;
Wherever they struck, bodies burst apart,
Blood poured in streams,
And the gnawed earth was gouged into hollows.
