Some of the people who stood watching
Felt their beards grow thin.
“If only that radiant, unfortunate woman
Were my wife,” they thought;
The boldest young men
Let their saliva run.
They marveled at the sight of Chürök:
“Among all human beings ever created,
She is the radiant Night of Power;
That shining, ill-fated woman
Is a noble princess reduced to bondage.
Her waist is slender as a scabbard, her face like the moon—
How renowned she is throughout the world!
Apple-necked, with an argali’s breast,
Her wide-set eyes surpass those of any mortal.
With broad hips and long tasseled braids,
She has beautifully wide-set eyes.
In a crimson coat, with a crescent waist,
That graceful beauty
Is the wondrous one left among the people.
That the great host was slaughtered
Is no lie but the truth;
She herself is the renowned beauty.
No ordinary mortal could produce such a soul;
Her eyes are those of a noble princess.
When one looks, her face shines;
Her complexion changes and glows.
When she eats and drinks, the food
Can be seen passing down her throat.
Her supple body stirs like soft leather,
And her face flashes like the sun;
She speaks softly, glowing warmly.
Golden buttons and forty fasteners
Dangle and chime
At the cuffs of her sleeves.
Judging the unfortunate woman,
Her whole figure is beauty itself.
The black hair braided down her back
Is thick as a wrist.”
Of radiant Chürök they said:
“She is no mere Chürök; this woman
Is a calamity who has thrown the people into turmoil.”
She smiled brightly,
Her white teeth gleaming like shovels.
With a thimble mouth and camel-calf eyes,
In a crimson dress, with a crescent waist—
Alas, the graceful, ill-fated woman!
When one looked, she stood apart in dignity;
Every movement of Chürök
Seemed bathed in moonlight.
Her glowing white brow
Satisfied every watching eye.
She was slow to turn away;
Longing to see Chürök,
Not only Kyyaz but all the Jediger
Stared with doubled eyes.
Of the extraordinary Lady Chürök,
The calamity on everyone’s lips,
They said: “She has a thimble mouth and camel-calf eyes,
And speech as sweet as honey and sugar.
Above her lip
There is the faintest trace
Of a tiny beauty mark.
May your sister-in-law’s suffering end—
Where is any flaw in her?
She has the finest long hair;
The noble, ill-fated woman
Is the mightiest among women.
Look upon Chürök’s beauty—
The purest wearer of a flowing dress;
Is this not the very beauty,
The queen of all women?”
That day warrior Kyyaz
Stirred the people and held his feast,
Carrying out a plan no man could conceive.
“Kyyaz has taken Chürök,” they said;
Of all men anyone had seen,
“Warrior Kyyaz has become the gray-maned champion,” they said.
