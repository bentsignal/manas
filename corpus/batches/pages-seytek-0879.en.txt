Their senses reeling and their strength gone;
No words remained for them to say.
What calamity was this, what deed,
Whose outcome none could foresee?
While his people grieved,
There was one lion devoted to his nation,
A hero born to heroic fortune,
A tempered blade of black steel.
Have you not heard the tidings
Of that noble lion’s descendant?
Famed and mighty in wrath,
He was the magnanimous hero’s son;
Those who once defied him were hewn down,
And hostile enemies were slaughtered.
His forefather before his father
Had received a blessing from Babadin;
He was called the son of a warrior lion.
Each time he dwindled, he blazed anew;
Each time he died, he multiplied.
God prospered all his deeds,
And the Forty Holy Ones and Khizr Ilyas
Accompanied and sustained him.
The hero-born warrior Seytek,
A mighty youth built like an elephant,
Had stirred a vast commotion throughout the world;
A warrior in courage, with a heart of stone,
A leopard blessed with surpassing fortune,
He was a saint born but once
Into this deceitful world.
His racehorse was Kulansur—
Not Kulansur, but Kuyunsur,
A rare gray steed born once in an age,
Which his fairy maternal ancestors
Had presented to him as a gift.
With flowing forelock and full mane,
The creature was magnificent;
Noble-backed and gentle-tempered,
It never wearied when ridden over hills,
Never tired in battle,
Never flagged on a distant ride,
And never flinched when ridden into combat:
A dark bay battle horse,
With sieve-like lungs and copper wings,
Its bit like a polished lance,
Its darting motion like a snake,
Its speed like a wild ass.
God Almighty fashions
Living creatures in every kind.
Broad in the chest and narrow behind—
Such was the racehorse Kulansur.
Upon that swift Kulansur
Were laid a leather saddlecloth and blue saddle,
A white felt pad beneath,
All fitted upon Kulansur.
More skillful than the most accomplished,
A friend of the prophet David,
The master Bölökbai of the Forty Kalmyks
Had fashioned your noble hero’s white saddle
In exquisite beauty.
Its pommel was white gold,
Its cantle solid silver,
And both sides were broad iron;
It did not pitch him forward on a slope.
Into the golden-soled stirrups
The noble lion, dearer than a father,
Leapt without touching them and sped away.
His lionhood stood revealed
As he mounted and galloped;
His eyes flashed, burning like fire,
And he sharpened his tongue like a millstone.
His courage and lionheartedness
Showed in his singular bearing;
The hearts of all who watched him
Surged and leapt within them.
Taking courage and receiving their blessing,
He set forth in valor.
Such was that noble lion,
That gallant hero, proud as a cock.
To face bloodshed, courage, and mighty strife,
To test himself in combat,
