Look at the deeds
Of that accursed Kojo.
I shall not submit to what he says;
Unless I, Khan Akun, die
And behold the face of the other world,
I shall not give Aichürök away.
Before I give Aichürök,
Let me behold the face of Judgment Day.
Let me die in slaughter
At the hands of mad Kojo and Toltoi;
Let me face in battle
The engulfing Zhediger;
Let me stand as a shelter
For Manas's only son, found late in life.
I shall open the mouth of my treasury
And scatter abundant wealth
And ten thousand livestock for the war.
Until the pliant reeds ripen,
Until the blossoms of the steppe grass fly,
Let me fight fiercely for six months
And test our strength against them.
He is no black, rugged mountain
Which the people cannot cross;
Chynkojo has turned black with rage,
But he is no enemy come from the Kalmaks.
He is no mountain with high cliffs;
This accursed Toltoi
Is no enemy come from China.
Saying he will marry a girl with in-laws,
Saying he will unleash an upheaval,
Accursed Toltoi has lost his senses.
While this worthy head remains,
How can I surrender my child?
The Zhediger have grown covetous;
Let me try bringing ruin upon them.
While this dear head remains,
How can I surrender my people?
This swine has turned black with rage;
Let me test myself against him."
He said this to the great people,
Speaking horrors for the common folk to hear;
He raised the pale-mottled banner,
Shouted the cry of "Khan Akun!"
And his heroes formed a separate band,
Going in apart—did you see?
Their khan, hero Khan Akun,
Gave courage to them all
And advanced to face the foe—did you see?
The renowned men were in front,
The valiant men at the flanks;
The fearsome men were in front,
The lions at the flanks.
Akun went out to war,
To face the peril,
And the Zhediger who loved danger
Set their faces toward battle.
They committed their bodies to the spear;
They raised and leveled
Their alengir bows and inlaid arrows.
For Aichürök's sake,
They stood casting away their lives.
With dear Akun at their head,
They came out of the city and drew apart;
The Zhediger's ten-thousand-strong army
Stood facing them head-on.
Shouting in anger,
They threw their remaining strength into the fight,
Swarming more thickly than worms
And teeming like ants.
Their banners stood apart;
The Zhediger and the Afghans
Faced each other as though to devour one another.
Standing without coming close,
They fought with guns.
Unable to close and grapple,
The two armies stood at a distance,
Drawing bows and shooting.
