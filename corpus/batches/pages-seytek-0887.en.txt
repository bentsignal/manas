The ill-fated swine’s
Bloodthirstiness stood revealed.
He turned Tookyzyl’s head,
Hellfire blazing from his eyes;
Thus did God strike
The warrior, brave Sarybay.
Raging and springing forward,
He slung crosswise his black-steel,
All-cutting sword.
His soul boiled with bitter fury,
His eyes blazed like a wolf’s.
He turned his courser’s head,
And the raving madman
At once renewed the battle.
Facing brave Seytek,
He rumbled and worked himself up,
Transfigured by his straining strength.
His shout could split a head,
Set snow aflame, and shatter stone:
“Since I became Sarybay,
My boy, since Sarybay first mounted a horse,
Since I grew to manhood as a hero
And earned the name of warrior and lion,
Since I towered aloft and mounted a steed,
Cutting through hell and warring against foes,
Since I have lived as Sarybay,
Taking sword in hand and bearing spear,
Charging the enemy with a cry,
I have driven over many lands.
Whenever my destined hour approached,
I drew my steel and bloodied it;
I enslaved the whole wide world
And laid low all who fought me.
Upon the face of the created earth,
No pass remains that I have not crossed,
No land that I have not trodden.
When I aimed my course, I camped in the desert.
Since I became Sarybay,
Springing like a blue lion,
Coiling like blue steel,
Though I frowned in sorrow,
My heart never turned black.
Mighty in spear and potent in venom,
Never before this Seytek boy
Have I met his like upon the earth.
That ill-fated boy’s
Shouting blow with the spear,
And the courage with which he stood,
Made the marrow of my bones ache.
Since I became Sarybay,
Since I grew to manhood as a hero,
And rose to monstrous size,
I have challenged countless men
And become embroiled in countless deeds.
I have seen many spears and guns,
Yet fate never reached me and I did not die.
Many a savage clash and mighty battle—
Never have I seen such calamity.
If I do not ride him down and spear him,
If I do not avenge this boundless grudge,
If I do not finish off
This insolent boy
Who boasts of being warrior and lion,
If I do not sever his head like a sheep’s
And split his belly like a melon,
If I do not avenge this boundless grudge,
May I sink away as though never born;
May I die rather than live.
He seems a resourceful boy,
A soul of surpassing courage;
His champion’s and lion’s prowess—
His valor is monstrous indeed.
As for that ill-fated boy,
In the name of brave Sarybay,
I shall tear apart his ribs,
Pour out his blood like kumiss,
Slaughter his village to the last soul,
And rest only after cutting them down.
I shall spill his blood while he yet lives;
The sly boy seems to have gone mad.
