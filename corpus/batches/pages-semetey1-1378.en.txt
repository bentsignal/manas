Muradyl’s foremost man—
His ears that heard and eyes that saw,
The champion called Zhöözheldet—
Chose and donned a fine gray coat,
Mounted an unhurried pacing horse,
With a battle-axe swaying in his hand
With blue mail and a felt cloak
Flashing across his broad back.
As the sun was sinking low,
At the spur of the distant mountain,
At a great, jutting promontory,
The sight caught his attention.
The devil take your father, Zhöözheldet—
He loomed like a gray-ridged mountain.
He rode up to the pass
From which he kept watch and looked;
Seeking a safe route through the land,
He looked off to that side—
Within Tüp lay Chaar-Tal,
With no cawing crow in flight,
No croaking raven in flight.
He looked off to the other side—
At Kara-Tal by the Jyrgalang,
On a spur of Boz-Señir,
There stood this dark peril.
A gray-mottled tent could be seen;
When Zhöözheldet saw the tent,
His heart grew deeply troubled.
On this side were fourteen horses,
True, dark-coated thoroughbreds,
With sieve-like lungs and copper forelegs,
Their broad rumps a whole armful,
Coursers bred on spacious land,
True stallions to ride into battle,
With blunt tails and solid hooves—
They were beasts to ride in war.
What kind of men are those accursed ones?!
With muscles like a bull’s back,
Fourteen coursers could be seen;
Every one of the fourteen
Was tied high upon a hitching line.
Those were no ordinary tails and manes;
They were not horses of our people of Beijing.
They would not panic at a battle cry;
Their marks as coursers could be seen.
In the darkness of the moon,
They would not misplace a single step.
Stepping on rock high in the mountains,
They were no beasts that would slip,
Surpassing the wild deer that roam the hills.
They would not tire when ridden on reconnaissance,
Nor grow sore when ridden with the baggage train;
Fourteen coursers could be seen.
“I will withdraw without being seen.
It is the sly Burut himself;
The day-chasing bastard
Has arrived here now.
I will slip away and turn back;
I will tell great Lord Muradyl.”
Zhöözheldet hurried in alarm
To the tent with bronze poles;
Before one could say this or that,
In the blink of an eye,
Toward hero Muradyl
He turned his horse’s head,
And openly told him now
What he had seen and learned:
“My lord and khan, Muradyl,
I saw a vast, empty tract;
I saw men as huge as mountains.
They are no idle wanderers;
They bear a manner all their own,
And to my eyes they looked like enemies!
When I counted them from afar—
