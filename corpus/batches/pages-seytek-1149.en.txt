Without so much as blinking,
The wrath of great Bilgich
The gray-maned hero heeded not, though it would drain him dry;
They fought in turns,
Neither yielding to great Bilgich’s fury
Nor to the venom he unleashed,
Standing firm without submission.
The incomparable Akjoltoy
Had saddled Kulunsur.
Every spell known to man,
He recited without omitting one.
Great Bilgich, the mighty sorcerer,
Sent forth an icy wind,
Splitting the blue-gray sky apart,
Wrapping everything in mist and confusion.
But Akjoltoy was a true champion:
He made the beguiled sun appear,
Subdued the hell of the cutting wind,
Akjoltoy held it down,
And opened up the day,
Making it brilliantly bright.
Great Bilgich’s fury left him,
And he came out into the sunlight.
That Bilgich ceased his infernal raging
And regained his speech.
When he rode out from the Great Khagan,
Great Bilgich fixed his hatred
On Akjoltoy and the valiant Seytek.
Intent on taking Kulunsur as spoil,
He charged with a roar,
Leveling his spear with a battle cry.
As he was about to reach Akjoltoy,
Brave Seytek, that peerless lion, thought:
“If the infidel reaches my uncle,
Then afterward, upon this land,
The end of days will surely fall.
I shall drive my horse without restraint
And bar the path
Of this wrathful calamity.”
So saying, brave Seytek
Turned all his attention there;
He loomed like Mount Opol,
While Kulunsur beneath him
Wheeled as he gripped his spear.
He reined the cloud-swift steed
Hard against the fetlocks,
And that matchless Kulunsur
Turned his neck toward the foe.
With a stout shaft and keen point,
The tasselled Syr spear—
“Creator, grant me passage!” he cried,
And straight at great Bilgich
He thrust it with a thunderous roar.
His eyes blazed like a lion’s;
His broad white teeth seemed set like stakes,
Teeth worthy of royal blood,
And he ground them section by section.
He roused a terrible rage,
Counting his precious life as a hair,
And launched a furious assault,
Hurling the spear with all his strength.
At the bristling boar
Before he could truly reach Joltoy,
He aimed for the face of the elbow,
The very brink of waiting death,
Between the lungs and heart,
The measured mark of mortal doom,
Level with the ribs:
“If I strike him exactly here, I have him!”
He chose the mortal spot,
Lashed Kulunsur with the whip,
And before that hulking champion
Could reach the hero Joltoy,
The peerless warrior struck Bilgich
With his spear.
His senses failed and his strength ebbed;
At that moment great Bilgich
Reeled unconscious and bewildered.
