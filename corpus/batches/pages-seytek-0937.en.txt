Our knives will be whetted,
Whatever we gain we shall share together,
Companions sharing every endeavor.
Our red tongues speak eloquently;
You, river-maned lion, must decide.
Look with favor upon the innocent people.
If a gathered nation flees,
Its abandoned livestock rots at bare stakes,
Yet while it endures, a people sustains life.
The guilt for this conflict is upon my hands;
Now, gray-maned lion, we stand
In your hands, my hero.
We lie within the iron cage
Of the iron-armored gray-maned lion.
Having encountered the bloodstained warrior,
Hero, we stand within your grasp.
We have entered the flames of hell
And stand amid its embers.
From your fathers and forefathers
None ever yielded to an enemy,
No mortal who faced them prevailed,
No man could withstand their coming.
They say fortune favors the worthy,
Yet when you look within,
They say the face of the earth is broad.
How many have come lamenting,
How much wealth is there,
How much lies in the treasury?
Every soul begs for its spoonful of blood;
Understand this, noble gray-maned lion.
We are captives fallen into your hands;
I renounce defiance—hero, heed my words.
Your first ancestor was Manas Khan,
Whose yurt walls were anointed with fat.
When men such as us fell into his hands,
He made them khans throughout the lands.
He enriched the lattice walls of their yurts,
And when they fell to him as plunder,
Your champion ancestor Manas Khan
Made them khans throughout the lands.
If you are a hero and a lion,
If you are Manas’s heir in times of trial,
May you spare us,
The common folk now in your hands.
If surging wrath seizes you,
You may slaughter us all.
You will come upon us without turning aside;
If your fierce fury begins,
Then commence the massacre.
A hero needs a people;
Leave our lives intact,
Hero, act according to your will.
Livestock and souls need land,
And you, hero, need a people,
A broad nation of common folk.
Know that this is what I say;
Generous sultan, heed my words.
Heed them and leave us alive;
If you do not, slaughter us.
We stand with lives held in trust,
Amid the embers of your judgment;
If you fire your gun, we are your bullets,
If you kindle a blaze, we are your fire.
Amid conflict and calamity,
We are trapped upon the road,
In a net from which no struggling frees us.
We are in so deep a pit, hero,
That no winged creature could escape.
We are in such a broad abyss
That no four-footed beast could cross.
Having cast down our spears and bowed,
We are steeds come before you,
Regard us as strangers taken in plunder,
And ask how much wealth we possess.
Following the custom inherited from our fathers,
Let us camp together as neighboring clans,
Breaking and sharing white wheaten bread,
Joining hands upon the sacred oath,
Making our pledge firm,
Swearing upon our mortal lives,
