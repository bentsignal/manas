No one could tell who was who.
The sky was clear, the earth overcast;
Not a speck of ground was visible.
Soil flew up and dust filled the land;
Not one person could be made out.
When Esenkan gave the order,
“Bloodstained Manas, the leopard,
Do not let him go—bring him bound!”
While their ruler spoke,
The iron-headed troops jostled;
They struggled with all their lives.
Their stone-badged men in short coats
Pressed against one another.
Charging in a noisy tumult,
They lost each other in the throng.
The eye could not pick them out;
May God never let you see it!
No one could tell who was who.
The Kytay were an orderly people,
Who had not been overwhelmed by the surging Deluge,
A people who had survived.
The accomplished champions made ready;
The seven divisions stirred.
Dust flew into the sky;
Champions marched in throngs.
Khan Zholoy of the Kalmyks,
His courage exceptional, his fury strong,
Leveled whoever attacked him.
If you see Zholoy, do not approach;
Abandon hope before this unbeliever!
They clothed him in blue armor
And mounted him on Achbuudan.
Broad of girth, short of stature,
Round-faced, broad-shouldered,
Sparing none who grappled with him,
With the meat of a whole young bull,
He was not sated at a single meal.
His prowess was extraordinary;
His valor was considerable.
Wrapped in blue armor,
If you saw great Zholoy,
Raging like a gray boar,
Smoke billowing from his mouth,
Flame streaming from his eyes—
Such was that mad wretch.
His champions of many kinds,
He took his army in full.
Not merely Manas—that mad wretch
Had power to match the world!
His mace was half a mountain's size.
Do not ask about Zholoy:
He seemed ready to devour anyone he saw.
The finest among champions,
If you ask about Zholoy,
He was a lord of the black Kalmyks.
Born in the west,
An outstanding champion, a great glutton,
He killed those who attacked
And raised an uproar among humankind.
The finest among those with gray yurt coverings,
Such was Zholoy:
A lord of the black Kalmyks,
The summit of a snowy mountain,
A prince of the black Kalmyks.
Zholoy took the road
With an army numbering one thousand.
The one he would bind was wealthy Jakyp;
The one he would fight was Akbaltay;
The one he would grapple with was Khan Manas.
If these fell into his hands,
If his strength and power sufficed,
He would gouge out their eyes in torment.
Manas and the Kyrgyz forty households—
He would drink their blood until sated.
Behind that Zholoy,
Clad in bright red finery,
One held precious in Beejin,
Whose fame was heard and known,
Whose prowess was extraordinary,
Whose valor was considerable,
Whose very name inspired fear,
A skilled horseman, swift on foot,
Learned in wondrous arts,
A powerful sorcerer, a great weather-conjurer,
Came before the khan.
One who came forth when trouble broke,
An expert, valiant marksman,
Among the skilled who drew the bow,
Among the finest who cast lassos,
Among the very bravest,
Among countless marksmen,
Among the skilled whose courage was full,
Among the mighty who never retreated,
Unhurried when floods overwhelmed him,
No ordinary man, that accursed one,
Never retreating before fire,
Among those who stood beneath the banner,
Charging at the cry of 'Enemy!'
Among those who girded their waists tight,
Karazhoy set out along the road
To capture Manas and bind him,
To bring calamity,
He too with an army of one thousand!
Behind him,
A mighty man wearing a short coat,
Skilled, a cultivator of wisdom,
Outstanding among spearmen,
Excellent and renowned in war,
In short, a hero in battle,
A master thoroughly versed in the enemy's tactics,
A cruel-hearted unbeliever,
A hero who knew the enemy's stratagems,
His sword with a precious keen edge,
His spear with an exceptional point,
Sated with dark blood,
Standing to fight without fleeing,
Posted in a perilous place,
As though about to strike,
Tethered with a collar and chain,
Choosing troops from the formidable,
Döödür set out along the road,
He too with an army of one thousand.
Taking orders from his khan
To unleash terrible wrath,
Upon the Burut lord Manas,
To descend upon his head,
Slaughtering, butchering and destroying,
To scatter his four kinds of livestock.
