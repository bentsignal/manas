Had cherished him as a future son.
He taught him to recognize the Zhydymak watch,
Trained him to shoot at targets.
Long ago, mighty gray-maned Semetey
Equipped him with every weapon
And made him khan of the gatekeepers.
A master marksman in battle,
A surpassingly skillful servant,
He stood facing
Kanchoro and Khan Kiyaz,
Firing guns and drawing bows,
Fighting in bloody clashes.
Akzholtoi charged in and broke their ranks.
The nobles guarding the gate,
The sentries standing watch,
The keepers at the gateway—
Many champions fell to bullets;
Hardship and uproar arose.
Akzholtoi would not consent
To the ruin of the people and sack of the palace,
To leaving the nation open to plunder.
He would not surrender
The widowed sister-in-law, the mourning woman,
As spoil to the Zhediger.
Trusting in God, they stood,
Split the ranks in two and fought.
Akzholtoi alone against that host
Gave battle.
At Akzholtoi's side,
Emil Chochmor the hero died;
Egizey and Kocher died together;
Akbalban, who swept the gate clean,
Died, leaving no support.
Akzholtoi remained alone.
Kanchoro with his vast army
Had begun his sweeping attack;
Every one of the gatekeepers,
The accursed traitor slaughtered.
Defending the gate,
For seven days and nights,
Akzholtoi refused to yield it
And fought without rest for seven days.
When seven days were complete,
Akzholtoi's means were exhausted.
He had no strength left to fight;
Bullets filled the space around him.
He had no strong back to rely on,
No hero in whom to place hope.
The beloved Kulchoro,
His liver an ember, his dearest support,
He had no supporting friend;
Even if Akzholtoi died now,
His elder Khan Bakai was absent.
Gray-maned Semetey, who had protected him,
Was absent too.
Valiant marksman Akzholtoi
Had no charger to catch and mount,
No bloodthirsty khan to protect him.
Mane and tail were wholly gone.
His heart burned, his liver was ember;
His hands boiled with fury,
Strength came to his flylike soul.
Unable to endure it, Akzholtoi
Fought without rest for seven days,
Receiving wounds in seven places.
His whole body was torn;
Every bone ached and throbbed.
The two bloodthirsty khans, Kiyaz and Kanchoro,
Flooded in with a roar.
The world narrowed in his anguish;
Akzholtoi stood humiliated.
He longed for a single swallow
Of saliva sweet as sugar;
His palate dried and hardened,
His eyes stared wide.
All the bones of his body
Sagged as though he were dying;
His body was crushed and bloody.
