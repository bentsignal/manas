"Oh Almambet, my soul's friend,
If you go out to joust,
This act of yours will bring shame.
If you truly are my friend,
Stand aside and watch impartially.
Lay down your agitation
And be our support.
Consent to what I say.
Sarala beneath you
Is a beast fit to ride into combat;
Give us the use of its strength.
If you give Sarala's strength,
We shall ride your swift horse.
Against that dog who boasts of strength
I shall clash and test myself.
He has mounted Algara
And has grown boastful of his valor.
Against that boastful dog
I shall meet chest to chest.
Against that dog who boasts of strength
Let me see how our clash will end!"
The hero Manas, the panther,
Before all the people, from Almambet
Asked for Sarala.
Almambet restrained his anger
And surrendered Sarala.
To the hero Manas, the gray wolf,
Unable to say, "I will not give it,"
And unwilling to reject the hero's wish,
He consented to what Manas said
And gave Sarala, bright as moonlight,
To his great-hearted lord.
Upon Sarala
Was Alake's white saddle;
Three girths and three cruppers—
He loosened all six,
And removed the white saddle.
Taking off its tack and leaving it bareback,
He took a shortcut down a narrow path,
Leading Almambet's Sarala
Along entirely bareback.
The gray sash that fell from the sky
Was wrapped around his slender waist;
The panther touched by Kydyr
Prayed to the Almighty.
In fury he made himself ready,
And buckled on Zulpukor,
The six blades that fell from heaven.
The lionlike gray-wolf panther
Moved with ringing confidence,
Holding the warrior Kongur he would fight
As worth no more than the hem of his robe.
He gave no thought to where it might end,
And never thought, "I shall die."
When the great host of dark-clad Muslims
Looked on, their hearts were stirred.
The panther's guardian spirit
Appeared like a snow-covered mountain;
Nine protectors appeared
On his right side,
And nine supporters appeared,
Bearing the likeness of takers of souls.
The blood-drinking lion was enraged,
Truly seized by his thirst for blood.
When one looked upon his front,
His eyes blazed like a lion's;
He charged like a tiger,
And none he struck could remain alive.
He frowned darkly in wrath—
Such was the panther's state.
From the giants Koshoy and Toshtuk,
From Azhybai and Khan Bakai,
With palms raised, saying "Amen,"
He stood asking their blessing.
The two khans, Bakai and Koshoy,
Hung their belts around their necks;
Raising their palms and saying "Amen,"
They prayed to the Almighty:
"O Almighty, amen!
His fortune has always been complete.
