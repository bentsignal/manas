They bound his hands fast.
Poor Kenen, the lion,
The champion who girded his waist,
My strength, the Kyrgyz hero,
The one ordained by Almighty God,
The one who loved the white-capped people.
Kenen stood alone with God.
They devastated what stood assembled,
Making their great violence known,
And burning the soul with its truth.
They summoned the Kalmyk to grapple.
He rebuked them in his wrath.
They twisted his white arms behind his waist.
Test the whole body and bind it tight.
They fastened the restraint they had made.
They forced his supple waist to bend.
They drove him toward the gray Kalmyks.
Before all the countless Chinese,
They left him with teeth of humiliation.
They had him bound in disgrace.
They made his two eyes spread wide.
They bound him before the many Kalmyks.
They trampled and plundered him.
Saying, “He avenged your forefather,”
They had the wrestlers beat him.
Then they drove him before their khan.
The cruel, cold, dark night,
Poor Kenen saw it.
His two hands were bound,
While his two golden eyes still shone.
They bound him tight before the khan.
They brought him down to the promenade.
To the golden-covered platform,
Before the hero Chengker,
They dragged him forward and did this.
