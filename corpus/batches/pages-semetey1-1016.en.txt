Turning the ill-fated gray horse at an angle,
He saw the maidens and grew excited;
Without looking all around,
Without sparing his life before that sea,
Without searching all about.
The doomed man who had seen the maidens,
Before the formidable Urgench he had entered,
Did not value his life by so much as a hair:
"Damn this childishness!
If I fear flowing water,
May God strike me outright!
Does the bare-fanged Azrael
Stand watching for me,
Thinking, 'Kulchoro will cross the river'?
If I truly fear the rushing water,
May God strike me outright!
I fear the rushing water;
Yet if a challenge arose in a place of torment,
Brandishing their spears and shouting,
If six thousand enemies came forth,
How could I not fear them?
My elder brother Semetey
Gave me his horse without begrudging it;
Ignoring the horse's own desire,
Saying, 'The flowing water is fierce,'
How can I disgrace myself and turn away?
He gave me his coat without begrudging it;
Ignoring the coat's own worth,
Before I have even seen hell,
Saying, 'The speechless water is evil,'
How can I disgrace myself and turn away?
Rather than hear reproach from my elder brother,
Let me die in the rushing water;
Whatever Allah has written for me,
Let me bear it upon my neck and see!
If the flowing water brings my death,
Let me die this very moment!"
Without searching around for a ford,
The formidable, extraordinary Kulchoro,
Not valuing his life by so much as a hair, said:
"If I should perish upon the road,
I trust in you—Allah, uphold me!
If I live, grant me what has been gathered;
If I die, grant me faith!
Rather than be mocked by the maidens,
Let me behold the face of Judgment Day;
Rather than be mocked by women,
Let me be swept away and die in this water,
And behold the world beyond this passing life!"
Mounted on Buurul, he entered the Urgench,
Shouting, "Almambet!"
And raising the battle cry, "Manas!"
He swung the black-jawed whip,
Gripping it in his hand;
He drew the spirit-like Buurul
Sideways and let him go.
As the maidens stood crowding the bank,
He broke away the top of the bluff,
Prayed to the Creator,
And Kulchoro plunged into the water at a gallop.
The top of the bluff broke apart,
And a mass of earth as large as a house collapsed,
Pouring into the water with him.
The truly spirit-like horse Buurul
Stumbled where he entered,
Recovered from the stumble,
Braced himself and gathered his strength,
Then vanished beneath the water where he entered.
When your Buurul disappeared,
Thirty brides and thirty maidens
Crowded around and wailed;
They clapped their palms together,
Crying out as though a husband had died.
Many maidens, seeking to help,
Ran up and down;
They despaired of Kulchoro,
And the maidens, distraught, cried out,
Wailing as though a husband had died.
