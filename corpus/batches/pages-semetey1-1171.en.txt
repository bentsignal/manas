The deftest spearman,
Akbaltay's Chubak,
Finest of the pale-yellow warriors.
The Chinese lost heart;
Your precious Chubak appeared,
And with a hungry shout and fierce battle cry,
He charged in shouting.
"We were guests, hero," they said.
The finest stone-bedecked Chinese
Gathered together,
And the Black Kalmyks and Manchus
Subsided and sat down heavily.
Along the Uch-Karkyra
A gathering now formed in a ring.
From the city of Kakanchyn,
Thinking, "If there is conflict with the Buruts,
He may prove useful,"
They had brought a champion
With strength unknown to man.
The champion of the infidels
Was hairy-soled Tukubay;
That indeed was his name.
For slaughter in particular,
They had prepared this formidable man.
He never spared one who grappled with him,
And a whole pig's meat
Did not fill him at one sitting.
When he charged in fury,
It was the end of days for a man;
He was a merciless champion.
The breadth of his nape
Was twelve spans.
Tukubay entered the wrestling ground
And stood staring with a grunt.
Kokotoy's Bokmurun
Issued another proclamation:
"My father Kokotoy has not died;
Kokotoy's wealth
Has grown like spring grass.
Today I open the treasury
That fills forty rooms.
For infidel and Muslim,
I will scatter it lavishly.
With lower lips drooping,
With tusks jutting,
Necks raised like ducks,
Their two humps towering,
I will slaughter one thousand camels.
With branching horns jutting,
I will slaughter one thousand cattle.
Making their foals cry out,
To begin this spectacle,
I will slaughter one thousand mares.
Making their lambs bleat,
Lining up their mothers,
Delighting those who slaughter them,
I will kill one thousand sheep.
Dividing gold like stones,
Showing my wealth to the people,
I will stake this much gold.
Infidel and Muslim,
Let the people assemble;
Hold another wrestling match.
Strive without regret,
Grip firmly and grapple,
And win your prize outright.
My wealth is inexhaustible;
Make a proper spectacle,
And divide Kokotoy's abundant riches
Without quarreling."
