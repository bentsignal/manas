Sobbing in grief,
Wailing and weeping bitterly,
What she had called early became late,
And in longing to see her dear son,
Her two eyes became four.
Her mind reeled and tears streamed,
Her heart surged and her senses fled;
To see the blue-maned lion,
To behold him and then die,
To turn her face toward God,
And surrender her entrusted soul,
Her spirit failed and her senses fled,
Bloody tears flowed from her eyes.
Snow veiled her bloodshot eyes,
Illness struck her blood-filled lungs;
Flying like an arrow and shrieking,
Kanykei had set out.
Now that Kanykei had grown old,
Upon the Kökcholok beneath her,
Striking the whip again and again,
That Bakai raced along,
Wrenching and tugging
At his mustache and beard in anguish,
Yearning to reach the blue-maned lion;
If he reached him, poor old Bakai
Was ready to sacrifice himself,
To part with his entrusted soul.
Your lion, Bakai,
Was ready to die before death’s appointed hour;
Your valiant grandsire Bakai—
Most of the old man’s life was gone;
See how hard he rode.
Before one could turn this way or that,
In the blink of an eye,
At the tent with the golden pillar,
They came and found
The bloodthirsty hero who had vanished.
The lost one was found,
The road opened and honor was restored;
Kanykei wept with piercing cries—
That man too was the finest of all.
They mounted him upon a chosen charger;
They had settled him there.
Driving the splendid black stallion,
They were leading him onward.
“My venerable white-haired mother,
My dearest mother,
The only one you cherished has been found,
And now your honor is restored.
What was broken in you is made whole,
What you lacked has been found.
Your hard bones have softened;
Your wasted body has grown stout,
Your hungry belly is filled,
And the hostile enemy is slaughtered.
Remain with us, Mother,” he said,
“My mother who cherished me.”
When she heard this news,
Your poor, passionate mother’s
Heart broke and flew apart,
And tears poured from her eyes.
She wailed and wept aloud,
Sobbing, unable to endure;
All the goodness became clear,
She burned fiercely in grief,
Her heart heaved and her eyes twitched,
Your dying mother rejoiced.
Her snow-white hair had thinned;
When her lost son was found,
How could that poor soul remain dead
And not rise back to life?
To see her Semetey,
To behold him and then die,
To turn her face toward the qibla,
And surrender her withered soul,
She began a fierce exertion.
Kanykei left her dear life
Hanging by a single thread.
The flesh of her palate clicked,
Her mouth parched and smacked with thirst,
