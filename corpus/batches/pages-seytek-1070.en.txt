Then summoned his muraas.
The six champions who tended his horses,
Warriors skilled against the foe,
The finest of his companions,
Would seize anyone who defied them
And hurl him down upon the ground.
When told to advance, they never turned back;
They expertly speared the enemy,
Their heavy muskets strapped upon them.
At the cry of “Charge!” they rushed with a roar,
All equally prepared for battle.
Warriors never sated with blood,
Panther-like, gray-maned champions.
Skilled in drawing the bow,
Master marksmen who never missed.
Each lion had a different bearing,
Hotheads capable of shattering the world.
The cause of their fierce temper was this:
Born of women who gazed upon true men,
They had come into the world as genuine heroes,
Making slaves of those they fixed their eyes upon,
And dealing punishment with their own hands.
They surged forward like wild boars,
And prowled like tigers.
When Seytek gave the command,
They mingled dust with dust.
When black muskets cracked,
They fought without tiring.
When a surging flood of blood advanced,
They stood beneath the banner.
When the fierce musket snapped,
They fought without flinching.
When waves of enemy troops approached,
Each one slaughtered a thousand.
If a mountain-covering flood came,
They stood firm and barred its way.
Throwing it head over heels,
They dashed the tiger to the ground.
If gray-maned Seytek commanded,
“Drink water,” they would drink poison;
“Take his cap,” they would cut off his head;
“Spill blood,” they would spill their very souls.
Those mighty men were ready to strike the foe;
When all were roused to fury,
Their strength could overcome multitudes.
Like great morning stars in stature,
All the companions of fortunate hero Seytek
Were heroes themselves.
They were born for valor,
And marched together against the foe.
And whenever their fury seized them,
They drove giants into the earth.
Through broad ravines where no bird flew,
They sounded brass pipes beneath silken canopies;
Through broad ravines where no water flowed,
They sounded trumpets of brass.
At that moment the gray-maned lion,
And all his well-honed warriors,
Ran as swiftly as arrows in flight,
Spending every last breath in their haste.
All the gray-maned champions together
Showed their nimble prowess.
Once the hero’s command had come,
They said, “Today the road shall be open,”
And took the shortcut along the narrow path.
The great beast Kulansur
They brought before Seytek,
Led by six strongmen.
A white halter tied in a round knot,
A halter fitted exactly to Kulansur;
They smoothed and secured his forelock,
And, lest a stray bullet strike him,
They covered him with armor.
Lest, on a long and distant journey,
The sharpness of his hooves be worn away,
They shod him with shoes of steel.
Such was Kulansur’s nature;
This was the appearance
Of the restless dark steed.
