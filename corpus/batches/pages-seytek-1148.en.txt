With no thought of truce,
And no mercy for precious life,
A fierce battle and great calamity raged;
Spears shattered and heroes fell,
And the turmoil grew immense.
They fought with spears,
Raising piercing cries and hoarse shouts
With all their might.
Seytek rode through, striking as he went,
And any foe who happened to fall,
Akjoltoi cut off his head.
Joltoi rode through, spearing as he went,
And any man he felled, without leaving him whole,
Seytek cut off his head.
They fought, scarcely pausing for breath,
Wreaking calamitous havoc;
When their spears broke and were lost,
They took up their battle-axes.
The falcons who confronted one another
Struck in turn,
Standing face-to-face;
Valuing their fleeting lives not a hair,
They utterly renounced them,
Standing firm for honor,
Fighting without wavering,
Until the tumult became immense.
Mighty Bilgich grew furious:
“We are ninety-four, and these are only two!
Have our strength and endurance departed?
Has all our might abandoned us?
All of you close in and attack!
If grievous death
Is written upon your brow, then face it;
Can a doomed man turn death aside?
Can a brave man with a warrior’s sash
Turn back from death?”
Shouting to embolden them,
The savage Chongbilgich once more
Drove all who remained alive
Into the fray in a surging mass.
He trampled and shattered the battle line,
Scolding all his champions,
Whirling his spear in a rushing arc.
The madman thundered,
Making the wide world seem narrow,
Showing a back as dreadful as hell,
Steeling courage as vast as a mountain,
Working mysteries no man could fathom,
Making his wit, cunning, and strength
Keener than anything in the world.
He dimmed the light and blinded the eye,
Toppling mountains and darkening the sun,
Raising a tumult equal to hell;
Caught in his fury,
The age grew cramped and disordered,
The earth clouded with dust and mist,
And hill, cliff, and lofty mountain
Vanished as though beneath snow.
The weather broke and turned to winter,
So fierce a storm he raised.
Ask no more of Chongbilgich:
A master horseman and swift runner on foot,
Ready and supremely skilled with the spear,
A consummate master of cunning,
His father was the marksman Kojozhash;
Watch his stratagems closely,
And when all means fail, flee.
His onslaught had deep-rooted force,
No living soul survived his bow,
And no creature could withstand
His terrible aspect and venom.
The earth darkened and the waters surged,
So masterful was the battle.
Riding twin gray steeds,
Wearing splendid, mighty coats,
The heroes Seytek and Akjoltoi
Were borne onward by their guardian spirits,
Who raised their hands in aid.
The spirits of their forefathers
Bore them mightily onward.
