United, we would die together,
Facing wrath as one;
If the one true God should will it,
We would give our precious lives.
We wished to see with living eyes
That hero Kenen was a warrior;
We knew he was a lion
And had long sent word of our desire!
What we desired has come to pass;
For the beautiful maiden Aikanysh,
God has granted our wish.
Bowing low in homage,
They clad him in pure golden brocade;
As the saying goes, they feasted on rich fare,
While abundant wealth shone all around.
They entered the royal camp and settled,
Gathering round as marriage kin;
To gray-wolf hero Kenen
They gave Aikanysh in marriage.
One fulfillment of the pledged word:
He became the son-in-law of the people of Rum.
The fearsome Kenen became khan,
And Bolot became his judge;
The two lions Ebegey and Kögöy
Stood at the head of the gathered people.
Just as matters stood thus,
What is seen today is gone tomorrow—
Such worthless dust is this world.
At the shrine of Jan-Jürbös,
While taking the bride and setting out,
They came to a certain place.
There they stopped as guests.
When the dawn shone white,
Akjoltoy and Seytek the lion
Were stricken by an illness.
It struck through their ribs,
And pierced the crowns of their heads;
They coughed with racking bursts
And turned their faces toward
The narrow road from which none return.
Their fly-sized souls cried out;
Their sweet living spirits,
Writhing in anguish, sobbed.
Their radiant faces changed,
And their dear souls, too great for their breasts,
Cried out, rising and falling.
One by one their mustaches parted,
And when the five-fingered angel reached in,
The warriors struggled for their lives.

Visions appeared before their eyes;
The mirage fled and the blood drained away.
They lay ill through the night,
And at the late-morning hour,
In daylight on Friday,
Akjoltoy and lion-khan Seytek
Mounted and set forth on their final journey.
They departed, leaving sorrow behind;
The valiant Kenen and Aikanysh
Were bereft of their two fathers.
Kenen cried aloud and began to speak:
—My two fathers, like the swift waters
Where six rivers run through narrows;
My two fathers, like all the peoples
Of the fabled Mount Kap!
It is as though a fire has gone out;
Fate overtook them on the journey.
To the true abode from which none return,
My two fathers have journeyed away.
They had them washed with kumis
And wrapped in cloth of gold brocade;
They entrusted them to holy men,
And had singers sing their praises.
They wrapped them in cloth of gold
And washed them with Zamzam water.
Ninety mighty diggers
Were made to dig their resting place;
They dug it broad and even,
And set their spears around it,
Digging a grave of unmatched grandeur.
They split open gold and jewels,
And at their feet and heads
Had lamps set burning.
Thus they buried the fathers.
The earth received them,
And the depths of the Chuk shrine were hollowed out;
The two lions Akjoltoy and Seytek
Were laid within the Chuk shrine.
They gathered those traveling back and forth,
Assembling all who passed by;
They raised a stone burial mound
And built an honored mausoleum.
Ebegey and Kögöy led the way,
Their grief unrelieved,
While the multitude drooped in sorrow.
Bereft of their two fathers,
They returned and settled in Talas;
Kenen kept the royal court and became khan.
