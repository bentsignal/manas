The impassioned warrior overtook him.
With one blow of the spear,
He struck hero Ümötöy—
The man well fitted to demand blood-price,
The sturdy man with a treacherous belly—
Preparing to sweep him down.
Ümötöy, son of Khan Köksö,
Was destined to see a different misfortune:
Külchoro hooked him away as he passed.
Then Külchoro wheeled back,
Tall as a pole,
Swooping like a hawk.
He reached the unhorsed Ümötöy
Before an arrow could reach him.
Your lion-warrior turned back
And confronted Khan Ümötöy.
Your impassioned warrior overtook him;
Before one could say a word,
In the blink of an eye,
Without killing or destroying him,
With all his robes and garments intact,
He put the blue mail upon him,
Rolled and bundled him,
And made the fallen warrior
Whine like a howling dog.
Leaving him sprawled like a craggy mountain,
He stretched Khan Ümötöy out.
At that moment your lion-warrior
Captured him alive
And bound him to the bay horse.
Saying, “Here is your kinsman and cousin,”
He brought him alive and bound
Before the falcon-hearted lion.
He stretched Prince Ümötöy
Down from the bay horse,
And mighty Külchoro
Took him from the saddle.
Then the warrior went to his uncle
And spoke to him:
“My lord, time after time
You send me into the crimson fire.
Your mighty Külchoro
Has never died wherever you sent him,
But when I tried to turn away, your kinsman,
This Ümötöy, would not relent.
He reared up in wrath;
Though I pleaded, he ignored my words,
And treated me shamefully.
He tore my coat with spear thrusts
And showed me wonders enough.
He would not heed me when I spoke;
My blood boiled, my heart burned,
And your lone-wolf cousin Ümötöy
Drove me into a towering rage.
They loosed arrows like falling rain
And fired bullets to take my life;
They broke my composure,
Set my blood boiling,
And sorely tormented my soul.
He brandished his pinewood spear
And demanded the blood-price of Khan Köksö.
When I reined in my horse, he would not stop;
At last my spirit could bear no more.
The massed Kazakhs gathered
And tried to bind my hands;
They meant to take as spoil
The coat upon my back
And show my eyes
A marvel no man had ever seen.
Since I first became Külchoro,
Since I first mounted a horse as Külchoro,
Since I first donned white armor,
Sat proudly astride my steed,
And charged the foe with a shout,
Since my fine mustache grew long
And I enjoyed the prime of youth,
Since I first charged the foe crying aloud,
Never have I faced such dire violence.
