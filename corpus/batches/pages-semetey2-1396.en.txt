Because he had seized the hero’s wife,
Warrior Kyyaz alone gained fame,
While the indignant Jediger
Were left to bear the quarrel.
The noble, ill-fated woman
Was Chürök, daughter of Akun;
She was no mere Chürök, but a dark hour,
A black calamity and disaster.
She was the very source of consuming fire,
The dark eye of affliction.
Aichürök was calamity itself,
The dark eye of turmoil.
Though taken as spoil and wed by force,
Chürök would not lose heart.
While Chürök’s eyes remained open,
The Jediger’s undertaking would not prosper.
That noble, formidable woman,
Renowned Chürök, would not vanish.
If you wronged Aichürök too greatly,
She would strip the flesh from a horse’s neck;
Flying through the air,
She would travel the whole world.
If she flew away, she would become a swan,
And uproar would break out among the Jediger.
If Aichürök gathered her wits,
Destruction would come from her.
Wait and see, if she survives,
Strife will arise from warrior Kyyaz.
Chürök is a woman taken as spoil,
Yet she is no mere woman but calamity.
While Chürök and Kyyaz live,
If Aichürök becomes enraged,
She will be the Jediger’s demon.
If she suddenly grows excited,
If she suddenly takes flight,
She will be the Jediger’s demon.”
The people who had come spoke in an uproar
And returned to their own lands.
The great host of khans departed;
Many souls grieved.
One urged another onward from behind:
“Go, for conflict will arise
From Aichürök’s suffering.”
The great people clamored, saying this,
Speaking of such ominous wonders.
Let us leave that matter there;
Of the great people in Jeti-Özön,
Let us begin the news of the nation.
Of warrior Kyyaz among the people,
Let us begin the tale of the lion.
When word came of Kyyaz’s command,
The restless Jediger
Gathered as a nation and filled the land;
By Almighty Allah’s will,
So many souls assembled.
Their old men came crawling,
Their children came tumbling,
And all arrived in throngs.
The Jediger people beheld
A vast feast that split the sky.
At the royal palace,
In the courtyard built for a khan,
One that surpassed all others in the land,
At Aichürök’s pavilion,
For the proud, fierce woman,
Rubies and precious stones
Had been set in alternating rows.
A jewel as large as a horse’s head
Had been set with ceremony
In the eye of the yurt’s crown-ring.
Golden nails were driven in;
Letters and figures were traced in gold
Around the lattice walls.
In making this royal yurt,
Much of the blood-shedding khan’s treasure
Had plainly been spent.
Poor Aichürök’s royal yurt,
While the gray-maned hero was alive,
