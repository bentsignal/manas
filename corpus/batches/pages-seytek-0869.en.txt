What hope can we ever attain?
Hero Külchoro and hero Seytek,
Wearing armor no bullet can pierce,
Riding steeds no wind can overtake,
Have crushed your bravest,
And spilled their blood like water.
They have swept away your renowned men,
And faced and slaughtered
Your champions and lions.
For you to sit there gaping
Is disgraceful;
Stand by your word
And wage your greatest battle.
The khan who vowed to grapple is dead;
All who clashed with them are dead.
The earth was shattered and shaken;
Seven thousand torments and a thousand deaths
Descended upon our heads at once.
A cannon shot came for nothing,
A troop of life-destroyers came;
The lion Choro and hero Seytek—
A shock like these two came,
A deep-dug chasm came,
And misery came upon every living soul.
A messenger has come—give your answer;
Understand what I have told you.
Oh Sarybai, heed my words!
Having attacked a lion blessed by Kyzır,
Let us not camp along the ravine;
Though guiltless and without blame,
Let us not be slaughtered for nothing!
Let us not lose our homeland
And flee away in panic;
Let us not lose our consummate army
And run away in flight!
Take my words as counsel;
Come, Sarybai, heed my words!
Driven by rage and boasting of valor,
We attacked the lion blessed by Kyzır;
Acting with savage intent,
Today we are caught in death’s snare.
No man has ever come forth
To face that ill-starred bloodthirsty tyrant;
No enemy has faced and defeated him.
No one has ever drawn close,
No neighboring foe has conquered him.
With an army covering the earth,
We unleashed a raid and loosed the host,
Raining arrows down like rain.
For every hero we tethered
Four steeds for him to ride.
Mounting the red-roan horse,
He broke the hard ground and opened a path;
Külchoro and Seytek, the two giants,
Came straight at us and attacked.
Seers died and khans died;
Everyone who witnessed them died.
The stars scattered from the heavens
As Külchoro and Seytek joined forces.
Though I pleaded, they would not hear my words;
They tormented me,
Stripped my robe away as spoil,
And wantonly dug my grave,
Bringing marvels down upon my head.
We have fallen into the fierce one’s grasp;
Our strength is gone though we remain alive,
Black blood pours from our lungs;
Only by dying did we escape.
You are the strength on which I relied,
My renowned one, my lion;
Thus have we fallen into torment—
How can we escape death?
The hour of our death has come.”
After he had spoken thus,
Like one pierced in a vital gland,
Like a prince stricken with fever,
Like a man whose wife is crushed by nightmare,
He paled, as though his roots were dead,
As though his whole world had been scattered;
His bellowing split the stones,
