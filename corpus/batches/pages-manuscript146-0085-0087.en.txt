Across ridge after ridge and many deserts,
They came marching onward,
Like a river flowing through a valley,
They came surging onward,
Making a wondrous uproar,
Raising tumult across the empty steppe,
The wind swept past the watching ibex,
The wind thundered where the kulan ran,
They came onward in splendor,
Advancing in confusion from many sides,
The great army that came from the Shibe,
Its afflicted commander of a thousand,
Drove them onward,
At the place where they halted,
That warrior Chyng Tengir,
Into the tent before him,
Before the inexorable hero Tengir,
He brought in his nobles,
The golden throne of Kakan,
He seated him upon it in ceremony,
Posting guards, they rested quietly,
Again taking tribute and appraisers,
Taking the enemy who had encircled and listened,
Guarding their sight, taking the mountain,
With an uproar that split the earth,
Such was the khan’s might,
Crossing ridge after ridge,
Crossing the lofty Aksa mountain,
Inflicting this suffering,
Driving the scattered deer before them,
Binding tigers upon the ridge,
Spreading his road-guard out,
It was the time he set the pacers moving,
Ruining all whom he encountered,
It was the time he cast them into the enclosure.
Many souls traveled between,
When he came to confront them,
If you perceive the calamity,
Now when he came forward,
Chyngteñir poured out his wrath,
He scattered a dragon’s venom,
Wearying horses and scouting the land,
Rousing the people and taking the day,
Taking every covered garden,
He brought disaster upon them,
Bursting forth from the mountain,
His eyes struck like lightning,
Taking away two old men,
Subduing the world,
Again he came to know his people.
The enemy became blood,
All the Kyrgyz learned it together.
When he saw it from the valley,
Leaving the world in grief,
He reached those who confronted him.
The champion’s great fortress,
He saw its blessing.
If one ruined paradise,
If the champion had an enemy,
He brought true death upon him.
He turned the encountered enemy to ash,
He destroyed whoever came against him,
His bitter wrath scattered,
Again it swelled violently,
Any soul who came before him,
Became one of his people.
Putting the unruly in their places,
Massing the wrathful host,
Contending against a thousand men,
Breaking through with the army,
He faced toward the sunset.
Its brushed side became like fire,
Despairing of the life caught in peril,
It brought grievous hardship upon them.
Drawing back his speech, he held to the faith,
Taking the lead before the people,
He made Dombai’s land rise in dust,
When they struggled, he fixed his gaze,
Making a dwelling where he saw a fit place,
He ruled the side that rode out,
His expedition became countless and vast,
His temples showed outward, swollen,
They dug defenses and halted there.
Billowing as though fire had swept over it,
Oh Tengir, what grievous hardship!
It became evident to every soul.
