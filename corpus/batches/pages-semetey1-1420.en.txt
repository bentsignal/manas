It was a land pouring forth radiance,
A people renowned as Beijing.
My father Manas challenged it;
It was fitting that he become its match!
If one faces the numerous people of Beijing,
Their multitude is like black worms.
No mortal has gone against them;
None who faced them remained alive.
He now left Beijing behind
And turned toward Kürpüldök,
Into the hollow of the Sary-Suu,
Toward the face of Kyr-Kaiyng,
To where the clear flowing river
Lies on its western edge.
Kongurbai's countless dark herds,
His myriad livestock taken in tribute,
Seethed like ants;
The accursed one's horses
Could not be distinguished from worms—
They glittered like stars.
Those luckless, barren horses
Resembled growing grass.
From east to west,
All the horses one had heard of and seen—
If all of these were gathered together,
His horses were more numerous still.
They had settled along the valley;
Khan Kongur's horses
Filled it like spring grass.
One kind among the horses
Was mottled black like a starling.
One kind among the horses
Was pale chestnut with slender tails.
One kind among the horses
Were all alike, tawny-colored,
With white blazes like amulets upon their brows;
All were splendid tulpars.
One kind among the horses
Was red-bay like boils.
The accursed one's horses
Came in countless different kinds.
One kind among the horses
Was dun-gray and dark bay,
Divided into kind after kind.
The accursed one's horses
Appeared different in every color.
Some kinds among the horses
Had black tails and black manes;
Among the horses filling the land,
The bays seemed most numerous.
One breed among the horses
Had bracelet-marked gray animals;
One breed among the horses
Had untamed animals never ridden.
One breed among the horses
Was bright yellow as curds;
One breed among the horses
Gathered in flocks like falcon hoods.
One breed among the horses
Were all matching brown ones;
One breed among the horses
Were all swift brown runners.
It was filled with horses,
The plain of Kürpüldök.
One breed among the horses
Were all matching black ones.
The bloodthirsty man blessed with livestock,
That cunning raider of men and horses,
Had one breed among the horses
Appearing white as tents.
Pursuing their stallions and tormenting them,
Was this not a place to drive them off!
Joining the wondrous dust,
And raising a shout over their horses,
Was this not a place to raid them!
The sight of the horses excited him,
And in his excitement he shouted.
