Our souls nearly left us under his grip.
Gathering troops for battle,
Mustering all the people without exception,
He unleashed a terrible calamity.
Women and little children
All began to flee.
He turned every place into a road,
And driving people before him, Sarybai
Brought the whole world into bondage.
We have fallen into your hands;
If you have need of us, heroic warrior,
Let us live.
If stubborn anger seizes you,
Kill and slaughter us.
Here is the broad Kyrgyz land;
Here are the people the hero has reached.
Here is a deeply dug pit;
Here is an army, if you would destroy it all.
The champion of that army
Was exceptional in wisdom.
Those who wielded their sharp tongues
And spoke eloquently before the people,
Skilled and fluent in speech,
Whose words were sweet as sugar,
Whose sayings spoke the truth,
Quick-witted masters of discourse—
These men proved outstanding,
Strong men who judged unerringly.
Those who fired without tiring
Pressed before the ranks of heroes,
Thrusting their spears together,
Seeking out the surest path,
And kept tumbling from their horses.
Their wickedness was revealed;
Falling headlong from their horses,
Sarybai’s vast army
Submitted to brave Seytek
And now showed sound judgment.
Its champions and heroes,
Its wise and valiant men,
Those who cared for the nation,
All submitted in their ranks.
They stood firm as curdled milk, without a twitch;
The vast host lay still without stirring.
They lowered and cast down their banners,
And ceased breaking their switches;
They began their account
And abandoned everything entirely.
Like a spring flowing down the hills,
Like openly devoted friends,
They gathered around the people,
Becoming friends worth remembering,
And soon became acquainted.
Once his army had slipped from his hands,
The mighty Sarybai
Fell into such a furious rage.
His terrible wrath poured out,
And his fierce violence showed;
He stirred up a savage conflict
And slaughtered the host he encountered.
Striking with his battle-axe, he wrought havoc
And plunged the whole world into strife;
A furious battle began.
May your wretched soul run mad—
He had the people bound and cast aside.
He raged against all who dwelt there,
In a land from which no one came
And which no traveler had seen;
It proved to be a wicked nation.
Sarybai, son of a monster,
Was the khan of that wicked people.
He was the wrath of God the Creator;
He did not rule his people justly,
But did nothing except evil.
He gathered an army from that wicked nation
And destroyed the peace of peaceful folk;
Yet through all this, the wretched people
Had endured his rule.
For many years before this,
He had driven this entire nation before him,
