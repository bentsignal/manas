After Sarykan,
The lame smith Bölökbai spoke—
While Lord Manas lived,
His nobility was beyond measure.
A star stirred in the heavens;
Bölökbai and David
Were friends through twelve journeys.
To put the matter briefly,
The noble smith Bölökbai
Was encountered through the unseen;
Dear smith Bölökbai
Was found in his youth
By lion Manas himself.
From every kind of material,
Guns that fired and sharp swords,
He made perfectly
For his companion Manas, the gray wolf.
He was his father's companion,
His surpassing smith and confidant.
Among round-headed mortals,
For noble Manas's hand
He forged and distributed arkesken blades.*
Almighty Allah
Had created your poor Bölökbai
As a man surpassing others.
Know his excellence from this:
For Lord Manas, the gray wolf,
He joined what was broken
And gathered what was scattered.
For glorious Manas,
He built houses as he traveled,
And planted gardens as he journeyed.
He brought all this about by his skill
And filled all his chests
With treasures of yellow gold.
He made his tent-cover rich with fat;
Whenever Manas came to him,
He readied every piece of his equipment.
He fitted Kylbolot* into its sheath,
And for the Forty Companions, forty spears,
Joining on their keen iron points
That felled every enemy who challenged them.
He made the spears splendid to behold;
Saying, "Ah, my poor Bölökbai,"
Manas had a thousand broodmares
From his herds driven to him as an allotment.
Bölökbai, inherited as a legacy
From grandfather Manas the gray wolf,
Was both a lord and a lion;
From Manas who passed before,
The lame smith Bölökbai
Was that hero left as a legacy.
The lame smith Bölökbai
Said this to Semetey:
"The Kakanchin are those you will fight;
My only one, you have undertaken
A holy campaign against Kakan.
Bakburchun are those you will confront;
You stand saying that you will go,
My only one born of a valiant man,
And have set out on campaign to Beijing.
When Bölökbai, Bakai, and Sarykan
Have all come to old age,
You abandon your uncles desolate!
May your journey be one of battle;
A campaign to Beijing requires arms and equipment.
I knew that you would strike
Kakanchin's Beijing;
I knew that you would bring
Turmoil upon China.
Knowing this, I myself prepared it,
So that when Semetey went on campaign
He might coil it around his arm and hang it there—
This work of his lame ritual father.
