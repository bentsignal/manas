Where are the days of old?
Will a day ever come
When we defeat this infidel son of an ogre?
Like a dark falcon of prey,
Will a day come when we swoop upon him?
Will there be a day when we deliver
His rightful punishment into his own hands?”
So saying, Külchoro
Loaded his ornate musket.
The lion born a peerless champion
Lamented the days gone by.
Combat had exhausted him;
He was pressed into dreadful straits.
His true humiliation became clear,
And he despaired of his fly-like life.
A terrible fury seized him;
He simply bound
His precious life upon a hair.
Heedless of high or low,
Never thinking he might die,
Looking at nothing around him,
He spared not his life at all
Against the Heaven-stricken swine.
He yearned to die
And to give the infidel ogre’s son
The punishment he truly deserved.
Külchoro, son of Almambet—
The warrior whose fortune was complete,
The hero supported by the spirits.
His wrestling strength was extraordinary,
His valor beyond measure.
When his fit of rage seized him,
He ranged across the vast steppe.
Whoever provoked his wrath,
He caught in his fangs and chewed,
Then drove beneath his feet.
Throughout the eighteen thousand worlds,
None remained who dared swing a lasso at him.
No enemy dared confront him,
And none prevailed against him in contest.
You named him Külchoro.
He felled all who faced him;
You made him a hero who shook the world.
Yet unable to overcome one vicious dog,
Almighty Kydyr and the one God,
You made him equal to the black earth—
Why did you diminish him so?
When we were created, O one God,
We were helmets of crimson steel,
The Kyrgyz people’s glorious bloom.
We charged like lions,
And hissed like dragons,
Thick-armed and stone-hearted.
We cast the world into great turmoil,
Wearing eighty-four pieces of armor;
Wherever we struck, crimson fire arose.
We readied ourselves like hawks,
And subtle wisdom came to us.
We were descendants of the dark gray wolf,
Who guarded the Ala-Too and made it home,
And disgraced all who fought us.
Were we not towering peaks and mountain passes,
Hardened by spilling blood,
Renowned for breaking the enemy—
Descendants of the dark gray wolf,
The plain where a nation might encamp,
The sons of gray-maned heroes?
He was the spring of the jagged mountain,
Almambet’s finest champion.
Never before had an enemy
Dared advance upon Talas,
Nor had any man prevailed against it.
When we desired glory against the cunning foe,
Was this a night when honor should perish?
Was this a day when Sarybay, the ogre’s son,
Would enter and possess Talas,
And subject it to disgrace?
O almighty God of majesty,
We thundered like blue steel,
And all who saw us shuddered.
