Is a girl rather than a boy,
My child Aichurok, send a letter;
When you send it, send it swiftly!
I shall braid the hair down my back;
Burning and smoldering like an ember,
I, Kanykei, shall die that day.
But if it is a son,
Hold his full celebration
And name him Seitek!
Hang a sign around his neck;
Raise him without dressing him in dirty clothes,
Without feeding him tainted food,
Without letting filth touch him!
My child, see how his grandfather's
Valor follows from his father!
When he reaches thirteen,
Tell him of Zhylgyndy and Keng-Kol,
Tell him of the joyous Kyrgyz people;
Tell him of river-rich Talas,
Tell him of the Argyn and Kyrgyz;
Tell him all the abundant wonders;
Tell him of me, the fierce, bereaved woman!
Oh, my dear Aichurok,
Silk does not fade into sackcloth;
My good woman, my little foal,
A noble woman captured by foes does not become a slave.
Bind on courage and act like a lion.
Your kin have become your enemies;
Your adversary is within.
My child, show courage now.
Oh, my little foal Aichurok,
Know and remember what I have said.
When Allah lays a burden on us,
There is no choice but to bear it.
Oh, Aichurok, heed my words.
Kulchoro has gone into captivity;
My foal may have died in misery!
If he is still alive,
He must be enduring humiliation.
Though a hero and a lion,
He has become a herdsman's slave,
His collarbone rubbed raw,
Both his hands aching,
Wearing a coat that keeps him alive,
Drinking water that keeps his spark from dying,
Burning and glowing like an ember,
Scorched to ashes,
My Kulchoro must live in anguish!
If you do not watch over him, it will bring disgrace;
Kulchoro is entrusted to you!
See his eyes each year;
When his felt coat tears,
Oh, my little foal Aichurok,
Patch it for him from time to time.
If he grows hungry, feed him;
Your younger brother Kulchoro—
If he grows thin, fatten him.
If you do not watch over him, it will bring disgrace.
Oh, my little foal Aichurok,
Kulchoro is entrusted to you!"
So Kanykei spoke,
Your dear, unfortunate mother,
At times grieving, at times weeping,
Urging her toward lionhearted courage.
At times lamenting, at times weeping,
She urged her toward heroic resolve.
Before she had closed her mouth,
Before she could finish every word,
May God never show such a sight!
The battle standard surged like a flag;
A din arose that split the sky.
A vast army flooded forth,
Firing cannon and drawing bows,
And covered the road.
They encircled the palace amid uproar;
A din arose that split the sky.
Spearheads clashed,
And all the champions raced forward.
