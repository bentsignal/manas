The Akolpok left to me as an heirloom
By my magnanimous father Manas
Has had its fiber plucked away;
The six bastards, Abyke and Köbösh,
Have worn it bare like this.
It has come to us by providence—
Look, boys!
We have found white fiber.
For the colt one means to favor,
The road is made ready;
For the colt one means to deny,
No fiber comes even when he buys it.
Let us take the fiber in the field
As our rightful spoil.
My valiant mother Kanykey’s
Stripped and ruined Akolpok—
Let us have it fitted out once more.
There are many places where lightning strikes,
And many peoples who may clash with us.
Think carefully, boys:
There are Russians on the lowland side,
Chinese along the ridges,
And muskets before our brows;
Many foes may contend with us.”
When your hero had said this,
Kulchoro flew like an arrow
And went toward the fiber.
Then he remembered the words
Of your delicate sister-in-law.
His sister-in-law had said:
“If fiber is found among the spoils,
Do not even glance at it.”
The son of Almambet
Was about to leave the fiber behind,
But would the road allow it?
He swept it together and seized it.
At the bottom of a great cave
He piled it all together,
And with a black stone as large as a house,
That tiger-natured boy
Closed and sealed it securely.
The horse beneath him was
Abyke’s Aktelki;
Kulchoro spurred it forward
And quickly caught up again.
When the magnanimous sultan came
Along the shore of Ala-Köl,
How wondrous Ala-Köl proved to be!
Its giant hogweed was thick as an arm;
When you looked at its maral deer,
They were like pale, white-faced cows.
Its apples ripened till the valley was broad with them;
Its walnuts fell and filled the ravines.
Its cuckoo sang among the willows;
Its laughing nightingales and sparrows
Talked together in the willow tops.
Its keskelidirik were like puppies,
And its wild asses like yearling colts;
When you looked at its snakes,
They were like ropes six fathoms long.
Almighty God Most High
Created every kind of living thing.
It had sparrows as large as snowcocks,
And plane trees tall as misted towers,
And marshes spread in separate patches.
Ah, wondrous Ala-Köl—
It held creatures of extraordinary kinds.
When they entered Ala-Köl,
There also was a creature called börsö,
Which ran with a rolling lope.
Across that peopleless, empty plain,
Its flanks bulging outward,
The offspring of that börsö
Could be seen on the hillocks.
It held creatures of every kind,
And beasts of Tügöngür
That no mortal had ever seen.
Ah, wondrous Ala-Köl—
