“Such a thing does not happen even in Urum;
There can be no second Jakyp,” he thought.
Offended at Khan Jakyp,
The lion noble Ajybay,
Flaring up like a leopard, said:
“While the honored, bloodthirsty one is here,
Why should I raid his camp?
By striking Manas's father,
What inheritance would I gain?
With that ill-intentioned unbeliever,
Why should I lower myself to contend?
‘Your father will not heed you,’
I shall go and tell the magnanimous one!”
His sword ringing in its sheath,
Ajybay turned aside
And now went to Kartküröng.
Kartküröng, the peerless wondrous steed—
He ran up like a sheep and mounted.
To his dear companion Manas,
Khan Ajybay now set out.
Your lion Ajy traveled the road,
Covering much ground, sparing no horse,
Crossing rugged ridges and hills,
To the moon-pale yellow plain
That Ajybay arrived.
Into a tent sewn of blue cloth,
Big enough for forty warriors to fit freely,
For all the forty warriors
To play their battle game with knucklebones
And lie about with ample room,
Ajybay, of Argyn Khan,
Entered in a fluster.
