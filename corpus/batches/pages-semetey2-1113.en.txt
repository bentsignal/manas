When the low ebb of fate arrives,
Is it always so faithless?
Damn this worthless world!
His heart was aflame, his soul an ember,
And he had no charger to ride to war.
Unable to contain his grief,
Semetey wailed aloud.
Allah’s sun in the heavens grew dark,
And sixty afflictions at once
Descended upon the noble lion’s head.
The layers of his ribs split apart,
And his wretched heart was torn;
The enmity of Chubak’s son Choro
Stood revealed.
Straining and gathering his force,
Your gray wolf fell into true despair
And lost all hope of his tiny soul.
Unable to collect his thoughts,
The lion-hearted gray wolf was distraught;
Before the votive meat was even cooked,
He suffered grievous tribulation.
The dear lion of a man
Put on his Akolpok armor,
Despairing of his borrowed life;
He strode heavily, bracing himself,
Leaning upon his Akkelte,
Slung his musket over his shoulder,
Prayed to the Creator,
And prepared his weapons.
He buckled on his scabbardless sword,
While wrath swept across his face
Like a storm cloud returning.
Thick-wristed and peerless,
Unlike ordinary mortals in appearance,
He had received letters from many lands
And blessings from holy fathers;
The Twelve had joined hands as one
And named him to become a lion.
He was that lion who never turned,
That warrior who never faltered.
He was his father’s true son,
That fathomless black gray wolf.
The offspring of one who grieved at ease
And smiled at hardship,
He rejoiced when told he would die—
Thus was his lionheartedness known.
He made the white doe his home,
Made those who shot at him his prey,
Chased the blue doe and made it cattle,
And made every land he sought his own.
He was both champion and lion.
He was a matchless warrior;
Having created this gray wolf,
God would never grant his like again.
His fortune was complete,
And the bloodthirsty hero’s heart
Was broader than the face of the earth.
He spared no foe who grappled with him;
In all the eighteen thousand worlds,
No one remained
Who would cast a lasso at the mighty gray wolf.
His courage was extraordinary,
His strength beyond measure.
Now grieving in anguish,
He whose learning was the lesson of the Qur’an,
Whose gray horse had been his wings,
Grieved in sorrow;
Deprived of Arbuurul,
The dear lion who never flinched,
Whom God had fashioned supreme,
The renowned hero favored by holy powers,
Grew wary of Kanchoro
And saddled Artoru.
Fourteen horses had come from Talas,
All fourteen swift thoroughbreds,
Broad-chested and low-maned,
Well-proportioned chargers,
Their lower legs as thick as a child’s waist;
