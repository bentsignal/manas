To rein in and turn its muzzle,
Against the infidel full of strength,
To charge and level the spear,
He armed himself with all his weapons,
And prepared to meet
His adversary Sarybai.
He appealed to the spirits of Manas, gone to the other world,
Of Almambet and Chubak,
Of Ajybai and Syrgak.
To their spirits he prayed.
“You who pledged yourselves to the twelve holy patrons,
Support me, my mighty fathers!
You who turned from the black faith to the white,
Who fled the infidels and became an exile,
My leopard father Almambet,
Set my undertaking right yourself!
Champion of the Kyrgyz,
He too was one of the precious few;
Foremost among the majestic,
The deftest of swordsmen,
The mightiest of the forty choros,
The leader of them all,
The finest of the forty choros,
Baktagül’s morning star,
The golden canopy of the Kyrgyz,
The warrior of the forty nobles.
Mighty Chubak, gray-maned hero,
We are hard-pressed—support us!”
So saying, Külchoro
Drew his Jazayil and rushed about,
Appealing to the spirits
Of the heroes and ill-fated lions
Who had passed away:
“O sovereign God, I am alone;
Do not let the infidel son of a monster
Bind and overpower my fortune,
Do not, O God, break my spirit.
Do not dig my grave deep,
Do not bend my back,
Do not disgrace me and make me groan.
He is like a saber from Bukhara.
Look at the front of his saddle,
Thick as the Kaldai people.
His beard is like a quiver-case,
And the breath issuing from his mouth
Is like flame from a forge—
Such is his appearance when you behold him.
The hero choro supported by the spirits,
Who rejoiced at the word “battle,”
Whose valor was known among the people,
Whose courage was extraordinary,
Whose strength was manifold,
Whose leonine nature was plain,
Whose nobility was renowned,
Whom no mortal dared confront,
Whom even the surging Flood could not strike—
He had a people numerous as black beetles.
It was his custom to spill the blood
Of all who assailed him.
Though the Chinese swarmed and attacked,
They could not bend and overcome him.
However many mighty giants thronged him,
Not one survived
The noble hero Külchoro.
His nobility became known,
And, saying, “He is famously mighty,”
They inscribed it in the annals
Of seething Khakhan.
His leonine nature revealed,
The gray-maned strongman Külchoro
Donned armor of blue steel,
Slung on his Jazayil,
And prayed to the Creator,
Ready to meet his fate if it had come,
And, if God truly willed it,
To surrender his fly-like soul,
To die if fate had arrived,
And, if God so willed, to behold
The true abode from which none return.
