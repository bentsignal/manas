While the army lay resting.
The two lions, Ebegei and Kogoi,
Stood beside brave Kenen.
Speaking quietly but with weight,
Ebegei and Kogoi began:
“Hero Kenen, you are an eagle;
You will do all that you know how to do.
You will stand beneath the banner,
Yet matters have suddenly gone awry:
That accursed Chynteñir
Looks ready to drive us hard.
Kakan’s people are a vast nation,
Their multitudes are like black worms in countless hosts.
Can any man stand equal to them?
Could one who matched them ever prosper?
Our strength will not suffice in battle.
Know that this is what I counsel;
Lion Kenen, heed my words.
Perhaps we should accept his demand,
Spare the people needless slaughter,
And give him what he wants.”
Ebegei and Kogoi said this
And had scarcely closed their mouths
When brave Kenen heard them
And his blood boiled like flame:
“To bow your lion heads,
To turn your hearts to black earth,
To make a lake of the tears from your eyes,
To expose your people to the enemy
And scorch their precious lives—
Rather than see the day of their ruin,
Rather than die a thousand deaths in one day,
Rather than endure such disgrace,
Dishonor the white-capped people,
And hand my people to the enemy,
Let me meet the fate written for me;
Before my afflicted people,
Let me die as their sacrifice.
I shall face
The frenzied black Kaldai!
If he makes me submit, I shall give up my people;
If he has these dear eyes, bright as lamps,
Pierced, I shall give up my people;
If he has this dear head, precious as gold,
Cut off, then I shall give up my people.
Without contending or quarreling,
Without facing one another,
Without shouting and leveling spears,
Without fighting a desperate battle,
Without testing how great his courage is,
Without clashing and dying together,
How could I give away my people?”
Brave Kenen gathered his courage;
He roared in anger.
All his stony courage burst forth,
And between his two cheeks
Hair sprang thick as felt stockings.
A sheath appeared fit to draw a sword;
He proved both hero and brave man,
And Kenen’s words proved true.
The courageous men grew excited:
“Hero Kenen has spoken well!”
All the warriors surged with passion.
“Do heroes not die in battle?
Do widowed women and young children
Not live on if they survive?
These dear men will never retreat
Before the force of bloody war!”
All the wrestlers roused themselves,
The heroes formed into ranks,
All the destroyers bellowed,
Their executioners grew excited,
Their warriors surged with fervor,
And the vast host rode out.
Before anyone could say this or that,
Before the words had left their mouths,
Before anyone could speak further,
Chynteñir had arrived.
Roaring like a dragon,
Chynteñir poured out his venom;
Bringing monstrous strife and great calamity,
Chynteñir poured out his fury.
Heaping humiliation upon Kyrgyz sons,
Chynteñir wrought an atrocity.
He thundered orders to the Manchu and Xibe,
To the Kaldai and Solon,
To the Torgut and Black Kalmyk,
To Mongol and Manchu,
To every tribe and people.
Chynteñir commanded:
“The Chinese are countless, the Kyrgyz few—
Sweep over them with devastation!
Do not leave a single one,
Slaughter and cut them down!
Kill all their heroes;
Finish them off quickly!
Bind their giant Ulukman;
Drive these people out—”
