Beneath your gray wolf,
Showing half a target.
When the wild ram appeared,
Akkelte, left by Manas,
He bent into position and took aim;
With a thunderous bang, at the ram,
At the accursed creature’s flank,
He sent the shot flying.
The excited braggart
Raised his gaze and watched;
From a spear’s length away, the wild ram
He had shot at long range.
Then your uncle Bakai
Tore at his beard and flung it,
Tugged at his mustache and cast it aside.
When placed in front, he was good fortune;
When following behind, he was like a countless army.
He found words in sudden peril,
And found sight in darkness.
Your uncle, old Bakai,
Was the holy sage left behind
By bloodthirsty lion Manas.
Do not ask how your uncle fared:
Your uncle Bakai roared,
Stamping the ground beneath him,
And scolded his young Semetey:
“Alas, swollen fool, be damned;
May there never be two such as you!
Did I not tell you earlier?
Did you not hear me earlier?
By shooting the wild ram from afar,
Have you set my sorrow boiling?”
In fury he scolded him,
And Bakai charged at him;
His bloodthirsty hero’s Akkelte
He tore now from his hand.
Fine-grained powder for rapid fire—
The careful, hapless Bakai
Readied it in his slender hand,
And poured the powder with a rattle.
He struck the gunstock once against the ground;
Your uncle, lion Bakai,
Held the gun firm as a bullet
And leveled it at Akkulja.
As it was about to cross
The rise shaped like a saddleback,
Your uncle Bakai, the keen hunter,
Judged the moment with cunning.
A tremendous commotion
Akkelte unleashed as it fired;
Straight through Akkulja’s waist
Akkelte’s shot passed!
Akkelte cracked aloud,
And the wild ram fell with a thud!
Having shot down the wild ram,
Gray-wolf elder Bakai
Had a thousand cares within his breast,
And tears enough for a thousand plains!
He struck his chest with a crack,
His heart torn apart;
From your uncle, old Bakai,
The tears poured down!
Smoke billowed from his mouth,
And tears streamed from his eyes.
“Alas, Manas, would that you had not died;
Would that Bakai had not lived to see this disgrace!
When I besieged Kara-Shaar and Kambyl,
When I held them under siege,
When I made the Manchus howl
And beat them down,
Why did I not die then!
When I wore at a jaunty tilt
My double-lapelled sable cap,
When I mounted Beijing’s throne
And lived in delight,
Why did I not die then!
When I disdained the whole world,
While my mighty Manas lived,
