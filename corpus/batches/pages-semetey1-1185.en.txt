By cunning he entered
His swift black stallion.
"Mighty Kalcha Khan Kongurbay
Has entered his Algara," they said;
The Muslim faithful did not recognize it
And were struck by God.
Ezkara, khan of the Manchus,
Came out surging too,
Eagerly saying, "I will race a horse."
They dressed the horses' tails,
Paraded them back and forth,
And did so for exactly three days.
At farthest Jelpinig,
Peoples of seven ages came;
Not a living soul remained behind.
For Khan Kokotoy's feast
Every kind of people assembled.
Kokotoy's red banner
With its golden finial
Was raised and fluttered.
"Drive out the horses!" they cried then;
The zurnay blared,
Dust curled toward the sky,
And people collided with people.
Hero Toshtuk, son of Eleman,
Chose and entered Chalkuyruk.
Jamgyrchy of the Esheks
Thought carefully and entered Akkokul.
Khan Koshoy of Katagan
Chose and entered Kabylan.
Kokboru of Urum Khan
Entered Kuu Kazyk as well.
Khan Kokcho of the Kazakhs
Chose and entered Kokala.
The black gray-maned leopard
Chose and entered Akkula.
With an armful of tack,
Muzburchak of Buudayyk
Began the commotion;
He too then entered
The racehorse Telkyzyl.
Hero Bagysh of the Jediger
Took the narrow shortcut,
Carefully leading and entering
Surkiyik, swiftest of livestock.
Not a single shod colt remained;
Every one entered the race.
The renowned tulpar steeds
At Khan Kokotoy's celebration
Now came out to compete.
They entered the horses on a list
And counted them;
The white paper filled with writing.
The racehorses at the feast
Numbered seven thousand forty-three.
Among those horses,
Young, tick-sized Kulatay
Was going away in sorrow.
He was neither a three-year-old nor a yearling,
Nor had his time to race arrived.
If only he raced at full age,
Then he might win a prize.
Or if only he raced at three,
Then he might win a prize.
When he reached his fifth year,
Would he not begin the commotion
And leave the racehorses behind?
Would he not earn renown
And spread his wings?
The flesh of his calves was not yet full;
His time to race had not yet come.
He was too young to know the land,
Still a child at present.
If his shoulders filled out,
And he grew fully among
Those tulpar horses—
There would be no overcoming Akkula,
And no questioning Surkula,
