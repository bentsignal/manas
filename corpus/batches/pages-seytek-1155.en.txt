“It turned out badly,” he said,
The physician was skillful,
From his wound, with a great probe,
They sucked it all out together.
In the populous realm of China
There are medicines for every need,
Every kind of remedy is there.
They gave him powdered red medicine
To take internally.
The pain of the wound subsided,
His closed eyes opened,
Chongbilgich returned to the light.
When seven days had passed,
Chongbilgich regained his speech,
And slowly began to speak with force,
Addressing his sovereign Kite,
He began these words:
“We were a hundred champions skilled in sorcery,
We broke through their defenses and opened a path,
We attacked that aroused nation.
We saw might surpassing all the world,
We saw great torments like those of hell.
We gathered up the riches,
But all ninety-nine companions
Were slaughtered in a yawning pit.
Chongbilgich alone remained,
I despaired of my precious life;
My prowess did not save me from death,
Never have I seen calamity like this.
I have no commander, no people,
No champion remains to shelter me.
The two giants Akjoltoy and Seytek
Caught me between them,
Yet I escaped their swift steeds,
And turned back after assailing the Buruts.
I went there boasting of valor,
But was parted from my champions,
My ribs and back were battered,
My poor head was split,
Most died beneath the ordeal,
Drenched entirely in blood.
With life scarcely remaining,
With strength enough only to gasp,
I escaped and came back to the khagan.
Would any man dare face them?
From the merciless Kyrgyz host,
Could any man escape alive?
That is what I say—understand it well;
My noble Kite, heed my words.
Many cares lie in my heart:
For the gray-maned hero born of your loins,
What woman could give birth
To a son named Chyngteñir?
I traced the lore of old,
And questioned those who know.
There in the land of India-China,
They say there is a woman worthy of you.
The great Kergi, as he is called,
Is the khan of that India.
In that Indian khan’s house,
I hear there is one worthy of you,
Burul, with her flowing, curling hair,
A daughter surpassing all others.
If you take her, she will bear a lion,
That woman will bear a hero;
No man will have the strength to overcome him,
Not a living soul will pass beyond him.
When that woman bears a son,
The earth will heave and waters overflow,
Cliffs will shift and rocks will fall,
Every living creature will truly writhe.
In fury he will clutch blood in his hands,
He will roar in dreadful majesty,
His lion nature will be revealed;
At his birth a standard will be raised.
When he reaches fifteen years,
He will bring turmoil upon the Buruts;
Countless beasts will be slaughtered for his feast,
And he will be named Teñir.
Chyngteñir himself will be born to you,
