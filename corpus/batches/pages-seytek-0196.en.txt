Though you came to us as a captive slave,
You have shown your valor.
Like a ravine untouched by wind,
You have proved yourself a mountain pass.
You have shown yourself a lion
Who could subdue the peoples of the world.
If you are a hero, if you are a lion,
Your terror is like a club-sized fist.
You charged and shouted,
Calling out, ‘Aichürök!’
If you are mighty, if you are a lion,
If you truly are hero Kyyaz,
Would you behave like this?
Why do you shout so?
Shall I go deliberately to the village,
Saying, ‘My lord, has the world ever seen the like?’
If you would slaughter me, here is my head;
If a woman has committed a crime,
First make your horse speak.”
So Aichürök spoke,
Seized the reins of the plunging Tootoru,
And turned his head;
With these words your Chürök
Stood speaking to Kyyaz.
For all the ages until now,
Peerless Kyyaz, you have done wrong;
Time and again,
You have scorned us as slave women.
The sable cap upon your head—
Who has not worn one tilted proudly?
While fortune crowns his head,
Who has not gone about roaring?
At the sight of a champion,
How many enemies have not panicked?
I was that same Chürök,
But captivity brought my head to shame;
Among us, holy Kyyaz held supreme power.
Hero, we are slaves in your hands,
The bare ground where horses are tethered,
Wretches fallen into captivity.
Seized by bloodthirsty fury,
Unable to make the black earth crack
Beneath the weight of Kyyaz’s wrath,
He flailed about in anger,
Saying, “What did the horse say?”
Brave Kyyaz of the Jediger
Came running in a swagger.
Aichürök, Akun’s daughter,
Spoke these words:
“If your horse speaks, heed his tongue,
By your father’s grave, brave Kyyaz!
You refuse to grant the horse’s strength
To your lawfully wedded wife—
What a fool you are, Kyyaz!
Forty carpets from the winding pass—
I know the nature of this horse.
Let us pass before him
And hear him speak, since you claim
To be a holy man.
Make your horse speak, brave Kyyaz;
Though we are women, let us hear.
Can one who merely tends livestock and keeps a household
Be a hero such as you?
Is it right to call a wretch like you
A hero?
Hero, you have braced your black Zulfiqar sword
Against your side
As though an enemy had come to grapple with you.
From the wife you took under ancestral blessing,
You withheld your horse’s strength.
If you were ever a khan, it was in vain;
If you roar in anger,
You may cut off this poor head.
Among all your people filling Jeti-Özön,
Among the countless assembled throng,
My lord and hero, among your people
Was there no slave woman but me
For your mockery and amusement?
You hung that blood-spilling, keen-edged blade
From your wrist.
