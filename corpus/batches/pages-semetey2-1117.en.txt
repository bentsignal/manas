He had tied up with a snap
The silken tail of his racer, Tootoru.
Kyyaz, hero of the Jediger,
Was both a warrior and a valiant man,
A champion unlike other mortals,
A renowned dark-gray stalwart.
The beloved land of the Seven Valleys
He had conquered through valor and made his domain;
The Jediger of the Seven Rivers
He had settled wherever he reached.
His right shoulder was broad,
He was a hero who could shatter the Oirats;
His left shoulder was broad,
He was a warrior well prepared for battle.
Kyyaz, son of Bagysh,
Was blessed with perfect fortune.
Had his nature been known from the start,
He was a son who would obey
Without arguing against what he was told,
A son destined from the first
To exact the blood-price for his elder brother Toltoy.
Broad-chested and wide-breasted,
Ah, what an imposing fellow he was;
With wide mouth and jutting brow,
Dark in complexion and sharp of eye,
His speech was sharper than that of most men.
Thick-wristed and stone-hearted,
He was powerfully built and fierce.
Such was Kyyaz’s nature:
His stature was like a lofty hill,
And he was a towering man;
Like the broad valley itself,
He stood leaning proudly back.
He had the gaze of a hunting eagle,
The chest of a white lion;
Dust rose from his stride,
And life fled at his angry rumble.
Thick through the body, low in stature,
His whole frame seemed cast from stone;
He was practiced against foes face-to-face—
At the sight of his majesty, flee far away.
The people assembled and the nation stirred;
Ninety beasts were slaughtered at his feast,
And he was given the name Kyyaz.
Having received the elders’ blessing,
He became known as Kyyaz the Hero,
A consummate giant in his own land.
Where the Jediger thronged across the earth,
He became lord of the Jediger,
A stronghold and shelter to his clan,
And over all those people
He became the greatest khan.
His word became law;
Among the Jediger of the Seven Valleys,
He became their prince.
He was a lofty fortress on the riverbank,
A khan of weighty authority,
Ruling the vast dark Jediger
Without allowing them to stir.
Dear warrior Kyyaz,
The hero who led his people,
Was blessed with perfect fortune.
The full-blooded warrior Kyyaz
Was hotheaded and harsh-tempered,
Always the first to charge at anyone’s word,
And when told to face death,
He would strike it headfirst.
He was a rash soul;
Kyyaz had little wisdom
And much foolhardiness.
Though Kyyaz was of noble worth,
He would dam many streams,
Stir quarrels wherever he appeared,
And unleash terrible slaughter
And mighty turmoil.
He was a son who subdued
And conquered all who fought him.
He was one who brought great conflict and calamity
