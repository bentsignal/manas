A kise that befriended the dear soul,
When he shouted and buckled it at his waist,
The Forty Saints became his companions.
When he cried aloud and buckled it on,
Before the valiant hero
A lion came roaming.
The gray kise that fell from heaven,
From his father, gray-wolf Manas,
A companion-kise left especially to him.
The gray kise that went with him
To unleash relentless havoc.
Yesterday, when your valiant mother Kanykei
Fled toward Bukhara,
When her wealth was scattered,
Saying, “May my valiant son Semetey
Take this along as a gift,”
Grieving and weeping in sorrow,
She girded it against her inner flesh—
This was the kise she took with her.
Weeping bitterly to the one God,
Destining it for Semetey,
She took along the gray kise.
When over Akolpok
He buckled the gray kise,
The sight stirred the heart;
He looked like a lion.
A black-mottled leopard
Raged at his flank,
And a stunted gray-wolf lion
Was found as his companion.
From his mouth blue flame
Poured like a loaded gun;
His dear teeth, broad as a gate,
Showed apart like grains of millet.
The breath leaving his nostrils
Was like burning red embers;
His two eyes flamed,
Fire that could burn all away.
Generation after generation,
He came from a blood-drinking line,
Setting grassy ground alight,
Setting fire to meadowland.
From Manas, gone to the other world,
Semetey was born to surpass him;
When fury seized him,
His face utterly changed.
The gray wolf’s courage overflowed,
And his blood-drinking aspect appeared.
When hardship pressed a living soul,
He took whatever he faced;
When he struck in fury,
Heads were cut away;
For anyone who came against him,
Death arrived needlessly.
Of the six sharp blades fallen from heaven,
The keenest sword
Was Zulpukor, taken by Manas.
When his father Manas died,
When Kanykei suffered plunder,
Into the hands of the six villains
Zulpukor fell as spoil.
The heroic orphan of Khan Manas
Grew to manhood while orphaned,
Rejoined his own land,
Grew imposing and became a hero,
Equal to the sons of the people.
When he came and saw his people,
When he came to Abyke,
At Köbösh’s palace,
Had he not unleashed
Surpassing havoc?
Saying, “This is the treasure left by my father,”
Had not the valiant prince Semetey
Seized it back?
Zulpukor, left as a gift
By valiant gray-wolf Manas—
When drawn from its sheath with a cry,
It never went idle without drinking blood.
