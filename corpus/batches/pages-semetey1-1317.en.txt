His ox grew old and he himself died;
He divided evenly the property of the dead.
He raised his wether as a sacred offering,*
The horse he rode became holy,
And he remained apart from women.
He saddled his unbroken horse;
There was a great idol the size of a house,
And saying that this was God,
He bowed down precisely to it.
Kongurbai, khan of the monks—
Against that wrathful infidel,
Hero Khan Kongurbai,
Let us level our guns fiercely
And summon all our courage.
Let us gird our waists firmly
And pursue from the Kalcha in Beijing
The blood-price of my father!
Against the seething infidel,
Hero Khan Kongurbai,
That poor man in his full strength,
Let us enter directly and level our spears;
Let us finish and seize the infidel
And offer him as a sacrifice.
We have our forefather's vengeance;
Let us go straight in and take it!
Against the notorious infidel,
Hero Khan Kongurbai,
Let us make a mighty holy campaign.
If death reaches us, let us die;
Let us try to crush
The mighty and renowned infidel
Who slaughtered my father,
And see whether we can bear
The line God has drawn upon our necks.
If you lead the way, uncle,
To the hellish Black Chinese,
Let us try our strength against them.
Can there be a remedy against fate?
If death comes, let us die.
Breaking ranks in the spear-fight,
Falling to a bullet if fate arrives,
Bakburchun is where I shall enter;
Kakanchin is where I shall meet my end.
If I campaign against the infidel,
I stand ready to grapple
With hero Khan Kongurbai,
Who slaughtered my father!
I hear that even Chalyiar never touched
The city of Kakanchin.
I wish to thrust in my hand,
And clash in combat
With the formidable Kalcha!
If my strength suffices, I wish
To fight and avenge myself in Beijing!
My camel-like uncle, hear my words;
I entrust myself to Almighty Allah.
The Chinese slaughtered my father;
Uncle Bakai, hear my words—
How can I endure this grief!
Some people say
That no flood can pass
Through surging Beijing.
I wish to circle into the land
Of Beijing, which no flood can reach,
And unleash an upheaval;
I wish to avenge my father
Upon the surging Black infidel!
I hear that no prophet has gone
To imperial Great Beijing;
I wish to ride in and level my spear
In Beijing, where no prophet has trod!
I wish to pursue my father's blood-price
From the vile Black Chinese.
They call it Chin-Machin, they call it Kakan—
How many names does Beijing have?
Those sovereign Chinese
Have slaughtered valiant heroes.
Lion uncle, hear my words:
What great worth lies in battle!
