When swung, its head blazed like an ember.
When he swung it in fury,
It never rested without drinking blood.
Its edge glowed red with fire;
Though its handle was rather short,
When he swung it in fury
It lengthened to a full fathom.
Those who saw its wrath panicked;
Those who heard its name grieved.
Any stubborn enemy who faced it
Despaired of his dark life.
He seized the blood-spilling axe,
Whirled it around,
And strained forward with both hands.
At Odukan, huge as a mountain,
The infidel of matchless strength,
The hero never sated with blood,
Calling him the accursed one,
Taking the dark pupil as his measure,
And the crown of the head as his mark,
He aimed carefully and struck,
Swinging with perfect accuracy.
When it struck, Odu was no more;
The poison-tempered battle-axe
Sank in to its socket.
When the axe struck Odu,
His cry was heard in the sky,
And he fell embracing the earth.
His head plunged into a hollow;
Dust and soil
Choked his mouth and nose,
And no fly-like breath remained.
When Odukan and Choyun had died,
Their warriors of every kind,
Their foremost rabble,
Strong mounted and on foot,
Their racers unsurpassed in the world,
Commanders over tens,
Champions over thousands,
Their Kaldays and stone-hearted men,
All rushed in with an uproar,
Their spears held firmly forward.
The earth split into dust;
The ground became a pit that trapped feet.
The vast army surged in,
And boundless misery boiled over.
As the Chinese closed around them,
When oppression and violent calamity
Were about to descend upon their heads,
Calling them fugitives from China,
When they were about to hang them by the elbows,
Cut reins from their backs,
Pierce them through from the saddle,
And hang them dangling
From the end of a tree,
Truly tormenting their souls,
The exceedingly resourceful Ebegey
Mounted Jeljetpes
On the rhinoceros he had drawn near,
Reviving the life that was about to die.
He showed the loyalty
Of a wise peer and comrade.
Though caught by the woeful army,
Er Jeljetpes escaped.
He turned the rhinoceros's head,
Called up the Cloud Horse,
And conjured weather in midsummer.
Gorges flew and cliffs collapsed;
He devastated the broad land
And revealed his sorcery.
Fog rose from the hollows;
Clouds covered the mountains.
Over foothill, slope, and plain,
Black banks of mist divided.
Looking toward the sky,
One saw hail pour down in six streams.
Across hills, knolls, and ravines,
It smashed and swept away all traces,
And choked the level uplands.
Beasts and living creatures
Were disturbed and destroyed.
The army gathered with frantic cries;
Ravens croaked and perished.
Wild asses fled and escaped;
Creatures that entered their dens
Survived without dying,
While animals above the earth
Were all seized by death.
He loosed a storm of extraordinary force.
Along slopes, gullies, and cliffs,
Bare rocks fell and streamed down,
Carrying away at their edge
The seething Chinese host.
He unleashed the weather without measure,
Blinding eyes and binding hands,
Plainly barring the road.
Jeljetpes, who had seen much,
Was an invincible sorcerer.
