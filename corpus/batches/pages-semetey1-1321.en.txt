But your father brought him hardship then
And slaughtered him utterly.
People fell into ruin on hearing his name;
The calamities your father wrought
Were inscribed in the register
Of the khan of Kakanchin.
Yesterday, at Kökötöi's feast,
An evil quarrel arose from the Chinese,
And many disputes arose from the Kyrgyz.
The twelve Muslim khans,
Along the broad Alai,
At hero Töshtük's feast,
Said, "We shall plunder the Bloodthirsty One
And bring an upheaval!"
They sent envoys,
Stirring up the ancient madness;
After rousing the Muslims
To charge and make war
Against Kakanchin's Beijing,
They said they would enter directly
Into lowering great Beijing and level their spears.
The road was blessed by Khizr,
And the army numbered forty-two tumens.
All the saints were there,
And all twelve Muslim khans
Were among them.
They had wrestlers and a great people;
All their heroes were there.
They had skillful spearmen,
And champions who knew the enemy's stratagems.
There were lions Almambet and Chubak,
And leopards Serek and Syrgak,
Heroes who had traversed the world.
They stood against the enemy
And fought without pause for six months,
Remaining there without pause for six months.
When they were exacting judgment
From Beijing, where even Solomon had not gone,
Swaggering heroes died,
Many generous souls died,
And everyone who met the foe died.
When they turned back this way,
Beijing's affairs recovered,
And all the valiant men were lost.
Great Beijing is a place from which none return;
It shears down everyone from the head.
Every living soul who went there died;
Many khans died.
Do not ask what Beijing is like:
Its passes are said to be dug trenches,
And its road one from which none return.
Whoever sets his face toward it is killed;
Great Beijing's danger is fierce.
Whoever goes there does not turn back;
Whoever meets it does not return alive.
Many people have gone
To Kakanchin's Beijing,
But none have come back.
It yielded no judgment to Suuk Kol,
And even Zulqarnain did not go there.
The road to Beijing has five branches,
Bringing peril upon the wretched head.
Beijing is no empty land,
Nor are its people to be approached lightly.
One road to Beijing
Is a road from which none return;
One road to Beijing
Has a deep-dug trench;
One road to Beijing
Has suffering set boiling;
On one road to Beijing,
Whoever meets it does not escape alive;
On one road to Beijing,
Whoever meets it does not return alive.
Beijing is that prolific land,
And the Chinese that mighty people.
My worthy one, I am Bakai,
Who knows all that can be known."
