The poor woman, almost a saint—
One cannot speak of her like that.
Whoever speaks of her so,
His affairs will never prosper.
She met him in her youth,
And splendid Manas
She built up like a house,
She tended like a garden,
Raised him to maturity and made him a man;
Matchless Manas
She made the equal of every hero's son.
Kanykei never disgraces herself;
She turns her lofty head into a standard,
And a woman's head into a warrior's.
Making armor is her custom;
At all times Kanykei
Is this Manas's wing.
This wondrous Kanykei
Was given by almighty Allah
To Manas himself—
A woman like a heavenly maiden.
Lion Manas gathered his strength
And donned the Akolpok armor;
Kalkaman's Black Horse
He sprang upon without a saddle.
The people surged together,
And stared at Manas in wonder.
The twelve Muslim khans
The boundless gray-wolf panther
Made equal among themselves.
To choose a swift horse to ride,
To go against Kongurbay,
The Muslims' horses
Were led out in a long line.
They brought saintly Bakai
To judge the horses.
The great host of dark-clad Muslims,
Its champions, filed past;
The splendid host of Muslims
All filed past together.
Dark native-bred chargers,
With bellows lungs and copper forelegs—
Every one of the thoroughbreds
Bakai examined in turn.
At Kokotoy's memorial feast
A vast multitude had gathered.
If they met chest to chest in a joust,
No horse there could endure it.
Khan Manas's forty companions,
His forty companions, forty comrades,
Each of the forty had his own distinction;
The forty were master riders
Who had come from forty lands.
Manas the blessed panther
Gathered his forty companions,
And before your elder Bakai
Had them lead their horses past.
At the sound of tumult it stood apart:
Almambet's Sarala
Showed every mark of a thoroughbred.
It never panicked at a battle cry,
And in the darkness of the moonless night
Never once set a hoof wrong;
It showed every mark of a thoroughbred.
No horse appeared that could bend it
When ridden out into a joust;
No horse appeared that could make it yield
When they stood chest to chest.
Almambet's Sarala,
With mattock hooves and broad hindquarters,
Was a beast to ride into battle.
With bowl-shaped hooves and a broad back,
Almambet's Sarala
Was a beast to ride in a race.
With camel chest and low mane,
It was a beast to ride into combat.
