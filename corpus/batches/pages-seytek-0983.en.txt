He had kept the tumult raging.
Strength spent, exhausted,
Blood flowed from our bodies,
The wide world became narrow,
For saliva sweeter than sugar,
We longed for a single swallow.
Bravo, Kuyaly!
How could I fail to say, 'Well done'?
Your valor is mighty, your wrath fierce;
I set Sarybay straight.
My poor head was sorely tried,
The final ordeal came upon me.
I was Sarybay the khan,
From time immemorial,
At every turn,
Fighting had been my custom.
Countless armies
I fought and crushed,
And cast them all into misery.
I used to fell the middling ones;
When a fierce fighting foe came,
I used to lay him flat on his back.
I used to destroy even the formidable ones,
And those who crept close,
I used to gouge out their glaring eyes.
When they came swaggering,
I left them at their wits' end,
And laid them flat on their backs.
Yet my appointed end had not come.
Swift to flee, fierce in wrath,
My dear, I had never seen your like.
Yesterday I heard word from a messenger,
Hearing that Kuyaly has arrived,
My tiny life trembled,
The bones of my thighs shuddered,
My ribs throbbed,
My insides heaved without cease,
The news spread among the people:
'A fearsome fiend has come,' they said.
Poor champion Sarybay,
He despaired of his precious life.
The wretched man writhed in anguish,
Time after time,
He had suffered in this way.
The silk settled close without parting,
You did wrong by stirring up strife,
You show no sign of sparing a life.
Like the silken waist of an argali,
When I look at your fierce bearing,
It is like the cold wind of a mountain pass.
With a low, swelling hum,
Muttering deep in his throat,
Sarybay thundered:
"Since I became Sarybay,
Since I mounted a horse as Sarybay,
Since I came of age and became a warrior,
A match for the sons of warriors,
Since I put on a golden crown,
Since I ascended a golden throne,
I ruled the people and cared for the land,
I have lived as Sarybay.
I held court and became a khan,
A chief seated in authority,
I gathered the people and held the realm,
Riding the clouds and scattering ice,
In those days when I roared,
Leaving neither a horse-catching pole nor a living soul,
I scattered their very core,
No herd escaped my raids,
My Zulpukor sword and my steel spear,
When I brandished them,
All the vast heavens
I held in my power in those days,
I sliced and ate the choicest horse haunch,
And asked, 'Where is the death-bringer?'
I sliced and ate horse-rib fat,
And asked, 'Where is calamity?'
Oh, wretched Kuyaly,
I have suffered greatly because of you.
