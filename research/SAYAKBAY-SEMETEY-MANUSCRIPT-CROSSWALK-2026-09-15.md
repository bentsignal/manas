# Sayakbay *Semetey* manuscript crosswalk and source check

Prepared 2026-09-15. This is a read-only reconciliation of the live manuscript catalogue's complete old-inventory run 925–953 against the project's 2013 printed edition.

## Result

The catalogue maps old inventories **925–953** consecutively to current inventories **104–132**. All 29 records have a public record and an `Obrazets` sample PDF, but no record exposes a full manuscript scan. The catalogue gives line counts for 27 units: **198,100 lines**. Counts are blank for 925 and 926; their known pagination is 203 and 400 pages. Known pagination for 21 units totals **6,554 pages**; pagination is blank for 945–948 and 950–953.

The *Manas* encyclopedia entry says this same 925–953 run contains **316,157 verse lines**. The live catalogue cannot substantiate that total: it would require **118,057 lines** in 925–926 alone, or about 196 lines per manuscript page across their combined 603 pages, while nearby catalogue units typically report roughly 20–50 lines per page. This makes the encyclopedia figure internally incompatible with the current item metadata. A likely but unproved explanation is a printed/transcription error (possibly **216,157**), which would require a plausible 18,057 lines across 925–926 and lies close to the 2013 edition's 216,333 extracted source rows. Consequently there is no evidenced ~100,000-line archival deficit on the public data now available.

The safest source-completion conclusion is that the 2013 edition may already represent essentially this archival sequence, with extraction rows differing modestly from verse-line accounting. Exact omission detection requires full scans or at least first/last lines for every unit. The catalogue supplies explicit anchors only for 925 and 926; the remaining records omit them. Episode titles show a continuous progression from Kanykei's flight to Bukhara, through the Kokotoy feast and the long Semetey–Konurbai conflict, to Semetey's death. Those broad episodes are present in the printed edition, so titles alone do not identify missing passages.
