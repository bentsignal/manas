From the flank, Kanchoro,
Girding his keen waist,
Entered the Jediger host
Alone and laid hands upon them.
The warriors wearied of combat,
And their pine-like spears
Were driven into the very heart.
Corpses of warriors piled like a mountain,
And all the spears fell in bundles.
Battle-axes shrieked,
And all the swords shattered.
Much ground was now filled with blood;
It became a fierce battle.
Shouting, they thrust their spears,
And when spears could not reach,
They fought with guns.
A pale haze of dust arose,
The open field became black blood,
And many people met their end.
Roaring in fury,
At all the Jediger
Külchoro, born of the keen one,
Charged and scattered them.
From the Afghan city,
Making the Jediger cry out,
He drove them out and swept them away.
While your falcons were present,
They wrought astounding havoc,
And when Afghanistan was about to perish,
They tore it free.
Kanchoro carved through their center
And came out the other side;
Baitaylak destroyed one wing
And came out the other side.
Kalyk spread wide on the edge
And came enclosing it.
In that remarkable battle,
All those sons of khans
Came plundering through.
The horses were gathered as spoil,
And the Jediger people were slaughtered.
The warriors' heads were cut off,
The lords' bellies were ripped open,
And the surging Jediger host
Was wholly broken apart.
The warriors who had boasted of strength
Dismounted and ran,
Putting their belts around their necks,
And pulling their horses forward.
Those who remained alive
Cast down their spears and sobbed,
Crying together, “We surrender!”
The common people gathered,
Their leaders having been slain;
The wicked begged for mercy,
And the good had been beheaded.
Saying, “We shall seize their wives,”
They drove them frantic from the front;
The devastated Jediger
Scattered all their possessions.
The captured Jediger
Saw what they had never seen;
Innocent, meek people
Fell in rows and died,
And saw the place of Judgment.
Intending to bring death,
They scattered their own possessions.
Let us leave that matter thus,
And begin the tale of Toltoy,
Khan of the Jediger:
Was Toltoy not a warrior?
Of Semetey and Choro,
That they were absent then,
Hero Toltoy had learned.
Using brute force, he slaughtered the people,
And Toltoy drove them before him.
