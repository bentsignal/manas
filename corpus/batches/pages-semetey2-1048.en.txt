If you have the strength, slaughter me.
Do not prance about, Kazakh host;
Stop, or you will be scattered.
You came as a guest, yet stand there bristling;
Do not stir up a bitter quarrel.
Do not let every last one of you
Perish in a massacre.
Do not push matters to the bitter end,
Lest your survivors say
That I crushed and overwhelmed the dead.
Become a moonless night;
Ümötöy and your two thousand Kazakhs,
Become one with all your people.
When I thrust with wrath boiling,
Would I prosper if I shrank from you,
You vain and teeming Kazakh host?
If the companion feared you,
Would his purpose remain firm?
You thrust your spear at a mediator
And stood there raging.
Can a hero behave like you?
Take care that your head is not shattered;
Would a man prosper by sparing you?
Be on your guard, Ümötöy;
I shall not stand dull-eyed without striking back.
If you call yourself a lord, I am a lord’s son,
One of noble birth.
If you call yourself a khan, I too am a khan’s son.
You rave, Ümötöy—
When was I ever a slave of the Kazakhs,
Living on watery ayran and blue dried curds?
If my anger rises and I stand bristling,
Do not flee in terror,
Crying, ‘Dear brother, mercy, mercy!’
And crawl away to your separate lands.
You are overwrought, Ümötöy;
Do not squander your possessions.
Do not cry, ‘We are dead, dear brother—mercy!’
And do not let your trailing host
Flee toward Sary-Arka!
If the companion stiffens in wrath,
May he not drain the Kazakhs dry;
May your people not grieve,
Crying, ‘God, Almighty God!’
If Külchoro strains his strength,
May he not tear out your very spine;
If he strikes in earnest by chance,
May his spear not suddenly land
And take your entrusted life.
May God not strike my bloodthirsty uncle
For saying, ‘My companion speared my cousin!’
May no one mock us, saying,
‘How wicked they are,
To wage war against blood kin from the same womb!’”
So saying, Külchoro
Still did not strike back or budge,
But turned his stallion’s head.
That formidable lion, with a voice like a trumpet,
Stood there laughing boldly.
Ümötöy, son of Köksö,
Leveled his spear in plain sight.
See the slave’s insolence:
Assaulting the companion,
He struck him again with the spear.
The companion did not even flinch an eyebrow;
Though the teeming Kazakhs thrust their spears,
The noble companion never stirred.
He was the tempered tip of keen steel,
The descendant of Khan Alakö.
Silver-eyed and sun-faced,
A peerless warrior radiant as the sun,
Of a noble father, a warrior vast as the sea.
His fortune was complete;
Could you see within the companion,
It was broader than the face of the earth.
God alone creates life,
And He would never soon grant the world
Another champion of the companion’s stature.
