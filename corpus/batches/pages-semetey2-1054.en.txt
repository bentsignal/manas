Since this warrior became a warrior,
I have never suffered such humiliation.
I offered six thousand head, led by horses,
As tribute, but he refused;
He would not lower his spear.
He drained much of my strength,
Tormented my soul,
And made my head spin.
When I offered horses and camels, he would not listen;
It seemed he demanded my very life.
Each of them thrust a spear at me
Until my head reeled.
They blocked and barred my road;
Your kinsman set me aflame,
And this cousin of yours showed me wonders.
If I fell into the hands of your Kazakhs,
They would ignore my pleas;
Such was their savage frenzy,
They meant to gouge out my eyes.
They meant to bring my age to an end,
Simply to crush the life from my body.
When the bay horse travels far,
It shows no sign of being a true racer;
When this cousin camps in your village,
He shows no sign of being kin.
Frightened and skittish, the accursed man
May camp as your neighbor today,
But if this cousin finds an opening,
He may become your enemy tomorrow.
If he camps beside you,
He may devour your beloved foal,
Open the mouth of your treasury,
And scatter your countless troops.
Should some desperate affair arise,
He may betray your secrets to the enemy.
He will listen closely to your words
And then deceive you.
If a mace should fall into his hand,
He would gouge out your eyes.
A hollow can never be firm ground;
This Ümötöy is a worthless fellow—
A hero who stirs turmoil can never make a nation.
A riverbank is no mountain ridge;
This dear cousin of yours is no true people.
Your courage is great, but you have no sense;
You have always been a heedless soul.
Your greatness is abundant, but wisdom is lacking;
Hero, you have been a vain and reckless khan.
You were always the reckless one
Who acted the instant you faced a matter;
Time after time,
I was the one who suffered for you.
You have heard my words; now heed them.
Hero, if your heart so wishes,
Accept your cousin as kin.
Hero, unless you deem him too violent,
Heed the words of your warrior.
Hero, if you will not heed your warrior,
Then heed the words
Of your elder kinsman Ümötöy.
Agree to what he asks:
Drive six thousand head, led by horses,
Loaded with gold,
And prepare six thousand men
To drive those horses there;
Consent to his demand.
Your father slew his father;
Without quarreling or feuding,
He has demanded blood-price—pay it!
With their forward saddles sagging,
Their two humps towering,
Their necks held high like ducks,
Drive seven thousand red camels
With every tail braided.
Load them with measures of gold and jewels,
Say, ‘This is your father’s blood-price,’
And give them to Ümötöy.
If you will not agree to these words,
