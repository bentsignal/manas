While my mighty Manas was alive,
Why did I not die then?!
It is the whiteness of my beard:
The children would not heed my words;
It was their time to leave on the journey!
It is the whiteness of my mustache:
When your Bakai was choked with grief,
It was time for him to vanish,
No longer remaining in this world!
While my mighty gray wolf was alive,
Bakai knew the ways of a campaign;
This wretched soul led not merely three boys,
But went at the head of a countless host!
While my lion was alive,
Manas would heed my words;
All the Forty Companions,
Their elders led by Kyrgyl,
When they heard my shout,
Would collapse where they stood!
I was a tulpar traveling the road,
But now I have grown weary.
Parted from my falcon,
No longer counted among the living,
I have grown old!
The city of Kakan-Beijing,
When I held it,
The kalday and jang-jun,
When I beat them down,
While the lion was alive,
When my courage overflowed,
Why did I not die then!
When I possessed my full strength,
While my mountainlike one was safe,
Why did I not die then?
With pinewood spear and Zulfiqar,
When I shook the world
And held dominion over it,
Why did I not die then?!
When I brought turmoil
Upon all who challenged us,
When I alone held
And ruled the face of the earth,
Why did I not die then?!
My falcon Manas, had you not died,
Khan Bakai would not have lived so long
And come to this pass!
May such humiliation
Never be given to a mortal!
The accursed ones have finished my days
By refusing to heed my words!
Your uncle Bakai has died
And beheld the face of the next world!”
Prince Bakai grieved;
Of Lord Manas, the gray wolf,
His death became clear only today!
“I have departed from the world;
Because the children did not heed my words,
Today Bakai has passed away!
Having let the horses be raided on the way,
How can I go to Kakan?
How can I gird my waist?
From the Kalcha who styles himself a hero—
Oh, what a cruel day!—
How can I pursue the blood-price?!
How can I strike Kakan?
How can I go to Beijing?!
From the Kalcha in Beijing,
How can I exact my vengeance?!
Accursed Bakai has died;
In his old age this Bakai
Has beheld the face of judgment!
Parted from fourteen horses,
How can I remain alive?
The land of Beijing lies six months away;
The road between is not short.
Refusing to heed my words,
My children, what you have done is not right!
Great Beijing lies seven months away;
