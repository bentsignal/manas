With great Mange at the army's rear
And great Zange upon its flank,
Men who held their people in their grasp;
Before them rode Lord Khan himself.
Regiment upon regiment,
They came pouring over the heights.
The mind reels and the tongue cannot tell it:
Swarming like grass upon the summer pasture,
They came surging forth,
Their instruments for cutting men
Chosen and carried with them;
So many Chinese came glittering forth
That the sun itself could not be seen.
They crossed vast reaches of land,
Fording rivers of running water,
Cutting through lofty, precious mountains,
Pressing onward without sparing themselves.
When they came out upon the uplands,
There at Bozdongul, the earth's midpoint,
They halted beside Bozdongul
And pitched their army's camp.
The tents went up and the host spread wide;
One cauldron for every ten men,
Their food was carefully apportioned.
The commanders of hundreds gathered,
The commanders of thousands made ready.
They raised mottled blue tents,
Shot maral deer on the cliffs,
And gave themselves to many pleasures.
The entire host settled into rest,
Sleeping propped upon their elbows,
Dozing briefly in repose,
Lying until their hips grew weary,
Reclining until sleep was sated.
Stars separated in the sky,
Pouring light upon the earth;
Overhead the stars began to scatter.
The dawn-sign Libra
Sped toward the qibla;
The whole dawn broke open,
And light poured upon the earth.
The cold wind before sunrise
Whistled down the valley;
Every living beast and creature
Stirred and raced across the ground.
The people woke and the camp grew restless,
Expecting an order from the commander;
The whole host stood waiting.
And there, behold Chyngtengir,
Clad in his robe of blue steel,
Raging like a blue-gray tiger.
Suurultai, resourceful in counsel,
Was an extraordinarily mighty khan,
And he had countless
Cunning judges of men.
Those who always traveled with him,
Those of surpassing wisdom,
Eloquent masters of speech—
He gathered many from the people.
The keen-eyed among them,
The sages who had seen much,
The judges who unraveled knots—
He roused them all together
And left none behind.
Men who would strike with an ax when challenged,
Men who could conceive far-reaching counsel,
The narrow men of wicked thought,
Craftsmen fit for every task,
Men who had cast aside weakness and proved their valor,
Steady, watchful champions—
Chyngtengir gathered them without exception,
Then raised his voice and began to speak,
Speaking quietly but with force:
Zhelzhetpes, who goes between the sides—
No soul can pass where Zhelzhetpes cannot.
Let Zhetpes go as the intermediary;
Let him carry word throughout the world.
Let him take six pack mules,
Let him take ample provisions,
Warriors who can drink and never sate on blood,
Who spare no life once battle begins.
Whose single head, if demanded,
Could be staked against a multitude—
Let him take such champion warriors beside him.
Let him travel the four corners
Of this round world in four circuits,
And through all five corners of the earth
Pass marking what he sees.
Let him find a mountain to strike,
Let him find an enemy to fight,
Let him find a man to face him.
Who dwell in Ala-Too,
Whose six forefathers were khans,
Who answer to no one in the world,
Who multiply whenever they are slain,
Whom holy Khizr himself follows—
The Kyrgyz race of sovereign will—
