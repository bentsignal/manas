“You have thrown my people into despair.
You left me alive without taking vengeance,
Me, brave Külchoro.
You made him renowned across the earth,
You made Sarybai a hero,
But Külchoro the lion—
Why have you humbled him so?
Casting him so often before hostile death,
Why have you brought him so low?
You made Sarybai mighty;
Damn you, treacherous world,
Why have you so disgraced
This head of a true fierce lion?
You shamelessly let me be bound,
My two arms of a lion’s strength;
You let my robe be taken as spoil,
And visited this wonder upon my head.
Take me, God, without tormenting me,
Without wearing me down in helplessness.
Do not disgrace me before the multitude,
Do not, before this settled nation,
Make Sarybai so mighty.”
So lamented Külchoro,
Grieving as he named reedy Кең-Кол and broad Talas,
Counting over every tract of land,
Grieving for the joyous Kyrgyz, his ancestral people,
Counting over all the tribes.
He could not die though he yearned to,
Could not take his own life,
Could not restrain the tears in his eyes,
Nor gather once again
His valiant Kyrgyz, his beloved people.
Unless the Almighty willed it,
He could not go to death himself.
He came to the gray hills and made ready,
Leaning upon his gray spear,
And halted at the great gate,
Lamenting an ancient grief.
Six thousand camels and countless colts,
The white tent bought at great price,
Lions afraid and all the people weeping—
A savage conflict arose.
The hero despaired of life,
The people cried aloud in grief,
Black night descended on the nation,
And Sarybai alone became a demon of hell.
Külchoro, once aided by the spirit,
Leant against Üch-Koshoy.
Wounded in ninety places,
His body drenched in blood,
Beneath the hero
Six thoroughbred horses had died.
His strength exhausted, he staggered,
And the spirit-aided hero
Stood there dumbfounded.
Damn you, treacherous world,
Filthy world that lives by deceit—
Your deception has proven true.
You cast misery upon mortals;
Sarybai, son of the ogress,
Was the one I lacked the strength to overcome,
Yet you made him a champion and a lion.
At the very end, filthy world,
You made Sarybai a hero.
You bent my precious falcon head,
O treacherous world,
And laid it level with the black earth.
You made his strength as vast as mountains;
Unable to prevail against Sarybai,
You plunged me into grim despair.
Fate that created and ordained me,
Calling me a lion among warriors,
You made me a crag like Alaskar.
Yet in the end, Almighty,
The descendants of the countless Kyrgyz,
The heirs of the khan’s son—
You took away the people’s hope,
And disgraced before Sarybai
The sons of Almambet and Manas.
You bent my back low,
