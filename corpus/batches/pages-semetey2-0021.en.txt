In wintertime
He turned black-gray.
He bent his neck like a stag;
If game appeared a shot's length away,
He bounded without hesitation.
When his excellence became known,
The khan of Bukhara asked for him.
He was not given when requested;
Far from giving Buurul away,
No ritually impure man ever rode him.
The foolish hero, son of Manas,
Resolved to inspect the herd
And release into it
The towering swift horse.
Then Semetey said:
“While my uncle Joodur is here,
I shall wear the bulletproof armor
Of hero Kongurbai the Kalcha;
I shall catch and ride myself
The Kalcha's winged Black Horse.
This year I shall have Buurul lead the herd
And drive it to Talas.
Uncle Joodur, your words are true;
Everything you said is true.
I shall grow fat and refreshed,
Gathering an army for Beijing,
Not a few but many,
And then bring slaughter
Upon hero Kongurbai.
Thus shall I avenge
My father who passed into eternity.
I shall gird my waist firmly,
Commit myself and pray,
And pursue my father's blood-price.
If fate arrives, I shall die;
I shall contend with Kongurbai,
The khan of the monks!”
Having said this, Semetey
Saddled Arbuurul
With the Akkangky saddle made by Almambet:
Its front bow of white gold,
Its rear bow of pure silver,
Both sides of broad iron.
Skilled when crossing passes,
Alman, who departed for the next world,
Had made it especially
For the boy Semetey.
The great saddlecloth and leather sweat-pad
Semetey stripped from Buurul,
Deceived by Karagul;
Your gray wolf began this deed.
He tied a white halter with a rosette knot
Around his neck.
Once released, Buurul
Led the vast herd.
Such was Buurul's excellence:
He never panicked at a battle cry;
On a moonless day,
In a night when eyes could not see,
He never misplaced a single step.
At a clamor he broke away;
When he ran, the black earth
Split layer by layer.
At all times that beast
Showed the marks of a tulpar.
When urged onto stone,
He did not slip from the heights.
In no way was he inferior
To the kulan and game of the mountains.
Broad-chested with a low mane,
Handsome Buurul horse,
His hindquarters were like a ravine;
His white front teeth
Were like hammered steel.
He did not tire after six months' riding;
Until sixty years of age,
His tusklike teeth did not grow old.
His calves were like a boy's waist,
