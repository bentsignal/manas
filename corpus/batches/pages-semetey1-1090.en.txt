"This is the moment he vanished from sight.
My father Kökötöy—
People, here above the grave
Is your last meeting with him.
Five hundred young camels and five hundred mares
Today, for my lordly father,
I shall offer as grave prizes.
Now that my dear father has passed,
How shall I hold his feast?
How shall I race the horses?
I put the matter before you.
All the mighty champions are here;
I shall take counsel."
I myself was right there then,
At Kökötöy's feast;
My gray-wolf father, hero Bagysh,
Was present at the summons.
If you ask how I was then,
My father, hero Bagysh,
Mounted me on a bay-piebald pacer,
Dressed me in a silk coat,
And led me about the feast.
He mounted me on a pale-piebald pacer,
Dressed me in garments of precious cloth,
And led me about the feast.
My poor head had not yet
Risen above the saddle's fork;
At that time my age
Had not yet passed six.
I saw how Kökötöy's
Vast riches overflowed;
I saw many people take plunder
And thereby prosper.
If you ask about that time,
Kökötöy's Bokmurun
Had reached twelve years of age;
He deemed no soul his equal
And had grown drunk on wealth.
Saying, "My khan-father has died,"
He gathered under his gaze
Infidel and Muslim alike,
Yet sent no word
To Zhakyp's only son, Manas.
Know his arrogance from this:
At Kökötöy's mourning feast,
Manas did not come to heap earth
And did not cast his handful of soil.
Akkula was mounted,
His white armor was donned,
His sharp steel was girded on;
Lion Manas, the tiger,
Vowed, "I shall strike down Bokmurun,"
And raged like a white tiger.
As Manas stood shouting,
Your gray wolf, wise Bakay,
Stepped between them:
"Kökötöy's Bokmurun
Is no yellow, rugged mountain
For you to go and strike down,
And no enemy with whom to test your strength.
Can one strike an idolater?
If he attacks an idolater,
Will Manas's deed prosper?
Can one strike an orphan?
Will such a complete deed prosper?
If you go and attack a child,
Entangling your deed in shame,
Will your affair then prosper?"
Noble Bakay, the tiger,
Stepped between them
And began his speech.
Thus he restrained
His magnanimous lord Manas.
Other elders besides Manas were there;
"When Khan Kökötöy died,
When the people gathered and buried him,
All the common folk were there.
