He had them splendidly loaded,
Had their necks rubbed raw,
And sent them roaming over the whole world;
He would survey the face of the earth.
Growing tyrannical, Bokmurun
Would have maidens sought for him.
Traveling among the common people,
He would look upon dark-eyed maidens.
Whether or not he liked the girl,
He would give her father
A camel-load of gold.
Of all that ears had heard and eyes had seen,
His fame thundered abroad,
And they called Bokmurun a manap.
Though still a child, Bokmurun
Had livestock led wherever he went;
Roaming through many cities,
He overran many peoples,
And squandered and scattered
All his great wealth.
When Kökötöy's Bokmurun
Mounted Maaniker,
He traveled four circuits
Around the world's four corners.
When he went seeking a wife,
When he traversed many lands,
When he squandered and scattered
Great wealth and countless herds,
When he mounted the wind-surpassing horse
And went seeking a wife,
Khan Kökötöy died.
As Kökötöy lay dead,
Bokmurun returned.
"My khan-father has died," he said,
And summoned the great men,
The troublemakers among the people.
"My dear father has died," he cried,
Wailing in grief,
And summoned all the people
To his mourning feast.
He had the finest racehorses tethered,
And had countless khojas and ishans
Driven there by force,
Saying, "Let them come receive their share."
He made their camel calves cry out;
Saying, "The mourning feast of Khan Kökötöy,"
He had gray cattle slaughtered,
Had foals left orphaned,
And had the mares slaughtered wholesale.
He slaughtered sheep and male camels,
Saying, "This is merit for my khan-father,"
And slaughtered many cattle and horses.
He had all the giants brought,
And placed his father's remains
In the hands of sixty giants.
He tethered ninety gray horses for the funeral gifts,
And tethered all the rest in groups of a thousand,
Had bells fastened to them,
And costly carpets spread over them.
He tethered six thousand pack camels and male camels;
His champions shouted,
"Qalandars, paupers, dervishes,
Let not one of you stay behind!"
He summoned the people
And had the funeral prayer recited.
Saying, "My dear father will ride it,"
He saddled exactly one thousand coal-black horses
As Buraq mounts.
In this way, Kökötöy's Bokmurun
Struck fear into the hearts
Of all the common people,
And began these doings
For his father's mourning feast.
Unable to endure his demands,
The Kyrgyz of broad Tashken
Began to perish.
Hearing that Khan Kökötöy had died,
The people heard and the nation came.
