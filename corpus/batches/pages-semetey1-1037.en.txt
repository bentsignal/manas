I have grown pale waiting for Dalai;
What am I, who has waited so long?!
Alas, because of your murderous fame,
I lived in fear of the ancestral spirits;
For many years I burned deliberately,
I knew your heart long ago,
And after all your promises and true words,
I kept pursuing what lay behind them!
Yesterday, when you did not come hunched over,
I put on my swan guise,
And at the shore of Ala-Köl
I cast down a walking mist;
The falcon in your hand
I swooped away and took.
The woman who knows not a bird's worth—
May her womanhood be damned;
I gave it knuckle meat from a yearling sheep,
And fed it until it was full;
God took your bird,
And it died of the flux.
I shall find you a bird like your bird;
My princely companion, hear my words.
That is what became of your bird.
It seems my name is woman,
And a woman's deed is maimed.
Yesterday, rejoicing after I took it,
I gave it too much forbidden meat
From a lean sheep;
It wasted away and died.
I shall find a bird like your bird;
That is what became of your bird!"
"Do not mock me, you bitch!
Give me my falcon and I shall leave;
If you sit there babbling,
I shall take you and make you pay!
I will accept no other bird,
I will not agree to what you say!
The proof that my bird was Akshunkar,
Its sure sign,
Is that for a wretch like you
Eighty maidens would be the fine.
Even if your father Akun offered in tribute
A hundred old women like you,
I would not accept them!"
As the prince stood there raging,
Chürök's anger rose:
"Alas, my prince, may ruin take you,
If we two cannot be reconciled!
Your falcon Akshunkar can be found again;
After your wretched, clamorous quarrel,
When can honor be found again?
The twelve joined hands as one,
And ten holy men gave their blessing;
Had the leopard now gone to the other world
Not been your father,
Had my father not driven livestock as in-laws,
Bowed in submission and eaten fat,
Started the great tumult,
Then cast me away saying he had given his daughter,
Had he not been my father,
Alas, because of your murderous fame,
And because of all the promises
Made by the blessed heroes,
I took you out of fear of this,
Though I did not like you as a husband!
Alas, because of your murderous fame,
How many men did I not test?
Though I tested him, why did I not marry
Kalcha there in Beijing!
I saw heroes in many lands;
Let me speak of the others.
If it is said again, it becomes another tale;
Let us not keep saying it again,
Saying, 'That is how she spoke.'
If it is told anew, it becomes a new tale;
Let us not ramble over it anew,
Saying, 'That is how she spoke then.'
