He thrust his hand inside her,
And the black blood of her heart
He scooped up in both hands and swallowed:
“Now my vengeance is sated;
Now what died in me has revived,
And my extinguished fire is kindled!”
While Külchoro was doing this,
Before Kanchoro could prepare against him,
Before Kanchoro could summon his courage,
Before he could unleash his fierce wrath,
Before his executioners could rush like wind
And reach him and surround him,
Before the people cried out and the nation panicked,
Before they could offer him as a sacrifice,
Before anyone could utter a word,
In the blink of an eye,
Aichürök, daughter of Akun,
Ready to die if death had come,
To stand as protector of Külchoro,
Her mighty mountain of support—
The wretched fairy-like beauty
Was torn by many thoughts;
Tears poured from her eyes,
Her heart boiled like embers.
Having donned black for the conflict,
How could she stand still?
Her wrist-thick hair came loose,
And tears flowing from both eyes
Streamed below her two breasts.
If conflict suddenly erupted,
If fate decreed his death,
How could she endure the loss
Of the brother-in-law who protected her?
His malice was deep, his venom strong,
A bloodstained destroyer of peoples,
That resourceful kinsman Kanchoro,
Yet his stature was that of a hero.
If the poor eagle were attacked,
If my brother-in-law were suddenly caught
In bitter, calamitous strife—
While she thought thus, Külchoro
Was seized by towering rage.
At the shrew upon the throne
He charged and swiftly reached her.
The warrior-lion, matchless in speed,
Seized that exceedingly venomous infidel
With a hero’s might,
Grabbing her by her black hair.
By the hearth stood a stone trivet;
Onto that stone trivet
He lifted and flung the woman.
The warrior upheld by the spirit Shay
Revealed himself a true lion;
When he struck the woman unerringly,
She split in two through the middle.
Its haft was steel, its point was sharp,
Black hardening ran along its edge,
Its blade gaped a handspan wide
And spread blood wherever it struck.
Laid upon grass, it set it aflame;
Those it struck died at once.
Its crown was like a mace’s fist,
And its haft was fashioned of steel;
When swung, its edge glowed red,
And no place it touched remained unharmed.
So that its victim would fester and rot,
So that death would overtake
All whom it touched without exception,
Bölökbay the master smith
Tempered it sixty times
In a dragon’s venom
And honed its edge keen.
Saying its blade was hard as crystal,
He polished it again and again;
The noble smith Bölökbay
Forged it together with Davut.
He snatched up and swung Aibalta,
The battle-axe left by great-hearted Manas,
And from the shrieking woman
He struck off the head.
