No mortal can deny it.
His fortunes were perfectly balanced;
When you looked into his heart,
It was broader than the whole earth.
God will never create another man
Made like your father.
Your father was a fierce gray-maned lion,
Who tethered fourteen steeds,
And dwelt in central Keng-Kol.
If an enemy came out shooting,
He drove them before him.
Whoever stirred his wrath,
He crushed between his fangs.
My son, such is my story.
Your dear father’s confidant,
The noble Choro, his companion,
Whose stallion carried him to Altai,
Was your father’s kinsman.
They kicked together like foals,
And contended for the same breast.
Liver and choice cuts
Were divided equally for them,
And the countless Nogai and Kyrgyz
Called them the foremost of sons.
So that we might become true brothers,
Our mother Kanykei gave both of us
Milk from her own breast.
From the left breast Kanchoro sucked,
Dark crimson blood flowed.
From the right breast Külchoro sucked,
Pure white milk welled like a spring.
“I have seen an omen in the boys,”
Your sorrowful mother wept.
“Külchoro, for whom milk sprang forth”—
These were my mother Kanykei’s words—
“If Külchoro survives
And you heed my words from the start,
He will be your inheritance and support.
But this Kanchoro here,
To tell you the truth,
Is a cursed and grievous menace;
It shows in your blood-uncle.”
The Jediger surged in wildly,
And mighty Kyyaz emerged as their chief.
The enemy among us was Kanchoro,
Who became a man of infamous name.
He shattered my happiness
And destroyed my golden throne.
He left my people wailing
And broke my back.
He felled my flourishing tree,
And tore down all my dwellings.
He made widows of braided women,
And trampled proud young lords
Underfoot like loose wool.
He bloodied the mouth of my gateway
And turned my black road to dust.
Many powerful lionhearted warriors
He utterly swept away.
At Talas he slaughtered
My giant, Semetey.
He battered my head again and again,
And brought me endless grief.
My helpless people were plundered;
I do not know whether he lives or is dead—
My uncle, heroic Bakai.
He left my land of Talas desolate
And scattered my Kyrgyz people.
He ignored all my pleas
And cut down my remaining men with swords.
In his day your Külchoro
Conquered Beijing and grew mighty,
But parted from his hero,
Your hapless uncle fell into disgrace.
Your father, the formidable Semetey,
Was a lion favored by the spirits.
God will not soon create
Among mortal men
Another like your father.
He was a mighty, steadfast stronghold.
