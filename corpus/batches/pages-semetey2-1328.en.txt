That retainer who was calamity itself,
The pale retainer who slew his own kin;
Greasing his bowels with fat,
When his stubborn rage seized him,
He destroyed his own elder brother.
No goodness or manly honor
Remained in Kanchoro.
Kanchoro himself was a lofty slave,
With a treacherous heart and sullen nature—
Such a son had come among mankind.
When frenzy overcame him,
He destroyed his own close kin
And made a bondwoman of the mother
Of his heroic kinsman who faced death.
The wretched son of a dog—
A truly corrupted nightmare;
Such a deed had never happened in an age.
To subject surpassingly beautiful Aichurok
To still greater torment,
And take that radiant beauty
As plunder for himself,
He urged the Jediger onward.
When the bloodstained warrior vanished,
The Jediger people laughed
At the frenzied swine.
The deep black earth beneath them
Endured the great weight
When Choro roared.
When that Choro strutted,
He seemed lord of earth and water;
When the broad brute barked,
He seemed master of the land and streams.
The doomed man's fury
Was like a giant in his swaggering;
When the madman barked,
He seemed a wrestler greater than the world.
His appearance and bearing
Were like a tyrant ruling a nation;
When the madman lurched about,
He seemed a man bent on ruining a people.
See how he strutted—
Like Kozkaman of old.
He roared in wrath
At the great host of people;
Taking the bloodstained lion as his model,
The madman had grown savage.
He summoned the whole multitude,
Crying, “Come, every one of you!”
Looking at him made the heart recoil.
The madman, possessed by a spirit,
Loomed like a towering mountain;
The blue flame issuing from his mouth
Poured out in gusts.
He meant to plunder the nation,
To subject his people to misery,
To erase the name of Semetey
And remain as sultan himself,
Taking both reins and the single bridle
Somehow into his own hands.
Kanchoro, brother to corruption,
When every other scheme failed,
Meant to remain sultan in Talas.
Fluent-tongued Kanchoro—
It is true he was conspicuous;
The most insinuating smooth-talker,
It is also true that Choro's conduct
Was unlike any other person's.
That Choro of treacherous intent,
Kanchoro, raising a battle cry, said:
“You many Black Jediger,
If you will not heed wise counsel,
If you do not know your place,
Then you have eaten filth!
Babbling two at a time,
What else did you say?
As for Churok, Akun's daughter,
That surpassingly beautiful wonder,
That marvelous woman—
