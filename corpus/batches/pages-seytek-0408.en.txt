As I prepare to leave this mortal world,
I have drawn forth my true blade.
I tested it by pinching every part,
And finding not a flaw as small as a fly,
I myself tempered it in earnest.
All my skill and remaining strength—
My child, I spent upon it.
Then he went away, weeping bitterly.
From the old man’s eyes
Dark blood poured like hail;
He screamed and wept aloud,
Kissing the hilt and sobbing.
Long ago, Bölökbay left
As an inheritance the white Tintay,
With a jeweled pommel and golden hilt,
And a golden sheath to hold it.
He twisted and tested its hilt,
Never ceasing to fear
That it might slip from the hand.
He often spoke of its noble form:
“I shall draw it against the foe I meet”—
Kanykey herself had made this vow.
When your mother escaped from slavery
And God granted her desire,
Aichürök, daughter of the peri,
Prepared to present Seytek
At the rock of Kayypchy,
As she had planned from the beginning.
The white Tintay, willingly bestowed—
“Let the children receive it as a gift,
So that upon the black-faced enemy
They may bring sudden ruin.”
The white Tintay given as a gift,
For Aichürök, champion among women,
To prepare against the wrathful khan,
Ready to spill blood;
With matchless agility,
She ran beyond all reckoning,
Bounding and gathering herself like a doe.
The white Tintay received as a gift—
May your enemy behold its fame;
May this wondrous blade become
A guardian of a precious life.
If swung with all one’s strength,
The foe it truly strikes will surely die...
Whoever feels the breath
Of its unyielding true temper will know it.
My precious kinswoman, I shall agree,
To take the son of your eldest son,
And preserve your battle cry.
When cataracts veil my darkening eyes,
When I have grown old,
My swan-white beard turned pale,
My raven eyes turned red,
My able body worn away,
My steel blunted and spent,
When I am no longer capable;
When I have eaten and drunk my fill,
And my kinswoman has given me my shroud,
When my time has come to depart;
When my breast grows cold and my eyes close,
When my time has come to depart;
When my fleeting soul leaves
This mortal world, my child;
When, without looking back on its pleasures,
My time has come to depart;
I shall obey your request
And prepare my final bequest.
Accept these words entrusted to you,
And to Semetey’s gray-maned-wolf son
Give it as a gift.
I honed its edge like a falcon’s claw;
So that whoever it touches will not remain unharmed,
But be struck down and die,
I watered it with the tears
Of mortal humankind,
And bound it with a ravenous demon’s spell.
I poisoned its other edge as well.
For the orphan left behind,
I have kept it, that he may receive it.
