Such is this wretched world,”
Said Külchoro, and thus
He finished his sorrowful account,
His heart choked with bitter grief.
The gray-wolf hero mounted his horse,
And from his falconlike, beloved eyes
Tears fell without a blink;
Grief-stricken, he folded his hands
And broke into ringing sobs.
As Külchoro stood weeping,
Old Kyzylsart began to cry,
And all the gathered people cried;
Overcome, the hero Seytek cried,
And all who understood cried,
Weeping that such bleak calamity
Could befall a man.
Then the hero Seytek’s
Eyes blazed like a lion’s;
Angrily he ground his teeth,
Wrathfully preparing himself,
And to the enraged Seytek
Wisdom came from the unseen.
The keen gray wolf grew restless,
And the mature Seytek roused himself:
“May my poor head never flinch;
If I do not choose and mount Taiküröң,
If this year I do not charge the foe unafraid,
If I do not beat the madness
From the hateful enemy’s skull,
If I do not wreak a glorious slaughter,
If I do not avenge this year
My father who passed into eternity,
May I die before I am born,
May I sink away unborn!
If the son of a prince
Cannot shatter the flood and move his people,
Cannot shake the world and take revenge,
Cannot overthrow the chief and spill his blood,
Cannot fight and take lives,
Cannot thrust his spear and break their ranks—
The mighty Toltoi and Chynkojo
We destroyed in carnage.
When we met them in battle
And slew their heroes,
The warrior most skilled with the spear died,
And the mightiest man in all the world died.
Many khans who came were slain,
And everyone we encountered died.
Over branching passes and high slopes
That was the road we used to travel;
Those whom we fought, your uncle
Swept away with his army.
While the great warrior lived,
Your lionhearted uncle was a hero,
A lion and gray wolf of a champion
Who made the whole world submit.
The one who crushed all challengers—
Alas, that Choro was I.
My fortune failed, my strength departed,
The panther, the gray-wolf hero, was gone;
My days passed in servitude,
And now, my son, brave Seytek,
I stand before you ruined thus.
While the panther lived,
I carved up whole fat-tailed sheep to eat.
I used to wonder where hell,
The greatest misery and disaster, could be.
While my magnanimous lord lived,
I knew the world and filled it with uproar;
When I lost my generous lord,
I suffered torment and withered away.
When I rode to battle, I lacked nothing,
And carved up fat-tailed sheep to eat;
While my mighty uncle lived,
I used to say I would wreak slaughter
Not only among the Kyrgyz, but across the world.
There is no strength left in the waist I gird,
My heroic, generous champion is gone;
What lives today is gone tomorrow.
One day all will be crushed—
